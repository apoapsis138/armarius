# Tribunal sheet
<!-- help: tribunal -->

A Tribunal is the council of a region's magi, meeting every seven years
(Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 2: The Order of Hermes). Its
sheet holds what belongs to the Tribunal itself: its name, how often and
where it next meets, its Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor, and how big it
really is. Everything else is worked out from its covenants.

A Tribunal follows either the core rules or one region's customs, from
that region's sourcebook, never a mix. Choose them as **Customs** in Edit
Tribunal. A region's customs are offered while its book is in play.
Customs can add lists of their own to the sheet, add a column to the
roster, and could change when notice of a case is due, though none of
the books' customs does. Switching customs hides
what the old ones recorded, and switching back shows it again. The
Rhine, Theban, Transylvanian, Levant, Greater Alps, Stonehenge, Novgorod
and Hibernian books' Tribunals of 1220 ship as content packs (see [How to
start a saga from a sourcebook](help:guide-book-packs)).

- **Core rules.** The Definitive Edition's Tribunal. Notice of a case is
  due a season before the meeting: three months, from Houses of Hermes:
  True Lineages p. 56, since the Definitive Edition gives no period.
- **Rhine: Gilds** (Guardians of the Forests). The Rhine's magi band
  together in gilds, each with its leaders and agenda. The sheet gains a
  Gilds list, and the roster a Gild column. Notice is due as under the
  core rules.
- **Theban: The Polity** (The Sundered Eagle), in the book's terms.
  - Every magus present votes once, by voting sigil, so Your votes counts
    as under the core rules.
  - Each covenant belongs to one of four phylai (Ionian, Aegean, Kretan,
    Propontian). Choose it on the covenant's own form or with the
    roster's Edit. The roster's Phyle column shows it.
  - The meeting follows the book's four phases, from the Hermetic
    Assembly to the Council of Archai (see [Hold
    Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)). A magus made a citizen at this meeting
    is never drawn for the archai.
  - Thebes has no Presiding Quaesitor, so its quorum needs none. The
    tamias is paid at the end of the term as the arche they are (The
    Sundered Eagle pp. 25, 32).
  - The sheet gains [The Polity](help:tab-trib-tokens).
- **Normandy: Vis tenure** (The Lion and the Lily). Every vis source is
  held on a tenure of the Perthean Compact, and the covenants' teams
  contest the Hermetic Tourney after each meeting. The sheet gains Tenure
  and Tourney.
  - Its eldest magus is Praeco. While the seat is empty the sheet's
    Praeco says **Vacant**, and the next meeting names one.
  - Each meeting ends by drawing the next host by lot.
  - New Covenant and Found a Covenant Here say what vis a covenant founded
    in it must hold.
- **Transylvania: Privileges** (Against the Dark). Its magi live in
  **oppida**, the places other Tribunals call covenants. Its roster, its
  oppida's sheets and its magi's sheets say Oppidum.
  - Each magus belongs to one of the five legal covenants the Sundering
    left it, and that covenant's privilege says what they may do. The
    members of one oppidum hold different privileges by rule.
  - The sheet gains [The Five Covenants](help:tab-trib-privileges), and a
    magus's sheet shows their privilege under their oppidum.
  - Each oppidum's aediles are chosen from the magi who live there, on
    its own form (Edit Covenant). The roster gains an Aediles column.
  - Saves that gave a covenant a class give it to each of that oppidum's
    magi.
- **Provence: Open founding** (Faith & Flame). Any magus may found a
  covenant without the Tribunal's leave. It's a rule, not a list, so it
  shows as a note under Peripheral Code.
- **Levant: The Treaty of Baghdad** (Blood and Sand). Its magi keep out of
  Crusade and jihad under a treaty renewed every ten years. Renewal is a
  motion like any other. Give its ruling a term, and the app puts it back
  on the docket as the term ends. The treaty shows as a note under
  Peripheral Code.
- **Greater Alps: Chairs and ostracism** (Sanctuary of Ice).
  - A magus votes only if their covenant has bought them a **chair**,
    registering ten pawns of vis a year for their upkeep with the
    Quaesitores at least a year before the meeting. A magus with no chair
    is a vagrant, a Low Crime.
  - Each covenant's form (Edit Covenant) sets its chairs. The roster
    gains a Chairs column beside its magi ("3 of 4").
  - The sheet gains [Ostracism](help:section-trib-ostracism).
- **Stonehenge: The meeting a year late** (Heirs to Merlin). Since 1201 it
  meets a year behind the rest of the Order, always at Blackthorn. A
  meeting may close and open again, holding two Tribunals, and the
  Quaesitores have an unGifted Redcap cast their sigils. It's a rule, not
  a list, so it shows as a note under Peripheral Code.
- **Novgorod: The Royal Embargo** (The Dragon and the Bear). No member may
  reveal themselves to the Russian or Polish royal houses, and the council
  decides where any new covenant in Slavic lands may settle. It's a rule,
  not a list, so it shows as a note under Peripheral Code.
- **Hibernia: Cathaigh and ambassadors** (The Contested Isle).
  - A covenant is represented only while it shows its **cathach**, the
    trophy it took when it was founded. Each covenant's form (Edit
    Covenant) says what its cathach is and who holds it if it was taken.
    For a covenant being founded, it gives the year it began holding its
    land, cattle and cathach. It's recognised a year later.
  - The roster gains a Cathach column, saying what it is, who took it, or
    when a new covenant is recognised. The roll call asks whether each
    covenant shows it (see [Attendance](help:section-meeting-attendance)).
  - New Covenant and Found a Covenant Here say what founding asks. Hibernia
    recognises no vassal covenants, so a covenant's Edit refuses a liege
    in the same Tribunal.
  - The sheet gains [Ambassadors](help:tab-trib-ambassadors).
  - Its meeting opens with the Sacred Council and ends with the tithe of
    Gifted children, whom the Coill Trí owe the Order (see [Hold
    Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)).
  - Its other customs are noted under Peripheral Code.

Older sagas typed a covenant's Tribunal as a name. Each name became a
Tribunal of its own when this version first opened the saga, and
covenants that typed the same name share it.

## The Tribunal
<!-- help: section-trib-info -->

The facts under the Tribunal's name:

- **Customs**: the core rules, or the region's customs it follows.
- **Meets every**: the years between its meetings, seven unless its
  customs say otherwise.
- **Next meeting** and **Meeting place**: when and where it next meets.
- **Praeco** and **Presiding Quaesitor**: chosen from the saga's
  characters, at any detail.
- **Covenants** and **Magi**: how many are recorded here, and of how many
  in all when you've said. Most are never written down.
- **Your votes**: the troupe's covenants' votes, proxies included, of
  every vote in the hall.
- **Cases**: how many are on the docket, and how many are against the
  troupe's covenants or their magi.

## Roster
<!-- help: tab-trib-roster -->

Every covenant of the Tribunal, the troupe's first, and the magi worth
keeping an eye on.

### Covenants
<!-- help: section-trib-roster -->

Each covenant of the Tribunal: how much of it is written down, its magi
(named or not), its votes at the next meeting, the proxies it holds, and
its attitude toward the troupe's covenant. The troupe's covenants come
first, marked ✦, above an "Other covenants" line. When the Tribunal's
size says there are more covenants than are recorded, a last row counts
them and the magi they hold. A Tribunal whose book has its own word for a
covenant uses it, so a Transylvanian Tribunal's list is headed Oppida.

A Transylvanian roster has an **Oppidum** column. It says "lawful", or
what the oppidum breaks of the Tribunal's rulings (Against the Dark
pp. 15-16):

- fewer than two members
- all of one House
- no cives
- the wrong number of aediles. It should have two where its cives and its
  other magi are as many, else one. The book's "even numbers" is read as
  equal ones.
- an aedile from the dominant group, cives against the rest

An oppidum with no named magi shows nothing. While a resident's privilege
isn't entered, or the oppidum has unnamed magi, the counts that need them
aren't checked. The aedile fields on the oppidum's own form say how many
it should have.

A covenant has one vote for each of its magi, and one for each proxy it
carries. A proxy is the sigil of an absent magus, given for one meeting.
An unGifted Redcap has no vote of their own at any Tribunal, while a
Gifted one votes (DE p. 15). A Redcap carrying an absent magus's sigil
casts that vote, so count it among their covenant's proxies. Proxies
change at every meeting, so they're kept here rather than on the
covenant.

### Magi of Note
<!-- help: section-trib-magi -->

The Tribunal's Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor, the magi of its covenants
the troupe doesn't play, and anyone prosecuting or defending a case on
its docket. Each shows their House, covenant and part in the Tribunal.
Edit a magus on their covenant's sheet or their own.

## History
<!-- help: tab-trib-history -->

How the Tribunal came to be, era by era. It's kept apart from In the Saga
(what the Tribunal is like now) so that stays short. Write it in the
History field of the Tribunal's own form. A Tribunal book fills it in.

Under the Tribunal's name the sheet shows only the first paragraph of In
the Saga, so the roster stays in view. When it runs longer, the rest is
shown here first, as **In the Saga, continued**, above the history.

## Docket
<!-- help: tab-trib-docket -->

The cases the Tribunal will hear at its next meeting, and those heard or
withdrawn before.

### Cases
<!-- help: section-trib-cases -->

Each charge, motion or appeal for the next meeting, with when it was
filed, when notice of it is due (as its Tribunal's
[customs](help:tribunal) say), and what is sought. A case whose notice
hasn't been given is flagged. A case against one of the troupe's
covenants or magi is drawn in red.

What a case records:

- **Case**: its name, as the docket lists it.
- **Kind**. A *charge* accuses someone of breaking the Code. A *motion*
  asks the Tribunal to decide or declare something, such as a new ruling,
  a boundary or recognising a covenant. An *appeal* asks it to reverse an
  earlier ruling. Two more come from Hermetic law, and neither needs
  notice:
  - a *Wizard's War*, a declaration of war kept on the docket
  - a *Wizard's March*, one a Quaesitor called between meetings, for the
    next Tribunal to ratify (Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 63)
- **Brought by** and **Against**: who brings it and who it's against, as
  names. A covenant or magus of the troupe's named in Against is flagged.
- **Victim**: who the crime was done to, when that isn't whoever brought
  the case. Blank means the one who brought it. Victim and prosecutor are
  separate roles. Anyone may bring the case of a slain magus, and a
  Quaesitor may prosecute a high crime (Houses of Hermes: True Lineages
  p. 56). Not asked of a Wizard's War.
- **Charge**: what is alleged or proposed, in a sentence or two.
- **Filed**: the year and season it was filed.
- **Notice given**: whether the parties were told of it in time, by the
  Notice due column's season.
- **Sought**: what the one bringing it asks the Tribunal to do if it
  succeeds. For a charge that's the penalty, such as a fine of vis or
  silver, the loss of a right, or a Wizard's March. For a motion or appeal
  it's the ruling wanted. Leave it empty while nobody has said.
- **Evidence**: what each side can show, one piece a line.
- **Prosecutor** and **Defender**: who speaks for each side at the
  meeting.
- **Status** and **Outcome** (when editing): open, decided or withdrawn,
  and what was decided. A decided or withdrawn case moves to the Rulings
  of Past Tribunals.

A charge's details say whether the Code of Hermes makes it a high crime
(Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 46):

- depriving a magus of magic
- slaying a magus
- defying the Tribunal
- abusing votes
- endangering the Order
- meddling with mundanes
- dealing with devils
- molesting the fay
- scrying on magi
- failing a parens's duty
- Bonisagus's rights and duty to share
- attacking the Order's allies or aiding its enemies

Only a high crime lets the prosecution call for a Wizard's March. The app
reads the charge's words, so it can be wrong. The Presiding Quaesitor's
word is the rule.

A charge whose words name a low crime of the Peripheral Code says so too
(Houses of Hermes: True Lineages):

- reading a text marked with another's sanctum or House mark (p. 71)
- reading Quaesitorial texts (p. 70)
- not helping a Quaesitor's investigation (p. 62)
- perjury, deceiving a Quaesitor, breaking a contract, or an assault short
  of life or magic (p. 56)
- vagrancy (p. 49)
- keeping a Gifted child as a lab assistant (p. 52)

**Wizard's War.** File a declaration as a case of the Wizard's War kind:

- **Brought by** is the magus who declares it.
- **Against** is the magus it's declared on. It names one magus, so to war
  on a covenant, declare on each of its magi.
- **Filed** is the season it arrived.
- **Arrived at the full moon of month** is which of the season's three
  full moons (1 to 3).

The war begins at the next full moon and lasts until the one after. That
gives a month to prepare, then a month of war (Ars Magica Definitive
Edition p. 21).

**Served** says how the declaration reached its target (HoH: Societates
p. 23):

- by a Redcap, who is its legal witness (HoH: True Lineages p. 47)
- into their hands
- nailed to their sanctum door while they were at the covenant, which
  most Tribunals hold duly served
- to their representative or forwarding address
- while they were away and named no one, which a Tribunal that asks magi
  to name a representative treats as wilfully ignored
- not delivered, when the messenger tells a Quaesitor, who rules whether
  it stands

A war stays on the docket, but no meeting hears it unless **Its service is
disputed** is ticked or it wasn't delivered. Otherwise its details read
"on the docket, not heard" instead of the next meeting's season.

The same two magi can't be at war again until the first war has ended.
So a renewed declaration gives a month of war, then a month of peace (HoH:
Societates p. 23). A magus may be at war with several others at once.

The details say when the war runs, and whether it's to come, this season
or over. They also say what the Code allows (HoH: Societates pp. 22-23;
HoH: True Lineages p. 47). Either magus may slay the other without
retribution, and neither is charged for declaring it. Harm to other magi
or to property shared with them stays a crime, as do scrying on or
attacking those not at war.

A magus who has declared three or more wars in seven years is warned of.
Unjust or constant use can be charged as endangering the Order, even
Marched (DE p. 21; HoH: True Lineages p. 48). The three in seven years is
a storyguide's default the app chose, not a rule. No book says how often
is too often (DE p. 21; HoH: Societates p. 24 speaks only of extreme
abuse).

A war being fought shows on both magi's Hermetic Background. When it's
over, **War's End** records how (see [War's End](help:wars-end)).

**Spell traces.** A charge, motion, appeal or March can record a spell
trace found as evidence. Give **Spell trace: the spell's level**, its
**Duration**, whether it was **a Ritual**, and **months since it ended**,
blank while the spell is still active. The details give the trace's
magnitude now (HoH: True Lineages p. 71; Hooks p. 83):

- It's half the spell's magnitude when the spell ends.
- It drops one for each of the spell's Durations that passes. A Momentary
  spell's falls each round.
- It then goes into negative magnitudes a month at a time for a
  non-Ritual Momentary, Concentration or Diameter spell. Any other spell's
  goes a year at a time, a Moon or Year spell among them.
- It's gone at -10.

Neither book says how to round the halving. The app rounds down, its own
assumption, and the details say so. They give the Acute Sense roll too,
to notice deceptive magic over the trace: Perception + Penetration +
stress die against 6 + the spell's magnitude (p. 71).

**Intrigue.** **Intrigue by** names who works on the case before the
meeting, and **Intrigue: to** what they seek. Each aim has its Ease Factor
(HoH: True Lineages p. 24):

- **6**: learning an open aim, or keeping someone on their course.
- **9**: learning allies, rousing suspicion, holding someone up, or
  bringing them to meet a rival.
- **12**: learning a hidden agenda, setting wavering allies against each
  other, changing a course, or settling a small dispute.
- **15**: learning hidden partners, breaking a trusted alliance, leading
  someone against their own good, or settling a great dispute.

To sway is Presence + Intrigue, and to learn Communication + Intrigue,
each with a stress die. **Intrigue: both under Parma Magica** is ticked to
start, as between magi at a Tribunal, and The Gift then costs nothing.
Untick it when either party isn't under Parma, such as a mundane or a
magus whose Parma is down. The Gift then costs 3, or 6 for a Blatant Gift.
Tick **Intrigue: his Gift is Blatant** for that, or give the character the
Blatant Gift Flaw (p. 24). Folk Ken rolls through the Tribunal may give
-3 to +3 to Intelligence + Intrigue at the storyguide's word (p. 25).

### War's End
<!-- help: wars-end -->

**War's End** (More on a Wizard's War on the docket) records how the war
came out, with a line on what happened:

- the month of war ran out with both alive
- the declarer slew their foe
- the foe slew the declarer
- they made peace

The war leaves the docket and is kept among the past rulings with its
outcome. A magus of the saga who keeps House Acclaim by House Flambeau's
table earns it there. Fighting bravely earns 1 to 3, and winning earns
twice the foe's highest Reputation (HoH: Societates p. 31). The foe's is
filled in when they're of the saga. Both magi's Diaries and the
Tribunal's record the war. A slain magus isn't marked dead on their
sheet. Do that as the saga does.

### Rulings of Past Tribunals
<!-- help: section-trib-past -->

The cases past meetings decided, with their outcome, and those withdrawn.
Edit one to correct it, or set its Status back to open to return it to
the docket.

## Arguments
<!-- help: tab-trib-arguments -->

Shown while Transforming Mythic Europe is in play. An argument is a
position someone means to win at a coming meeting, argued beforehand in
writing. A polemic sets it out, diatribes attack it, and apologia defend
it (Transforming Mythic Europe pp. 27-35). Choose an argument to see its
total and its texts, and choose a text to read it in the panel below.

### The arguments
<!-- help: section-trib-arguments -->

Each argument with its reach, its proponent, its Argument Total and the
debate bonus it buys ("−7: −3"). It says "not out" while its polemic isn't
circulated.

What an argument records:

- **Argument**: the position, in a line.
- **Its ideal**: what its texts' Reputations name. That's Supporter of
  (ideal) for a polemic or apologia, and Opposed to (ideal) for a diatribe
  (pp. 31, 33, 35). Blank means the argument itself.
- **It concerns**: its reach on the Prevailing Wisdom Table (p. 29):
  - a magus 3, or their Reputation if higher (**His Reputation**)
  - a covenant 6
  - a Tribunal 9
  - a House 12
  - the Order 15
- **Proponent**: who argues it.
- **Written on**: any knowledge Ability (p. 28). The book's suggestions
  come first: Code of Hermes, Order of Hermes Lore, and Area Lore with the
  troupe's agreement. Then come the other Academic and Arcane Abilities
  and the Lores.
- **Goes to**: the case or motion on the Docket where it's debated.
- **It backs**: the case or motion (its prosecutor or proposer), or the
  defence against it. A case or motion hears one argument for each side,
  and a second is refused. Adding two arguments' bonuses together would
  be a house rule, so answer the first with a diatribe or apologia
  instead.
- **Last debate** (editing): not yet debated, won, or lost. Holding the
  debate sets it.

### The chosen argument
<!-- help: section-trib-argument -->

It shows the argument's reach and Prevailing Wisdom, its proponent, and
its Argument Total worked out (pp. 31, 33). The total is the polemic's
Quality − the Prevailing Wisdom, + each circulated apologia's Quality, −
each circulated diatribe's, whoever wrote it. There's one total per
argument. It also shows what the argument does in debate, what the next
text still circulating would make it, and where it's debated.

At the hearing the total is spent as experience on the Art table (DE
p. 49). 1 buys +1, 3 buys +2, 6 buys +3 and 10 buys +4. A negative total
buys a penalty the same way. The bonus goes on every Attack and Defense
roll of the side the argument backs, on top of Houses of Hermes:
Societates's testimony and the rest. Won, the bonus carries to later
Tribunals. Lost, at that Tribunal or a later one, it's gone (p. 35).
Choosing not to debate it keeps the total for the next meeting.

### Texts
<!-- help: section-trib-argument-texts -->

The chosen argument's polemic, diatribes and apologia. A text is a link to
a book in the saga's library, not a copy. Its Quality and level are read
from the book, so add the summa or tractatus to the library first. It may
belong to no one. A tractatus counts as level 5 (p. 28).

What a text records:

- **Kind**: one polemic per argument, written first. Diatribes and
  apologia answer it.
- **Book**: a summa or tractatus on the argument's Ability.
- **Author**: blank takes the book's author. When the text is added, a
  diatribe's author gains Opposed to (ideal) 1, and an apologia's
  Supporter of (ideal) 1. It's gained "on writing or circulating"
  (pp. 33, 35). An author who has it already gains 5 experience toward it.
- **Studied the work it answers**: a diatribe or apologia is written only
  after a season studying the work it answers (pp. 31, 35). It can't be
  circulated until this is ticked.
- **Circulated to**: a Tribunal (6 copies), a House (9) or the Order (18).
- **Circulated by**: a sponsor who circulates it with their own
  Reputation (p. 29). Blank means its author.
- **Seasons spent circulating**, **New works bound with it**: each adds
  to the next Circulation roll (see [Circulate a Text](help:circulate-text)).
- **Circulated**, **Out since**: whether it's out, and when.

More's **Circulate a Text** rolls a season's circulation.

## Circulate a Text
<!-- help: circulate-text -->

A season spent circulating the chosen text (Transforming Mythic Europe
pp. 29-31). The roll is a simple die plus:

- the circulator's highest Reputation, good or bad, House Acclaim among
  them
- the text's level, 5 for a tractatus
- 1 for each season already spent
- 1 for each new work bound into every copy

It's against 15 for a Tribunal, 18 for a House or 24 for the Order. Each
season is a distraction of 5, 10 or 15 days.

Short of it, the season counts +1 toward the next roll. Once out, the text
counts toward the Argument Total, and Reputations are gained:

- A polemic's author gains Supporter of (ideal) 1 (p. 31).
- A diatribe's or apologia's author had theirs on writing it (see
  [Texts](help:section-trib-argument-texts)). A circulator who isn't its
  author gains it now (pp. 33, 35).
- One who has it already gains 5 experience toward it instead, Hermetic,
  reaching the Tribunal, the House or the Order.
- No one gains it twice from the same text.

## Meeting
<!-- help: tab-trib-meeting -->

Holding the Tribunal: the meeting being held, or, before it opens, the
order of business the next one would hold. At the top:

- **Now** names the step in hand and its number ("Step 5 of 12").
- **Then** names the step after it.
- **To go on** says how to hold it.
- **Present** and **Quorum** say who is in the hall.

See [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal) for how the steps run, and [How
to hold a Tribunal](help:guide-tribunal).

<!-- gui -->
At the top of the chapter is **The Hall**, the meeting drawn as a
hemicycle with one seat for each vote. A covenant's seats sit together
under its pennant, showing its arms if it bears any. Each seat shows its
magus's House and is coloured by the side its vote went on the case in
hand. The magi who spoke in the debate stand at the centre, with a pair
of scales weighing the tally. **Call the Vote** calls the covenants one by
one in the order of the roll, filling their seats as the Praeco calls
them. When **Hold** opens a meeting, a card announces the year, the
Tribunal and where it meets, the arms of its covenants, and its numbers.
A key or a click enters the hall.
<!-- /gui -->

### Order of Business
<!-- help: section-meeting-business -->

The meeting's steps, day by day:

- the first day: the roll call and the Opening
- each case and motion on the day the Opening set for it: its hearing,
  then its vote, unless it was settled at the private hearing
- the last day: the rulings, the treasury and the Closing

A step held is marked ✓, and the step in hand ▶. The chosen step is
described under the list. First comes what was entered at it, then what
the app works out at it and what each entry changes.

At a hearing each side's argument, one at most, adds its debate bonus or
penalty, and the form lists them. Holding the hearing marks each won or
lost (see [The chosen argument](help:section-trib-argument)).

<!-- tui -->

### The Vote
<!-- help: section-meeting-vote -->

The vote on the case in hand, or the last one voted on. Each covenant is

## Treasury
<!-- help: tab-trib-treasury -->

What the Tribunal holds in common, and what is owed to it.

### The accounts
<!-- help: section-trib-treasury -->

Each entry is vis, silver, a loan or a fine, and Add asks which first.

- **Vis** and **silver** are taken in (a positive amount) or paid out (a
  negative one).
- **A loan** is silver or vis lent out of the treasury. It leaves the
  treasury until it's marked repaid.
- **A fine**, from a case, comes in once it's marked paid.

Most entries are typed in by the storyguide. A meeting adds one by itself
when a case is decided with a fine. The fine owed is entered, naming who
owes it and the case. A new Tribunal, or one from a content pack, starts
with an empty treasury, since the books give no figures.

The balance above the list adds them up: the silver and vis in hand, the
loans still out and the fines still unpaid.

A Theban Tribunal's balance also gives its silver in the Byzantine
emperors' coins (The Sundered Eagle pp. 44-45):

- the gold hyperpyron, the Westerners' bezant: a sixth of a pound, 40
  pence
- the electrum aspron, a third of a hyperpyron
- the billon, a sixteenth of an aspron, a little under a penny
- the copper tetarteron, an eighteenth of a billon

The Venetian grosso the Latins trade in is 2 pence, twenty to the bezant.
Theban loans of vis are made with Lend Vis under The Polity, and the
common vis comes in at each meeting. The treasury itself lies under a
leaden trapdoor on Delos, opened only with the archon's iron key, and
holds several hundred pawns (p. 77).

In Transylvania (Against the Dark p. 19) Add also offers two more:

- **A levy**, owed to the treasury by an oppidum for the Praeco's projects
  and counted once paid.
- **A promissory note**, which the Praeco issued against the treasury's
  income. It's owed to its holder, and its pawns leave the treasury when
  it's redeemed.

The balance then shows the levies unpaid and the notes outstanding.

### The Great Library of Durenmar
<!-- help: section-trib-library -->

### Credit
<!-- help: section-trib-library-credit -->

## Search the Great Library
<!-- help: great-library-search -->

Choose the magus, what they seek, and its Art, Ability, spell or subject.
The Stacks, at the foot of the form, says what the die must show and what
each result means. **Roll** rolls it and records it in Searches (Guardians
of the Forests pp. 56-57):

- **A summa on an Art**: a simple die + 10, against the level wanted. Add
  +3 for a master, a Bonisagus or one of Durenmar's, or +6 for an archmage
  (their whole bonus), to at most 20.
  - Beat it: found.
  - Equal: a magus of equal rank wants it too, and certamen decides.
  - Below: none so high, or, likelier, one of higher rank is reading it
    this season. Every Art has summae of level 17 at least, so below that
    it's always in use.
- **A summa on an Arcane Ability**: a simple die alone against the level.
  Below it, the library has none.
- **Quality of a summa**: a simple die + 5, at most 30 - the level or 24,
  whichever is lower.
- **A tractatus**: no roll to find one. Its Quality is a simple die + 2,
  at most 12.
- These caps are suggestions, which the storyguide may raise with Edit on
  the search (see [Searches](help:section-trib-library-searches)).
- **Durenmar Lore** may be added to a book's Quality, -1 for each earlier
  time the magus added it in the same Art or Ability. The app counts those
  from their earlier searches.
- **A spell's Lab Text**: a stress die against 3 for a standard spell (in
  the core book), or 6 for a version of a general standard spell up to
  level 40. Any other is the troupe's 6 to 21.
  - A standard spell's failure means one of higher rank has it this
    season. Its botch means the library has none.
  - Any other's failure means none. Its botch finds one with a serious
    flaw.
  - Equal: certamen.
  - Each full 6 over finds a tractatus on the spell's Mastery, Quality a
    simple die + 2.
- **An enchanted device's Lab Text**: a stress die against the troupe's 6
  to 21. Meeting it finds a text for a very similar enchantment, and
  beating it by 6 the very one.
- **Lab notes**: a season, and Intelligence + Magic Theory + a stress die
  against the troupe's Ease Factor. Leave the Ease Factor blank for a
  named author's notes, found within days with no roll.
- **The Tribunal records**: leave the Ease Factor blank for a season's
  search, which extracts every entry on the subject with no roll. Give one
  to seek one entry, an Intelligence + Order of Hermes Lore roll a day, -1
  for each day after the first.

A summa or tractatus found is lent to the magus. It's added to the saga as
their book, marked as the library's, so the planner's Study from a Book
offers it.

A Library book never leaves the library (p. 55), so the loan covers only
the seasons of credit paid for it. That's the search's season if the
magus reads it then, and each season more paid with Edit's **Seasons of
credit paid**. An exempt magus pays nothing, but the loan still covers
only those seasons. A plan studying the book in any other season is
refused until a season more is paid. The book can't be copied or given to
the library for credit.

Reading what is found takes a season of the magus's credit, unless
they're exempt. That's everything but lab notes and the records. Untick
**He reads what is found this season** to search without reading. The
loan then covers no season until one is paid. A magus with no credit left
can't read, so Contribute first.

## Contribute to the Great Library
<!-- help: great-library-contribute -->

A magus gives the library one of these (Guardians of the Forests p. 55):

- a book or Lab Text, for two seasons of credit
- an exceptional work, for whatever the librarian grants, three by
  default
- a season copying, for one season
- a vis "donation", taken and never acknowledged. It stands in for a

## The Polity
<!-- help: tab-trib-tokens -->

A Theban Tribunal's own records, named as The Sundered Eagle names them:

### Tokens and Shards
<!-- help: section-trib-tokens -->

Each magus of note, with:

- their covenant
- their status: polites, a citizen who passed Gauntlet in the Tribunal,
  or metoikos, a resident foreigner. A metoikos is a guest, or a polites
  who has lost their rights.

- **Neutralize**, on the chosen magus, who must hold a shard. Another

<!-- help: section-trib-ostracism -->

Each ostracism the Tribunal has voted, with the magus, the meeting that

## The Five Covenants
<!-- help: tab-trib-privileges -->

A Transylvanian Tribunal's record of which legal covenant each magus
belongs to. Only a Tribunal following the Transylvanian customs has it.

### Each covenant's privileges
<!-- help: section-trib-privileges -->

Each magus, with the oppidum they live in, their privilege with its legal
covenant, and their offices. They're grouped by legal covenant, in order
of rank:

- **Cives (Coeris)**: full citizens. Any office, Praeco too, and they may
  take apprentices. Every Tremere belongs to Coeris.
- **Socii (Domostron)**: allies. Any office but Praeco.
- **Coloniae (Asclepius)**: settlers. No office but aedile, and
  apprentices by leave.
- **Hospites (Diodorus)**: hired specialists. Office and apprentices by
  leave.
- **Foederati (Pannonia)**: keep their own customs. Trade with outsiders
  by leave.

The Office column shows Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor from the Tribunal,
Aedile from the oppidum's own form, and any other office entered, such as
the Curantis. Add a magus, edit their entry or delete it. A magus has one
entry, so adding a second for them is refused.

The book ranks the five no further than cives first, and the app lists
them in this order. What a privilege says of office is checked (Against
the Dark pp. 15-18, 58):

- Only a cives, or a foederatus, who may stand for any office, may be
  Praeco. Edit Tribunal refuses a socius or colona there, and a hospes
  needs leave.
- A colona may hold no office but aedile, and the Curator of Tablinum must
  be a cives. This form refuses those offices.
- A hospes holds office only by the Praeco's leave, and their sheet's
  Office line says so. An aedile is chosen, not sought, so no leave is
  noted for that.

Each magus may wear one **Decoration**: Laurel, Myrtle, Oak, Grass or
Parsley, highest first. A higher replaces a lower, and a lower never
replaces a higher (Against the Dark p. 17).

A magus's sheet lists their privilege's other terms (pp. 15-18):

- What it forbids. A colona may not take the title archmagus, invite magi,
  or choose their aedile.
- What needs the Praeco's leave. For a hospes that's an apprentice,
  familiar, talisman, oppida and aedile vote. For a foederatus it's the
  archmagus title, oppida, invitations, and vis or item sales to
  outsiders.
- A hospes's familiar or talisman is noted as held by leave.

The Curator of Tablinum is chosen by the Tribunal's vote, and must be held
a master when chosen (p. 58).

### The Tourney
<!-- help: section-trib-tourney -->

Each team's result at each Tourney, the latest first. A result's

Hermetic Reputation of 2 for seven years. Teams level on points keep the

the senior first. The 21st team keeps 6 pawns and gives 6 to the teams
below it.

<!-- help: section-trib-diary -->

Entries newest first, each with its year and season, the numbers set in
its margin and the first line of its details. Add one, edit it or delete
it. A meeting writes its minutes here a step at a time, and its Acta when
it closes (see [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)).
