# Tribunal sheet
<!-- help: tribunal -->

A Tribunal is the council of the magi of a region, meeting every seven
years (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 2: The Order of Hermes).
Its sheet holds what belongs to the Tribunal itself: its name, how often
and where it next meets, its Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor, and how big
it really is. Everything else is worked out from the covenants that
belong to it.

A Tribunal follows either the core rules or one region's customs, from
that region's sourcebook, never a mix: choose them as **Customs** in Edit
Tribunal (a region's customs are offered while its book is in play).
The customs can add lists of their own to the sheet, a column to the
roster, and change when notice of a case is due. Switching customs keeps
what the old ones recorded, hidden, and switching back shows it again.

- **Core rules**: the Definitive Edition's Tribunal; notice of a case is
  due a season before the meeting.
- **Rhine: Gilds** (Guardians of the Forests): the Rhine's magi band
  together in gilds, each with its leaders and agenda; the sheet gains a Gilds list and
  the roster a Gild column. Notice is due a season before the meeting,
  as under the core rules.
  The book's Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content pack: see [How to start a
  saga from a Tribunal book](help:guide-book-packs).
- **Theban: The Polity** (The Sundered Eagle), in the book's terms: its
  magi are politai (citizens) or metoikoi (resident foreigners), and
  every magus present votes once, by voting sigil, so Your votes counts
  as under the core rules. A polites earns tokens for services to the
  community and shards for acts against its unity. Each covenant belongs
  to one of four phylai (Ionian, Aegean, Kretan, Propontian), chosen on
  the covenant's own form or with the roster's Edit, and shown in the
  roster's Phyle column. The Council of Magistrates (the archon, the
  polemarch, the logothete and the archai) is chosen at each meeting.
  The meeting follows the book's four phases: the Hermetic Assembly's
  decrees and ostrakismos on the first day, the Law Council's motions
  and the People's Court's cases, and on the last day the Council of
  Archai pays the outgoing magistrates, draws the new archai by
  sortition (never a magus made a citizen at this meeting) and elects
  the archon and polemarch (see [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)).
  Thebes has no Presiding Quaesitor, so its quorum needs none, and the
  tamias is paid at the end of the term as the arche he is (The Sundered
  Eagle pp. 25, 32). The sheet gains a Polity tab.
  The book's Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content pack: see [How to start a
  saga from a Tribunal book](help:guide-book-packs).
- **Normandy: Vis tenure** (The Lion and the Lily): every vis source is
  held on a tenure of the Perthean Compact, and the covenants' teams
  contest the Hermetic Tourney after each meeting; the sheet gains Tenure
  and Tourney tabs. Its eldest magus is Praeco: while the seat is empty
  the sheet's Praeco says **Vacant**, and the next meeting names one on
  its first day. Each meeting ends by drawing the next host by
  lot. New Covenant and Found a Covenant Here say what vis a covenant
  founded in it must hold.
- **Transylvania: Privileges** (Against the Dark): its magi live in
  **oppida**, the places other Tribunals call covenants, so its roster,
  its oppida's sheets and its magi's sheets say Oppidum. Each magus
  belongs to one of the five legal covenants the Sundering left it, and
  that covenant's privilege says what they may do; the members of one
  oppidum hold different privileges by rule. The sheet gains a Five
  Covenants tab, and a magus's sheet shows their privilege under their
  oppidum. Each oppidum's aediles are chosen on its own form (Edit
  Covenant), from the magi who live there, and the roster gains an
  Aediles column. Saves that gave a covenant a class give it to each of
  that oppidum's magi.
  The book's Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content pack: see [How to start a
  saga from a Tribunal book](help:guide-book-packs).
- **Provence: Open founding** (Faith & Flame): any magus may found a
  covenant without the Tribunal's leave. A rule, not a list: it shows as
  a note on the Peripheral Code tab.
- **Levant: The Treaty of Baghdad** (Blood and Sand): its magi keep out
  of Crusade and jihad under a treaty renewed every ten years. Renewal
  is a motion like any other: give its ruling a term, and the app puts
  it back on the docket as the term ends. A rule, not a list: it shows
  as a note on the Peripheral Code tab. The book's Tribunal of 1220
  ships as a content pack: see [How to start a saga from a Tribunal
  book](help:guide-book-packs).
- **Greater Alps: Chairs and ostracism** (Sanctuary of Ice): a magus
  votes only if his covenant has bought him a **chair**, registering ten
  pawns of vis a year for his upkeep with the Quaesitores at least a year
  before the meeting; one with none is a vagrant, a Low Crime. Each
  covenant's form (Edit Covenant) sets its chairs, and the roster gains a
  Chairs column beside its magi ("3 of 4"). At each meeting the magus
  most named on the sherds is ostracised for twenty-one years, if one
  magus in four named him: the sheet gains an Ostracism tab, and an
  ostracised magus's sheet says until when. The book's Tribunal of 1220
  ships as a content pack: see [How to start a saga from a Tribunal
  book](help:guide-book-packs).
- **Stonehenge: The meeting a year late** (Heirs to Merlin): since 1201
  it meets a year behind the rest of the Order, always at Blackthorn; a
  meeting may close and open again, holding two Tribunals, and the
  Quaesitores have an unGifted Redcap cast their sigils. A rule, not a
  list: it shows as a note on the Peripheral Code tab. The book's
  Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content pack.
- **Novgorod: The Royal Embargo** (The Dragon and the Bear): no member
  may reveal himself to the Russian or Polish royal houses, and the
  council decides where any new covenant in Slavic lands may settle. A
  rule, not a list: it shows as a note on the Peripheral Code tab. The
  book's Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content pack.
- **Hibernia: Cathaigh and ambassadors** (The Contested Isle): a covenant is represented
  only while it shows its **cathach**, the trophy it took when it was
  founded. Each covenant's form (Edit Covenant) says what its cathach is,
  who holds it if it was taken, and, for a covenant being founded, the
  year it began holding its land, cattle and cathach: it is recognised a
  year later. The roster gains a Cathach column, saying what it is, who
  took it, or when a new covenant is recognised. At a meeting,
  Attendance asks whether each covenant shows its cathach, ticked unless
  it was taken or the covenant is still being founded; one that doesn't
  has no votes, not even the proxies it carries. New Covenant and Found a
  Covenant Here say what founding asks. It recognises no vassal
  covenants, so a covenant's Edit refuses a liege in the same Tribunal.
  The sheet gains an Ambassadors tab: each supernatural faction's
  ambassador, who votes at the meeting once the Sacred Council confirms
  them, and the treaties that make much of its law. Its meeting opens
  with the Sacred Council and ends with the tithe of Gifted children,
  whom the Coill Trí owe the Order (see [Hold
  Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)). Its other customs are noted on the
  Peripheral Code tab. The book's Tribunal of 1220 ships as a content
  pack: see [How to start a saga from a sourcebook](help:guide-book-packs).

Older sagas typed a covenant's Tribunal as a name. Each name became a
Tribunal of its own when the saga was first opened by this version, and
covenants that typed the same name share it.

## The Tribunal
<!-- help: section-trib-info -->

The facts under the Tribunal's name:

- **Customs**: the core rules, or the region's customs it follows.
- **Meets every**: how many years between its meetings (seven, unless
  its customs say otherwise).
- **Next meeting** and **Meeting place**: when and where it next meets.
- **Praeco** and **Presiding Quaesitor**: chosen from the saga's
  characters, at any detail.
- **Covenants** and **Magi**: how many are recorded here, and of how many
  in all when you have said (most are never written down).
- **Your votes**: the troupe's covenants' votes, proxies included, of
  every vote in the hall.
- **Cases**: how many are on the docket, and how many are against the
  troupe's covenants or their magi.

## Roster tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-roster -->

Every covenant that belongs to the Tribunal, the troupe's first, and the
magi worth keeping an eye on.

### Covenants
<!-- help: section-trib-roster -->

Each covenant of the Tribunal: how much of it is written down, its magi
(named or not), its votes at the next meeting and the proxies it holds,
and its attitude toward the troupe's covenant. The troupe's covenants
come first, marked ✦, above an "Other covenants" line. When the Tribunal's size
says there are more covenants than are recorded, a last row counts them
and the magi they hold.
A Tribunal whose book has its own word for a covenant uses it: a
Transylvanian Tribunal's list is headed Oppida.

A Transylvanian roster has an **Oppidum** column: "lawful", or what the
oppidum breaks of the Tribunal's rulings: fewer than two members, all of
one House, no cives, the wrong number of aediles (two where its cives
and its other magi are as many, else one: the book's "even numbers" is
read as equal ones), or an aedile from the dominant group, cives against
the rest (Against the Dark pp. 15-16). An oppidum with no named magi
shows nothing; while a resident's privilege isn't entered, or the
oppidum has unnamed magi, the counts that need them aren't checked. The
aedile fields on the oppidum's own form say how many it should have.

A covenant has one vote for each of its magi, and one for each proxy it
carries: the sigil of an absent magus, given for one meeting. An unGifted
Redcap has no vote of his own at any Tribunal, while a Gifted one votes
(DE p. 15); a Redcap carrying an absent magus's sigil casts that vote,
so count it among his covenant's proxies. Proxies
change at every meeting, so they are kept here rather than on the
covenant.

### Magi of Note
<!-- help: section-trib-magi -->

The Tribunal's Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor, the magi of its covenants
the troupe doesn't play, and anyone prosecuting or defending a case on
its docket, with their House, covenant and part in the Tribunal. Edit a
magus on their covenant's sheet or their own.

## History tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-history -->

How the Tribunal came to be, era by era, kept apart from In the Saga
(what it is like now) so that stays short. Write it in the History field
of the Tribunal's own form; a Tribunal book fills it in.

Under the Tribunal's name the sheet shows only the first paragraph of In
the Saga, so the roster stays in view. When it runs longer, the rest is
shown here first, as **In the Saga, continued**, above the history.

## Docket tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-docket -->

The cases the Tribunal will hear at its next meeting, and those heard or
withdrawn before.

### Cases
<!-- help: section-trib-cases -->

Each charge, motion or appeal for the next meeting: when it was filed,
when notice of it is due, and what is sought. Under the core rules,
notice is due a season before the meeting; a case whose notice hasn't
been given is flagged, and a case against one of the troupe's covenants
or magi is drawn in red.

What a case records:

- **Case**: its name, as the docket lists it.
- **Kind**: a *charge* accuses someone of breaking the Code; a *motion*
  asks the Tribunal to decide or declare something (a new ruling, a
  boundary, recognising a covenant); an *appeal* asks it to reverse an
  earlier ruling. Two more come from Hermetic law: a *Wizard's War* is a
  declaration of war kept on the docket, and a *Wizard's March* is one a
  Quaesitor called between meetings, for the next Tribunal to ratify
  (Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 63). Neither needs notice.
- **Brought by** and **Against**: who brings it and who it is against,
  as names. A covenant or magus of the troupe's named in Against is
  flagged.
- **Victim**: who the crime was done to, when that is not whoever brought
  the case (blank: the one who brought it). Victim and prosecutor are
  separate roles: anyone may bring the case of a slain magus, and a
  Quaesitor may prosecute a high crime (Houses of Hermes: True Lineages
  p. 56). Not asked of a Wizard's War.
- **Charge**: what is alleged or proposed, in a sentence or two.
- **Filed**: the year and season it was filed.
- **Notice given**: whether the parties have been told of it in time.
  Notice is due a season before the meeting; the Notice due column says
  when.
- **Sought**: what the one bringing it asks the Tribunal to do if it
  succeeds: the penalty for a charge (a fine of vis or silver, the loss
  of a right, a Wizard's March) or the ruling a motion or appeal wants.
  Leave it empty while nobody has said.
- **Evidence**: what each side can show, one piece a line.
- **Prosecutor** and **Defender**: who speaks for each side at the
  meeting.
- **Status** and **Outcome** (when editing): open, decided or withdrawn,
  and what was decided. A decided or withdrawn case moves to the Rulings
  of Past Tribunals.

A charge's details say whether the Code of Hermes makes it a high crime
(Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 46): depriving a magus of magic,
slaying a magus, defying the Tribunal, abusing votes, endangering the
Order, meddling with mundanes, dealing with devils, molesting the fay,
scrying on magi, failing a parens's duty, Bonisagus's rights and duty to
share, attacking the Order's allies or aiding its enemies. Only a high
crime lets the prosecution call for a Wizard's March. The app reads the
charge's words, so it can be wrong: the Presiding Quaesitor's word is the
rule.

A charge whose words name a low crime of the Peripheral Code says so too
(Houses of Hermes: True Lineages): reading a text marked with another's
sanctum or House mark (p. 71), reading Quaesitorial texts (p. 70), not
helping a Quaesitor's investigation (p. 62), perjury, deceiving a
Quaesitor, breaking a contract or an assault short of life or magic
(p. 56), vagrancy (p. 49), keeping a Gifted child as a lab assistant
(p. 52).

**Wizard's War.** File a declaration as a case of the Wizard's War kind:
Brought by is the magus who declares it, Against the magus he declares it
on (one magus: to war on a covenant, declare on each of its magi), Filed
the season it arrived, and **Arrived at the full moon of month** which of
the season's three full moons (1 to 3). The war begins at the next full
moon and lasts until the one after: a month to prepare, then a month of
war (Ars Magica Definitive Edition p. 21). **Served** says how the
declaration reached him: by a Redcap, who is its legal witness (HoH: True
Lineages p. 47); into his hands; nailed to his sanctum door while he was
at the covenant, which most Tribunals hold duly served; to his
representative or forwarding address; while he was away and named no
one, which a Tribunal that asks magi to name a representative treats as
wilfully ignored; or not delivered, when the messenger tells a Quaesitor,
who rules whether it stands (HoH: Societates p. 23). A war stays on the
docket but no meeting hears it, unless **Its service is disputed** is
ticked or it was not delivered: its details then read "on the docket, not
heard" instead of the next meeting's season. The same two magi can't be at war again
until the first war has ended, so a renewed declaration gives a month of
war, a month of peace (HoH: Societates p. 23); a magus may be at war with
several others at once. The details say when the war runs, whether it is
to come, this season or over, and what the Code allows: either may slay
the other without retribution and neither is charged for declaring it,
but harm to other magi or to property shared with them, and scrying on or
attacking those not at war, stay crimes (HoH: Societates pp. 22-23; HoH:
True Lineages p. 47). A magus who has declared three or more wars in
seven years is warned of: unjust or constant use can be charged as
endangering the Order, even Marched (DE p. 21; HoH: True Lineages p. 48).
The three in seven years is a storyguide's default the app chose, not a
rule: no book says how often is too often (DE p. 21; HoH: Societates
p. 24 speaks only of extreme abuse).
A war being fought shows on both magi's Hermetic Background. When it is
over, **War's End** records how (see [War's End](help:wars-end)).

**Spell traces.** A charge (or motion, appeal or March) can record a
spell trace found as evidence: **Spell trace: the spell's level**, its
**Duration**, whether it was **a Ritual**, and **months since it ended**
(blank while the spell is still active). The details give the trace's
magnitude now: half the spell's magnitude when it ends, less one for each
of its Durations that passes (a Momentary spell's falls each round), then
into negative magnitudes a month at a time for a non-Ritual Momentary,
Concentration or Diameter spell and a year at a time for any other (a
Moon or Year spell among them), and gone at -10 (HoH: True Lineages
p. 71; Hooks p. 83). Neither book says how to round the halving: the app
rounds down, its own assumption, and the details say so. They give the Acute Sense roll too: Perception + Penetration +
stress die against 6 + the spell's magnitude, to notice deceptive magic
over it (p. 71).

**Intrigue.** **Intrigue by** names who works on the case before the
meeting and **Intrigue: to** what he seeks, each with its Ease Factor
(HoH: True Lineages p. 24): learning an open aim or keeping someone on his
course (6); learning allies, rousing suspicion, holding someone up or
bringing him to meet a rival (9); learning a hidden agenda, setting
wavering allies against each other, changing a course or settling a small
dispute (12); learning hidden partners, breaking a trusted alliance,
leading someone against his own good or settling a great dispute (15).
To sway is Presence + Intrigue, to learn Communication + Intrigue, each
with a stress die. **Intrigue: both under Parma Magica** (ticked to
start, as between magi at a Tribunal) means The Gift costs nothing;
untick it when either party is not under Parma (a mundane, or a magus
whose Parma is down) and The Gift costs 3, or 6 for a Blatant Gift: tick
**Intrigue: his Gift is Blatant**, or give him the Blatant Gift Flaw
(p. 24). Folk Ken rolls through the Tribunal may give -3 to +3 to
Intelligence + Intrigue at the storyguide's word (p. 25).

### War's End
<!-- help: wars-end -->

**War's End** (More on a Wizard's War on the docket) records how the war
came out: the month of war ran out with both alive, the declarer slew his
foe, the foe slew the declarer, or they made peace, with a line on what
happened. The war leaves the docket and is kept among the past rulings
with its outcome. A magus of the saga who keeps House Acclaim by House
Flambeau's table earns it there: 1 to 3 for having fought bravely, and
for winning, twice his foe's highest Reputation (HoH: Societates p. 31;
the foe's is filled in when he is of the saga). Both magi's Diaries and
the Tribunal's record it. A slain magus is not marked dead on his sheet:
do that as the saga does.

### Rulings of Past Tribunals
<!-- help: section-trib-past -->

The cases past meetings decided, with their outcome, and those
withdrawn. Edit one to correct it, or set its Status back to open to
return it to the docket.

## Arguments tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-arguments -->

Shown while Transforming Mythic Europe is in play. An argument is a
position someone means to win at a coming meeting, argued beforehand in
writing: a polemic sets it out, diatribes attack it, apologia defend it
(Transforming Mythic Europe pp. 27-35). Choose an argument to see its
total and its texts; choose a text to read it in the panel below.

### Arguments
<!-- help: section-trib-arguments -->

Each argument: its reach, its proponent, its Argument Total and the debate
bonus it buys ("−7: −3"), or "not out" while its polemic isn't circulated.

What an argument records:

- **Argument**: the position, in a line.
- **Its ideal**: what its texts' Reputations name: Supporter of (ideal)
  for a polemic or apologia, Opposed to (ideal) for a diatribe (pp. 31,
  33, 35). Blank: the argument itself.
- **It concerns**: its reach on the Prevailing Wisdom Table (p. 29): a
  magus 3 (or his Reputation, if higher: **His Reputation**), a covenant
  6, a Tribunal 9, a House 12, the Order 15.
- **Proponent**: who argues it.
- **Written on**: any knowledge Ability (p. 28). Code of Hermes, Order
  of Hermes Lore and Area Lore (with the troupe's agreement), the book's
  suggestions, come first; then the other Academic and Arcane Abilities
  and the Lores. Every text on the argument is a summa or tractatus on
  it.
- **Goes to**: the case or motion on the Docket where it is debated.
- **It backs**: the case or motion (its prosecutor or proposer), or the
  defence against it. A case or motion hears one argument for each side:
  a second is refused (adding two arguments' bonuses together would be
  a house rule); answer the first with a diatribe or apologia instead.
- **Last debate** (editing): not yet debated, won, or lost. Holding the
  debate sets it.

### The chosen argument
<!-- help: section-trib-argument -->

Its reach and Prevailing Wisdom, its proponent, the Argument Total worked
out (one per argument: the polemic's Quality − the Prevailing Wisdom,
+ each circulated apologia's Quality, − each circulated diatribe's,
whoever wrote it, pp. 31, 33), what it does in
debate, what the next text still circulating would make it, and where it
is debated.

At the hearing the total is spent as experience on the Art table (DE
p. 49): 1 buys +1, 3 buys +2, 6 buys +3, 10 buys +4; a negative total
buys a penalty the same way. The bonus goes on every Attack and Defense
roll of the side the argument backs, on top of Houses of Hermes:
Societates's testimony and the rest. Won, the bonus carries to later
Tribunals; lost, at that Tribunal or a later one, it is gone (p. 35).
Choosing not to debate it keeps the total for the next meeting.

### Texts
<!-- help: section-trib-argument-texts -->

The chosen argument's polemic, diatribes and apologia. A text is a link
to a book in the saga's library, not a copy: its Quality and level are
read from the book, so add the summa or tractatus to the library first
(it may belong to no one). A tractatus counts as level 5 (p. 28).

What a text records:

- **Kind**: one polemic per argument, written first; diatribes and
  apologia answer it.
- **Book**: a summa or tractatus on the argument's Ability.
- **Author**: blank takes the book's author. A diatribe's author gains
  Opposed to (ideal) 1, an apologia's Supporter of (ideal) 1, when the
  text is added (it is gained "on writing or circulating", pp. 33, 35),
  or 5 experience toward it if he has it already.
- **Studied the work it answers**: a diatribe or apologia is written
  only after a season studying the work it answers (pp. 31, 35); it
  can't be circulated until this is ticked.
- **Circulated to**: a Tribunal (6 copies), a House (9) or the Order (18).
- **Circulated by**: a sponsor who circulates it with his own Reputation
  (p. 29); blank: its author.
- **Seasons spent circulating**, **New works bound with it**: +1 each to
  the next Circulation roll (pp. 29-30).
- **Circulated**, **Out since**: whether it is out, and when.

More's **Circulate a Text** rolls a season's circulation.

## Circulate a Text
<!-- help: circulate-text -->

A season spent circulating the chosen text (Transforming Mythic Europe
pp. 29-31): the circulator's highest Reputation, good or bad, House
Acclaim among them, + the text's level (5 for a tractatus) + 1 for each
season already spent + 1 for each new work bound into every copy + a
simple die, against 15 for a Tribunal, 18 for a House, 24 for the Order.
Each season is a distraction of 5, 10 or 15 days.

Short of it, the season counts +1 toward the next roll. Out, the text
counts toward the Argument Total. A polemic's author gains Supporter of
(ideal) 1 (p. 31); a diatribe's or apologia's author had his Reputation
on writing it, and a circulator who isn't its author gains it now
(pp. 33, 35): Opposed to (ideal) for a diatribe, Supporter of (ideal)
for an apologia. One who has it already gains 5 experience toward it
instead (Hermetic, reaching the Tribunal, the House or the Order), and
no one gains it twice from the same text.

## Meeting tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-meeting -->

Holding the Tribunal: the meeting being held, or (before it opens) the
order of business the next one would hold. At the top, **Now** names
the step in hand and its number ("Step 5 of 12"), **Then** the step
after it, **To go on** how to hold it, and **Present** and **Quorum**
who is in the hall. Each step is saved as it is held and opens the next
straight away, so you can run the meeting in one go or leave it at any
point and come back to it later at the step in hand. See [Hold
Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal) and [How to hold a
Tribunal](help:guide-tribunal).

<!-- gui -->
At the top of the tab is **The Hall**: the meeting drawn as a
hemicycle, one seat for each vote, the seats of a covenant together
under its pennant (its arms, if it bears any). Each seat shows its
magus's House and is coloured by the side its vote went on the case in
hand, with the magi who spoke in the debate at the centre and a pair of
scales weighing the tally. **Call the Vote** calls the covenants one by
one in the order of the roll, filling their seats as the Praeco calls
them. When **Hold** opens a meeting, a card announces it: the year,
the Tribunal and where it meets, the arms of its covenants, and its
numbers; a key or a click enters the hall.
<!-- /gui -->

### Order of Business
<!-- help: section-meeting-business -->

The meeting's steps, day by day: the roll call and the Opening on the

### The Vote
<!-- help: section-meeting-vote -->

The vote on the case in hand (or the last voted on): each covenant as
the Praeco calls it, in the order of the roll, with its votes for,
against and undecided, and how many of them the debate swayed; then the
tally, with a bar for the hall.

Each covenant starts from its leaning: a party votes its own side; in
the Rhine a covenant whose gild a party shares votes with its gild. Gilds
are factions of magi, and a magus is expected, not bound, to vote with his
(Guardians of the Forests pp. 27-28): a covenant leaning with its members'
gild, ahead of its attitudes, is the app's reading, and you can change any
leaning at the meeting; and any other leans by its attitude toward a troupe covenant that is a
party (Warm or better with it, Wary or worse against it), otherwise
undecided. The winner of the debate then wins a share of the undecided
votes, and from a Triumphant Victory a share of the other side's, taken
from the first covenants called. On a tie the Praeco casts the deciding
vote. See [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal).

## Treasury tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-treasury -->

What the Tribunal holds in common, and what is owed to it.

### Treasury
<!-- help: section-trib-treasury -->

Each entry is vis, silver, a loan or a fine; Add asks which first. Vis
and silver are taken in (a positive amount) or paid out (a negative
one). A loan is silver or vis lent out of the treasury: it leaves the
treasury until it is marked repaid. A fine, from a case, comes in once it
is marked paid.

Most entries are typed in by the storyguide. A meeting adds one by
itself: when a case is decided with a fine, the fine owed is entered,
naming who owes it and the case. A new Tribunal, or one from a content
pack, starts with an empty treasury, since the books give no figures.

The balance above the list adds them up: the silver and vis in hand,
the loans still out and the fines still unpaid.

A Theban Tribunal's balance also tells its silver in the Byzantine emperors' coins: the
gold hyperpyron (the Westerners' bezant, a sixth of a pound, 40 pence), the electrum aspron
(a third of a hyperpyron), the billon (a sixteenth of an aspron, a little under a penny)
and the copper tetarteron (an eighteenth of a billon); the Venetian grosso the Latins trade
in is 2 pence, twenty to the bezant (The Sundered Eagle pp. 44-45). Its loans of vis are
made from the Polity tab's Lend Vis, and its common vis comes in at each meeting. The
treasury itself lies under a leaden trapdoor on Delos, opened only with the archon's iron
key, and holds several hundred pawns (p. 77).

In Transylvania (Against the Dark p. 19) Add also offers **A levy**, owed
to the treasury by an oppidum for the Praeco's projects and counted once
paid, and **A promissory note**, which the Praeco issued against the
treasury's income: it's owed to its holder, and its pawns leave the
treasury when it is redeemed. The balance then shows the levies unpaid and
the notes outstanding.

## Great Library tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-library -->

Durenmar's Great Library, the Order's greatest, open to every magus on its own terms (Guardians of the Forests pp. 55-57). Only a Tribunal following the Rhine's customs has this tab. Choose a magus's credit or a search to see it in full in the panel below the lists (on the desktop, in the inspector).

### The Great Library of Durenmar
<!-- help: section-trib-library -->

The librarian (Andrus of Bonisagus, p. 58), how many study there in a season (about a dozen) and who is exempt, then the rule of access (p. 55): those who study from the Great Library must contribute to it. Each contribution the librarian accepts (a tractatus or a new spell is welcome; a common spell or a low-level, mediocre summa is not) gives two seasons of free study, more for an exceptional one at his say; a copy will do. A season copying gives one season. A "donation" of vis is quietly taken instead. Lab notes and the Tribunal records need no credit, and House Bonisagus and Durenmar's own members are exempt. Books never leave the tower: one found is lent only for the seasons of credit paid for it, and is never copied or traded.

<!-- tui -->
More (`m`) here has **Search the Great Library**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Its **Search the Great Library** button opens the search.
<!-- /gui -->

### Credit
<!-- help: section-trib-library-credit -->

Each magus who studies at the Great Library: his covenant, what he gave, and the seasons of credit he has left (green: two or more; amber: one; red: none; "exempt" for a Bonisagus or one of Durenmar's). **Add** (Contribute) records a gift: the magus, what he gives (a book or Lab Text, two seasons; an exceptional work, at the librarian's say; a season copying, one; a vis "donation"), which of the saga's books (a copy: the book stays where it is) and the seasons it earns. **Edit** sets the seasons the librarian granted besides (or took back), the seasons used and a note. **Delete** forgets his credit; his searches stay. Every magus of the saga may hold credit: magi travel to Durenmar from all over. Guardians of the Forests p. 55.

### Searches
<!-- help: section-trib-library-searches -->

Each search of the library: the season, the magus, what he sought, the roll and the result. **Add** (or More's Search the Great Library) rolls a search; **Edit** corrects one as recorded (one rolled at the table), lets the storyguide raise a book's Quality above the suggested cap up to the absolute limit (a tractatus 14, a summa 28; p. 57), and sets **Seasons of credit paid** for the book it lent: each season more takes a season of his credit; **Delete** removes it, returning the book it lent and the seasons of credit it took. Guardians of the Forests pp. 55-57.

## Search the Great Library
<!-- help: great-library-search -->

Choose the magus, what he seeks and its Art, Ability, spell or subject. The Stacks, at the foot of the form, says what the die must show and what each result means; **Roll** rolls it and records it in Searches. Guardians of the Forests pp. 56-57:

- **A summa on an Art**: a simple die + 10, +3 for a master, a Bonisagus or one of Durenmar's, +6 for an archmage (his whole bonus), at most 20, against the level wanted. Beat it: found. Equal: a magus of equal rank wants it too, and certamen decides. Below: none so high, or (likelier) one of higher rank is reading it this season; every Art has summae of level 17 at least, so below that it is always in use.
- **A summa on an Arcane Ability**: a simple die alone against the level; below it, the library has none.
- Quality of a summa: a simple die + 5, at most 30 - the level or 24, whichever is lower. These caps are suggestions: the storyguide may raise it to 28, the absolute limit, with Edit on the search.
- **A tractatus**: no roll to find one. Quality: a simple die + 2, at most 12 suggested; the storyguide may raise it to 14, the absolute limit, with Edit on the search.
- **Durenmar Lore** may be added to a book's Quality, -1 for each earlier time the magus added it in the same Art or Ability; the app counts those from his earlier searches.
- **A spell's Lab Text**: a stress die against 3 for a standard spell (in the core book), 6 for a version of a general standard spell up to level 40, or the troupe's 6 to 21 for any other. A standard spell's failure: one of higher rank has it this season; its botch: the library has none. Any other's failure: none; its botch: one with a serious flaw. Equal: certamen. Each full 6 over: a tractatus on the spell's Mastery, Quality a simple die + 2.
- **An enchanted device's Lab Text**: a stress die against the troupe's 6 to 21; meeting it finds a text for a very similar enchantment, beating it by 6 the very one.
- **Lab notes**: a season, and Intelligence + Magic Theory + a stress die against the troupe's Ease Factor; leave the Ease Factor blank for a named author's notes, found within days with no roll.
- **The Tribunal records**: leave the Ease Factor blank for a season's search, which extracts every entry on the subject with no roll; give one to seek one entry, an Intelligence + Order of Hermes Lore roll a day, -1 for each day after the first.

A summa or tractatus found is lent to the magus: it is added to the saga as his book, marked as the library's, so the planner's Study from a Book offers it. A Library book never leaves the library (p. 55), so the loan covers only the seasons of credit paid for it: the search's season when he reads it then, and each season more paid with Edit's **Seasons of credit paid** (an exempt magus pays nothing, but the loan still covers only those seasons). A plan studying it in any other season is refused until a season more is paid, and it can't be copied or given to the library for credit. Reading what is found (all but lab notes and the records) takes a season of his credit, unless he is exempt; untick **He reads what is found this season** to search without reading (the loan then covers no season until one is paid). A magus with no credit left can't read: Contribute first.

## Contribute to the Great Library
<!-- help: great-library-contribute -->

A magus gives the library a book or Lab Text (two seasons of credit), an exceptional work (the librarian may grant more: three by default), a season copying (one season) or a vis "donation" (taken, never acknowledged; two seasons by default, as it stands in for a contribution). Choosing what he gives fills in its seasons; change them as the librarian says. A book given is a copy: the original stays where it is. Edit on a magus's credit sets the seasons granted besides, those used and a note. Guardians of the Forests p. 55.

## The Polity tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-tokens -->

A Theban Tribunal's own records, named as The Sundered Eagle names them:
each magus's place in the polity, tokens and shards, and the Council of
Magistrates who hold its offices. Only a Tribunal following the Theban
customs has this tab.

### Tokens and Shards
<!-- help: section-trib-tokens -->

Each magus of note: their covenant; their status, polites
(a citizen, who passed Gauntlet in the Tribunal) or metoikos (a resident
foreigner: a guest, or a polites who has lost his rights); the tokens
they hold, each earned by a service to the community; the shards against
them, each for an act against the Tribunal's unity; the services they
have done; and their standing: an office they hold, or that they are
ostracised and until when. Tokens are not votes: every magus present
votes once. A metoikos holds no tokens or shards, so the form refuses
them. Any polites with a shard may be ostracised. Add a magus, edit
their entry or delete it.

<!-- tui -->
Four commands in More (`m`) work on this list.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Four buttons beside the list work on it.
<!-- /gui -->
**Neutralize** (on the chosen magus, who must hold a shard): another
polites spends a token, and the chosen magus serves him a season, with
the service named; the Redcap who witnesses it takes a token and a
shard. A magus can't neutralize his own shards, nor those of a magus of
his own covenant, and the one with the shard may always refuse (The
Sundered Eagle pp. 28-29). **Longevity Ritual**: the Ritual the Tribunal
sponsors once a year. The applicants are the politai with no shard and
no Longevity Ritual in force; the one with the most tokens wins (the
elder on a tie) and gives up all his tokens; whoever crafts it earns a
token, two for a Ritual of the tenth magnitude or more. Each year's
award is kept, and a second the same year is refused (pp. 28, 30).

**Lend Vis** (on the chosen magus, who must be a polites): a loan of vis from the Tribunal's
coffers, which a polites asks of the tamias. Enter the Art, the pawns, the year the plan
ends (the next meeting to start with) and the plan agreed. The loan carries no interest,
since usury is a sin; one of more than ten pawns that is not for an emergency needs the
People's Assembly's approval (The Sundered Eagle p. 28; the book says only "large", so the
ten is this app's). The loan becomes a line of the treasury, and the pawns join the magus's
own vis unless you untick it. **Repay a Loan** (on a magus who owes vis): the loan is marked
repaid, the pawns lent and no more, taken from his own vis unless you untick it; the form
refuses if he hasn't them.

### The Council of Magistrates
<!-- help: section-trib-archai -->

The Tribunal's offices: who holds each (or that it is vacant), when
their term ends, and its duties. The archon chairs the Council and is
the Tribunal's Praeco; the polemarch enforces its sentences; the
logothete, the Chief Redcap, keeps the register of tokens and shards;
and the sixteen archai, four from each phyle, serve seven years on the
Judicial, Legislative and Conciliatory Boards and the Treasury Council,
led by its tamias (treasurer). The Council is chosen at each meeting.

## The Ostracism tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-ostracism -->

A Greater Alps Tribunal's record of the magi it has ostracised. Only a
Tribunal following the Greater Alps customs has this tab.

### Ostracism
<!-- help: section-trib-ostracism -->

Each ostracism the Tribunal has voted: the magus, the meeting that voted
it, the sherds that named him of all those cast (blank ones too), when
his exile ends and why. As the first business of each meeting every
magus writes on a sherd the name of the one he most despises, or leaves
it blank; the magus most named is ostracised, provided at least one
sherd in four names him, and must leave the Greater Alps before the
second full moon, for twenty-one years, or be Marched (Sanctuary of Ice
p. 12). The form refuses a vote where fewer than a quarter of the
sherds name him. Leave **Exile ends** blank for twenty-one years after
the meeting; the exile ends the day before that year's Tribunal, so he
may be ostracised again on his return. An exile still running shows in
red, and on the magus's sheet. Add an ostracism, edit it or delete it.

## The Five Covenants tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-privileges -->

A Transylvanian Tribunal's record of which legal covenant each magus
belongs to. Only a Tribunal following the Transylvanian customs has this
tab.

### The Five Covenants
<!-- help: section-trib-privileges -->

Each magus: the oppidum they live in, their privilege with its legal
covenant, and their offices. Grouped by legal covenant, in order of
rank:

- **Cives (Coeris)**: full citizens; any office, Praeco too; may take
  apprentices. Every Tremere belongs to Coeris.
- **Socii (Domostron)**: allies; any office but Praeco.
- **Coloniae (Asclepius)**: settlers; no office but aedile; apprentices
  by leave.
- **Hospites (Diodorus)**: hired specialists; office and apprentices by
  leave.
- **Foederati (Pannonia)**: keep their own customs; trade with outsiders
  by leave.

The Office column shows Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor from the
Tribunal, Aedile from the oppidum's own form, and any other office
entered (the Curantis, say). Add a magus, edit their entry or delete it;
a magus has one entry, so adding a second for them is refused.

The book ranks the five no further than cives first; they are listed in
this order. What a privilege says of office is checked: only a cives (or
a foederatus, who may stand for any office) may be Praeco, so Edit
Tribunal refuses a socius or colona there (a hospes needs leave); a
colona may hold no office but aedile and the Curator of Tablinum must be
a cives (Against the Dark pp. 15-18, 58), so this form refuses those
offices. A hospes holds office only by the Praeco's leave: his sheet's
Office line says so (an aedile is chosen, not sought, so no leave is
noted for that).

Each magus may wear one **Decoration** (Laurel, Myrtle, Oak, Grass,
Parsley, highest first): a higher replaces a lower, and a lower never
replaces a higher (Against the Dark p. 17). A magus's sheet lists his
privilege's other terms: what it forbids (a colona the title archmagus,
inviting magi, choosing his aedile) and what needs the Praeco's leave (a
hospes's apprentice, familiar, talisman, oppida and aedile vote; a
foederatus's archmagus title, oppida, invitations and vis or item sales
to outsiders); a hospes's familiar or talisman is noted as held by leave
(pp. 15-18). The Curator of Tablinum is chosen by the Tribunal's vote,
and must be held a master when chosen (p. 58).

## Tourney tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-tourney -->

A Normandy Tribunal's Hermetic Tourney, held after each meeting. Only a
Tribunal following the Normandy customs has this tab.

### The Tourney
<!-- help: section-trib-tourney -->

Each team's result at each Tourney, the latest first. A result's
**Points** is the team's own tally: a point for each member of its
covenant (one for a visiting team or a coenobium's member) and any points
the events don't give. Choose its placing in each of the six events and
their points are added (The Lion and the Lily p. 31): the hastiludium 20,
10 and 5 for the semifinalists; the certamen 25, 15, 8; the dimicatio
23, 12, 6; the hosting covenant's choice 12, 8, 3 and 3; the joust 20, 8,
3 and 3; each with a point for entering; the melee 6, 2 for second to
fourth and 1 for fifth, with nothing for entering. Its rank, its prize pawns
and any named rank (the Sieges of Normandy, Valor, Notatus, Shame and
Alms, each a Hermetic Reputation of 2 for seven years) are worked out.
Teams level on points keep the order they were entered, since the book
breaks ties by seniority: enter the senior first. The 21st team keeps
6 pawns and gives 6 to the teams below it.

## Delegates tab
<!-- help: tab-trib-delegates -->

### Delegates
<!-- help: section-trib-delegates -->

House) and, if one, the magus of the saga they are (or, if not, the
covenant he comes from). A Tribunal can't send more than three. Holding
the meeting counts the delegates for its quorum, any legal Tribunal's:
at least twelve magi from at least four covenants, and a Quaesitor, the
Primus of Guernicus or a Guernicus magus among them (DE p. 22). Add one,
edit it or delete it.
