# The Bestiary
<!-- help: bestiary -->

The Bestiary holds the saga's creatures: the beasts, spirits, faeries
and demons the troupe meets, each with its stat block as the books print
one. It belongs to the whole saga, not to a covenant, and a creature can
be found at a place in the Region and linked to any neighbour (a threat
it serves, a lord whose woods it haunts).

<!-- tui -->
In the navigation tree the Bestiary sits after The Region, with each
creature under it by name. Selecting the Bestiary shows its list over
the chosen creature's stat block; selecting a creature shows its own
sheet.

Press `n` (New) here, on a creature or on the saga, and choose **New
Creature**. In the list, `a` adds one, `e` edits the chosen one's facts
and `d` deletes it.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
On the saga's page, the Bestiary chapter shows each creature as a card
that opens its own page, and a New card that adds one.
<!-- /gui -->

**New Creature** can start from the book: **Start from the book** lists
the Definitive Edition's 64 creatures (DE pp. 428-492), from the bear and
the wolf to the elementals, the faerie knight, Baal-Peor and the angel
Gabriel. The chosen one's whole stat block is copied into the saga under
the name you give (blank: the book's), to change as you like; only its
name and where it is found are asked. A Tribunal book's pack brings the
creatures its stat blocks describe; see [How to add a creature from a
book](help:guide-bestiary).

The book's mundane beasts are also the Book of Mundane Beasts in Realms of
Power: Magic, pp. 140-144; where the two differ (the lion's Cunning, the
falcon's Communication, the boar's Fatigue Levels) the Definitive Edition's
figures are used, and the entry's notes say so. The charger and palfrey (DE
p. 432) are there as their own horses.

**See also:** [A creature](help:creature), [The Region](help:region)

## The Bestiary's facts
<!-- help: section-bestiary-info -->

How many creatures there are, how many of each realm (Magic, Faerie,
Divine, Infernal, and mundane beasts with no Might), and how many are
found at a place.

## Creatures tab
<!-- help: tab-bestiary-creatures -->

Every creature in the saga, by name, over the stat block of the one
chosen.

### Creatures
<!-- help: section-bestiary -->

Each creature's name (marked "many" when it stands for a kind, such as
a pack of wolves), its Might and Form, its Size and where it is found.
Add, edit or delete one; editing changes its facts, and its own sheet
holds the rest.

# A creature
<!-- help: creature -->

A creature's sheet: its facts at the top, then its Traits, its Combat,
its Powers & Vis, and its Description and Links. Everything a book's
stat block gives has a place here, and what follows from it (its
Penetration, its Wound Penalties by Size) is worked out for you.

<!-- tui -->
Press `1` to `4` for the tabs. `e` on the facts at the top edits them;
in a list, `a` adds, `e` edits the chosen row and `d` deletes it.
<!-- /tui -->

**See also:** [The Bestiary](help:bestiary)

## A creature's facts
<!-- help: section-cr-info -->

Its name; whether it stands for **many** of a kind, and how many; its
**Realm** and **Might** (leave the Realm blank for a mundane beast,
which has no Might), the Might's Form and its current **Might Pool**;
its **Size**; the **season** it is strongest in, if any; its **Age** as
the book prints it (the apparent age in brackets); its **Confidence
Score** and Points, if it has any; where it is **found** (a place in the
Region, and a level of it); how much detail its record has; and the
book it comes from.

A creature with Magic Might that has Magic Qualities or Inferiorities
shows its **Qualities**: the points its Qualities spend (1 a Minor, 3 a
Major) against its Might (a magic animal's with its Size added back) plus
its Inferiorities (1 a Minor, 3 a Major) (Realms of Power: Magic pp.
30-36). A creature made for a story needn't balance, so an overspend is
only reported. **Check** lists what doesn't fit the Definitive Edition's
rules: The Gift on a creature whose Might isn't Magic, a power that costs
more than the Might (it could never be used, since the Pool refills only
to the Score) and a Might Pool above the Might (DE pp. 425-426).

A creature with Magic Might found at a place with a Magic aura shows its
**Might recovery** resting there: the time to regain its whole Might Pool,
by the aura less a fifth of its Might (24 hours at 0 down to 2 minutes at
9 or more), and so how long a point takes (Realms of Power: Magic p. 30).

A faerie built by Realms of Power: Faerie (one with Humanoid Faerie,
Faerie Beast or Hybrid Form, or Increased or Decreased Might) shows a
**Faerie design** fact: the Might its Virtues give it (5, +5 for each
Minor Increased Faerie Might, +15 for each Major; 1 with Decreased Might)
when its own differs; the choices every faerie makes that it lacks (a
physical form, a cognizance, a Social Interaction Virtue or Flaw, a
taboo); and what a faerie can't have (The Gift, Hermetic or Social Status
Virtues, a Confidence Score, more than three Minor or one Major
Personality Flaws). Time or Place of Power's +10 is noted. A faerie's
power Virtues give their powers' cost and Initiative as a magic
creature's do (RoP: Faerie pp. 45-64). The Definitive Edition's own
faeries are written another way and aren't checked.

An Infernal creature also has the facts Realms of Power: The Infernal
gives a demon: its **Order**, its **Hierarchy** score (the highest of
its Infernal Reputations, or of its rank) and its **Weakness**.

A demon whose Order names one of the nine orders (Tempters, Accusers,
Furies, Aerial Powers, Deluders, Avengers of Evil, Vessels of Iniquity,
Spirits of Deceit, False Gods) and perhaps a rank (Master, Lord, Duke,
Prince) is checked against Realms of Power: The Infernal (pp. 28-44): the
most Might its order and rank allow, its Hierarchy (the higher of its
rank's and its Infernal Reputations), its sin's Personality Trait and its
order's traits, the Powers every demon has (Coagulation, Obsession,
Envisioning) and its order's, a weakness, and its Characteristic points.
"As a demon" says what its weakness, True Name, Second Sight, recovery
and corpse come to. A spirit with the Magic Spirit Virtue is checked
against Realms of Power: Magic (pp. 100-117): the Magical Air Flaw, a
ghost's Social Status and worldly tie, the common spirit Powers' cost and
Initiative, and what its Presence and vis come to. A jinni (the Jinni
Virtue) is checked against The Cradle and the Crescent (pp. 62-63). All
of these are advice: the books' own creatures needn't balance.

**Vampire design**, **As a vampire** and **As a player character.** A
Transylvanian vampire (a Faerie creature named as the book names them --
vampire, kukudhi, moroi, platenik, vukodlak, zduhacz, gadja and the rest --
or with both Feast of the Dead and Sap the Life) is checked against
*Against the Dark*, as advice, never a refusal (pp. 114-121):

- Every vampire has a physical form, a level of cognizance and a Social
  Interaction Virtue or Flaw, Feast of the Dead, a Traditional Ward of
  sunlight and the Lesser Power Sap the Life (p. 116).
- It obeys one to three limitations, which are traditional wards (p. 116):
  running water, garlic, a line of thorns, black hawthorn, red thread,
  uncounted millet, stories being told, the same question three times, the
  Cross and prayers, its resting place by day, and the Saturday ones (a
  Sovereign Ward of those born on a Saturday; Restricted Might for "cannot
  spend Might on a Saturday"). The sheet names those its wards hold.
- Its notes should say the one to three precautions that stop it forming
  (p. 115) and the one way to kill it for good (p. 117); slain any other
  way it re-forms in days or weeks.
- A *kukudhi* (one with kukudhi in its name) is an old vampire living far
  away among people: it passes for human (Passes for Human or Infiltrator),
  is no longer kept to its resting place by day though direct sun hurts
  it, and its children are dhampirs (p. 115). It is the vampire a player
  may play, best in a high-power saga among older magi (pp. 115-116), so
  the sheet shows its Virtue and Flaw points against a faerie player
  character's (in place of a companion, up to 10 points balanced by Flaws;
  of a magus, 1 free and up to 20 more at 2 for each Flaw point) and its
  Pretense points, 15 x (the magi's average age - 5) + 120 (21 when no
  magus has a year of birth, 360 points), with those it has spent (Realms
  of Power: Faerie pp. 45-46).

**Magic design** and **Magic being.** A creature of Magic is checked against
*Realms of Power: Magic*, as advice, never a refusal:

- *Every magic being* (Magic realm, Might above 0) shows how it advances: its Might comes off
  every Advancement Total, 2 less for each pawn of vis it eats (p. 52) (the book's example, Might 10 and 4 pawns, keeps 13 of 15: Errata p. 63); bound as a Hermetic magus's familiar it learns as a human does instead, and keeps those Abilities if the bond breaks (Errata p. 63; Definitive Edition p. 267); and how it keeps off
  Acclimation for the year: the Magic aura it must live in (always, half the time, or by
  frequent visits), the seasons it must affect a magical being without Might, or Might / 2
  pawns of vis eaten (pp. 53-54).
- *A Beast of Virtue* (Magic Animal with an Essential Virtue, or "of Virtue" in its name) has
  an Essential Virtue and no Flaw that makes it a worse example of its kind (Lame, Blind and
  the like); most have a magical Social Status, their powers are most often Lesser, Personal
  or Greater, and the corpse holds about a pawn of vis for each 5 Might (pp. 55-56).
- *A transformed animal* has no realm and a Warping Score (set on the Edit form when the realm
  is blank). Warping brings a Minor Flaw at 1, another at 3, a Minor Virtue at 5 and a Major
  Flaw for each Score after; from Magic these are Warped Senses or (Form) Monstrosity, Lesser
  Power, then Hunger for (Form) Magic or Bound to Magic (pp. 78-79). A creature with Might has
  no Warping Score.
- *An awakened tree* (a tree's name: oak, willow, rowan...) has Cunning and no Might, unless a
  spirit animates it with Manifestation: then its Might is the power's activation cost while it lasts (pp. 128-129). *A Magic Thing* (an awakened item, an
  elemental) never tires (p. 135).
- *An elemental* (Magic, aligned to Terram, Aquam, Auram or Ignem, with an elemental's Flaws
  or "elemental" in its name) is Size -18 at Might 1, 3 more for each magnitude (Might 15 is
  Size 0; DE p. 450); has Poor Memory, Short Attention Span, Simple-Minded and Ways of the
  (Land), its element's attack (Crush, Drown, Suffocate or Burn), and Brawl and Athletics
  (Swim for water) at its Might's magnitude; four of its Characteristics are never negative
  before Size (RoP: Magic p. 134). The sheet shows its typical Characteristics, how it
  recovers Might (resting a season in an aura of Might / 5, or eating vis) and what wounds
  take from its Might Pool (DE pp. 449-451).

When it rules or leads a neighbour (a faerie court's queen, a dragon
lording it over a valley), its facts say so under **Rules**. The place
it is found at and each neighbour it rules are links that open them:
see [Following a record's links](help:record-links).

Deleting a creature removes the links to it from other records, and a
neighbour it ruled keeps its name as a name typed; nothing else
changes.

**A vampire** (Against the Dark pp. 115-117) is a faerie: its Edit form also asks the
**precautions** that stop it forming (one to three from the book's list, all of which must be
met), the **limitations** it obeys (one to three; that it can't leave its resting place by day
is almost universal) and **the one way that kills it for good** (any other, it reforms in days
or weeks). They are typed, as the storyguide picks them from the book's lists; the sheet shows
them beside its Role. Leave them blank for a faerie that isn't a vampire.

**Role.** A faerie plays a part in the stories it takes part in, and wants a story to play it
in. Its Edit form (for a Faerie creature) asks its **Role in its stories**, one of the
dramatis personae: the Hero, the Villain, the Donor (who grants the hero a magical gift, not
always willingly), the Helper, the Princess (the object of the quest, any person or thing),
the Task-Setter or the False Hero; and **The story it wants**, in a line. The sheet shows them
as its **Role**, and reminds you of them for a faerie built by Realms of Power: Faerie
(Realms of Power: Faerie pp. 40, 140-142). Building a faerie story from story elements,
Arcadia, Elysium and Eudokia (pp. 31-44) is the storyguide's:

- *Arcadia* (the Path of Chance): pick a story element (an actor, a prop, a piece of scenery)
  for each story experience point to be handed out, give each a role or an act, link each to
  one to three others, and choose where the characters start; the players may describe what
  they meet, the storyguide decides its role (pp. 31-34).
- *Elysium* (the Path of Destiny): the characters re-enact a known story, one of them declaring
  himself its hero (a Confidence Point, and his first fable point); the fable points the hero
  earns measure the reward: 0-3 failure, 4-6 mediocre, 7-9 accomplished, 10+ unmitigated
  (pp. 34-38).
- *Eudokia* (the Forking Path): a dilemma for a character at a turning point of his life, its
  theme set by the Threshold; more right choices than wrong bring a Charmed Virtue (Major if
  all were right), more wrong a Flaw that fades (pp. 38-39).

An angel's **Order** is its Choir (Realms of Power: The Divine pp. 18-26): Seraphim, Cherubim,
Thrones, Dominations, Authorities, Potentates, Principalities, Archangels or Angels, or the fallen
Grigori. An archangel names its own Choir and the Archangels' ("Seraphim and Archangels"), and has
the traits of both (p. 25). An angel whose Order names a Choir shows a **Divine design** fact:
the least Divine Might its Choirs allow (Seraphim 35, Cherubim and Thrones 25, Dominations and
Potentates 10, Authorities and Principalities 5, Archangels 50, Angels 1); Love of God +5 and its
Choir's two Personality Traits at +4 (Servile -4 for a Domination); the three powers every angel
has, Angelic Mantle, Enfolding and Envisioning (p. 19; DE p. 489), and its Choir's powers, when
it lacks one or its cost or Initiative differ from the book's; and its Intelligence, Perception,
Presence and Communication, +1 for each 10 points of Might or fraction (p. 19). **As an angel**
says what its Mantle's physical Characteristics cost (the book buys them with 7 points, more for
a powerful angel); that in spirit form only Mentem and Vim reach it, in its Mantle the Form of its
Might; that magic can't make it act against its Personality Traits, Might below 0 destroys it,
and Hermetic magic can't summon or compel it (p. 21); its True Name (an Arcane Connection, +5 to
the Penetration multiplier, p. 20); the Penetration of its speech, its Penetration Ability
(p. 20); its Second Sight for finding regiones, Might / 5 (p. 21); and its Choir's own sums
(Smite Evil's level, Radiant Mirror's Holy Influence Total, the Principalities' costs by area,
Irresistible Light). Any creature with Divine Might is checked for True Faith, which it can't
have (DE p. 488). The Definitive Edition's angels name no Choir and aren't checked.

## Traits tab
<!-- help: tab-cr-traits -->

Its Characteristics, its Virtues, Flaws and Qualities, its Personality
Traits and Reputations, and its Abilities.

### Characteristics
<!-- help: section-cr-characteristics -->

The eight Characteristics, as a stat block lists them (Int, Per, Pre,
Com, Str, Sta, Dex, Qik). A creature with an animal mind has a
**Cunning** score in place of Intelligence, shown as Cun. Then its
**Soak**; whether it **can tire** and its **Fatigue levels** (as the
book gives them, such as "OK, 0, -1, -3, -5, Unconscious", or blank for
a person's); whether it **can be wounded**; and its **Encumbrance**,
with its Burden in brackets.

### Virtues, Flaws and Qualities
<!-- help: section-cr-virtues-flaws -->

Its Virtues and Flaws, and the creature Qualities and Inferiorities of
Realms of Power (Magic Animal, Improved Attack, Lesser Power...). Add
them from the catalog, which offers what a creature may take; Edit changes
what the chosen one names (a Puissant Ability's Ability, say), keeping its
place in the list.

### Personality Traits
<!-- help: section-cr-personality-traits -->

Its Personality Traits and their scores, such as Proud +3.

### Reputations
<!-- help: section-cr-reputations -->

What it is known for, and where, such as Haunts the ford 2.

### Abilities
<!-- help: section-cr-abilities -->

Its Abilities, with their scores and specialties, as for a character.

## Combat tab
<!-- help: tab-cr-combat -->

Its natural weapons and its health.

### Combat
<!-- help: section-cr-attacks -->

Each attack (Bite, Hooves, Claws...) with its Initiative, Attack,
Defense and Damage totals, as the stat block gives them.

### Health
<!-- help: section-cr-health -->

Its Wound Penalties, worked out from its Size (each band is 5 plus Size
wide), its Fatigue levels, and the wounds and Fatigue it has now. Edit
to record the wounds it takes and how tired it is. A creature that
cannot be wounded, or never tires, says so here instead.

## Powers & Vis tab
<!-- help: tab-cr-powers -->

Its powers over the chosen power's details, and the vis in its body.

### Powers
<!-- help: section-cr-powers -->

Each power: its Might cost, Initiative, Form, Range, Duration and
Target, and its **Penetration**, which is worked out as its Might less
five times the power's Might cost, plus its Penetration Ability (as for
a magus: its score, any Puissant bonus, and 1 more when its specialty
is the power or its Form), plus any other bonus the power has. The
aura's modifier for its realm is added where it is used, to its
Penetration and to its Magic Resistance (Might + the aura's modifier, DE
p. 412); a fight adds its Magic aura. A Penetration of zero or less still
affects what has no Magic Resistance, but nothing that has any. Choose
a power to read what it does. A power's level, when the book gives one, is the level of the
effect it matches.

**From Quality** (optional; Typed is the default) names the Magic Quality
a power comes from, and suggests its cost and Initiative from the effect
level's magnitude (Realms of Power: Magic p. 36): a Focus or Lesser Power
costs the magnitude, a Greater or Personal Power half of it, rounded down;
Initiative is Quickness less the magnitude (Focus), twice it (Lesser,
Ritual) or half it (Greater, Personal). A new power with its cost and
Initiative left at 0 takes them, and you can change them; typed figures
that differ show as a Check on the creature's facts, never refused, since
mastery points and constant powers change them and the DE lets a power's
numbers break the rules (p. 428). A Ritual Power is at least level 20,
and a power of level 50 or more must be one.

### Vis in its body
<!-- help: section-cr-vis -->

The pawns of vis a magus can harvest from the creature: how many, of
which Art, and where in its body (its horn, its heart, its blood).

## Description tab
<!-- help: tab-cr-description -->

What it looks like, what it carries and any notes, then its links.

### Description
<!-- help: section-cr-description -->

Its appearance, its equipment and any notes. Each can use
[formatting](help:guide-formatting): bold, italic, strikethrough and
bulleted or numbered lists.
