# Assumptions made for Guardians of the Forests: The Rhine Tribunal
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-rhine -->

The Rhine pack follows the book wherever this page doesn't say otherwise: its founding years, ages, auras, Might scores and stat blocks are the book's, and where the book gives no value and nothing points to one, the record says "not given" and leaves it blank. These are the places where the pack had to infer a value from the text, make a judgement call where the book is silent, or correct the book. You can change any of them in your saga.

### Inferred from the text
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-rhine-inferred -->

- **Caecilius, Schadrit and Eule**: age 160, from the book's "approximately 160" (Ch. 6, Ch. 8).
- **Gudrun Tigurina and Xavier**: Masters. The book says Durenmar has three masters from other Houses and names these two among them (Ch. 6).
- **Unnamed magi**: Durenmar 4 (three more Bonisagus and the unnamed third master), Fengheld 7 (the book's 23 magi less the 16 it names), Irencillia 3, as the book says; Roznov 5, its three Ex Miscellanea, three Tremere and a Tytalus less Igor Rastvan and Severicus (Ch. 6, Ch. 8, Ch. 10).
- **Sites with several auras** take one figure: the Brocken Faerie 4 at the summit (the slopes are 2) (Ch. 8); the Rhinefalls Magic 2 on the island (the summit is 4) (Ch. 5); the Source of the Danube Faerie 3 round the pool (5 in the water) (Ch. 6); the woods of Irencillia Faerie 1, the lowest within the wall, with the sancta's stronger patches as a regio level (Ch. 10).
- **The Four Seasonal Courts**: aura Faerie 3, Might 40. Each court's aura peaks at 3 in its own season and its Might runs from 20 to 50 through the year; the pack takes the peak aura and 40 for the rulers (Ch. 4).
- **Heorot**: a Spring covenant, as it was founded only in 1215 (Ch. 9).
- **The Tribunal**: 130 magi, from "approximately 130"; the next meeting is spring 1221, at Walpurgis Night (Ch. 1, Ch. 3).
- **Peripheral Code years**: the book dates rulings Anno Aegis; the pack gives them in AD, 139 years earlier. "A season at Durenmar" and "Ranks" have no year, as the book gives none (Ch. 3).
- **Diary years for loosely dated events**: Terschelling turns to piracy 1200 ("about twenty years ago"), Oculus settles in Lübeck 1160 ("around 1160"), Durenmar's lean years 1100 ("about 1100 to 1150"), the God of Truth is preached 1210 ("over the last ten years"), and five things that happened "lately" 1220: the Lusatia chapter, the theft of the Nebelkönigin, Caprea blinding Carolus's familiar, Fengheld's new masters and journeymen, and the Gorge ban running out (Ch. 2, Ch. 3, Appendix B).
- **Proxy sigils**: every master holds one retired magus's sigil and every archmage two, as the book's rule says, though it names only a few. Stentorius also votes Triamore's two Tremere sigils and Severicus votes Hygwald Veritas's. Covenant totals: Durenmar 12, Fengheld 12, Crintera 7, Irencillia 5, Roznov 4, Dankmar 3, Oculus 3, Triamore 2, Heorot 1 (Ch. 3, Ch. 8, Ch. 10).
- **Gild members the book points to**: the Elder Gild has Irencillia and Dankmar, whose magi are "virtually all" members; the Apple Gild has Oculus Septentrionalis, Triamore and Fengheld, its "substantial support" (Ch. 3).
- **Town populations**: Cologne 20,000 ("more than 20,000"), Frankfurt 10,000 ("about 10,000"), Prague 3,000 ("about three thousand") (Ch. 5, Ch. 8, Ch. 10).

### Judgement calls
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-rhine-judgement -->

- **Oculus Septentrionalis**: aura Divine 0. The book says Lübeck has no Magic aura and a "typical" Divine one, with no figure (Ch. 7).
- **Heorot**: aura 0; the book gives none (Ch. 9).
- **Triamore**: a Summer covenant, the app's default, as the book gives no season (Ch. 5).
- **Crintera**: at the surface of the Stubnitz regio, so it shows an aura of 0. The book doesn't say which of the regio's four levels (auras 1, 3, 5 and 8) is its home (Ch. 9).
- **Other gild members**: the book names each gild's leader and the Houses it draws, not its covenants. The pack puts Durenmar and Fengheld in the Oak Gild, Crintera in the Hawthorn Gild, Fengheld in the Linden Gild, and no covenant in the Ash Gild; Roznov and Waddenzee are in none (Ch. 3).
- **Gild dues**: the book gives none, so each gild's Dues read "None: a member owes the gild their vote and reasonable aid" (Ch. 3).
- **The docket**: the nine quarrels the book leaves open in 1220 are cases for 1221, each filed in spring 1220. The pack chose whether each is a charge or a motion and who brings it, and only Heorot's application and the Lusatia border dispute have had notice given (Ch. 3, Ch. 7 to 10, Ch. 12).
- **Lords' titles**: the app's highest title is Duke, so the Emperor, King Valdemar II, King Otakar, the Landgrave of Thuringia and the Margraves of Brandenburg and Meissen are Dukes; the Count Palatine and the Princes of Mecklenburg are Counts; the Lords of Cleves are Barons.
- **Lords' wealth**: the book rates none. Wealthy: the Emperor, the Welfs, the Count Palatine, Brabant, the Zähringens, the Hohenstaufens, Valdemar II, the Landgrave of Thuringia, Otakar and Henry the Rich of Nassau. Poor: the Princes of Mecklenburg. Every other lord is Average.
- **Coats of arms**: about 35 lords, churches and towns carry their historical arms, from medieval heraldry rather than the book, simplified to what the app can draw (Cologne's three crowns lose their chief).
- **The Seasonal Courts on the map**: inside the Hercynian Forest. The book leaves this to the storyguide and suggests the Westerwald, Odenwald, Palatinate Forest or Hunsrück (Ch. 4, Ch. 12).
- **Rudolph of Bonisagus**: a member of Fengheld, which the book implies but doesn't state (Ch. 8).
- **Magi of no stated rank** (Günther Lupus, Rudolph, Marguerite, Odorpes and Caprea): plain magi, with no Rhine rank and so no proxy sigil (Ch. 8, Ch. 9).
- **Libraries**: a covenant whose library the book only describes has one "library" Book record; Durenmar's Great Library has a record for each floor's collection, plus the books it names (Ch. 6).
- **The Curse of the Rhine Gorge**: one threat in eight stages, from the Praeco's commission to the seventy-seventh year. What happens when a stage is ignored, stopped or turned is the pack's own wording (Ch. 12).

### Corrections to the book
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-rhine-corrections -->

- **Durenmar**: founded 767, when the Order was founded there; Bonisagus had settled there about 754 (Ch. 3, Ch. 6).
- **Dates where the book gives two**, the pack takes: Merinita's disappearance 792, not 785 (Ch. 10, Appendix B); Dankmar founded 1052, not 1053 (Ch. 6, Appendix B); Rethra abolished 1207, not 1202 (Ch. 3, Ch. 9); Oakdell founded 934, not 931 (Ch. 3, Ch. 12); Grand Silesia destroyed 1008, not 1009 (Ch. 3, Appendix B). Each Diary entry gives the other date too. Ulm's freeing keeps both 1155 and 1164 in the text (Ch. 6).
- **Felicia**: Dorana's apprentice is spelled as her stat block's heading has it; the book also writes Felecia (Ch. 3, Ch. 8).
- **The Oak Gild**: Murion leads it. Only her raven Kolkrabus believes Stentorius has usurped her place, and Stentorius's record says so (Ch. 3, Ch. 8).
- **Rhine customs over the core rules**: the pack keeps the book's year of notice for a case (the core rules say a season) and its proxy sigils (the core rules give each magus one vote): a master holds one retired magus's sigil and an archmage two, by their rank Virtues. A meeting that would fall in a Grand Tribunal's year (every 33 years, 1228 and 1261) is held a year earlier, so the meeting after 1221 is in 1227 (pp. 140, 143). Journeyman, Master and Archmage each stand for Hermetic Magus (p. 20). Its rule that the higher rank wins a dispute certamen would settle is recorded as a ruling only (Ch. 3).

### For every magus
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-rhine-shared -->

- **Generated stats**: the 60 magi the book describes without a stat block have stats built by the creation rules, following what the book says they are good at. Each sheet says so, and you can import them as the book's sketches instead (see [How to start a saga from a sourcebook](help:guide-book-packs)). The two peregrinatores of Chapter 3, Tabanus (38, Gauntleted at 23) and Felicia (24), have the book's own blocks.
- **Assumed ages**, from rank and history, where the book gives none: Gudrun Tigurina 70, Xavier 60, Indulius 65, Günther Lupus 55, Rudolph 45, Clemens 65, Marguerite 40, Wilhelm Weiss 60, Garrinchus 27, Hassan El-Megrayhi 80, Odorpes 60 and Caprea 50. Each sheet says the age is assumed.
- **Hedge factions and traditions**: the Witches of the Harz are played as folk witches, the two Volkhvy factions as Volkhvy and the hedge wizards of the forest paths from the Forest Paths record; the priesthoods of Woden and Hertha have no tradition record.

Those the book doesn't place sit at Kerguntuil on the Rose Coast, Caput Capra in the Ile de France, Bibracte in Burgundy, Baiocassium and Baiocassium Minor in the county of Bayeux, Rotomagus in the Seine basin, and Branugurix at Mount Dol.

- **How well known places and ruins are**: the book doesn't rate this, so the pack set each one.
- **The King of France**: a Duke, as the app's titles go no higher.
- **Coats of arms**: the book prints almost none, so lords carry their historical arms as far as the app can draw them: Brittany plain ermine (the later form; Peter of Dreux bore checky with an ermine quarter), Normandy gules two lions or, France azure three fleurs-de-lis (strewn across the field in 1220), Flanders or a lion sable, Champagne's bend without its cotises, Burgundy bendy without the bordure, the Templars argent a cross gules.
- **Story seeds as threats**: Chapter 10's story seeds are threats with stages and outcomes for when each is ignored, stopped or turned; those stages are the pack's own (Ch. 10).
- **Creatures' Fatigue**: the book gives none for the lutin or the Boar King, so none is entered.

### Corrections to the book
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-normandy-corrections -->

- **Anacrôn's spells**: Irresistible Pressgang is CrMe 35 and Twisted Spine Mu(Pe)Co 20, as their descriptions say; his spell list gives level 40 and MuCo 25 (Ch. 11).

- **Florum's move**: 1117, the timeline's date; Chapter 8 says the tenth century, and the record notes the clash.
- **The Spider's Palace**: the book puts it both "two days north of Ghent" and "between Bruges and Ghent"; the record notes both (Ch. 8).
- **Slips kept, with a note on the record**: Mnemon's age of 97 fits his history badly, and Skuld is "resident at Atramentum" before 1178, though Atramentum was founded in 1200.

### For every magus

- **Language**: the Tribunal's magi speak Irish unless the book points elsewhere: Helles French, Pollices and Lugardis Italian, Dyfrgi and Lewis Welsh, Dorján and Konstanczia Hungarian, Tug and Palantus High German, Peter and Igneous Drake English; Oswald adds English and Styrbjórn Old Norse.
- **Hedge factions and traditions**: the Céli Dé, the Cailleacha Phiseogach and the Ollamhain name their tradition records; the Druids, the Coill Trí, the Pact of Oireadh and the Bards have none.

# Assumptions made for Blood and Sand: The Levant Tribunal
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant -->

The Levant pack is built from *Blood and Sand* and its free web companion *Secrets in Sand*, which holds seven of the eight printed stat blocks. *Blood and Sand* is a Fourth Edition book, so its numbers had to be converted: the stats follow the Definitive Edition's Fourth Edition conversion guide where it applies, and where the guide is silent the pack made a judgement call, each one listed on this page. Otherwise the pack follows the book: its names, dates, Might scores, libraries and vis stores are the book's. Where the book is silent or contradicts itself, the pack inferred a value from the text or chose one. You can change any of them in your saga.

### Inferred from the text
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant-inferred -->

- **Stances the book implies but doesn't state**: each covenant's summary opens with its stance on the Crusade. Four are read from the covenant's account rather than stated: Para Belli is Crusader, from its warrior founders and the quaesitores' suspicion; Venti Rosa is messengers, a caravan of Redcaps; Urbs Rubra and both Pyramids are aloof.
- **Places with a holy or magical character but no number**: the Hidden Temple of Jerusalem is a Divine regio at 8 over the city's Divine 4; the Great Umayyad Mosque Divine 6, a great mosque with a major relic; Mount Sinai Divine 7; the Holy Fire takes the Holy Sepulchre's Divine 10; Petra Magic 3, like Urbs Rubra; Masyaf Divine 2, as a Muslim castle-town. Places that are neither plainly holy nor magical, such as the Horns of Hattin, Mount Carmel and Aleppo's Tower of the Serpents (an enchantment, not an aura), have no aura.
- **Dates**: contact with the caliphate ends with Harun's death in 809; Felix's Gauntlet is 1215, five years before the book's date; the border with Thebes is entered under 1204; the Hermetic Embassy is founded in autumn 1193, after that summer's meeting.
- **Ages**, where the book gives none, are chosen from rank and standing: Wraith 155 and Fade 150 (Wraith the elder, as the book says), Heliobus 110, Hergar 120 and Hildegard 115, Tiberius 90 and Leo 85, the young Flambeau of Para Belli between 29 and 40, David 30 and Xydas 28. Each sheet says the age is assumed.
- **Native tongues**, which the book doesn't give: Greek, Armenian, Arabic, Persian, Italian, French, High German or English, from each magus's name and history. Tau speaks French and Aurora High German.

### Judgement calls
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant-judgement -->

- **The Tribunal**: it meets every seven years at Domus Pacis, next in 1221; the book gives no season, so it meets in Summer. Tau is the Praeco by the 1207 ruling that the office goes to the eldest magus who can be reached, although Urbs Rubra's notes make Wraith the elder; the book gives no reason, and the pack invents none. The totals are the book's 15 covenants and 59 magi, so the roster counts the covenants and magi it doesn't name.
- **The Treaty of Baghdad and the 1221 vote**: renewal is a motion on the docket, brought by the Embassy, Domus Pacis and Aedes Veritatis against Foothold, Para Belli and the True Cross, so each party's magi lean their own way. **When the renewal passes**, give its ruling a term ending at the meeting of 1228, the last before the Treaty lapses in 1233, and the app puts it back on the docket then. The docket's other case is Foothold's charge that 'Umar killed a Crusader magus with no witness; the book's other quarrels, which nobody has brought, are stories.
- **The Treaty against *The Cradle and the Crescent***: that book has the Order of Suleiman still at war with the crusaders and the Order of Hermes in 1220, with no Hermetic covenant in its lands. The pack lets both accounts hold: the Treaty binds the Order of Hermes and the caliph's court, with Salah al-Din's mages and their Ayyubid heirs, but the Order of Suleiman as a body never signed it, so its war goes on. The Hermetic Embassy stays in Baghdad, and its summary says the sahirs' order distrusts it.
- **Covenants, rebuilt rather than converted**, as the conversion guide says: the four with statistics (Aedes Veritatis, Domus Pacis, Foothold and the Hermetic Embassy) keep the book's site, season, founding, aura, Aegis, members, library, vis and allies, and drop the old point totals. Their Boons and Hooks are chosen from the *Covenants* catalog to fit the old scores and description, as lines on the record: Aedes Veritatis, for example, has Natural Fortress, Veteran Fighters and Secondary Income, with Infamous, Rival and Dark Secret. The other eight are sketches. Aedes Veritatis is Summer for the book's "Summer/Autumn", still being at war; the second Pyramids is Winter, an old priesthood that only renews itself.
- **Auras the book doesn't give**: Urbs Rubra, Urania, Para Belli and al-Arama Magic 3 each; the second Pyramids' temple a regio of Giza at Magic 6, as by the great pyramids; the military orders' castles Divine 3. The book's Dominion is the app's Divine, and *Realms of Power: The Divine*'s guidance on its strength replaces the book's Islamic aura table, which the Levant's summary names as optional.
- **Both Pyramids** are entered, each saying the book offers them as alternatives or both at once; **Venti Rosa** has no site, its route and twice-yearly visits in its summary. The eleven priests of the second Pyramids are sketches with no stats, since the book says nothing of their magic.
- **Suggested vis sources are notes, not records.** The book gives each covenant's vis store and yearly supply but places no source. Where it gives a place vis, its notes add two or three suggested sources (not in the book) to fit the place and its aura, for you to use, change or ignore. They are never Vis Source records and don't count in a covenant's income. Esther's secret Terram source is her Personal Vis Source Virtue, the place unnamed.
- **Libraries**: the named summae keep the book's level and quality, which fall inside the Definitive Edition's range; the summae of Domus Pacis and Foothold are taken to be in Latin. Aedes Veritatis's Qur'an is a summa on Theology: Islam with no level or quality, noted as an authority.
- **Lords**: the app's titles are European, so the kings of Jerusalem, Cyprus and Lesser Armenia are dukes and the princes, sultans and emirs counts or barons, with the real title in each name. Tripoli's count is Bohemond IV of Antioch, Bohemond I of Tripoli; the Armenian king holds Tarsus, as the book has it. Konya's ruler is Kayqubad I, as the shared Sultan of Rum, with the book's Kay Kawus dying this year in its summary. Rulers' dates beyond 1220 go in the lords' summaries as what happens if history runs on.
- **What history supplies**: the book names no castles for the military orders, so the great orders hold history's chief castles of 1220, with their Grand Masters; the patriarchs of Jerusalem and Antioch and the archbishop of Nicosia are history's, and the heads of the eastern churches are left unnamed.
- **Stories**: the renewal vote and Aedes Veritatis's secret war on Foothold are active; eleven more are dormant, from 'Umar's double loyalty to the opening of the pyramids. Their steps are built from the book's one-paragraph situations. The book's story seeds are one line each on the record they hang on.
- **The book's alternatives** stay open where no later book settles them: Scipio's three readings, the three heads of John the Baptist, God as Law or as Love, the planets as spirits or as demons. Each is named in its summary, none chosen. The book's five possible Orders of Suleiman and its orders of jinn give way to *The Cradle and the Crescent*'s order and peoples.
- **Left out**, as the app has no record for them: the sahirs' and raqis' rules, the Divine Might of rulers, the Muslim calendar and the book's social statuses with no Definitive Edition Virtue (Turcopole, Dragoman, Member of Commune, Subject People), which are described in summaries. Magic items are in their holders' biographies, with their effects at Definitive Edition levels.

### Where the book disagrees with itself
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant-corrections -->

- **The first founding**: the Tribunal of the East is reckoned in 832, the timeline's year, though Chapter 2 puts it soon after 806.
- **The meeting of 1214**: meetings fell in 1193, 1200, 1207 and 1214, and each renewal is voted at the last meeting before it falls due, so the 1214 meeting had nothing to renew; its Diary entry says so.
- **Young founders**: Foothold's founders would have been 11 and 13 when it was founded in 1188; with the conversion's added fifth they are 20 and 22, just past Gauntlet, and 1188 is kept. Amber and Esther likewise join at 33 and 29, not 22 and 20.
- **'Umar's age** is *Secrets in Sand*'s 72 before conversion; his summary has him taking over after Hattin without the book's age for it.
- **Head counts**: the book says four magi live at Aedes Veritatis and at Domus Pacis but names three, so each has one unnamed magus.
- **The Embassy** is affiliated with Guernicus, which pays for it through Magvillus, though no Guernicus magus lives there, as the book has it.
- **Kept as the book says**: the Romans destroying Alexandria's library early in the first century, and Tarsus as the Armenian king's seat.

### Converting the printed magi
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant-conversion -->

- **The eight printed magi** ('Umar, Amber, Felix, Esther, Guillaume, Rufus, Spiral and Wardat) are converted by the guide: age raised by a fifth (so 'Umar is 86, as he is 72 in the book), apparent age as printed; Characteristics and Arts unchanged; Twilight Points divided by three as Warping ('Umar 4); Confidence 4 becomes 2 and 3 becomes 1. Their Virtue and Flaw totals are kept as printed, even where they no longer balance, as these magi aren't in creation.
- **Virtues and Flaws** take the catalog's tier; where it has both, a Flaw worth −2 or worse is Major. Renamed ones become their Definitive Edition names (Elementalist is Elemental Magic, Silent Magic Quiet Magic, Secret Vis Source Personal Vis Source); those with no new entry (No Sigil, Old, Outsider Trained in Order) are dropped. Each sheet's biography lists what changed.
- **Abilities** keep their scores; dropped ones move to the nearest Definitive Edition Ability (Scribe and Disputatio to Artes Liberales, Forgery to Guile, Read Lips to Awareness), and Certamen is dropped.
- **Spells** take the catalog's level and Art, so some move (Edge of the Razor, on Aedes Veritatis's swords too, from 5 to 20). A spell the catalog lacks is swapped for a catalog spell of the same Technique, Form and level; scrying the past or future, impossible now, becomes an Intellego spell of the same level. Each swap is in the biography.
- **'Umar and the Islamic Tradition**: his spells are the catalog's Hermetic ones, and his Virtues still let a troupe cast them the Islamic way. The Wrath of Allah is his own spell with its Islamic Range, Duration and Target typed in, at level 35. The Tradition is a faction, not a catalog tradition, since its magi are Hermetic; *Secrets in Sand*'s four Virtues and Flaws for it are in the catalog, reworded for the Definitive Edition.
- **Wardat is a Hermetic Sahir**: a magus of Ex Miscellanea in the Hermetic Sahirs tradition of *Houses of Hermes: Societates*, with its Sihr, Minor Magical Focus for jinn and Twilight Prone as her free House Virtues. That tradition is Iberian and rare in the Levant, so she is an outlier. She keeps no Arts and no Parma, as printed, though the later book's sahirs have Arts. Her Sihr of 6 is scored like an Art, a modest score there. The two jinn among her Familiarities are her bound jinn; the other three beings are linked from her biography.
- **Luigi**, Amber's cat, is her familiar: his cords are worked out again from her binding Lab Total, not copied, and the bond powers keep their effects at Definitive Edition levels.
- **Creatures**: Might is kept as printed; powers are priced in Might points at Definitive Edition levels; Soak, attacks and wounds are rebuilt from the Definitive Edition's rules, and the jinn's realms follow *The Cradle and the Crescent*. The five beings with only a Might score are marked so.

### For every magus
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-levant-shared -->

- **Generated stats**: the 30 other named magi, Tau, Aurora and Scipio among them, have stats built from the book's description with the core rules' experience for their age, at the book's age without the conversion's added fifth; each sheet says they are not an official write-up. Venti Rosa's four Redcaps without the Gift are generated without it. Arash, who swore the Treaty of Barcelona and not the Oath, has no House. You can import them as sketches instead (see [How to start a saga from a sourcebook](help:guide-book-packs)).
- **Shared records**: the Order of Suleiman and its families, the jinn, the Nizaris, the caliph and the Ayyubid princes, Georgia, Iraq and Baghdad, the Fifth Crusade and the Mongols follow *The Cradle and the Crescent*'s account, and the Sultan of Rum and the Emperor of Trebizond the Theban pack's, so importing another pack that shares them updates them and never doubles them. The Levant pack adds only its own records beside them.
- **Hedge factions and traditions**: the Order of Suleiman and its two families, the Nizaris and the Syrian Nizaris, and the Karaites name their tradition records; the jinn, the Islamic Tradition, the Bedouin, the Turcomans, the Druze and the other faiths have none.

# Assumptions made for The Cradle and the Crescent
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-cradle -->

The Cradle and the Crescent pack is built from *The Cradle and the Crescent*, a Fifth Edition book, so nothing in it had to be converted: its names, dates, Might scores, stat blocks and vis are the book's. It is a region book, not a Tribunal book: Arabia, Mesopotamia, Persia and the Silk Road lie east of the Levant, outside every Hermetic Tribunal, so the pack holds no Tribunal, Praeco, Quaesitor, Peripheral Code or docket. Where the book is silent or contradicts itself, the pack inferred a value from the text or chose one, and this page lists each. You can change any of them in your saga.

### Inferred from the text
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-cradle-inferred -->

- **Auras the book's rules give but don't number**: a house of wisdom or shrine in a city takes Divine 4, the book's value for a city; Mecca and Medina take 5 (a city's 4 and one more for a holy city), their harams Divine 3; Zamzam takes the Haram Mosque's Divine 10. Where a place has two auras by day and night, or one beside another, it takes the stronger: Isma'il Samanid's tomb Magic 3, the Shahr-i-Zindah Faerie 5, the Taklimakan Magic 7, the Mosque of the Jinn Faerie 5.
- **Ages**, where the book gives none: Kha'trazh 97, looking 50; Niya 35; Meriq 75. 'Arkhaman is 25, as printed; the book gives no age for Nil or for Cartophilius of Mercere.
- **Dates and seasons**: the Dar al-Hikma is founded in 787, the year of the First Council its house grew from. Where the book gives only an era, its history takes a round year, and each Diary entry says so (the guides to Nur al-Din and Salah al-Din in 1174, Kha'trazh's election in 1190, and so on). Entries about the Majlis and the Crusades are in Summer, the rest in Spring. The Majlis is in the calendar each Summer, on 15 to 17 August in 1220.
- **Places the book leaves vague**: the Stone Lovers in the Najd, Iram in 'Uman, Lukman's nest in the Hijaz, Arzanjan in Greater Armenia, Takht-i-Suleiman in Jibal; a creature with no printed home lives where its people or its story do (the Bedouin beasts in the Najd, the girtablullu and mushhushshu in the Jazira, Azi Dahak at Mount Damavand). The qareen and the 'afrit of the Bottle have no place.
- **Who answers to whom**: the Grand Sharif and Medina's governor to al-Kamil, Atabeg Uzbek to the Khwarazm-shah, the Hazarasp atabeg to the shah; rulers the book doesn't name are left unnamed.

### Judgement calls
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-cradle-judgement -->

- **No Tribunal**: the houses of wisdom belong to none, and nothing on the Tribunal pages comes with the pack. The Order of Suleiman's history, from the first wazirs to Kha'trazh's election, is the Dar al-Hikma's covenant Diary; the yearly Majlis is a calendar entry.
- **The houses of wisdom are covenants**: the app's covenant stands for a bayt al-hikma. The Dar al-Hikma, Savah's and the House of Healing are sketches; the other ten are line records. The several houses of Nizwa are one record. Kha'trazh belongs to none, as the book names his city but no house in it.
- **Covenant auras the book doesn't give**: a house in a city has Divine 4; Savah's sits in its observatory at the printed Magic 5; the House of Healing is Divine 4 with its laboratories in a Magic 3 regio below. Dayr Qunna, a walled house posing as a monastery outside the town, is given Magic 3, the pack's value for a magical site the book leaves unnumbered, since sahirs are unlikely to settle where there is no aura at all. Other unnumbered Magic pockets take 3 too, and an unnumbered holy site Divine 3.
- **The Stone's and River's seats** are covenants at Damascus and Cairo with no place record: those cities lie outside the book's lands and are the Levant pack's to describe.
- **Sahirs are companions**, not magi: Kha'trazh, Niya, Meriq, Nil and 'Arkhaman belong to the Sahirs (Suhhar Sulayman) tradition, with the Sahir Virtue, The Gift for Niya and 'Arkhaman, and Vizier for the book's viziers. Kha'trazh, Niya, Meriq and Nil are sketches; the creation tools are still Hermetic, so they don't generate a sahir's stats. **'Arkhaman** is entered as the book prints him, his seven spells as tradition effects (Kharrat's summoning with Summoning, the others with Naranj) at their printed levels and Penetration, and **Kharrat**, his Infernal jinni, is bound to him and in the Bestiary.
- **Turn the traditions on**: importing a pack doesn't change which traditions a saga offers, as it doesn't change the year. Before creating new members, turn on the Sahirs, Nizari Mustajibs, Mobeds and Bedouin Hakima under [Traditions Offered](help:section-traditions). The sahirs already in the pack show their tradition's chapter either way.
- **Factions and traditions**: the Order and its six families name the Sahirs tradition, the Nizaris the Nizari Mustajibs, the Mazdeans the Mobeds, and the Bedouin sahirs and their code the Bedouin Hakima. The yatus, Sabians, ashipu, brass-smiths, witches of Nizwa and peris have no tradition record; their powers are in their summaries.
- **Suggested vis sources are notes, not records.** Where the book says a place is rich in vis but places no source (the Empty Quarter, the Stone Lovers, Mazandaran), its notes suggest two or three sources that are not in the book, for you to use, change or ignore. They are never Vis Source records and add nothing to a covenant's income. Printed sources keep the book's Form and, where it gives one, amount; the houses of wisdom have no vis, as the book gives none.
- **Catalog names the book uses that the app lacks**: Pass as Human is Passes for Human (Jumayl), Tough Feathers is Thick Fur/Thick Scales (the ruq), Water Lore is Nature Lore (Water) (Ighraq), Increased Might is Increased Faerie Might (the aladlammu); Gluttonous, Positive Reputation and Thick Skin are left out and named in the creature's notes. Theology: Mazdean is Theology: Zoroastrian, Mobed Lore is Organization Lore: Mobeds, and Rostam's line is the general Mythic Blood, its power and Flaw in Seistan's summary. The Ghuzz's great size is the catalog's Large Virtue, which the book gives every Ghuzz, so no Large Blood entry is needed. Every jinni takes the Jinni Free Virtue, and 'Arkhaman's Sihr is the Sihr Virtue.
- **Creatures**: Might Forms the book leaves out are chosen from the being's nature (Ighraq Aquam, the faerie ghula Imaginem, the Infernal ghul Vim); a template's Might takes the book's usual value; a score the book marks n/a is 0, named in the notes.
- **Stories**: the Majlis and the succession, the counter-crusade, the Nizari feud, Mecca's succession, Azi Dahak's search for a host, the demons of Ghur and Kabul, Merv's books and the apparition at Durenmar are under way; Itineris Serica and the return of the Iberian sahirs are dormant. Whether Azi Dahak stands behind the Mongols is left open, in the threat's secrets. The book's story seeds are one line each on the record they hang on.
- **The jinn's lands** (Amerabad, Golden Baghdad, the jinn's Khashgar and Al-Khadar) are Rumoured places with no aura, as the book numbers none; all but Al-Khadar, a Magic barrier, lie in Faerie across a Threshold rather than in a regio.

### Where the book disagrees with itself
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-cradle-corrections -->

- **Al-Khayzuran** is Harun al-Rashid's mother, not a grand vizier, as the timeline has her.
- **The Iberian split** runs from 912 to 925, as Chapter 3 tells it, ending in the timeline's year; the timeline's out-of-order entry for 848 is left out.
- **Kharrat's Envisioning** costs 1 point for a dream and 5 for a waking vision; his text's "1 or 2" is noted on the power.
- **The racehorse** is Bali' al-Ard: "Hesaan" in the text only means horse.
- **Page numbers** on creatures are read from the extracted text's page markers, so one may be a page off.

### Shared with other packs
<!-- help: pack-assumptions-cradle-shared -->

- **Shared records**: the Order of Suleiman with its Stone and River families, the jinn, the Nizaris, the caliph and the Ayyubid princes, Georgia, Greater Armenia, Iraq, Baghdad and Mosul, the Fifth Crusade and the Mongols follow this book, and are built exactly as the Levant pack builds them (the Sultan of Rum and the Emperor of Trebizond as the Theban pack does).
