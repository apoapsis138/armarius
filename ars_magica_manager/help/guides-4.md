# How to bind and keep a familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars -->

This guide follows a familiar from the magus's first meeting with the creature to a bond strengthened with powers. Use it when a magus in your saga is ready to bind a familiar, or when you are adding a magus who already has one. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Familiars.

### Finding the familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars-finding -->

Finding a suitable creature and winning it over is a matter for your stories, not for the app. Play it out at the table; the app only comes in once the creature has agreed to the bond.

A magus can have one familiar at a time, so Bind Familiar is only offered while at least one magus in the saga has none. A Bjornaer, whose heartbeast takes its place, can't bind one (DE p. 234).

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-familiars-limits -->

The app works out the point cost of each cord, whether a binding or empowerment can be done, its Lab Total, its vis, and the progress of a power over several seasons. The Bronze Cord is taken off the magus's aging roll each Winter.

Most of the familiar's own figures are typed in: its Soak, Magic Resistance and Might, its wounds and penalties, and each bond power's figures. The Golden Cord takes its botch dice off casting totals (a hedge power's too); the Silver Cord is added by Roll beside Personality Traits (a trait roll, resisting mental influence, or the familiar freeing its magus's mind), and the True Friend Virtue is recorded but has no effect the app applies.

Deleting the magus also deletes their familiar, and exporting the magus takes the familiar with them.

To remove only the familiar, press `d` on it in the tree.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->

<!-- /gui -->
Then the bond is broken, and the magus loses True Friend, Loyal (Partner) and any planned familiar seasons.

# How to initiate a character into a Mystery
<!-- help: guide-initiation -->

This guide follows a Mystery Initiation from the Script to the new Virtue on the Initiate's sheet. Use it when a character joins a Mystery cult, takes the next step in their House's Mysteries, or initiates themselves into a Virtue. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 10: Long-Term Events, Mystery Initiation; The Mysteries Revised Edition and Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults give the full treatment, and the Definitive Edition wins where they differ.

### Before the season
<!-- help: guide-initiation-before -->

An Initiation needs three things, settled at the table first:

- **A Mystagogue** who knows the cult's Lore (House Bjornaer Lore, Mithraic Lore and so on). A character can be their own Mystagogue: a self-initiation uses the Initiate's own scores.
<!-- tui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it on the [Abilities tab](help:tab-abilities) (`2`) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it in the [Abilities chapter](help:tab-abilities) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /gui -->
- **A Script**, written like a Lab Text. Its bonus is the sum of its components, as the storyguide agrees them: +3 for a special place at a special time, +3 for each Quest, +1 for serving the cult (once), +1 for a sacrifice of wealth, +3 to +6 for a sacrifice of something of great value, and the Ordeal's +3 (a Minor Flaw, or losing a Minor Virtue), +6 (raising a Minor Flaw to Major) or +9 (a Major Flaw, or losing a Major Virtue). Quests and other sacrifices are played out in their own seasons or stories.

### Planning the Initiation
<!-- help: guide-initiation-plan -->

The Initiation is the Initiate's season, planned like any other.

<!-- tui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their Planning tab (`6` for a magus, `5` for anyone else), and press `e` in [This Season](help:section-char-season). A covenant's [Seasons tab](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their **Planning** chapter, and click **Plan** (or **Edit**, if a plan is there already) in [This Season](help:section-char-season). **Plan** in a covenant's [Seasons chapter](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /gui -->
2. In **Activity**, choose **Undergo an Initiation**.
3. In **Teacher / master**, choose the Mystagogue. Leave it blank for a self-initiation.
4. Type the **Script bonus** and the **Mystagogue's Lore**, spelt as it is in the Mystagogue's Abilities. To vary the Script, choose the change in **Vary the Script** (see below). When the Script makes a sacrifice, choose in **Sacrificed** what is given up: one of the initiate's devices (a talisman among them), carried items, books or familiar, an apprentice, mundane wealth, vis, or a season serving the cult. It is made when the season is played, Initiation or not, and the Diary says so. A device, item, book or familiar is gone, so it can't be sacrificed twice; a season of service takes the next season as Other, unless it is planned already; vis is taken from the initiate's own raw vis, as many pawns of the Art as **Vis of** and **Pawns sacrificed** say. A plan whose vis isn't there to give is refused: a Sacrifice that isn't made invalidates the whole Script (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16). The bonus it brings is the Script's Sacrifice component (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 12, 15-16).

The Ordeal's size follows from what it costs: a Minor Flaw or Virtue makes a Minor Ordeal, a Major one a Major Ordeal, and a raised Flaw a Medium one. Leave Ordeal blank for none.

It shows the Initiation Total (the Mystagogue's Presence + Lore + Script bonus) against the Target Level, and whether the Initiation will succeed. Save refuses a plan that is missing something or breaks a rule, and says why.

There is no die: the Initiation succeeds when the total reaches the Target Level. The Target is 15 for a Minor Virtue and 21 for a Major one, or 18 and 30 when the Mystagogue doesn't have the Virtue (always so for a self-initiation into a Virtue the character lacks).

**House Verditius.** Every magus with Verditius Magic can follow the House's sample Scripts, listed under House Verditius (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field: one for each of the eight Inner Mysteries, the Outer Mystery and four confraternities' (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 118-135). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Verditius (the book's Verditius Cult Lore). The Initiate needs it at 3 for a Minor Inner Mystery and 5 for a Major (p. 118). The first Inner Mystery always brings the Flaw Hubris as a Minor Ordeal: the season adds the Flaw and says what to set the Hubris trait to (1, +1 with Proud, +3 with Proud as a Major Flaw), and each Inner Mystery's note gives the Hubris experience it earns (1 Minor, 3 Major; DE p. 242). The House's own Ordeals may each be suffered once; Prone to Chance and Averse to Risk exclude each other, and no one in the Primus's line may Sever the Ties that Bind (pp. 120-121).

**House Merinita.** Every magus with Faerie Magic belongs to the House and can follow its lineages' sample Scripts, listed under House Merinita (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field: the Line of Quendalon's Animae Magic, the Heirs to Merlin's Charm Magic, the Followers of Pendule's Spell Timing, the Shadow-Masters' Glamour, the Vestals' Nature Lore (as Lares Lore) and the True Merinitae's Wilding (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 83-106). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Merinita (the book's House Merinita Lore); an Initiate needs it at 1 for an Inner Mystery (p. 88). The Arcadian, Folk and Illusion Mysteries need Faerie Magic; Binding the Gift is the House's own; the Nature Mysteries (Awakening, Wilding, Guardian of Nature) need Nature Lore instead and not the House's Lore (pp. 86, 102-104). Glamour always brings the Major Flaw Vulnerable Magic as its Ordeal: the season adds it, unless the plan's Ordeal names it already, and a Script written out as components counts its Major Ordeal. Type its condition on the plan (Vulnerable Magic's condition): it must be one the magus doesn't have the Flaw for already, since the Flaw is taken again only with a new condition (pp. 87, 102). Perpetuity's season notes that it usually brings Unnatural Magic.

**House Bjornaer.** Every magus with the Heartbeast can follow the House's Scripts, listed under House Bjornaer (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 24-33). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Bjornaer (the book's House Bjornaer Lore, which the rules also read, the Initiation Total included: a character with either name scores it; HoH:MC pp. 28, 30, Errata p. 54). **The Ritual of Twelve Years** gives the Heartbeast: Target 15, Script +6, plus the Participation Modifier typed as the other modifier, +1 for each witness (the Mystagogue included) whose heartbeast shares the probationer's temperament and -1 for each opposed (sanguine opposes melancholic, choleric phlegmatic; p. 26). Choose Heartbeast as the Virtue initiated: the season then adds Heartbeast 1, and the familiar forgone counts as a Minor Ordeal. It fails for one who has ever bound a familiar, even one since dead (DE p. 234; p. 24): one bound to him now, or a familiar season in his Diary; tick Has ever bound a familiar on the plan for one the app can't see and is refused to an Initiate of another House's Mystery (p. 18), and it is never repeated. Clan Ilfetu leads the Ritual and the House Mysteries, Secret Name (+8), Theriomorphy (+6) and Sensory Magic (+9) (p. 25); a Clan Mystery's Trial, Deed or Oath (+7, the Trial +13 with a Major Ordeal) needs a Mystagogue with Presence + the Lore 8, less the Initiate's past Ordeal's reduction. **The Mystery of the Inner Heartbeast** (Epitome, Chimera or Anima) needs the Lore 5: Target 21, Script +13, its Ordeal a Major Hermetic or Supernatural Flaw, or for a magus who changes shape the loss of his Skinchanger or Shapeshifter (pp. 17, 28-30). With no Mystagogue chosen, the Great Beast leads it, with Presence +2 and the Lore 8. The Epitome raises one Characteristic: the inner heartbeast's to the species' + 3 and the human form's by 1; the outer heartbeast changes only through that +1, combined as usual (pp. 23, 30). So the book's example ends with an inner Perception of +5 and an outer +2 (the eagle's own, one above where it started). The app follows this rule text: Errata p. 54 gives the outer heartbeast +5, but that is the inner figure and contradicts the rule, and Through the Aegis p. 14 (Hilde Oddfish, Strength +7 outer and +10 inner) applies the rule as written. **Refinements** of the inner heartbeast are the magus's own: the embryonic Script (+7, and +3 where his inner heartbeast resonates: an Epitome with Virtues and Characteristics, a Chimera with Qualities and Size, an Anima with Powers) against 15 (a Quality), 18 (Size, a Characteristic, a Minor Virtue), 21 (a Minor Power) or 30 (a Major Virtue or Power), lowered by past Ordeals as usual. Each earlier increase of the same Size or Characteristic adds 3: take 3 from the other modifier for each. The season notes the vis eaten (a pawn per three points of the Ease Factor) and the Warping Point; a Refinement changes only the inner heartbeast, so it is noted on the sheet by hand (pp. 32-33).

**House Criamon.** A Criamon magus's paths are listed under House Criamon
(Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field, one Script for each
avenue (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 61-71; Errata p. 55), and The
Enigma's Follow an Avenue plans one. The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore:
Criamon (the book's House Criamon Lore, which counts too); a station
needs it at its place on the path + 1. Each avenue's Target is a Major
Virtue's 21, as the book prints it whether or not the guide holds the
station, lowered by past Ordeals as usual. With no Mystagogue chosen, the
guide the House provides guides at Presence + Lore 6, the printed
Scripts' assumption, or a ghostly Primus (Lore 9, with the Presence set on
Change Path) when The Enigma names him (p. 60). The Mystagogue need only
know House Criamon Lore (DE p. 387); the app notes one not of the House
as unusual, its own reading. When the plan names no Ordeal of its own, the station's
Ordeal Flaws are added (a Major Pious replacing Vow or Higher Purpose),
and the Ordeal counts at the size the book's next Targets show; the
avenues are followed in order along a path. Some stations add catalogue
Virtues too: Clear Thinker, Common Sense and Keen Vision; the Gentle Gift,
or the Blatant Gift lifted to an ordinary Gift; Free Expression and
Inspirational (pp. 64, 70-71). His aptitude for a path, from a head bound
in infancy, adds +3 to its first station (p. 51).

**Script components.** A Hermetic Mystery's Script can be written out on the plan as its **Script components** (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 15-16; DE p. 388): a special place at a special time +3 (once), each specific Quest +3 (a unique Quest +6), the Initiate's time serving the cult +1 (once), material goods +1 (once), something of great symbolic value +3 to +6 (a talisman, familiar or powerful device), the Mystagogue's time +3, and the sympathy of the rite to the Virtue, usually 0 to +3 but -5 to +5, agreed with the storyguide. The Ordeal's own bonus is added from the plan's **Ordeal**: +3 for a Minor Flaw gained or Minor Virtue lost (a Minor Virtue replaced by its Major one counts as that), +6 for a Minor Flaw raised to Major, +9 for a Major Flaw or Virtue; a Verditius's first Inner Mystery counts its Hubris as a Minor Ordeal when no other is named. The **Script bonus** typed above is added to the components. A component listed twice that may be held once, or a size out of its range, invalidates the whole Script (TMRE p. 16). With no components listed, the typed bonus is the whole Script, as before, except that a Verditius's first Inner Mystery adds its Hubris Ordeal's +3 to it (and to a catalog Script's printed bonus), since that Ordeal always comes and the bonus can't know of it (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 117-118). A Verditius Initiate is also offered the House's own components (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 117): a lesser enchanted item of the 4th magnitude sacrificed +3, a greater one of the 7th or his talisman +6, the Mystagogue's wealth +1 (once), and the Outer Mystery given as the Arts are opened +6. A hedge or House Script from the catalog keeps its printed bonus; the season's note lists its components as the book gives them (the Verditius Outer Mystery's add to 14, though the book prints +12, which is used).

The House's Hubris is now kept on the sheet: the first Inner Mystery makes the Hubris Personality Trait (1, +1 with Proud, +3 with Proud as a Major Flaw), each Inner Mystery adds its experience (1 Minor, 3 Major), and the Increased Hubris Ordeal adds enough for one more point (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 121). Hubris grows like an Ability: its experience toward the next point is kept with the trait. A confraternity's Script also notes the Mystery and Flaw it gives its members, their Craft and the confraternity's path (Roland, Balento, Irene the Younger, Himinis the Mad; pp. 121-123). See [Hubris](help:verditius-hubris) for what a sale, the Contest or a vendetta does to it.

For a successful Initiation, the app adds the Virtue (with what it names) to the Initiate's [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf) and applies the Ordeal: it adds the Flaw gained, takes away the Virtue lost, or replaces the Minor Flaw raised with its Major Flaw. A Great Characteristic Virtue gained raises the score, and one lost lowers it again. A failed Initiation changes nothing, and its Ordeal is not suffered: the Mystagogue sees that a Script will fail before any sacrifice is made. Two failures still cost the Ordeal: one that distractions made fail (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12), and an experimental Script whose rite fails, since its Ordeals are suffered before the rite is checked (p. 18). Either way, the Diary records the Initiation Total against the Target.

### Past Ordeals make later Initiations easier
<!-- help: guide-initiation-ordeals -->

An Ordeal lowers the Target Level of the Initiate's next three Initiations, never below 9 (DE p. 388): by 3, 2 and 1 after a Minor Ordeal, 6, 4 and 2 after a Medium one, and 9, 6 and 3 after a Major one. It doesn't help the Initiation it was suffered in. When the Initiate has suffered several, only the one that lowers the Target most counts: a Major Ordeal followed by a Minor one still takes 6 off the next Initiation. The app keeps every Ordeal and counts each one down after each Initiation that gives its Virtue; a failed Initiation uses up no step. The book doesn't say whether a failed one counts among "the next three" (DE p. 388), so that is the app's reading. An unGifted Initiate's past Ordeals lower nothing (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). The Initiate's [This Season](help:section-char-season) section lists the past Ordeals still counting and what they take off the next Target. Flaws taken at character creation don't count as Ordeals.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-initiation-limits -->

**The app works out:** the Initiation Total and Target Level, whether the Mystagogue has the Virtue, the largest reduction among past Ordeals, the checks on the Lore and the Ordeal, the Ordeal's size, and the Virtues and Flaws added or taken away on success.

**Varying the Script:** the Mystagogue may make one change in an Initiation. Choose it in **Vary the Script** (add a component, Ease 9; change one, 12; remove one, 15) and type in **The change adds** what it does to the Script bonus (negative when removing). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore: success adds the change to the Script bonus, and failure fails the Initiation, with no Virtue and no Ordeal, though the Script as it was may be tried another season (DE pp. 388-389). Before then, the line under the form gives the roll the change needs and the Initiation Total if it works. The Mysteries Revised Edition (p. 17) says instead that the original Script still works; the Definitive Edition wins, so this isn't a bug. Removing a component (Ease 15) is The Mysteries Revised's own addition, and it doesn't let a Minor Virtue's Script become a Major one.

**Overlapping Scripts:** a Script that shares Quests or Ordeals with one the Initiate already followed can't be used as it is: the Mystagogue changes the shared component (**Change a component**, Ease 12) or puts a different one in its place (**Replace a component**, Ease 15), and types in **The change adds** what that does to the bonus (Legends of Hermes pp. 50-51). Each Quest a Script written out as components names (its note) is recorded on the Initiate when the rite is carried out, and shown as **Quests performed** on the Initiate's This Season, beside the past Ordeals; an Initiation whose Script names one again says so on the plan and the season, and that Quest gives the Script nothing, since a Quest counts toward one Initiation only (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16): change the Script first. An Ordeal is spotted the same way by the Flaw the Initiate already has.

**An experimental Script:** with no Script, or one that falls short, tick **Experimental Script**, type the proposed rite's components in **Script bonus**, and choose a **Risk** of 0 to 3 (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 18). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + risk on the Extraordinary Results chart (with 1 + risk botch dice) and reads it for a rite: side effects and minor flaws or benefits become Personality Traits of the Script, a major flaw an extra Ordeal Flaw with no bonus, a major benefit a stronger Virtue or a second Minor one, a Discovery experience for the Mystagogue, a modified effect a different Virtue (the Diary says what to add by hand); a fatal flaw or a complete failure gives no Virtue, No Benefit takes the risk off the total, and Disaster fails the rite and gives the Mystagogue and the Initiate each a Major Flaw. Then it rolls the rite's validity: a simple die + risk + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore against 9 + the proposed bonus, or 15 + it when the Initiate is someone else; a failure gives no Virtue. Any failure still costs the rite's Ordeal. The Initiation Total adds the storyguide's **Sympathy bonus** and the risk; the Script written down afterwards keeps the sympathy but not the risk. An experimental Script isn't also varied.

**UnGifted Initiates:** an unGifted Initiate's Initiation must include an Ordeal, his past Ordeals don't lower its Target, and an unGifted Mystagogue must know the power, so an unGifted character can't initiate himself (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). The app applies this to every Initiation, Hermetic cults' included. A magus counts as Gifted.

**Hedge Scripts:** to follow one of a hedge tradition's printed Scripts, choose it in **Hedge Script** (the list holds the Scripts of the traditions someone in the saga belongs to). The tradition's Characteristic and Lore are used instead of Presence and the Lore typed, the Script's own bonus is used and anything typed in **Script bonus** is added to it, and its Ease Factor is the Target. When the Mystagogue doesn't know the power the Ease Factor rises by 3 for a Minor Virtue, 9 for a Major (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 13). The app counts him as knowing it when he has the **Virtue initiated**; when the power is an Ability (a gruagach's Give, say), tick **Mystagogue knows the power**. The Mystagogue must belong to the tradition, a tradition without self-initiation can't initiate itself, and a Script for the Gifted needs a Gifted Initiate. With a hedge Script the **Virtue initiated** may be left blank: the Diary then names what the Script gives, to add by hand. Past Ordeals lower a hedge Script's Ease Factor too, never below 9, and never an Ease Factor already lower.

**Who may be Initiated:** two Mysteries check it. **Secret Name** fails for a magus with a talisman or a familiar (DE p. 235), and **Faerie Magic** needs one touched by the fay, by a fay Virtue or Flaw or a Warping Point from a faerie (DE p. 236). The app can't tell where Warping came from, so any Warping Point counts.

**Distractions:** **Months lost** on the plan takes a third of the Initiation Total a month, rounded up. If that makes the Initiation fail, the Ordeal is still suffered and the Initiation must be repeated (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12).

**You type:** the Script bonus, as the storyguide agrees it. For an experimental Script, the proposed bonus, the risk and the storyguide's sympathy bonus. Which Major Flaw a Minor Flaw is related to is the troupe's call: the app checks only that it is a Major Flaw.

A Mystagogue who spends a whole season on the Script (teaching a Supernatural Ability, say) plans **Act as Mystagogue**, and a season spent on a Quest is **Serve a Mystagogue / Quest season**; both need The Mysteries Revised Edition in play.

**See also:** [Plan Season](help:plan-season), [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf), [Advancing a season](help:advance-season), [How the app handles advancement](help:guide-advancement)

[Importing an exported record](help:import-data-records) explains the rules. In short:

- **Nothing is overwritten.** Everything imported gets new ids. A record with the same name as one already here is added beside it, and the preview says so.

- **A missing covenant.** When a character's or laboratory's covenant has no namesake in this saga, a covenant with that name and its old aura is added, so the aura comes too.
- **Spells, House Rules and Abilities.** A saga-made spell is matched to one here with the same name, Technique, Form and level. A House Rule is matched by name, type and tier, and a saga Ability by name, against the catalog's and the saga's. Anything not matched is added.

### When a name is already taken
<!-- help: guide-import-export-duplicates -->

When an imported book or spell has the name of one already in the saga, the app asks about each one. **Skip** leaves it out, and **Duplicate** adds it anyway. **Skip All** and **Duplicate All** give the same answer for the rest of that import. Spell names are compared in any case. Nothing is added until every question is answered, and cancelling a question imports nothing.

### Keeping backups
<!-- help: guide-import-export-backups -->

Each saga is one file, the saga's id followed by `.json`, in the `sagas` folder of your save folder. To keep it somewhere else, use [Change Save Folder](help:saga-picker-actions) on the saga picker. The app saves every change as you make it.

- **Before each season.** Before a season or downtime run is applied, the app copies the saga to `sagas/backups` and keeps the last five. The newest has the saga's own file name, and older ones end `.1.json` to `.4.json`. Each has a copy from just after the advance beside it, with `.after` in its name. **Undo Last Season** uses them (see [Undoing a mistake](help:guide-seasons-undo)).
- **Anything older.** That's the only backup the app keeps, so copy the saga's file yourself from time to time.
- **One record.** To keep a single character, laboratory or covenant safe before a big change, export it as above.
- **A damaged file.** Every saga file, export and pack is checked as it's read. A field holding the wrong kind of thing, from a file edited by hand or damaged, is named, and the saga isn't opened or changed. Copy a backup over its file to go back to before the damage.

# How to host a saga for your troupe
<!-- help: guide-hosting -->

The storyguide can let the players open the saga in a web browser on their own computers, while it stays on the storyguide's. The app and the saga file never leave your computer; each player who opens the page gets their own copy of the app, running on your machine.

You need a private network that you and the players share. The app doesn't come with one: use your home network, **Tailscale** or **ZeroTier** (recipes below). Never host on an address the whole internet can reach: anyone who reaches the address gets a copy of the app and its sign-in, and the browser page isn't encrypted beyond what the network adds. The app refuses such an address (and "every address", `0.0.0.0`) unless you tick **I know this is reachable from the internet**. What can go wrong this way, and what the app does about it, is in the app's threat model (`docs/threat-model-browser-and-ssh-hosting.md` beside its source).

**What the app does for you.** The host answers only requests addressed to the address you chose, and the app's own connection only from its own page, so a web page from another site can't open a copy. Three wrong passcodes for one name are free; after that each try waits, longer each time (30 seconds, then 2 minutes, 10 minutes and an hour), in every tab at once. A passcode shorter than 14 characters signs in once more and asks for a new one (see [Signing in](help:sign-in)). A copy opened from a browser or over SSH has no command palette, can't change your settings, and its file chooser (Import, for a storyguide who signs in) shows only your exports folder.

What the players get in their browser (or over SSH) is the terminal version of the app, so their screens look like it rather than like this window. You keep using the desktop window while you host: it and the players' copies all save into the same saga file, each change merged with the others.

### Remote seats: hosting safely
<!-- help: guide-hosting-safety; gui -->

The app on your computer listens to **this computer only** (port 8790
unless you change it): nothing on your network or the internet can reach
it directly. A **tunnel** carries players to it: a small program on your
computer that keeps one connection open to a company's servers, which
give you an https address. Inside the tunnel the app runs its own
encryption, locked to your host's key, so the tunnel company carries what
it cannot read (it sees that someone connected, and when, not what
passed). Choose one of the tunnels below; the steps are as of November
2026, and each service changes its pages from time to time, so follow its
own guide where these differ.

- **Cloudflare Tunnel** (free; you need a domain on Cloudflare). Install
  `cloudflared`, then `cloudflared tunnel login`, `cloudflared tunnel
  create armarius`, and `cloudflared tunnel route dns armarius
  saga.example.com`. In its `config.yml`, point the hostname at
  `http://localhost:8790`, then `cloudflared tunnel run armarius` (or
  install it as a service so it starts with Windows). In Host for
  Players choose **Cloudflare Tunnel** and the address
  `https://saga.example.com`. Where to go: developers.cloudflare.com,
  Cloudflare Tunnel. Its quick tunnels (`trycloudflare.com`) change
  address each time, so every Invite would be lost: use a named tunnel.
- **Tailscale Funnel** (free; no domain needed). Install Tailscale and
  sign in; in the admin console's DNS page turn on HTTPS and Funnel. Then
  `tailscale funnel --bg 8790` publishes it at
  `https://YOUR-PC.YOUR-TAILNET.ts.net` (the public side must be port
  443, 8443 or 10000; the 8790 is your computer's). `tailscale funnel
  8790 off` stops it. In Host for Players choose **Another tunnel**.
  Where to go: tailscale.com/docs, Tailscale Funnel.
- **ngrok** (a free account gets one fixed address). Install ngrok, add
  your token, then `ngrok http 8790 --url YOUR-NAME.ngrok-free.dev`. A
  random address changes each time, so use your fixed one. Choose
  **Another tunnel**. Where to go: ngrok.com/docs.
- **Your own server** (a small rented server with a domain). On it, run
  a web server that adds https and passes the player's address on, such
  as Caddy (`reverse_proxy localhost:8790`); from your computer, open a
  reverse SSH tunnel to it: `ssh -N -R 8790:localhost:8790 you@server`.
  Choose **Another tunnel**.
- **Port forwarding on your router** is not offered: the app won't
  listen beyond this computer, and opening your router would show every
  player (and anyone who finds it) your home address. If you must, put a
  web server such as Caddy on this computer in front of it, as on your
  own server above, and forward only port 443 to that.

Whichever you use, the address goes in **Tunnel address** (`https://`,
nothing after the name), and the tunnel points at `http://localhost:8790`.
The address is not secret; the Invite carries it, always as a secure
`wss://` address, and a player's app refuses an Invite naming a plain
`ws://` one anywhere but on its own computer. The app tells players
apart by the address the tunnel passes on (Cloudflare's own header, or
the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, which ngrok and Caddy add); a tunnel
that passes on none makes the limits count every player as one.

**Issuing and revoking.** Issue each player their own Invite, and send it
by a different way from your call if you can (a chat message, an email).
It works once, on one device, for 24 hours. On the call, read all six of
your words, and have the player check every one; approve a device only
when its player reads you the three words your screen shows. A changed
passcode, or one you reset, ends every seat still signed in with the old
one at once. If a device is lost or a player leaves, **Revoke** its
seat at once (Seats): it is cut off within a second. A storyguide's
device sees everything in the saga, so revoke it as soon as it is lost.
If you think your host key itself has leaked (a copy of your keychain),
**Rotate Host Key**: every Invite and seat ends, and everyone needs a new
Invite and new six words. Seats nobody uses stop working after the days
you set in **Idle Expiry** (90 unless you change it).

**The Log** (Host for Players) records who did what and when: Invites
issued and redeemed, approvals and rejections, sign-ins and wrong
passcodes, lock-outs, revokes, which records each seat read or changed,
passcode changes and resets asked, role changes, and seasons advanced or
undone by a storyguide. It never holds a passcode, a credential, an
Invite's code or what was typed, and it lives in your save folder, not in
the saga, so players never receive it. It keeps a year.

**What the app does for you.** A wrong passcode slows that device down
(three free tries, then growing waits), without locking anyone else out.
A connection that floods, sends what the app doesn't understand, or stays
silent too long is closed. Each player's seat receives only what that
player may see, and every change they send is checked against what is
theirs to change. The full list is in the app's threat model
(`docs/threat-model-remote-seats.md` beside its source), and what the
players are told in [What keeps you safe](help:join-game-safety).

**Hosting from a box that is always on.** Instead of this computer, a
NAS, a small Linux machine or a rented server can host the saga for
Remote seats day and night, with no window: see [How to host on your own
box](help:guide-self-hosting).

1. On ZeroTier's website, create a network and copy its 16-character Network ID. Keep it Private.
2. On your computer, join that network from the ZeroTier icon, then on the website tick **Auth** for your computer and note its Managed IP: that is the ADDRESS to host on.
3. Windows often marks the ZeroTier connection as a Public network; set it to Private in Windows' network settings, or the firewall keeps players out.
4. Each player installs ZeroTier, joins with the Network ID, and you tick **Auth** for their device.
5. On a ZeroTier network every device can reach every other one. To let players reach only the app on your computer, set the network's Flow Rules along these lines (with your address), then check a player can still connect:

```
accept ethertype arp;
accept ipdest ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and dport 8000;
accept ipsrc ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and sport 8000;
drop;
```

### Over SSH instead of a browser
<!-- help: guide-hosting-ssh -->

A player who prefers a terminal can connect with SSH instead. Tick **Also over SSH** when you start hosting (the SSH port is 2222 unless you change it; from a terminal, add `--ssh-port 2222`); the browser page keeps working alongside. The SSH server is the app's own: no Windows accounts are involved, and any user name is accepted, because who the player is comes from the same sign-in as in a browser. The first time, the app makes a host key and keeps it in your computer's keychain (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain or a Linux Secret Service), never in the save folder, which a cloud service may sync; players' SSH clients ask once whether to trust it. A key an older version left in the save folder (`ssh_host_key`) is moved into the keychain and the file deleted, so players' clients still know your computer. With no keychain, SSH hosting is refused and says why.

Players connect with `ssh -p 2222 player@ADDRESS` from Windows Terminal (Windows 10 and 11 include `ssh`), macOS Terminal or any Linux terminal, and get the same saga list and sign-in. Their terminal should be at least 120 columns by 45 rows and support true colour and Unicode: Windows Terminal, iTerm2, macOS Terminal and the usual Linux terminals do; the old Windows console does not. Over SSH, Export isn't offered, since the file could only land on your computer.

### What players see
<!-- help: guide-hosting-players -->

- The app fills the browser window. It is laid out for at least 120 columns by 45 rows: on a 1080p screen, add `?fontsize=12` to the address (`http://ADDRESS:8000/?fontsize=12`) or zoom the browser out.
- The browser keeps some keys for itself (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T, F5), so don't use them in the app.
- A player changes only the characters they play and what belongs to them; everything else they can look at and Export, which downloads in their browser. See [Players](help:tab-content-players).
- Everyone's changes reach the others within a few seconds. See [Signing in](help:sign-in).

**See also:** [Players](help:tab-content-players), [Signing in](help:sign-in), [Saga picker](help:saga-picker)

# How to host on your own box
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting -->

A box that is always on (a NAS, a small Linux computer, or a rented
server) can host one saga for Remote seats (players joining from their own
Armarius, over the internet) day and night, so your players can reach it when your own computer is
off. It runs the app's container image with no window: the listener for
Remote seats, and beside it a tunnel that brings players in. Nothing on
the box is opened to the internet; the tunnel connects out. You look
after it with the **admin** word, in a terminal on the box.

Only Remote seats are hosted this way: players need Armarius on their
own computers. Hosting for browsers and SSH stays on your desktop, until
the app can cap how many copies a host runs for them.

You will need: the box, with Docker and its compose plugin (most NAS
systems have a Docker app; on Linux, Docker Engine); a domain on
Cloudflare for the tunnel (or Tailscale, below); the saga's file from
your desktop; and a terminal on the box (SSH). The steps below use the
example address `saga.example.com`: use your own.

### The files and folders
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-files -->

Make a folder for it on the box (say `armarius`) and put in it, from the
app's source on GitHub (its `deploy` folder, at the version you run):
`compose.yaml`, `example.env` and `tunnel.env.example`. Then, in that
folder:

1. `cp example.env .env` and fill it in: `ARMARIUS_VERSION` (the version
   to run, such as `4.13.0`; always a number, never "latest") and
   `ARMARIUS_SAGA` (the saga's id: the name of its file in your save
   folder's `sagas`, without `.json`).
2. `cp tunnel.env.example tunnel.env`; the token goes in later.
   `chmod 600 .env tunnel.env`.
3. Make the three folders, owned by the app's own user (number 10001)
   and private: `sudo install -d -o 10001 -g 10001 -m 700 data keys
   backups`. `keys` holds the host key: on a Linux box, an encrypted
   disk is a good home for it (set `KEYS_DIR` in `.env` to it); a NAS's
   encrypted shared folder is not (see the UGREEN section).

The compose file runs the app locked down: as its own user, with its
system files read-only, no extra privileges, 512 MB of memory at most,
and no port published.

### A Linux box or a small rented server
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-linux -->

Any always-on Linux computer will do: an old laptop, a mini PC, a
Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 (the image has an ARM version), or the smallest
server a hosting company rents (one processor and 1 GB is plenty).

1. Install Docker Engine and the compose plugin, following Docker's own
   guide for your system (docs.docker.com, Install Docker Engine).
2. Make the files and folders above. On a home computer, put `keys` on
   an encrypted disk if the system offers one.
3. On a rented server, its company can read its disks: the key is safer
   at home. If you rent one anyway, choose one with disk encryption, and
   let no one else onto it. Close every port in its firewall but SSH: the
   tunnel needs none.

Then bring the saga in, set up the tunnel and start it, as below.

### A UGREEN NAS
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-nas -->

A UGREEN NAS runs it in the Docker app of UGOS Pro. Docker picks the
image's version for the NAS's processor (amd64 for Intel, arm64 for ARM),
and the app uses at most 512 MB of memory. The steps follow UGOS Pro
1.21; menu names may move in later versions.

1. In Control Panel, **Service**, turn on **SSH** (Terminal), port 22.
   Set it to turn itself off after a few hours: you only need it while
   setting up and for admin chores.
2. In the Files app, make an ordinary shared folder, `armarius`, and copy
   the three files into it (the Files app's upload works; so does
   `\\NAS-ADDRESS\armarius` in Explorer once SMB is on, under Service).
3. Sign in with SSH (`ssh YOU@NAS-ADDRESS`) and go to the folder:
   `cd /volume1/armarius` (`ls -d /volume*/armarius` finds it).
4. Make the three folders the app's own, and strip UGOS's access lists
   from them (UGOS shows those as a `+` after the modes; they open a
   folder to every NAS account, and the app refuses a key folder others
   can open): `sudo mkdir -p data keys backups`, `sudo chown
   10001:10001 data keys backups`, `sudo setfacl -b data keys backups`,
   then `sudo chmod 700 data keys backups` (last, since stripping the
   lists can reset the modes). `ls -ld data keys backups` should show
   `drwx------ ... 10001 10001` with no `+`.
5. Leave `KEYS_DIR=./keys` in `.env`, and `sudo chmod 600 .env
   tunnel.env`.

**Not UGOS's encrypted shared folders, for the keys.** They look like
the right home for a key, but the app's user can't use one: UGOS mounts
it as a filesystem of its own (`fuse.uggocryptfs`) that answers user
10001 with "Input/output error", reports every folder in it as open to
all (`rwxrwxrwx`) whatever its modes, and stripping its access lists sets
it to no access at all. Keep the keys in the ordinary `keys` folder
above, private to the app's user. What that gives up: someone who steals
the NAS's drive could read the key, and then you would rotate it and
send everyone a new Invite.

Then bring the saga in, set up the tunnel and start it, as below. Over
SSH, `sudo docker compose ...` works from the `armarius` folder; the
Docker app's **Project** view shows the same containers.

### Bringing the saga in
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-move -->

The saga moves to the box; your desktop's copy becomes history.

1. On your desktop, make sure you are a Player in the saga with the
   **Storyguide** role and a passcode of 14 characters or more: you will
   join your own saga from the desktop like everyone else. Stop hosting
   there.
2. Copy two files from your save folder (the [saga picker](help:saga-picker)
   shows where it is) to the box, into the `armarius` folder: the saga's
   file, in `sagas`, and its passcode records, `remote/NAME-passcodes.json`
   (NAME being the saga's name). The records let players keep their
   passcodes; without them, every passcode must be set again on the box.
   On a NAS, files copied in belong to your NAS account and its access
   lists, which the app's user can't read ("Permission denied"): give
   them to it first, `sudo chown 10001:10001 FILES`, `sudo setfacl -b
   FILES`, `sudo chmod 600 FILES`.
3. On the box, bring them in (the file names are yours to fill in):
   `docker compose run --rm -v "$PWD/SAGA.json:/in/saga.json:ro" -v
   "$PWD/NAME-passcodes.json:/in/passcodes.json:ro" armarius admin saga
   import /in/saga.json --passcodes /in/passcodes.json`. The saga is
   checked as the app checks any saga it opens. A passcode record comes
   over only for the very passcode it was made for, so one for a
   passcode changed since is dropped. Then delete the copied files
   (`sudo rm`, since they now belong to the app's user).
4. The box makes a host key of its own, so every Invite and seat from
   your desktop has ended: issue everyone a new Invite, your own
   desktop's storyguide seat included. `admin players list` names anyone
   whose passcode must still be set again (`admin players set-passcode
   NAME`, below).
5. Move the desktop's copy of the saga file out of the `sagas` folder,
   for example into a folder of old copies. A change made there never
   reaches the box, and a copy only renamed inside `sagas` can save
   itself back under the saga's own name.

### The tunnel
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-tunnel -->

**Cloudflare Tunnel** (the compose file's own). In Cloudflare's
dashboard, under Networking, Tunnels (older dashboards: Zero Trust,
Networks, Tunnels), create a tunnel of the cloudflared kind and name it
(`armarius`, or `nas-armarius` if your desktop already has a tunnel of
its own: give the box its own tunnel, never a second connector on the
desktop's, or Cloudflare would share players between the two hosts). Cloudflare shows a command to
install it, holding a long token: copy only the token into `tunnel.env`
after `TUNNEL_TOKEN=`. Give the tunnel a public hostname,
`saga.example.com`, whose service is `http://localhost:8790` (the
tunnel shares the app's network, so "localhost" is the app). Whoever has
the token can serve your saga's address: keep `tunnel.env` like the key.
Where to go: developers.cloudflare.com, Cloudflare Tunnel.

**Tailscale Funnel** instead (no domain needed). In the compose file,
put a `tailscale/tailscale` service in place of `cloudflared`, with the
same `network_mode: "service:armarius"`, an auth key in its own env file,
and a Funnel serve setting that sends `https://` traffic to
`http://127.0.0.1:8790`. Tell the listener which tunnel it is behind:
give the armarius service `command: ["seats", "--tunnel", "other"]` and
run `admin tunnel --tunnel other`. Where to go: tailscale.com/docs,
Tailscale in Docker and Tailscale Funnel.

### Starting it
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-start -->

1. `docker compose up -d` (or start the Project in the NAS's Docker
   app). `docker compose ps` shows the app **healthy** within a minute.
2. Tell it the tunnel's address, which Invites carry:
   `docker exec armarius python main.py admin tunnel --url
   https://saga.example.com`.
3. Set passcodes, `docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin
   players set-passcode NAME` (the `-it` lets it ask without showing
   what you type), then issue Invites: `... admin invites issue NAME`.
   Approve each device on the call as on the desktop, with `admin
   invites pending` and `admin approve ID`.

`docker compose logs armarius` shows what the app said if it won't
start: it names what is wrong (a key folder others can read, a saga it
can't find).

### When a step goes wrong
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-trouble -->

- **"Permission denied" reading a file, or a key folder refused as
  open to others:** the NAS's own access lists (a `+` after the modes in
  `ls -l`). Give it to the app's user and strip them: `sudo chown
  10001:10001 PATH`, `sudo setfacl -b PATH`, then `sudo chmod 700 PATH`
  (a folder) or `600` (a file). Check with `ls -ld PATH`.
- **"The key folder ... isn't there":** the app never makes the key
  folder itself, so that a volume not yet unlocked (after a restart, say)
  can't be given a fresh key in its place. Unlock the volume, or, the
  first time, make the folder as the message says.
- **"Input/output error" or "Not a directory" in the keys folder:** it
  is an encrypted shared folder of the NAS's, which the app's user
  can't use. Use an ordinary folder (see the UGREEN section).
- **A folder shows `d---------` after `setfacl -b`:** set its modes
  again with `sudo chmod 700 PATH`.
- **Testing as the app's user:** `sudo -u` can't, since the NAS has no
  account 10001. Ask the image instead: `sudo docker run --rm --user
  10001:10001 -v PATH:/keys --entrypoint sh armarius:VERSION -c 'ls -lnd
  /keys; touch /keys/t && ls -ln /keys/t; rm -f /keys/t'`.
- **`\\NAS-ADDRESS\armarius` won't open in Explorer:** the shared
  folder doesn't exist yet, or Windows file sharing (SMB) is off on the
  NAS. Upload through the NAS's Files app instead.
- **`.env` missing after copying:** Windows hides names starting with a
  dot; turn on View, Show, Hidden items before copying.
- **`rm` asks about a "write-protected" file:** it belongs to the app's
  user now; answer `y`, or use `sudo rm`.
- **Where's the tunnel's token?** On the tunnel's setup page choose
  **Docker** as the system: the command shown ends with the token (it
  starts `eyJ`). Copy only the token, and don't run the command: the
  compose file runs cloudflared. The page's Continue button stays grey
  until the box connects, so add the public hostname after starting it.
  If the tunnel shows Healthy but "No routes", open it and add a route
  (Routes, Add route, Published application): the hostname, and as
  Service URL `http://localhost:8790` (newer dashboards have no
  separate type box: the `http://` says it). Until the route exists
  the address has no DNS record, and a player's Join says to check the
  storyguide is hosting. Add the route before anyone tries to join: a
  home router remembers "no such name" for up to half an hour, even
  after the route exists. To tell that apart from a broken tunnel, ask
  Cloudflare's own DNS (`nslookup NAME 1.1.1.1`): if it answers there,
  only the router's memory is in the way, so wait or restart the router.
- **The token didn't save in `tunnel.env`:** in Windows Terminal, paste
  into `nano` with a right-click or Ctrl+Shift+V. Check without
  showing it: `sudo awk -F= '{print NR": "$1"  length="length($0)}'
  tunnel.env` should show one `TUNNEL_TOKEN` line of about 200
  characters (13 means it is empty).

### Looking after it: the admin word
<!-- help: guide-hosting-admin -->

A host with no screen does what the Host for Players window does with the
**admin** word, typed in a terminal on that computer (in a container:
`docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin ...`; from a source
checkout: `uv run python main.py admin ...`). It reads and writes the
saga's files there and nothing goes over the network. `--saga` names the
saga by its id or name; with only one saga, or `ARMARIUS_SAGA` set, it
can be left out. `admin --help` lists everything; `--help` after any word
says more.

- `admin sagas` lists the sagas. `admin saga import FILE` brings a saga
  file in (checked as the app checks any saga it opens), with
  `--passcodes FILE` for the old host's passcode records; `admin saga
  delete` deletes one after you type its name.
- `admin players list` says who may have a Remote seat; `admin players
  set-passcode NAME` asks for a new passcode twice, without showing it
  (it is never typed on the command line, where others could see it);
  `admin players set-role NAME storyguide` (or `player`).
- `admin tunnel --url https://saga.example.com` sets the address
  Invites carry; `admin tunnel` shows it, and where to point the tunnel.
- `admin invites issue NAME` prints the Invite to send and the six words
  to read on the call; `admin words` prints the words again. `admin
  invites pending` lists what waits: who asks, from which device, its
  three words to check on the call, and under each the exact command
  that approves it or turns it down, ready to copy (with a short form
  of the request's id).
- `admin devices list` lists the seats, each live one with the command
  that revokes it; `admin devices revoke ID` does so;
  `admin log` (with `--player NAME` or `--limit N`) for the Log.
- `admin rotate-key` gives the host a new key; restart the listener
  afterwards (`docker compose restart armarius`) so it uses it.
- `admin backup` copies the saga and its Remote seat files (Invites,
  seats, the Log, the tunnel address) into one zip in the backups folder
  (`--to FOLDER`, or `ARMARIUS_BACKUPS`, which the container sets to
  `/backups`), named for the saga and the time. It is safe while players
  play: each file is copied whole, between saves. It keeps the newest 30
  (`--keep N` to change that) and never holds the host key. To restore,
  unzip it into the data folder with the listener stopped.

Anything that changes a seat asks first; add `--yes` to skip the
question in a script. The checks are the window's own: a passcode under
14 characters, an Invite for a player with no passcode, or an approval
of a request that has lapsed is refused the same way.

### The host key on the box
<!-- help: guide-hosting-headless-key -->

On a desktop the host key lives in the computer's keychain. A box with
no screen has none, so the app keeps it in a file instead, but only
when told to: `ARMARIUS_KEYSTORE=file`, with `ARMARIUS_KEYS` naming the
folder (`/keys` if left out). The container image sets both; a desktop
never needs them.

The folder must be one of its own, never inside the data folder, so the
keys never travel with the saga or its backups. The app makes it private
(only the app's own user may open it) and refuses to start if anyone else
can read the folder or a key in it, if it belongs to another user, or if
the app runs as the administrator (root). Whoever can read that folder
can pretend to be your host: on a NAS that is its administrators, and
anyone who may run Docker there. Keep it on an encrypted folder.

When it refuses, it says the fix: `sudo chown 10001:10001 FOLDER` for a
folder made by another user (as a NAS's web pages make them), `chmod 700`
for the folder or `chmod 600` for a key others can read, and for root,
starting it as the image's own user (no `user: root`).

### Backups off the box
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-backups -->

**On the box, every night.** The compose file's `backup` service runs
`admin backup` when the project starts and then once a day, keeping the
newest 30 (`BACKUP_EVERY`, in seconds, and `BACKUPS_KEPT` in `.env`
change that). It needs no scheduler on the box, never sees the keys, and
has no network. `docker compose logs backup` shows each one; the zips
are in `backups`. A NAS's own snapshots of the `armarius` folder are a
good extra, but they stay on the same disks, and they hold the `keys`
folder too: never copy them off the box.

**Off the box.** `admin backup` keeps copies on the box; a second copy
elsewhere survives the box. **restic** does it well: it encrypts everything before it leaves
the box, so the copy can sit on your desktop or in a cloud folder.

1. Install restic on the box (its website, restic.net, has the steps; on
   a NAS, its Docker image `restic/restic` works the same way). Keep its
   password in a file only you can read (`chmod 600`), and a copy of it
   somewhere safe: without it the backups can't be opened.
2. Make a repository. On your desktop, over SSH:
   `restic -r sftp:you@desktop:/backups/armarius init`. In a cloud
   drive such as Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive, through rclone
   (rclone.org, set up a remote called `cloud` first):
   `restic -r rclone:cloud:armarius init`.
3. Each night, after the backup service has run (from `cron` on a
   Linux box): `restic -r REPOSITORY backup backups`, then `restic -r
   REPOSITORY forget --keep-daily 14 --keep-weekly 8 --prune`.

Back the `keys` folder up apart, to a different restic repository with a
different password, or not at all: a lost key costs only new Invites for
everyone, while a leaked one lets someone pretend to be your host.

### If the key or the token leaks
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-leak -->

If the `keys` folder, `tunnel.env`, or a backup of either may have been
seen by someone else:

1. `docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin rotate-key`, then
   `docker compose restart armarius`. Every Invite and seat ends at once.
2. In Cloudflare's dashboard, make the tunnel a new token (or delete the
   tunnel and make another), put it in `tunnel.env`, and
   `docker compose up -d`.
3. Issue everyone a new Invite, your own desktop's seat included, and
   read each of them the new six words on a call.
4. Read the Log (`admin log`) for sign-ins from before you rotated that
   you don't recognise.

### A newer version
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-update -->

The box never updates itself. To move to a newer version, read its notes
on the Releases page, take a backup (`admin backup`), set
`ARMARIUS_VERSION` in `.env` to it, then `docker compose pull` and
`docker compose up -d`. Within the same first number (4.13 to 4.14,
say) seats keep working; a new first number (5.0) means every player
moves to it too, since a seat refuses a host of another major version.

**See also:** [How to host a saga](help:guide-hosting)

**And:** [Remote seats: hosting safely](help:guide-hosting-safety), which the box's own hosting follows too.

# How to start a saga from a sourcebook
<!-- help: guide-book-packs -->

Your troupe wants to play in the Rhine, and you'd rather not type in ten covenants and seventy magi before the first session.

The app ships a content pack for each Tribunal sourcebook it covers, written in its own words from the book's open content. A pack brings the Tribunal with its customs, its covenants and named magi, the region's places, towns, lords, churches and factions, its Peripheral Code and open quarrels, and its history as Tribunal Diary entries. Use one to begin a saga in a Tribunal already peopled, or to add that Tribunal to a saga you're running.

A region book, whose lands lie outside every Tribunal, has a pack with no Tribunal. Its covenants belong to none, its history is in one covenant's Diary, and what is coming (a yearly council, say) is in the saga's calendar.

### Starting a saga from a pack
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-start -->

1. **Start a new saga.** On the saga list, choose **New Saga**, and fill in the name, storyguide and date as usual. A pack is written as of its book's year (the Rhine's is 1220). A saga at another date keeps its own date, and the pack's records keep their years.
2. **Pick the book.** In **Start from a pack**, choose it. The list shows the shipped book packs first, then the saga starters, such as Dammërstein, then any pack in your exports folder.
3. **Read its introduction.** A pack with an introduction shows it under the choice while it's chosen: what the pack sets up and where the saga begins. The form scrolls to read it all.
4. **Choose the stories that begin.** Under the introduction is a **Begin** tick for each of the pack's stories, its threats. Untick the ones to keep for later. They come in dormant, to begin from their More when you want them.
5. **Create the saga.** It begins with everything in the pack, and the pack's book is put in play (see [The saga](help:saga)), so the book's rules and neighbour kinds show.

A saga starter is a ready-made covenant or saga shipped with the app. One built on a book pack says so under the choice (Dammërstein is built on the Rhine's). The saga then starts from that book pack first, with its stories and supplements asked about too, and the starter comes in over it, its records matched to the book's by name.

### Adding a book to a saga you already have
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-add -->

1. **Import the pack.** In the saga, choose Import Data, **A Content Pack**, and pick the pack's file from the app's `data/packs` folder, next to the program.
2. **Choose what to bring.** Untick the parts you don't want, such as the mundane world or the history. The Tribunal, if the pack has one, and its covenants always come (see [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack)).
3. **Preview, then import.** A covenant, character, place or neighbour the saga already has one of the same name of is kept as it is. So importing over a saga that already holds part of the Tribunal doubles nothing.

A pack is a copy. A saga keeps what it imported and never reads the pack's file again, so a corrected pack in a new version of the app changes new sagas and new imports, not the sagas you have.

Importing the corrected pack over a saga adds what the saga doesn't have and leaves what it has, your edits included. So a correction to a record it already holds doesn't come through, and a Tribunal it already has keeps its own Code, cases and Diary. A record the new pack has renamed comes in beside the old one. The preview lists what's added, so check it first.

### Turning on a supplement
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-overlay -->

A supplement such as *Through the Aegis* sits on top of the Tribunal packs, so a saga without it matches the Tribunal book.

1. **Bring it in.** Import its file from `data/packs` the same way, or tick **With Through the Aegis** on New Saga.
2. **Check the overwrites.** It adds to each Tribunal the saga has and overwrites records where the two books differ. A form lists each overwrite, unticked if you've changed that record yourself.

It stays on, so a Tribunal pack imported later takes its part too. See [Through the Aegis on top of the Tribunal packs](help:pack-overlay-through-the-aegis).

### Bringing in the Magi of Hermes
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-magi -->

*Magi of Hermes* is a pack of fifteen magi with no Tribunal. Each is printed at Gauntlet and every fifteen years after, and all of those ages come with the pack.

1. **Import it.** Import it the same way as any pack.
2. **Set the base year.** The form asks for a **Base year**, which starts on the saga's year.
3. **Choose each magus's age.** Pick an age for each magus, or **Leave out**. A magus's year of birth is the base year less the age you choose, so pick the age they have at that year.

Conscientia joins Durenmar, whether the Rhine pack comes in first or later. If the Theban pack's sketch of Marcus Tauros is in the saga, the printed Marcus replaces it. Without this pack the sketch stays.

### Africa and the Nile
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-africa -->

*Lands of the Nile* and *Between Sand and Sea* are region packs with no Tribunal, as *The Cradle and the Crescent* is. They can be imported together. They share the Great Hunger, the Hope and the pact of 1225, and those come in once. Their stories come in dormant, except the Fifth Crusade.

### Iceland
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-iceland -->

*Land of Fire and Ice* is a region pack with no Tribunal, as *The Cradle and the Crescent* is, and it includes *The Icelandic Wars*. It brings the island, its people and law, the galdramen and trollsynir ([how to play one](help:guide-galdor)), and two long stories. The stories come in dormant, so nothing starts until you begin one.

### Antagonists
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-antagonists -->

*Antagonists* is a pack of ten recurring opponents, with no Tribunal, for any saga. Its stories come in dormant, and a person printed in two versions is a choice on the import form. With the book in play, its people with printed stats are also templates on **New Character**, named **Antagonist -**. Pick one to start from a copy of the printed record.

### Legends of Hermes
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-legends -->

*Legends of Hermes* is a pack with no Tribunal. It holds the sites, guardians and treasures of Conciatta, Fortunata, Hérisson, Hermanus and Thomae, with their stories dormant. Import it into any saga and begin a story from its More when you want it.

### What a pack puts where
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-where -->

- **The Tribunal.** Its sheet has the book's customs (the Rhine's gilds) and its officers. Its Peripheral Code is under Rulings, the open quarrels under Cases, and its Hermetic history in the Diary. The mundane history is in its summary, by era.
- **Covenants.** Sketch records, not played, each with a summary, what matters (vis, income, tensions) and the magi the book names. The count of magi the book leaves unnamed is on the covenant.
- **Magi.** Every magus the book names is a full character. Those it prints a stat block for have the book's stats. The rest have stats inferred by the creation rules from what the book says of them, such as their House, age and favoured Arts. That isn't an official write-up, as their Biography says.
- **Sketches instead.** Untick **Generated stats for magi** when importing to bring those magi in as sketches. Where the book gives no age, one is assumed from the magus's rank and noted there too.
- **Places.** The regions and forests are places, with the regiones inside them and their levels and auras. Covenants and towns sit in them.
- **The mundane world.** Lords, towns, churches, schools, factions and threats, with historical arms where they're known.
- **The Bestiary.** The creatures the book prints a stat block for, such as the Rhine's boglins and Novgorod's dragons. Each is found at the place it haunts and linked from the threat or faction it belongs to. Untick **The Bestiary** when importing to leave them out.
- **Assumptions.** Where a book leaves a value out (an aura without a number, a founding year, a season), the pack fills it in, and importing it says so. Each pack's assumptions page, linked in the list above, lists those values.
- **Secrets.** What the book tells the storyguide and not the players is on the [Secrets](help:tab-secrets) of each covenant, magus, Tribunal, neighbour or creature. The few that belong to part of a record, such as a regio level, a case or a Diary entry, are at the end of its text under a **Secrets** heading.

The packs follow the Definitive Edition where a book's numbers differ from it. Each pack's assumptions page lists the corrections it makes.

**See also:** [Content packs](help:export-pack), [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack), [Tribunal](help:tribunal), [How to import and export](help:guide-import-export)

# How to fight a battle
<!-- help: guide-battle -->

The baron's knights meet the count's levies at the ford, and one of your companions is in the thick of it.

Lords of Men plays a battle as a few events, each a fight with a player character at its heart, rather than every blow. The app keeps the battle in the saga's Conflicts, between two armies, and shows it in the **Fief** of each lord it names. It works out each event's total.

### Before the battle
<!-- help: guide-battle-setup -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Open the battles.** Open the lord's sheet, a neighbour of the lord kind, and go to its **Fief** tab. Or open **Conflicts**.
2. **Add a battle.** In [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), press `a`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Open the battles.** Open the lord's page, a neighbour of the lord kind, and go to its **Fief** chapter. Or open **Conflicts**.
2. **Add a battle.** Under [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), click **Add**.
<!-- /gui -->
3. **Name it and choose the armies.** Each army is a lord, town or other power of the Region, a covenant, or just a name. Added from a lord's Fief, the first army is that lord's. To pit two lords against each other, choose both.
4. **Fill in the combat groups.** Leave a lord's army's **combat groups** blank and the lord's title fills them in, in groups of five. Levies come only when the lord fights on home ground, which the form asks. Type a covenant's or a named army's groups.
5. **Add allies and mercenaries.** Add the allies the lord raises and the mercenaries they hire (see [Muster and Aftermath](help:battle-muster)).
6. **Type the Deployment Totals.** Each commander rolls Intelligence + Area Lore + a stress die. Type both. The difference is the first army's **Ground** in every event, and the second's as much below 0.
7. **Set the baggage and save.** Leave **baggage groups** blank for as many as each army needs (one for every ten combat groups), or type fewer. Save.

### Each event
<!-- help: guide-battle-events -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Open the event.** With the battle chosen, press `m` and choose **Battle Event**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Open the event.** Choose the battle and click **Battle Event**.
<!-- /gui -->
2. **Choose who leads it.** Choose the character at the heart of the event, the army they fight for, and its Maneuver, Enemy and Size.
3. **Check the total.** The line above Record shows the Event Total and whether it reaches this stage's Ease Factor (see [Battle Event](help:battle-event)). A total too low can't win, so pick a smaller task, or give up the initiative for +1.
4. **Play it out.** Play the event as an ordinary fight with the character and the enemies it names. Tick **Play it out as a fight** to have the app make it a fight in Conflicts.
5. **Record the result.** Back in the form, say who won and type the losing commander's Morale roll, and their rally at the next event if it failed. Click **Record**. The losing side loses combat groups, and the battle's line shows the new numbers for the next event.

Most battles take three events. The battle is over when one side has won enough of them, or its commander fails a Morale roll and then the rally. Each event is already written in the Diary of each army's lord or covenant. Add a line there saying how the battle ended.

# How to run a siege
<!-- help: guide-sieges -->

The count's army has ringed the castle, the garrison won't come out, and the troupe wants to know how long the walls will hold.

1. **Add the siege.** Add a siege to the Sieges list in Conflicts, or in the besieging lord's Fief.
2. **Describe the castle.** Give its Size, Garrison, Defenses and Supplies. The form's hints give each step's interval and bonus.
3. **Describe the besiegers.** Give their miners, engines and soldiers, where each side stands at the four walls, and the experts' totals.
4. **Link two lords.** For one lord besieging another, link **The castle in the Region** to the lord holding it and **The besieging lord** to the attacker. Leave the Garrison and the soldiers blank, and their armies fill them in. The siege then shows in both lords' Fiefs.
5. **Play an event.** Choose **Next Siege Event**, then the besiegers' move or the garrison's sally. For an escalade or a sally, play the battlefield event out and say who won.
6. **Watch the days pass.** The siege moves on by the castle's interval each time, and any supplies roll that comes due is made for you, each an interval after the last. Each line of the chronicle gives the day of the siege it came on. The siege's page shows every event, and the page scrolls.
7. **End it.** When the castle surrenders or is breached, the siege says so. If the besiegers give up, edit it and set Ended to Raised.

**See also:** [A siege](help:sieges), [How to fight a battle](help:guide-battle-events)

# How to play a hedge wizard
<!-- help: guide-hedge-magic -->

A player wants a folk witch for the troupe, someone with a craft of their own rather than a Hermetic Gift and a laboratory.

A hedge wizard belongs to a non-Hermetic tradition, such as the folk witches, the gruagachan or the vitkir, with Arts and Abilities of their own. Each tradition is offered once its book is in play and the saga turns it on. The app has the traditions of these books:

- the six traditions of Hedge Magic Revised Edition
- the Realms of Power books, the Tribunal and regional books, Rival Magic and Ancient Magic
- Houses of Hermes: Societates, as Ex Miscellanea traditions

### Offering the tradition
<!-- help: guide-hedge-magic-offer -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Put the book in play.** On [Saga Content](help:section-books-in-play), press `e` in Books in Play and tick Hedge Magic Revised Edition.
2. **Trim the list.** Its traditions are offered at once. To keep the pickers short, open [Traditions Offered](help:section-traditions), below the books, press `e` and untick the traditions your saga doesn't use.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Put the book in play.** On [Saga Content](help:section-books-in-play), click the **Not in play** chip on Hedge Magic Revised Edition's card, under Non-Hermetic Magic. Or click **Edit** in Books in Play and tick it.
2. **Trim the list.** Its traditions are offered at once. To keep the pickers short, flip the switches of the ones your saga doesn't use in the [Traditions](help:section-traditions) chapter. Or flip the book's heading switch to offer none, then turn on the few you want.
<!-- /gui -->

### Joining it
<!-- help: guide-hedge-magic-join -->

A new hedge wizard is made on the [Creation Worksheet](help:creation-worksheet). On Concept, **Choose tradition** makes them a member and adds the tradition's Virtues, and a Gifted member also takes The Gift there. The **Tradition Training** step then spends the apprenticeship the book gives. With **Open the Arts**, it gives a Gifted member the tradition's Arts at 0.

To add a tradition to a character already made, or one In Play:

<!-- tui -->
1. **Make the character.** Make them as usual (`n`, **New Character**). A hedge wizard is usually a companion, never a magus.
2. **Choose the tradition.** On their sheet's top panel, press `m` (More) and choose **Traditions**. Tick theirs. A tab named after the tradition's magic appears, such as Folk Witch Magic.
3. **Add its Virtues.** In [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf), add the tradition's Social Status (Folk Witch, Gruagach, Vitki...) and the powers the character has.
4. **Add the Arts.** In the tradition's tab, press `a` to add each Art or Ability they have and type its XP.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Make the character.** Make them as usual. A hedge wizard is usually a companion, never a magus.
2. **Choose the tradition.** In their Abilities chapter, click **Traditions** and tick theirs. A chapter named after the tradition's magic appears, such as Folk Witch Magic.
3. **Add its Virtues.** In [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf), add the tradition's Social Status (Folk Witch, Gruagach, Vitki...) and the powers the character has.
4. **Add the Arts.** In the tradition's chapter, click **Add** for each Art or Ability they have and type its XP.
<!-- /gui -->

### What the app works out
<!-- help: guide-hedge-magic-app -->

Each Art's score comes from its XP on the table its book gives it. Most hedge Arts and the vitkir's runes advance on the Art table, as Hermetic Arts do. A Difficult Art, such as Elementalist Fire, the gruagach's Give and Take or the learned magicians' Techniques, advances on the Ability table.

The [chapter](help:tradition-chapter) works out the rest, such as each power's Casting Score and Lab Total, the tradition's Magical Defenses, its gatherings' bonuses (a folk witch's sabbat) and the guidelines for its effects. More's [Cast a Power](help:hedge-cast) rolls a casting there and then.

At creation, Tradition Training spends the apprenticeship the book gives. The ten years of it that a folk witch spends from about age ten are left out of Later Life, as a magus's are (Hedge Magic RE p. 34). Sorginak and bouda train as folk witches do (Faith and Flame pp. 93-94; Between Sand and Sea p. 119). Settuten train for ten years from 7 to 12, which the app takes as 10 (Between Sand and Sea pp. 95-96). The Bedouin hakima's book gives no years, so none are left out.

# How to play a galdraman
<!-- help: guide-galdor -->

Your saga sails to Iceland, and a player wants a galdraman who sings the land spirits into service.

Iceland's two magus-equivalents are hedge wizards here, and both come with *Land of Fire and Ice* in play. They're Gifted companions, the equal of a magus at the table (Hedge Magic RE p. 11). They have no Magic Resistance and no Twilight, as Hedge Magic gives every hedge tradition (p. 10). That's the app's conversion. *Land of Fire and Ice*, written for the 4th edition, lets galdramen enter Twilight (p. 50) and gives trollsynir Magic Resistance (p. 53).

### A traditional galdraman
<!-- help: guide-galdor-traditional -->

1. **Make a Gifted companion.** On the worksheet, give the character The Gift.
2. **Join the tradition.** Choose the tradition Traditional Galdramen, whose chapter is Galdor. Then use the Concept step's **Add the tradition's Virtues**. Its Virtues come free: Free Expression, Second Sight, Premonitions, Galdor Conjuring and Galdor Enchantment.
3. **Take the ground's Flaw.** Take Deleterious Circumstances (touching the ground) as well. The totals are halved on the ground either way.
4. **Spend their xp.** Spend it on Galdor Wisdom, Music, Bargain and two Song Talents. A Song can't pass Galdor Wisdom, and it goes up by practice and experience, never by teaching.

In play, a galdraman works in three ways:

1. **Sing** with [Conjure a Spirit](help:galdor-conjure) for anything up to Concentration.
2. **Go to a lord** with [Bargain with a Lord](help:galdor-bargain) for anything longer. The deal stays in Bound Beings, with the vis it tithes a year.
3. **Make things** with [Bind an Item](help:galdor-bind), after a deal.

For longevity, strike a deal with biarg-galdor, conjure the biarg spirit as a ritual, and enter the conjuring total as a Longevity Ritual on the character's sheet. The mead takes a pawn a decade of their age each year. The app doesn't track Galdor Wisdom falling to 0 in another country.

Curse and Hamfarir roll through [Cast a Power](help:hedge-cast). Curse has its Ease Factors as guidelines. A nidstong is a Curse that rolls daily while it stands.

### A trollson
<!-- help: guide-galdor-trollson -->

A trollson takes the tradition Trollsynir, whose chapter is Trollskap. They cast as a gruagach does, with Jotnar for Pictish (see [hedge wizards](help:guide-hedge-magic)). They must have Giant Blood (*Land of Fire and Ice* p. 52) and, as the app's conversion to a gruagach, No Tattoo Magic.

### Fylgjur
<!-- help: guide-galdor-fylgja -->

Premonitions brings a fylgja, of Might 10 and Size -3 at most. A trollson's is the Fetch.

- **Mighty Fylgja** gives 10 Might and the next of Phantom, Spirit Voice and Corporeality. Take it as often as you like. The book makes it one Virtue with points put into it (p. 54), and the app takes it again for each.
- **Large Fylgja** sets its Size. The first allows Size 0, and each more +1.

The chapter's Bound Beings shows the fylgjur a character has. Hamfarir is the Virtue that lets them use them.

### Other Icelanders
<!-- help: guide-galdor-others -->

A schooled galdraman is a Learned Magician, and a seithkona a Folk Witch with Hamfarir. A bondi, a landowner or a godi takes that Social Status, which sits beside Galdraman for a character who is both.

This guide walks one meeting from the roll call to the Closing: a Rhine Tribunal meeting in Summer 1228 with one charge on its docket, Fengheld v. The rules are Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 2: The Order of Hermes, Tribunals, with Houses of Hermes: True Lineages (House Guernicus, Tribunal Procedures) for hearing a case and Houses of Hermes: Societates (House Tytalus, The Fine Art of Debate, pp. 90-92) for the debate. [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal) has every step in full.

On the desktop a card announces the meeting as it opens; close it, and the next step opens.

### Opening and the days
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-opening -->

Say where the meeting is held and how many days it lasts (3). Each case and motion is given a day, spread over every day but the last, which is kept for the rulings and the Closing; change a case's day to move its hearing and vote.

### Hearing a case
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-hearing -->

Fengheld v. Keep comes on its day. Choose the **Prosecutor** and **Defender** from the drop-downs: the magi present, each with their covenant. Leandro is absent, so the defence starts as **A Guernicus advocate**, who defends an accused who didn't come; a charge no one else will prosecute can be brought by **The Presiding Quaesitor**. The book has a Quaesitor step in only for a high crime, and drops a low crime no one prosecutes (True Lineages p. 56); the app offers the Quaesitor for any charge. A speaker with no debate scores written down (the advocate, or a sketch magus) gets **debates as**: Middling, Able or Formidable.

Leave **Levels lost** blank and the debate is rolled, round by round: the speakers' Communication and Intelligence with their best debating Abilities (by lot among equals each round), the case's Evidence backing the prosecution and the **Defence testimony** the defence. Or play the debate out at the table and enter both sides' levels lost. Either way, the side that loses fewer wins, and the difference is its Argument Strength.

If both sides accept the Presiding Quaesitor's view at the private hearing, tick **Settled in private** instead: the case has no debate and no vote, and goes straight on to its ruling.

For the motion, the proposer speaks for it and **The Tribunal** against: a motion is debated against the whole hall.

### Calling the vote
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-vote -->

The vote follows its hearing. Each covenant is listed with its leaning and why: "the case is against it", "Warm (+1) toward Fengheld, which brings it". Change a leaning if the story says otherwise; the tally under the form follows as you do. The debate's victor wins a share of the undecided votes when the covenants are called. If the votes tie, choose how the Praeco casts.

<!-- gui -->
**Call the Vote** in The Hall shows the covenants called one by one, their seats filling.
<!-- /gui -->

### Rulings and their terms
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-rulings -->

On the last day, each case is ruled on. A ruling can be **Written out** (it starts as "Carried, 3 to 1") or made from a **ruling template**. Choose **Holds for a term** for Fengheld v. Keep, set any fine (3 pawns of Aquam, paid by Keep) and whether it goes into the Peripheral Code, and save: the template's blanks come next. Fill them in, and the sentence under the form shows the ruling as you go:

> Keep holds the Rhine ford's vis for 7 years, until it is debated again.

The {years} blank sets a term. With a written-out ruling, its **term** (in years) sets one the same way. The templates are the saga's own: add or change them in the saga's settings, **Ruling templates**, where any word in braces is a blank to fill.

### Closing, and what comes back
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-closing -->

The Treasury step shows the balance and the loans due. Closing then decides each case with a ruling (the fine owed to the treasury, though the book gives a third of it to the prosecutor, True Lineages p. 57; the ruling into the Peripheral Code, a party's attitude moved, the term set), writes the Acta into the Tribunal's Diary and sets the next meeting. Each step held has already written its minutes there.

The term comes back on its own. [Ahead](help:section-ahead) lists the season before it ends (Spring 1235) and the season it ends (Summer 1235). Advancing into Spring 1235 says the term ends next season; advancing into Summer 1235 says it has ended and puts "Fengheld v. Keep (debated again)" on the Docket, with the old ruling in its charge, for the next meeting.

# How to format your notes
<!-- help: guide-formatting -->

You'd like a covenant's Secrets to read as a tidy list, with each name in bold. The longer text you write yourself, such as a Biography, a covenant's Description, a record's Secrets or a book's Description, can carry a little formatting. A field that takes it says so in the hint under its box. You type the marks into the box as plain text, and they turn into formatting wherever the app shows what you wrote.

### The marks
<!-- help: guide-formatting-marks -->

| Type this | To show |
| --- | --- |
| `**words**` | **bold** |
| `*words*` | *italic* |
| `***words***` | bold and italic together |
| `~~words~~` | ~~struck through~~ |
| `- ` at the start of a line (or `* `, `+ `) | a bulleted item |
| `1. ` at the start of a line (or `1) `) | a numbered item, with your own number |
| two spaces before the `- ` or `1. ` | an item nested under the one above |
| a blank line | a new paragraph |

A mark needs words right beside it, so `5 * 3` stays as written, and so does a mark left unclosed. To show a mark itself, put a backslash before it: `\*` shows `*`. Emphasis stops at a blank line and at the next list item, so a stray mark can't run on through the rest of the text.

### An example
<!-- help: guide-formatting-example -->

Typed into a covenant's Secrets:

```
Hidden Resources (not yet found):

- **Matthaios's sealed study**, in the Echoing Halls.
- **Nikephoros's vis cache**, under the Watchtower: ~~60~~ 40 pawns.
```

The sheet shows the first line as written, then two bulleted items, each opening with its name in bold and the 60 struck through. A long item wraps with its lines starting under its words, not under the bullet.

### What it doesn't do
<!-- help: guide-formatting-limits -->

There are no headings, tables or links. The sheets give each piece of text its own heading already. Fields that are read one entry a line, such as a case's Evidence or a random table's Rows, take no formatting, since the app reads their lines itself. The box you type in shows the marks, not the formatting. Save to see it.

# How to install a magic item in a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item -->

An enchanted device kept in a laboratory can improve the laboratory itself (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Magic Items for Laboratories, p. 298; Covenants p. 121). This guide covers the two kinds of benefit, installing an item in play and while a laboratory is being set up, and when an item is a Feature instead.

### Two kinds of benefit
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-kinds -->

The item is installed as a **Magic Item** Virtue, which is Free (Minor, and so takes a point of space, when the item is very large), or as an **Enchantment** of the laboratory itself, also Free. One Virtue covers all of an item's effects. Each effect then gives one of two things:

- **A Virtue or Flaw it duplicates.** An effect that does what a laboratory Virtue or Flaw does (a cauldron that always stays at the right heat, say) simply grants it. The laboratory gains it with no space cost, and so no Build Points at creation, and its modifications apply as usual.
- **Points bought by its level.** Otherwise the effect's final level buys one Specialization point for every 10 levels, or one Characteristic point for every 20: a level 20 lectern's effect could give Texts +2, or Safety +1. These points need no Virtue at all beyond the Free Magic Item.

An effect must be new to the laboratory: one that grants a Virtue the laboratory has already is flagged. A benefit counts only while its device is kept in the laboratory: move the device elsewhere and its points and the Virtue it grants stop counting, and its row says so, until the device comes back.

### Install one in play
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-play -->

<!-- tui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: press `5` for the laboratory's Inventory tab and `a` in [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) to add it with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Press `4` for the [Attributes tab](help:tab-lab-attributes) and `a` in [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then press Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: choose **Stores** in the laboratory's rail and add it under [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Choose **Specializations** in the rail and click **Add** under [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then click Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /gui -->

The Virtues & Flaws list shows a granted Virtue as "from" its item, and the Magic Item Virtue with its item's name. The points join the laboratory's Specializations and Characteristics, each named "(magic item)" where its sources are listed.

### Install one while setting up a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-creation -->

On a new laboratory's **Setting Up** step, **Install a magic item** offers **A new enchanted device**, or a device already kept in the laboratory. A new device is made in three forms: the device (kept in this laboratory), its effect, then what the effect gives, as above. The device and its effect are kept as each is saved, so cancelling later leaves them in the laboratory's Enchanted Devices, ready to install another time. The step lists what each magic item gives.

For a starting laboratory, the covenant's Build Points pay for the device as for any enchanted item (2 for every 5 levels of each effect), on the covenant's Resources step under Enchanted items, and its effects count against the Power Level's highest effect level. A Virtue the item grants costs no Build Points.

### When the item is a Feature
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-feature -->

An item can instead be made a Feature: a Lesser or Greater Feature, with the Aesthetics and the Specializations a Feature brings. Then it is not installed here: add it under [Features](help:section-lab-features), and its Feature Virtue takes space and costs Build Points as usual. The usual reason to choose a Feature is for what a Feature gives that points bought by level don't, such as Aesthetics or a Focus.

### What the app does for you
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-app -->

**The app works out:** the effect's final level, how many points it can buy, the space and Build Points each Virtue takes (none for a granted one, a Minor Virtue's for a very large item), the Specializations and Characteristics with the item's points added, and the Build Points of a starting laboratory's devices.

**You type:** the device and its effects, how the item is installed, and what each effect gives.

When the device or one of its effects is deleted or destroyed, what it gave goes with it, and the Magic Item Virtue once no other effect uses it.

# How to run Mythic Locations' set pieces
<!-- help: guide-mythic-locations -->

The troupe is heading for the Cursewood, or the Temple of Mercury, and the site has rules of its own. Mythic Locations gives four such set pieces. The app holds the Temple of Mercury's rituals as catalog spells with their rules, and the other three are run at the table, with their rules summed up here.

### The Mercurian rituals
<!-- help: guide-ml-mercurian -->

The Temple of Mercury's library holds Lab Texts for nine rituals (Mythic Locations pp. 96-100). They're in the spell catalog with source ML, and a ritual's details panel says how it's learned.

- **Reading** one for its gist takes a day and needs Latin and a Magic Theory score. It gives the Arts, the magnitude and roughly what the ritual does, but can't be cast or invented from (p. 97).
- **Translating** it into a Hermetic Lab Text is lab work. Each season adds the Lab Total in the ritual's Arts less its level, and at twice the level the copy is done. That means a Lab Total above the level, and three times the level for a single season. Later translations get a bonus equal to the highest magnitude of any ritual already translated (one bonus, not a sum), but invention doesn't. Experimenting is allowed (p. 97).
- **Inventing** it from the Hermetic copy is the usual Lab Text rule, one season when the Lab Total is at least the level. Range, Duration and Target can't be changed. Any magus with the copy may invent it (p. 97).
- **Casting** is as a Hermetic Ritual of its Arts. It can be dispelled like one, but anyone with Magic Theory sees it isn't Hermetic (p. 97).

Most rituals need a temple consecrated to Mercury, made by Consecration of Mercury's House, which also gives the temple a Magic aura of 1 where it had none (p. 99).

- **Purification** takes off a Decrepitude score, five years of apparent age and up to five Aging Points. It can't be cast on oneself (pp. 97-98). The player chooses which Characteristics the Aging Points come off, as the Definitive Edition also leaves to the player (DE pp. 391-392). The app takes them off those with the most first unless another choice is given.
- **Transformation of Flesh to Spirit** raises the aura by one, to at most 10. It works only if none of the caster's Arts is under twice the aura and the Penetration is above five times it (p. 99).
- **Wizard's Initiation** never works, and adds a botch die (pp. 99, 101).

Five integrations open from these rituals, offered as research goals (pp. 100-101):

- a CrVi 50 aura-making guideline, 30 points
- a CrVi 55 aura-raising guideline, 30 points
- a Cr(Re)Vi 20 regio entrance, 30 points
- the Mercurian Magic Virtue, 45 points
- a mended Wizard's Initiation that gives The Gift, 60 points

Their kinds follow the Definitive Edition's sizes for a breakthrough, about 30 points for a Minor, 45 for a Major and 60 for a Hermetic (DE p. 277; Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 28).

The aqueduct that fills the Scrying Pool takes 20 man-years to repair and five workers every season to keep running. Each season without them, roll a simple die against 3 + the seasons neglected in a row. Under it, the aqueduct clogs. Its spring gives 5 pawns of Aquam vis each Summer solstice (pp. 96-97).

### Trekking in the Cursewood
<!-- help: guide-ml-cursewood -->

The Cursewood is crossed in five stages, each costing a Long-Term Fatigue level (Mythic Locations pp. 37-40). Choose a leader and a guide for each stage. Together they can lead as many people as their Leadership scores added together, and each person over that adds a hazard.

Each stage, the guide rolls for the terrain (p. 37):

| Terrain | Roll |
|---|---|
| Forest trail | Sta + Survival |
| Forest floor | Per + Survival |
| Swamp wading | Sta + Athletics |
| Boat | Dex + Profession: Boating |
| Swimming | Str + Swimming |
| Hill path | Dex + Athletics |
| Hill climb | Str + Athletics |

The roll takes -1 for each member besides the leader and guide, and +1 for each other member who has the Ability. It also takes off the heaviest Encumbrance and the guide's Wound and Fatigue penalties (p. 37).

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 0 or botch | Lost, with no progress and four hazards |
| 3 | Four hazards |
| 6 | Three hazards |
| 9 | Two hazards |
| 12 | One hazard |
| 18+ | The first time, one hazard and +3 next stage; later, no hazards |

A stage takes an hour for each hazard, at least one. Retreating takes an hour a stage, without rolls. Short-Term Fatigue takes twice as long to recover here. A hazard during the night means no Fatigue is recovered. Eating or drinking anything from the wood gives a Light Wound (p. 38).

The wood is an Infernal aura of 5. That's -5 to Recovery rolls, +5 to Damage, and five extra botch dice on physical rolls, the trek rolls included (p. 40). Its spirit's direct attacks must Penetrate, with +7 from the spirit's Magic regio, against Magic Resistance at -5 (p. 39). Forest Lore 3 or more tells a character more about the spirit, and from 7 lets them pass alone unhindered (p. 37).

### The Faerie Toy Market
<!-- help: guide-ml-toy-market -->

The merchants of Devil's Lane sell faerie toys made like Hermetic items (Mythic Locations pp. 107-112).

- **Prices** are in vis, of any Form and any realm's taint. The lowest price is the pawns the item would take to make, +1, and +1 more for an effect of the sixth magnitude or higher. A charged item costs at least a pawn a charge, a mundane item 1 pawn. Merchants open at twice that and haggle down. They ask for odd payments that hold the right vis, and refuse one whose vis was taken out first (p. 107).
- **Commissions** cost twice the vis, in Arts that suit the item, paid in advance. The opening price is four times the vis (p. 107).
- **Lifetime.** Every toy is glamour and stops working seven years after purchase, charges included (p. 107).
- **The workshops** do a Lab Total of 100 of work a season for each merchant. A character can negotiate up to that much for themselves. Unused work doesn't carry over, and characters can't pool theirs. The elves can make any Hermetic item, faerie Ranges, Durations and Targets included, but not a talisman (p. 112).
- **Walter the book merchant** sells glamour books that last seven years, at 1 pawn for a tractatus and 1 a level for a summa. A season with one is practice at Quality 5. His exercise slates last a year and a day, for 1 pawn (p. 109).

### Hunted by wolves
<!-- help: guide-ml-wolves -->

A wolf hunt has five phases, in this order (Mythic Locations pp. 139-142).

**The stalk.** The pack finds the trail with Per + Awareness and follows it with Per + Hunt, usually against 9. A group is 3 easier to follow, and a lone, careful quarry adds its Stealth. Then the pack rolls Dex + Stealth against the prey's Per + Awareness. Each success brings it 10 + Qik paces closer. Two failures in a row, three in all, or getting within ten paces moves the hunt to the encounter (p. 140).

**The encounter.** Prey that holds its ground is attacked. Prey that runs starts the rush (p. 140).

**The rush.** Rounds of six seconds. Each side rolls Qik + Athletics - Encumbrance + stress die. The winner moves the gap by 10 + Qik - Encumbrance paces on four legs, or 6 + Qik - Encumbrance on two. Both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 9 each round, or lose a Short-Term Fatigue level. At zero paces comes the kill. Wolves usually turn to the chase when they lose a Fatigue level, or once the gap passes 100 paces (p. 140).

**The chase.** Rounds of five to thirty minutes. Each round both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 12, or lose a Long-Term Fatigue level. A group rolls for its most tired member, but wolves for their freshest. The wolves' Pursuit Total is Per + Hunt - Fatigue + modifier + stress die, with +3 against two-legged prey and -3 for two-legged hunters of four-legged prey. The prey's Evasion Total is Dex + Athletics - Fatigue + stress die, with -6 to +6 for who knows the ground. If the prey wins four rounds, it escapes. If the wolves win four, it's cornered (p. 141).

**The kill.** The pack attacks as a trained group led by its leader, and the rest cut off escape. A wolf may try a trip, a Brawl Attack against Defense. A fleeing or unaware target defends only with Brawl or Evasion. Beating it by 3 or more knocks the target down, and its Defense is Qik - Encumbrance + stress die until it stands (pp. 141-142).

# How to set up a threat's clock
<!-- help: guide-threat-clock -->

A threat can move on whenever the troupe fails something the saga asks
of it, as a clock fills in a Powered by the Apocalypse game. The
Watchtower's due to Abnoba each Winter, unpaid, brings the Hollow Crown
a step nearer.

1. **Make the threat move along a track.** Make a New Threat, or Edit
   one, and set **Moves by** to *A track* or *An omen count*. Set its
   position to where it stands now, 0 for a fresh one.
2. **Give its stages their steps.** Add each stage with the step it
   **Comes at**, such as 2, 4 and 6. The last stage's step is the
   clock's size, so the threat's page shows ■■□□□□ 2 of 6.
3. **Tie a place's trigger to it.** In the place's Regio, add or edit a
   dated trigger, such as *Abnoba's due*, Winter, each year. Choose the
   threat in **Moves a threat**, and how many steps failing it moves the
   threat.
4. **Play the seasons.** When the trigger's season is played, Advance
   Season asks. **Failed** moves the threat on, and **Kept** leaves it.
   Either is written in the threat's Diary and the place's, and the
   stage it reaches comes.
5. **Push it back when the troupe acts.** If the troupe thwarts it some
   other way, choose **Delay or Hasten** in the threat's More and move it
   back, with the reason.

A threat moving by fixed dates has a clock too, with a square for each
stage. Delay or Hasten moves all its stages still to come by the
seasons you give.

# How to draw up and keep a charter
<!-- help: guide-charter -->

Four magi found Dammërstein in the Rhine and swear the Charter of Arae
Flaviae. Years later, one of them breaks it.

1. **Start from a template.** In the covenant's Charter, choose
   **Use a Template** (More on the charter) and the Charter of Arae
   Flaviae. Its oath, its 25 clauses under the charter's sections and its
   seven-season probation are filled in with the covenant's and the
   Tribunal's names. Used unaltered, it counts as ratified (Covenants p.
   33).
2. **Swear it.** **Edit Charter** sets when it was sworn and its Official
   Seal.
3. **Seal it.** **Seal the Charter** ticks each founder, with the season
   and their standing. A newcomer sealed as a probationary member serves
   the charter's probation, and the charter tells you when it has run.
4. **Make it yours.** Amend clauses with Edit, or add your own. Any change
   ends the automatic ratification, so tick **Notarized by a Quaesitor**
   once one has seen it. To reuse it for another covenant, **Save as
   Template**, for any Tribunal or the Rhine's alone.
5. **Record a breach.** Choose the clause and **Break a Clause**, then say
   when and how.
6. **Censure the breaker.** **Censure** says what the covenant did. That
   may be privileges withheld, their standing lowered, or expulsion.
   Withheld privileges don't take their laboratory, unless the charter
   says so.
7. **Expel, and tidy up.** On expulsion the app asks whether to take them
   off the covenant's lists now. Say No to move their books and vis
   first. The charter reminds you, and Advance Season asks again each
   season.
8. **Take it to Tribunal.** A charter that binds under the Peripheral
   Code, notarized or sworn in Provence, can be broken as a Low Crime.
   **Charge at Tribunal** puts the case on the Tribunal's docket.

The Walkthroughs are seventeen illustrated guides that follow one troupe
through the app, from making a magus to a covenant on the move. Each
starts from a saga at a set point (Rabenstein in Spring 1220, the Rhine
Tribunal on the eve of its meeting, and so on), and the app ships every
one of those sagas, so you can do each step yourself as you read.

1. **Open the guides.** They are one book, *The Walkthroughs.pdf*, sized
   for reading on a phone, in the app's `data/tutorials` folder.
2. **Start the tutorial.** On the title page choose **Learn**, then
   **Tutorials**
<!-- tui -->
   (or, on the saga list, press `m` for More and choose **Start a
   tutorial**),
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   (or click **Start a Tutorial** on the Sagas page),
<!-- /gui -->
   and choose the guide you are reading. The list gives each guide's
   number, title and the season its saga opens in.
3. **Follow along.** A fresh copy of the saga joins your sagas under the
   guide's title, and opens. Everything you do changes only your copy.
4. **Start again any time.** Starting the same tutorial again gives a
   new, clean copy beside the old one; delete the one you're done with
   from the saga list.

The guides are: 1 Making a Magus, 2 The Covenfolk, 3 A Year at
Rabenstein, 4 The Laboratory, 5 Enchantments, 6 The Mystery Cults, 7 The
Hidden Arts, 8 Beyond the Order, 9 Founding and Keeping a Covenant,
10 Great Works, 11 A Knight's Life, 12 War, 13 Hermetic Politics, 14 The
Saga and Its World, 15 The Four Realms, 16 Rules of the House and 17 On
the Move.

**See also:** [Saga picker](help:saga-picker), [Getting started](help:guide-getting-started)

# How to plan and record a Session
<!-- help: guide-sessions -->

Friday's game is set: Ana and Bruno are coming, Clara is away.

<!-- tui -->
1. **Add the Session.** Open **Saga Content**, its
   [Sessions tab](help:tab-content-sessions), and press `a`. Type the
   Date (2026-11-14), leave Played unticked, and set Clara to **Away**.
   The game season and year are optional: a Session may span seasons.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Add the Session.** Open the saga page's **Saga Management**
   chapter, its [Sessions](help:tab-content-sessions) section, and click
   **Add**. Type the Date (2026-11-14), leave Played unticked,
   and set Clara to **Away**. The game season and year are optional: a
   Session may span seasons.
<!-- /gui -->
2. **Check who plays whom.** **Next** shows Who Played Whom, filled in
   from who usually plays each character. A character two coming
   Players share is left for you: pick who plays it tonight.
3. **Look at Needs You.** An open story about a character only Clara
   plays is named there as **Away**: keep it for another evening.
<!-- gui -->
   The Troupe shows the Session as Next Session.
<!-- /gui -->
4. **After the game,** edit the Session and tick **Played**, changing
   Who Played Whom if the evening went differently.
5. **Pooled grogs.** In a grog's Biographical Detail, choose a
   **Steward**: the Player who keeps them between Sessions and plans
   their seasons, even from their own computer.

**See also:** [Sessions](help:tab-content-sessions), [Players](help:tab-content-players)

# How to choose a theme
<!-- help: guide-themes; gui -->

Your magus is a Bonisagus, and you'd like the app to look like her House's
books.

1. **Open Settings.** Click the **gear** at the right of the top bar. The
   Settings window opens beside the page, and stays open while you work.
2. **Open the Theme drop-down.** Scriptorum and Scriptorum Dark come
   first. The House themes follow, under their House family: True
   Lineages, Mystery Cults, Societates; House Diedne comes last, under
   Renounced. Each row shows a small sample of its page, the House's mark,
   its name and a few words on it.
3. **Choose one.** The whole app is drawn in it at once, the Settings
   window too. There is nothing to confirm. To compare, leave the
   drop-down closed and press the up and down arrow keys: each theme is
   drawn as you reach it. Choose Scriptorum to go back.
4. **Look at a record In Creation.** It is drawn on the theme's drafting
   page, cooler and ruled, so a draft still stands out from a finished
   record.
5. **Close the window.** The app remembers the theme on this computer and
   opens in it next time. Printed pages stay in Scriptorum whatever the
   theme.

**See also:** [The Settings window](help:settings-window)
