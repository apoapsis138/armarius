# How to bind and keep a familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars -->

This guide follows a familiar from the magus's first meeting with the creature to a bond strengthened with powers. Use it when a magus in your saga is ready to bind a familiar, or when you are adding a magus who already has one. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Familiars.

### Finding the familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars-finding -->

Finding a suitable creature and winning it over is a matter for your stories, not for the app. Play it out at the table; the app only comes in once the creature has agreed to the bond.

A magus can have one familiar at a time, so Bind Familiar is only offered while at least one magus in the saga has none. A Bjornaer, whose heartbeast takes its place, can't bind one (DE p. 234).

4. The familiar's sheet opens. In the tree, the magus's node now has the familiar beneath it, as "Familiar: " and its name; select it there to come back to the [Familiar sheet](help:familiar) later.

With **Bound already** ticked (a familiar bound before play), making the record grants the bond at once: both the magus and the familiar gain the True Friend Virtue and a Loyal (Partner) +3 Personality Trait. <!-- help: guide-familiars-powers -->

1. On the familiar's [Bond Powers tab](help:tab-fam-powers) (`5`), press `a`. Type the Power Name, Level, Technique, Form and the rest, and set **Instilling (blank = finished)** to 0. [Go there](go:tab-fam-powers)
2. In the laboratory's Planning tab, choose a season and press `e`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. In the familiar's [Bond Powers chapter](help:tab-fam-powers), click **Add**. Type the Power Name, Level, Technique, Form and the rest, and set **Instilling (blank = finished)** to 0. [Go there](go:tab-fam-powers)
2. In the laboratory's Seasons chapter, click a season in Planning, then **Plan**.
<!-- /gui -->
In **Lab work**, choose **Empower the familiar's bond**, then choose the power in **Bond power** and the vis Art.
3. Choose **Schedule**. The Lab Total must be above the power's level. Each season adds the Lab Total less the level in points, and the vis (a tenth of the level, rounded up) is paid in the first season. The app plans the following seasons the work is projected to need.

When the power's points reach its level, the power is finished. A power the familiar already had can be added with Instilling left blank.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-familiars-limits -->

The app works out the point cost of each cord, whether a binding or empowerment can be done, its Lab Total, its vis, and the progress of a power over several seasons. The Bronze Cord is taken off the magus's aging roll each Winter.

Most of the familiar's own figures are typed in: its Soak, Magic Resistance and Might, its wounds and penalties, and each bond power's figures. The Golden Cord takes its botch dice off casting totals (a hedge power's too); the Silver Cord is added by Roll beside Personality Traits (a trait roll, resisting mental influence, or the familiar freeing its magus's mind), and the True Friend Virtue is recorded but has no effect the app applies.

Deleting the magus also deletes their familiar, and exporting the magus takes the familiar with them.
<!-- tui -->
To remove only the familiar, press `d` on it in the tree.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
To remove only the familiar, choose Delete in the **Manage** chapter of its page.
<!-- /gui -->
Then the bond is broken, and the magus loses True Friend, Loyal (Partner) and any planned familiar seasons.

# How to initiate a character into a Mystery
<!-- help: guide-initiation -->

This guide follows a Mystery Initiation from the Script to the new Virtue on the Initiate's sheet. Use it when a character joins a Mystery cult, takes the next step in their House's Mysteries, or initiates themselves into a Virtue. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 10: Long-Term Events, Mystery Initiation; The Mysteries Revised Edition and Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults give the full treatment, and the Definitive Edition wins where they differ.

### Before the season
<!-- help: guide-initiation-before -->

An Initiation needs three things, settled at the table first:

- **A Mystagogue** who knows the cult's Lore (House Bjornaer Lore, Mithraic Lore and so on). A character can be their own Mystagogue: a self-initiation uses the Initiate's own scores.
<!-- tui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it on the [Abilities tab](help:tab-abilities) (`2`) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it in the [Abilities chapter](help:tab-abilities) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /gui -->
- **A Script**, written like a Lab Text. Its bonus is the sum of its components, as the storyguide agrees them: +3 for a special place at a special time, +3 for each Quest, +1 for serving the cult (once), +1 for a sacrifice of wealth, +3 to +6 for a sacrifice of something of great value, and the Ordeal's +3 (a Minor Flaw, or losing a Minor Virtue), +6 (raising a Minor Flaw to Major) or +9 (a Major Flaw, or losing a Major Virtue). Quests and other sacrifices are played out in their own seasons or stories.

### Planning the Initiation
<!-- help: guide-initiation-plan -->

The Initiation is the Initiate's season, planned like any other.

<!-- tui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their Planning tab (`6` for a magus, `5` for anyone else), and press `e` in [This Season](help:section-char-season). A covenant's [Seasons tab](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their **Planning** chapter, and click **Plan** (or **Edit**, if a plan is there already) in [This Season](help:section-char-season). **Plan** in a covenant's [Seasons chapter](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /gui -->
2. In **Activity**, choose **Undergo an Initiation**.
3. In **Teacher / master**, choose the Mystagogue. Leave it blank for a self-initiation.
4. Type the **Script bonus** and the **Mystagogue's Lore**, spelt as it is in the Mystagogue's Abilities. To vary the Script, choose the change in **Vary the Script** (see below). When the Script makes a sacrifice, choose in **Sacrificed** what is given up: one of the initiate's devices (a talisman among them), carried items, books or familiar, an apprentice, mundane wealth, vis, or a season serving the cult. It is made when the season is played, Initiation or not, and the Diary says so. A device, item, book or familiar is gone, so it can't be sacrificed twice; a season of service takes the next season as Other, unless it is planned already; vis is taken from the initiate's own raw vis, as many pawns of the Art as **Vis of** and **Pawns sacrificed** say. A plan whose vis isn't there to give is refused: a Sacrifice that isn't made invalidates the whole Script (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16). The bonus it brings is the Script's Sacrifice component (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 12, 15-16).
<!-- tui -->
5. Choose **Virtue initiated**. It opens the Virtues & Flaws picker on Virtues: search, filter by type or tier, and press Enter.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->

<!-- /gui -->
A Virtue that names something, such as Affinity with an Art or Puissant with an Ability, asks for it.
6. If the Script has an Ordeal, choose what it costs in **Ordeal**: **Gains a Flaw** asks for the **Flaw gained** (the same picker, on Flaws); **Loses a Virtue** asks for the **Virtue lost**, one of the Initiate's own Minor or Major Virtues; **Raises a Minor Flaw to Major** asks for the **Minor Flaw raised**, one of their own, and the related Major Flaw it becomes as the **Flaw gained**. The Ordeal's size follows from what it costs: a Minor Flaw or Virtue makes a Minor Ordeal, a Major one a Major Ordeal, and a raised Flaw a Medium one. Leave Ordeal blank for none.

Delete on a chosen field clears it.
7. Watch the line above Save.

<!-- gui -->
A chosen Virtue or Flaw field's **Clear** button clears it.
7. Watch the line under the fields.

It shows the Initiation Total (the Mystagogue's Presence + Lore + Script bonus) against the Target Level, and whether the Initiation will succeed. Save refuses a plan that is missing something or breaks a rule, and says why.

There is no die: the Initiation succeeds when the total reaches the Target Level. The Target is 15 for a Minor Virtue and 21 for a Major one, or 18 and 30 when the Mystagogue doesn't have the Virtue (always so for a self-initiation into a Virtue the character lacks).

**House Verditius.** Every magus with Verditius Magic can follow the House's sample Scripts, listed under House Verditius (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field: one for each of the eight Inner Mysteries, the Outer Mystery and four confraternities' (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 118-135). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Verditius (the book's Verditius Cult Lore). The Initiate needs it at 3 for a Minor Inner Mystery and 5 for a Major (p. 118). The first Inner Mystery always brings the Flaw Hubris as a Minor Ordeal: the season adds the Flaw and says what to set the Hubris trait to (1, +1 with Proud, +3 with Proud as a Major Flaw), and each Inner Mystery's note gives the Hubris experience it earns (1 Minor, 3 Major; DE p. 242). The House's own Ordeals may each be suffered once; Prone to Chance and Averse to Risk exclude each other, and no one in the Primus's line may Sever the Ties that Bind (pp. 120-121).

**House Merinita.** Every magus with Faerie Magic belongs to the House and can follow its lineages' sample Scripts, listed under House Merinita (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field: the Line of Quendalon's Animae Magic, the Heirs to Merlin's Charm Magic, the Followers of Pendule's Spell Timing, the Shadow-Masters' Glamour, the Vestals' Nature Lore (as Lares Lore) and the True Merinitae's Wilding (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 83-106). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Merinita (the book's House Merinita Lore); an Initiate needs it at 1 for an Inner Mystery (p. 88). The Arcadian, Folk and Illusion Mysteries need Faerie Magic; Binding the Gift is the House's own; the Nature Mysteries (Awakening, Wilding, Guardian of Nature) need Nature Lore instead and not the House's Lore (pp. 86, 102-104). Glamour always brings the Major Flaw Vulnerable Magic as its Ordeal: the season adds it, unless the plan's Ordeal names it already, and a Script written out as components counts its Major Ordeal. Type its condition on the plan (Vulnerable Magic's condition): it must be one the magus doesn't have the Flaw for already, since the Flaw is taken again only with a new condition (pp. 87, 102). Perpetuity's season notes that it usually brings Unnatural Magic.

**House Bjornaer.** Every magus with the Heartbeast can follow the House's Scripts, listed under House Bjornaer (Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 24-33). The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Bjornaer (the book's House Bjornaer Lore, which the rules also read, the Initiation Total included: a character with either name scores it; HoH:MC pp. 28, 30, Errata p. 54). **The Ritual of Twelve Years** gives the Heartbeast: Target 15, Script +6, plus the Participation Modifier typed as the other modifier, +1 for each witness (the Mystagogue included) whose heartbeast shares the probationer's temperament and -1 for each opposed (sanguine opposes melancholic, choleric phlegmatic; p. 26). Choose Heartbeast as the Virtue initiated: the season then adds Heartbeast 1, and the familiar forgone counts as a Minor Ordeal. It fails for one who has ever bound a familiar, even one since dead (DE p. 234; p. 24): one bound to him now, or a familiar season in his Diary; tick Has ever bound a familiar on the plan for one the app can't see and is refused to an Initiate of another House's Mystery (p. 18), and it is never repeated. Clan Ilfetu leads the Ritual and the House Mysteries, Secret Name (+8), Theriomorphy (+6) and Sensory Magic (+9) (p. 25); a Clan Mystery's Trial, Deed or Oath (+7, the Trial +13 with a Major Ordeal) needs a Mystagogue with Presence + the Lore 8, less the Initiate's past Ordeal's reduction. **The Mystery of the Inner Heartbeast** (Epitome, Chimera or Anima) needs the Lore 5: Target 21, Script +13, its Ordeal a Major Hermetic or Supernatural Flaw, or for a magus who changes shape the loss of his Skinchanger or Shapeshifter (pp. 17, 28-30). With no Mystagogue chosen, the Great Beast leads it, with Presence +2 and the Lore 8. The Epitome raises one Characteristic: the inner heartbeast's to the species' + 3 and the human form's by 1; the outer heartbeast changes only through that +1, combined as usual (pp. 23, 30). So the book's example ends with an inner Perception of +5 and an outer +2 (the eagle's own, one above where it started). The app follows this rule text: Errata p. 54 gives the outer heartbeast +5, but that is the inner figure and contradicts the rule, and Through the Aegis p. 14 (Hilde Oddfish, Strength +7 outer and +10 inner) applies the rule as written. **Refinements** of the inner heartbeast are the magus's own: the embryonic Script (+7, and +3 where his inner heartbeast resonates: an Epitome with Virtues and Characteristics, a Chimera with Qualities and Size, an Anima with Powers) against 15 (a Quality), 18 (Size, a Characteristic, a Minor Virtue), 21 (a Minor Power) or 30 (a Major Virtue or Power), lowered by past Ordeals as usual. Each earlier increase of the same Size or Characteristic adds 3: take 3 from the other modifier for each. The season notes the vis eaten (a pawn per three points of the Ease Factor) and the Warping Point; a Refinement changes only the inner heartbeast, so it is noted on the sheet by hand (pp. 32-33).

**House Criamon.** A Criamon magus's paths are listed under House Criamon
(Mystery Cult) on the Initiation's Script field, one Script for each
avenue (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 61-71; Errata p. 55), and The
Enigma tab's Follow an Avenue plans one. The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore:
Criamon (the book's House Criamon Lore, which counts too); a station
needs it at its place on the path + 1. Each avenue's Target is a Major
Virtue's 21, as the book prints it whether or not the guide holds the
station, lowered by past Ordeals as usual. With no Mystagogue chosen, the
guide the House provides guides at Presence + Lore 6, the printed
Scripts' assumption, or a ghostly Primus (Lore 9, with the Presence set on
Change Path) when The Enigma names him (p. 60). The Mystagogue need only
know House Criamon Lore; one not of the House is noted as unusual (p. 59;
DE p. 387). When the plan names no Ordeal of its own, the station's
Ordeal Flaws are added (a Major Pious replacing Vow or Higher Purpose),
and the Ordeal counts at the size the book's next Targets show; the
avenues are followed in order along a path. Some stations add catalogue
Virtues too: Clear Thinker, Common Sense and Keen Vision; the Gentle Gift,
or the Blatant Gift lifted to an ordinary Gift; Free Expression and
Inspirational (pp. 64, 70-71). His aptitude for a path, from a head bound
in infancy, adds +3 to its first station (p. 51).

**Script components.** A Hermetic Mystery's Script can be written out on the plan as its **Script components** (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 15-16; DE p. 388): a special place at a special time +3 (once), each specific Quest +3 (a unique Quest +6), the Initiate's time serving the cult +1 (once), material goods +1 (once), something of great symbolic value +3 to +6 (a talisman, familiar or powerful device), the Mystagogue's time +3, and the sympathy of the rite to the Virtue, usually 0 to +3 but -5 to +5, agreed with the storyguide. The Ordeal's own bonus is added from the plan's **Ordeal**: +3 for a Minor Flaw gained or Minor Virtue lost (a Minor Virtue replaced by its Major one counts as that), +6 for a Minor Flaw raised to Major, +9 for a Major Flaw or Virtue; a Verditius's first Inner Mystery counts its Hubris as a Minor Ordeal when no other is named. The **Script bonus** typed above is added to the components. A component listed twice that may be held once, or a size out of its range, invalidates the whole Script (TMRE p. 16). With no components listed, the typed bonus is the whole Script, as before, except that a Verditius's first Inner Mystery adds its Hubris Ordeal's +3 to it (and to a catalog Script's printed bonus), since that Ordeal always comes and the bonus can't know of it (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 117). A Verditius Initiate is also offered the House's own components (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 117): a lesser enchanted item of the 4th magnitude sacrificed +3, a greater one of the 7th or his talisman +6, the Mystagogue's wealth +1 (once), and the Outer Mystery given as the Arts are opened +6. A hedge or House Script from the catalog keeps its printed bonus; the season's note lists its components as the book gives them (the Verditius Outer Mystery's add to 14, though the book prints +12, which is used).

The House's Hubris is now kept on the sheet: the first Inner Mystery makes the Hubris Personality Trait (1, +1 with Proud, +3 with Proud as a Major Flaw), each Inner Mystery adds its experience (1 Minor, 3 Major), and the Increased Hubris Ordeal adds enough for one more point (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 121). Hubris grows like an Ability: its experience toward the next point is kept with the trait. A confraternity's Script also notes the Mystery and Flaw it gives its members, their Craft and the confraternity's path (Roland, Balento, Irene the Younger, Himinis the Mad; pp. 121-123). See [Hubris](help:verditius-hubris) for what a sale, the Contest or a vendetta does to it.

Press `t` to [advance the season](help:advance-season).
<!-- /tui -->

Click **Advance Season** in the top bar to [advance the season](help:advance-season).

For a successful Initiation, the app adds the Virtue (with what it names) to the Initiate's [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf) and applies the Ordeal: it adds the Flaw gained, takes away the Virtue lost, or replaces the Minor Flaw raised with its Major Flaw. A Great Characteristic Virtue gained raises the score, and one lost lowers it again. A failed Initiation changes nothing, and its Ordeal is not suffered: the Mystagogue sees that a Script will fail before any sacrifice is made. Two failures still cost the Ordeal: one that distractions made fail (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12), and an experimental Script whose rite fails, since its Ordeals are suffered before the rite is checked (p. 18). Either way, the Diary records the Initiation Total against the Target.

### Past Ordeals make later Initiations easier
<!-- help: guide-initiation-ordeals -->

An Ordeal lowers the Target Level of the Initiate's next three Initiations, never below 9 (DE p. 388): by 3, 2 and 1 after a Minor Ordeal, 6, 4 and 2 after a Medium one, and 9, 6 and 3 after a Major one. It doesn't help the Initiation it was suffered in. When the Initiate has suffered several, only the one that lowers the Target most counts: a Major Ordeal followed by a Minor one still takes 6 off the next Initiation. The app keeps every Ordeal and counts each one down after each Initiation that gives its Virtue; a failed Initiation is not one of "the next three", so it uses up no step (DE p. 388). An unGifted Initiate's past Ordeals lower nothing (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). The Initiate's [This Season](help:section-char-season) section lists the past Ordeals still counting and what they take off the next Target. Flaws taken at character creation don't count as Ordeals.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-initiation-limits -->

**The app works out:** the Initiation Total and Target Level, whether the Mystagogue has the Virtue, the largest reduction among past Ordeals, the checks on the Lore and the Ordeal, the Ordeal's size, and the Virtues and Flaws added or taken away on success.

**Varying the Script:** the Mystagogue may make one change in an Initiation. Choose it in **Vary the Script** (add a component, Ease 9; change one, 12; remove one, 15) and type in **The change adds** what it does to the Script bonus (negative when removing). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore: success adds the change to the Script bonus, and failure fails the Initiation, with no Virtue and no Ordeal, though the Script as it was may be tried another season (DE pp. 388-389). Before then, the line under the form gives the roll the change needs and the Initiation Total if it works. The Mysteries Revised Edition (p. 17) says instead that the original Script still works; the Definitive Edition wins, so this isn't a bug. Removing a component (Ease 15) is The Mysteries Revised's own addition, and it doesn't let a Minor Virtue's Script become a Major one.

**Overlapping Scripts:** a Script that shares Quests or Ordeals with one the Initiate already followed can't be used as it is: the Mystagogue changes the shared component (**Change a component**, Ease 12) or puts a different one in its place (**Replace a component**, Ease 15), and types in **The change adds** what that does to the bonus (Legends of Hermes pp. 50-51). Each Quest a Script written out as components names (its note) is recorded on the Initiate when the rite is carried out, and shown as **Quests performed** on the Initiate's This Season, beside the past Ordeals; an Initiation whose Script names one again says so on the plan and the season, and that Quest gives the Script nothing, since a Quest counts toward one Initiation only (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16): change the Script first. An Ordeal is spotted the same way by the Flaw the Initiate already has.

**An experimental Script:** with no Script, or one that falls short, tick **Experimental Script**, type the proposed rite's components in **Script bonus**, and choose a **Risk** of 0 to 3 (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 18). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + risk on the Extraordinary Results chart (with 1 + risk botch dice) and reads it for a rite: side effects and minor flaws or benefits become Personality Traits of the Script, a major flaw an extra Ordeal Flaw with no bonus, a major benefit a stronger Virtue or a second Minor one, a Discovery experience for the Mystagogue, a modified effect a different Virtue (the Diary says what to add by hand); a fatal flaw or a complete failure gives no Virtue, No Benefit takes the risk off the total, and Disaster fails the rite and gives the Mystagogue and the Initiate each a Major Flaw. Then it rolls the rite's validity: a simple die + risk + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore against 9 + the proposed bonus, or 15 + it when the Initiate is someone else; a failure gives no Virtue. Any failure still costs the rite's Ordeal. The Initiation Total adds the storyguide's **Sympathy bonus** and the risk; the Script written down afterwards keeps the sympathy but not the risk. An experimental Script isn't also varied.

**UnGifted Initiates:** an unGifted Initiate's Initiation must include an Ordeal, his past Ordeals don't lower its Target, and an unGifted Mystagogue must know the power, so an unGifted character can't initiate himself (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). The app applies this to every Initiation, Hermetic cults' included. A magus counts as Gifted.

**Hedge Scripts:** to follow one of a hedge tradition's printed Scripts, choose it in **Hedge Script** (the list holds the Scripts of the traditions someone in the saga belongs to). The tradition's Characteristic and Lore are used instead of Presence and the Lore typed, the Script's own bonus is used and anything typed in **Script bonus** is added to it, and its Ease Factor is the Target. When the Mystagogue doesn't know the power the Ease Factor rises by 3 for a Minor Virtue, 9 for a Major (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 13). The app counts him as knowing it when he has the **Virtue initiated**; when the power is an Ability (a gruagach's Give, say), tick **Mystagogue knows the power**. The Mystagogue must belong to the tradition, a tradition without self-initiation can't initiate itself, and a Script for the Gifted needs a Gifted Initiate. With a hedge Script the **Virtue initiated** may be left blank: the Diary then names what the Script gives, to add by hand. Past Ordeals lower a hedge Script's Ease Factor too, never below 9, and never an Ease Factor already lower.

**Who may be Initiated:** two Mysteries check it. **Secret Name** fails for a magus with a talisman or a familiar (DE p. 235), and **Faerie Magic** needs one touched by the fay, by a fay Virtue or Flaw or a Warping Point from a faerie (DE p. 236). The app can't tell where Warping came from, so any Warping Point counts.

**Distractions:** **Months lost** on the plan takes a third of the Initiation Total a month, rounded up. If that makes the Initiation fail, the Ordeal is still suffered and the Initiation must be repeated (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12).

**You type:** the Script bonus, as the storyguide agrees it. For an experimental Script, the proposed bonus, the risk and the storyguide's sympathy bonus. Which Major Flaw a Minor Flaw is related to is the troupe's call: the app checks only that it is a Major Flaw.

A Mystagogue who spends a whole season on the Script (teaching a Supernatural Ability, say) plans **Act as Mystagogue**, and a season spent on a Quest is **Serve a Mystagogue / Quest season**; both need The Mysteries Revised Edition in play.

**See also:** [Plan Season](help:plan-season), [Virtues & Flaws tab](help:tab-vf), [Advancing a season](help:advance-season), [How the app handles advancement](help:guide-advancement)

### When a name is already taken
<!-- help: guide-import-export-duplicates -->

When an imported book or spell has the name of one already in the saga, the app asks about each one: **Skip** leaves it out, **Duplicate** adds it anyway, and **Skip All** and **Duplicate All** answer the same for the rest of that import. <!-- help: guide-import-export-backups -->

- To keep a single character, laboratory or covenant safe before a big change, export it as above.

### Tailscale
<!-- help: guide-hosting-tailscale -->

Everyone, you and each player, installs Tailscale and signs in with an existing Google, Microsoft, GitHub or Apple account.

1. On your computer, Tailscale shows its address (it starts with `100.`). That is the ADDRESS to host on.
2. In Tailscale's admin page, share your computer with each player by their email. They accept the invite, and can then reach only your computer.
3. Players open `http://ADDRESS:8000`.

### ZeroTier
<!-- help: guide-hosting-zerotier -->

Only you need a ZeroTier account; players just install the ZeroTier app.

1. On ZeroTier's website, create a network and copy its 16-character Network ID. Keep it Private.
2. On your computer, join that network from the ZeroTier icon, then on the website tick **Auth** for your computer and note its Managed IP: that is the ADDRESS to host on.
3. Windows often marks the ZeroTier connection as a Public network; set it to Private in Windows' network settings, or the firewall keeps players out.
4. Each player installs ZeroTier, joins with the Network ID, and you tick **Auth** for their device.
5. On a ZeroTier network every device can reach every other one. To let players reach only the app on your computer, set the network's Flow Rules along these lines (with your address), then check a player can still connect:

```
accept ethertype arp;
accept ipdest ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and dport 8000;
accept ipsrc ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and sport 8000;
drop;
```

### Over SSH instead of a browser
<!-- help: guide-hosting-ssh -->

A player who prefers a terminal can connect with SSH instead. Tick **Also over SSH** when you start hosting (the SSH port is 2222 unless you change it; from a terminal, add `--ssh-port 2222`); the browser page keeps working alongside. The SSH server is the app's own: no Windows accounts are involved, and any user name is accepted, because who the player is comes from the same sign-in as in a browser. The first time, the app makes a host key (`ssh_host_key` in your data folder); players' SSH clients ask once whether to trust it.

Players connect with `ssh -p 2222 player@ADDRESS` from Windows Terminal (Windows 10 and 11 include `ssh`), macOS Terminal or any Linux terminal, and get the same saga list and sign-in. Their terminal should be at least 120 columns by 45 rows and support true colour and Unicode: Windows Terminal, iTerm2, macOS Terminal and the usual Linux terminals do; the old Windows console does not. Over SSH, Export isn't offered, since the file could only land on your computer.

### What players see
<!-- help: guide-hosting-players -->

- The app fills the browser window. It is laid out for at least 120 columns by 45 rows: on a 1080p screen, add `?fontsize=12` to the address (`http://ADDRESS:8000/?fontsize=12`) or zoom the browser out.
- The browser keeps some keys for itself (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T, F5), so don't use them in the app.
- A player changes only the characters they play and what belongs to them; everything else they can look at and Export, which downloads in their browser. See [Players](help:tab-content-players).
- Everyone's changes reach the others within a few seconds. See [Signing in](help:sign-in).

### Starting a saga from a pack
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-start -->

1. On the saga list, choose **New Saga**.
2. Fill in the name, storyguide and date as usual. The packs are written as of the book's own year (the Rhine's is 1220), so a saga at another date keeps its own date and the pack's records their years.
3. In **Start from a pack**, pick the book. The list shows the shipped packs first, then any pack in your exports folder. Under it, a **Begin** tick for each of the pack's stories (its threats): untick the ones to keep for later, and they come in dormant, to begin from their More when you want them.
4. Create the saga: it begins with everything in the pack, and the pack's book is put in play (see [The saga](help:saga)), so the book's rules and neighbour kinds show.

### Adding a book to a saga you already have
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-add -->

1. In the saga, choose Import Data, **A Content Pack**, and pick the pack's file from the app's `data/packs` folder (next to the program).
2. The form asks what to bring: untick the parts you don't want, such as the mundane world or the history; the Tribunal (if the pack has one) and its covenants always come (see [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack)).
3. Preview, then import. A covenant, character, place or neighbour the saga already has one of the same name of is kept as it is, so importing over a saga that already holds part of the Tribunal doubles nothing.

A pack is a copy: a saga keeps what it imported and never reads the pack's file again, so a corrected pack in a new version of the app changes new sagas and new imports, not the sagas you have. Importing the corrected pack over a saga adds what the saga doesn't have and leaves what it has, your edits included, so a correction to a record it already holds doesn't come through, and a Tribunal it already has keeps its own Code, cases and Diary. A record the new pack has renamed comes in beside the old one: the preview lists what is added, so check it first.

<!-- help: guide-book-packs-overlay -->

A supplement such as *Through the Aegis* sits on top of the Tribunal packs, so a saga without it matches the Tribunal book. Import its file from `data/packs` the same way, or tick **With Through the Aegis** on New Saga. It adds to each Tribunal the saga has and overwrites records where the two books differ; a form lists each overwrite, unticked if you have changed that record yourself. It stays on, so a Tribunal pack imported later takes its part too. See [Through the Aegis on top of the Tribunal packs](help:pack-overlay-through-the-aegis).

### What a pack puts where
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-where -->

- The Tribunal: its sheet, with the book's customs (the Rhine's gilds), its officers, its Peripheral Code under Rulings, the open quarrels under Cases, and its Hermetic history in the Diary. The mundane history is in its summary, by era.
- Covenants: sketch records, not played, each with a summary, what matters (vis, income, tensions) and the magi the book names; the count of magi the book leaves unnamed is on the covenant.
- Magi: every magus the book names is a full character. Those it prints a stat block for have the book's stats; the rest have stats inferred from what the book says of them (their House, age, favoured Arts, Virtues and Flaws) by the creation rules: not an official write-up, as their Biography says. Untick **Generated stats for magi** when importing to bring those magi in as sketches instead. Where the book gives no age, one is assumed from the magus's rank and noted there too.
- Places: the regions and forests as places, with the regiones inside them and their levels and auras; covenants and towns sit in them.
- The mundane world: lords, towns, churches, schools, factions and threats, with historical arms where they are known.
- The Bestiary: the creatures the book prints a stat block for (the Rhine's boglins, bockmen and great trees, Normandy's korrigans and the Boar King, Provence's faeries and demons), each found at the place it haunts and linked from the threat or faction it belongs to. Untick **The Bestiary** when importing to leave them out.
- Assumptions: where a book leaves a value out (an aura without a number, a founding year, a season), the pack fills it in, and importing it says so. Each pack's page lists what it inferred, what it chose and where it corrects the book: [the Rhine](help:pack-assumptions-rhine), [Normandy](help:pack-assumptions-normandy), [Provence](help:pack-assumptions-provence), [the Theban Tribunal](help:pack-assumptions-theban) and [Transylvania](help:pack-assumptions-transylvania).
- Secrets: what the book tells the storyguide and not the players is on each covenant's, magus's, Tribunal's, neighbour's or creature's [Secrets](help:tab-secrets) tab. The few that belong to part of a record (a regio level, a case, an entry in the Tribunal's Diary, a book) are at the end of its text under a **Secrets** heading.

The packs follow the Definitive Edition where a book's numbers differ from it; the differences are listed in the project's notes.

# How to fight a battle
<!-- help: guide-battle -->

Lords of Men plays a battle as a few events, each a fight with a player character at its heart, rather than every blow. The app keeps the battle in the saga's Conflicts, between two armies, and shows it on the Fief tab of each lord it names; it works out each event's total.

### Before the battle
<!-- help: guide-battle-setup -->

1. Open the lord's sheet (a neighbour of the lord kind) and go to its **Fief** tab, or open **Conflicts**.
<!-- tui -->
2. In [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), press `a`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
2. Under [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), click **Add**.
<!-- /gui -->
3. Name the battle and choose its two armies: each a lord, town or other power of the Region, a covenant, or just a name. Added from a lord's Fief tab, the first army is his. To pit two lords against each other, choose both.
4. Leave an army's **combat groups** blank and a lord's title fills them in (the groups of five his knights, serjeants, infantry and levies make, the levies only when he fights on his home ground, which the form asks); then add his allies (a Presence + Leadership + Affinity roll: 6 adds half his forces, 12 as many again, 18 double, 24 treble) and the mercenaries he hires. Type a covenant's or a named army's groups.
5. Each commander rolls Intelligence + Area Lore + a stress die for a **Deployment Total**; type both. The difference is the first army's **Ground** in every event, and the second's as much below 0.
6. Leave **baggage groups** blank for as many as each army needs (one for every ten combat groups), or type fewer. Save.

### Each event
<!-- help: guide-battle-events -->

<!-- tui -->
1. With the battle chosen, press `m` and choose **Battle Event**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Choose the battle and click **Battle Event**.
<!-- /gui -->
2. Choose the character at the heart of the event, the army they fight for, and its Maneuver, Enemy and Size. The line above Record shows the Event Total and whether it reaches this stage's Ease Factor (9, then 12, then 15). A total too low can't win, so pick a smaller task or give up the initiative for +1.
3. Play the event out as an ordinary fight with the character and the enemies it names.
4. Back in the form, say who won, type the losing commander's Morale roll (and, if it failed, his rally at the next event), and click **Record**. The losing side loses combat groups, and the battle's line shows the new numbers for the next event.

Most battles take three events. When one side has won enough of them, or its commander fails a Morale roll and then his rally, the battle is over. Each event is already written in each army's lord's or covenant's Diary; add a line there saying how the battle ended. See [Battle Event](help:battle-event).

**See also:** [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), [A lord](help:tab-nb-fief)

# How to run a siege
<!-- help: guide-sieges -->

1. Add a siege to the Sieges list in Conflicts, or on the besieging lord's Fief tab. Give the castle's Size, Garrison, Defenses and Supplies (the form's hints give each step's interval and bonus), the besiegers' miners, engines and soldiers, where each side stands at the four walls, and the experts' totals. For one lord besieging another, link **The castle in the Region** to the lord holding it and **The besieging lord** to the attacker: leave the Garrison and the soldiers blank and their armies fill them in, and the siege shows on both lords' Fief tabs.
2. Play it with **Next Siege Event**. Each time, choose the besiegers' move (or the garrison's sally), and for an escalade or a sally play the battlefield event out and say who won.
3. The siege moves on by the castle's interval each time, and any supplies roll that comes due is made for you, each an interval after the last. Each line of the chronicle gives the day of the siege it came on; the siege's page shows every event, and the page scrolls.
4. When the castle surrenders or is breached, the siege says so. If the besiegers give up, edit it and set Ended to Raised.

### What the app works out
<!-- help: guide-hedge-magic-app -->

Each Art's score comes from its XP on the table its book gives it: most hedge Arts and the vitkir's runes advance on the Art table, as Hermetic Arts do, and a Difficult Art (Elementalist Fire, the gruagach's Give and Take, the learned magicians' Techniques) on the Ability table. The [chapter](help:tradition-chapter) works out the rest: each power's Casting Score and Lab Total, the tradition's Magical Defenses, its gatherings' bonuses (a folk witch's sabbat) and the guidelines for its effects; More's [Cast a Power](help:hedge-cast) rolls a casting there and then. At creation, Tradition Training spends the apprenticeship the book gives, and the ten years of it that a folk witch spends from about age ten are left out of Later Life, as a magus's are (Hedge Magic RE p. 34). Sorginak and bouda train as folk witches do (Faith and Flame pp. 93-94; Between Sand and Sea p. 119), and settuten for ten years from 7 to 12, which the app takes as 10 (Between Sand and Sea pp. 95-96). The Bedouin hakima's book gives no years, so none are left out.

<!-- help: guide-galdor -->

Iceland's two magus-equivalents are hedge wizards here. They are Gifted companions, the equal of a magus at the table (Hedge Magic RE p. 11), with no Magic Resistance and no Twilight. Both come with *Land of Fire and Ice* in play.

2. **Go to a lord** with [Bargain with a Lord](help:galdor-bargain) for anything longer. The deal stays in Bound Beings, with the vis it tithes a year.

**Other Icelanders.** A schooled galdraman is a Learned Magician and a seithkona a Folk Witch with Hamfarir. A bondi, a landowner or a godi takes that Social Status, which sits beside Galdraman if he is both.

<!-- help: guide-tribunal-opening -->

<!-- help: guide-tribunal-hearing -->

# How to format your notes
<!-- help: guide-formatting -->

The longer text you write yourself, such as a Biography, a covenant's Description, a record's Secrets or a book's Description, can carry a little formatting. A field that takes it says so in the hint under its box. You type the marks into the box as plain text, and they turn into formatting wherever the app shows what you wrote.

### The marks
<!-- help: guide-formatting-marks -->

| Type this | To show |
| --- | --- |
| `**words**` | **bold** |
| `*words*` | *italic* |
| `***words***` | bold and italic together |
| `~~words~~` | ~~struck through~~ |
| `- ` at the start of a line (or `* `, `+ `) | a bulleted item |
| `1. ` at the start of a line (or `1) `) | a numbered item, with your own number |
| two spaces before the `- ` or `1. ` | an item nested under the one above |
| a blank line | a new paragraph |

A mark needs words right beside it: `5 * 3` stays as written, and so does a mark left unclosed. To show a mark itself, put a backslash before it: `\*` shows `*`. Emphasis stops at a blank line and at the next list item, so a stray mark can't run on through the rest of the text.

### An example
<!-- help: guide-formatting-example -->

Typed into a covenant's Secrets:

```
Hidden Resources (not yet found):

- **Matthaios's sealed study**, in the Echoing Halls.
- **Nikephoros's vis cache**, under the Watchtower: ~~60~~ 40 pawns.
```

The sheet shows the first line as written, then two bulleted items, each opening with its name in bold and the 60 struck through. A long item wraps with its lines starting under its words, not under the bullet.

### What it doesn't do
<!-- help: guide-formatting-limits -->

There are no headings, tables or links: the sheets give each piece of text its own heading already. Fields that are read one entry a line, such as a case's Evidence or a random table's Rows, take no formatting, since the app reads their lines itself. The box you type in shows the marks, not the formatting; save to see it.

# How to install a magic item in a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item -->

An enchanted device kept in a laboratory can improve the laboratory itself (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, and Covenants, Chapter 9: Magic Items for Laboratories). This guide covers the two kinds of benefit, installing an item in play and while a laboratory is being set up, and when an item is a Feature instead.

### Two kinds of benefit
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-kinds -->

The item is installed as a **Magic Item** Virtue, which is Free (Minor, and so takes a point of space, when the item is very large), or as an **Enchantment** of the laboratory itself, also Free. One Virtue covers all of an item's effects. Each effect then gives one of two things:

- **A Virtue or Flaw it duplicates.** An effect that does what a laboratory Virtue or Flaw does (a cauldron that always stays at the right heat, say) simply grants it. The laboratory gains it with no space cost, and so no Build Points at creation, and its modifications apply as usual.
- **Points bought by its level.** Otherwise the effect's final level buys one Specialization point for every 10 levels, or one Characteristic point for every 20: a level 20 lectern's effect could give Texts +2, or Safety +1. These points need no Virtue at all beyond the Free Magic Item.

An effect must be new to the laboratory: one that grants a Virtue the laboratory has already is flagged. A benefit counts only while its device is kept in the laboratory: move the device elsewhere and its points and the Virtue it grants stop counting, and its row says so, until the device comes back.

### Install one in play
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-play -->

<!-- tui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: press `5` for the laboratory's Inventory tab and `a` in [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) to add it with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Press `4` for the [Attributes tab](help:tab-lab-attributes) and `a` in [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then press Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: choose **Stores** in the laboratory's rail and add it under [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Choose **Specializations** in the rail and click **Add** under [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then click Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /gui -->

The Virtues & Flaws list shows a granted Virtue as "from" its item, and the Magic Item Virtue with its item's name. The points join the laboratory's Specializations and Characteristics, each named "(magic item)" where its sources are listed.

### Install one while setting up a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-creation -->

On a new laboratory's **Setting Up** step, **Install a magic item** offers **A new enchanted device**, or a device already kept in the laboratory. A new device is made in three forms: the device (kept in this laboratory), its effect, then what the effect gives, as above. The device and its effect are kept as each is saved, so cancelling later leaves them in the laboratory's Enchanted Devices, ready to install another time. The step lists what each magic item gives.

For a starting laboratory, the covenant's Build Points pay for the device as for any enchanted item (2 for every 5 levels of each effect), on the covenant's Resources step under Enchanted items, and its effects count against the Power Level's highest effect level. A Virtue the item grants costs no Build Points.

### When the item is a Feature
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-feature -->

An item can instead be made a Feature: a Lesser or Greater Feature, with the Aesthetics and the Specializations a Feature brings. Then it is not installed here: add it under [Features](help:section-lab-features), and its Feature Virtue takes space and costs Build Points as usual. The usual reason to choose a Feature is for what a Feature gives that points bought by level don't, such as Aesthetics or a Focus.

### What the app does for you
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-app -->

**The app works out:** the effect's final level, how many points it can buy, the space and Build Points each Virtue takes (none for a granted one, a Minor Virtue's for a very large item), the Specializations and Characteristics with the item's points added, and the Build Points of a starting laboratory's devices.

**You type:** the device and its effects, how the item is installed, and what each effect gives.

When the device or one of its effects is deleted or destroyed, what it gave goes with it, and the Magic Item Virtue once no other effect uses it.

# How to run Mythic Locations' set pieces
<!-- help: guide-mythic-locations -->

Mythic Locations gives four set pieces with rules of their own. The app holds the Temple of Mercury's rituals as catalog spells with their rules; the other three are run at the table, and this page sums up their rules.

### The Mercurian rituals
<!-- help: guide-ml-mercurian -->

The Temple of Mercury's library holds Lab Texts for nine rituals (Mythic Locations pp. 96-100). They are in the spell catalog with source ML, and a ritual's details panel says how it is learned.

- **Reading** one for its gist takes a day and needs Latin and a Magic Theory score. It gives the Arts, the magnitude and roughly what the ritual does, but can't be cast or invented from (p. 97).
- **Translating** it into a Hermetic Lab Text is lab work. Each season adds the Lab Total in the ritual's Arts less its level; at twice the level the copy is done. That means a Lab Total above the level, and three times the level for a single season. Each ritual already translated adds its highest magnitude to later translations, but not to invention. Experimenting is allowed (p. 97).
- **Inventing** it from the Hermetic copy is the usual Lab Text rule: one season when the Lab Total is at least the level. Range, Duration and Target can't be changed. Any magus with the copy may invent it (p. 97).
- **Casting** is as a Hermetic Ritual of its Arts. It can be dispelled like one, but anyone with Magic Theory sees it isn't Hermetic (p. 97).

Most rituals need a temple consecrated to Mercury, made by Consecration of Mercury's House, which also gives the temple a Magic aura of 1 where it had none (p. 99). Purification takes off a Decrepitude score, five years of apparent age and up to five Aging Points; it can't be cast on oneself (pp. 97-98). The player chooses which Characteristics the Aging Points come off, as the Definitive Edition also leaves to the player (DE pp. 391-392); the app takes them off those with the most first unless another choice is given. Transformation of Flesh to Spirit raises the aura by one, to at most 10, only if none of the caster's Arts is under twice the aura and the Penetration is above five times it (p. 99). Wizard's Initiation never works, and adds a botch die (pp. 99, 101).

Five integrations open from these rituals, offered as research goals: a CrVi 50 aura-making guideline, a CrVi 55 aura-raising one and a Cr(Re)Vi 20 regio entrance (30 points each), the Mercurian Magic Virtue (45), and a mended Wizard's Initiation that gives The Gift (60) (pp. 100-101). Their kinds follow the Definitive Edition's sizes for a breakthrough: about 30 points for a Minor, 45 for a Major and 60 for a Hermetic (DE p. 277; Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 28).

The aqueduct that fills the Scrying Pool takes 20 man-years to repair and five workers every season to keep running. Each season without them, roll a simple die against 3 + the seasons neglected in a row; under it, the aqueduct clogs. Its spring gives 5 pawns of Aquam vis each Summer solstice (pp. 96-97).

### Trekking in the Cursewood
<!-- help: guide-ml-cursewood -->

The Cursewood is crossed in five stages, each costing a Long-Term Fatigue level (Mythic Locations pp. 37-40). Choose a leader and a guide for each stage. Together they can lead as many people as their Leadership scores added together; each person over that adds a hazard.

Each stage, the guide rolls for the terrain: forest trail Sta + Survival, forest floor Per + Survival, swamp wading Sta + Athletics, boat Dex + Profession: Boating, swimming Str + Swimming, hill path Dex + Athletics, hill climb Str + Athletics. The roll takes -1 for each member besides the leader and guide, +1 for each other member who has the Ability, less the heaviest Encumbrance, and less the guide's Wound and Fatigue penalties (p. 37).

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 0 or botch | Lost: no progress, four hazards |
| 3 | Four hazards |
| 6 | Three hazards |
| 9 | Two hazards |
| 12 | One hazard |
| 18+ | The first time, one hazard and +3 next stage; later, no hazards |

A stage takes an hour for each hazard, at least one. Retreating takes an hour a stage, without rolls. Short-Term Fatigue takes twice as long to recover here. A hazard during the night means no Fatigue is recovered. Eating or drinking anything from the wood gives a Light Wound (p. 38).

The wood is an Infernal aura of 5: -5 to Recovery rolls, +5 to Damage, and five extra botch dice on physical rolls, the trek rolls included (p. 40). Its spirit's direct attacks must Penetrate, with +7 from his Magic regio, against Magic Resistance at -5 (p. 39). Forest Lore 3 or more tells a character more about the spirit, and from 7 lets them pass alone unhindered (p. 37).

### The Faerie Toy Market
<!-- help: guide-ml-toy-market -->

The merchants of Devil's Lane sell faerie toys made like Hermetic items (Mythic Locations pp. 107-112).

- **Prices** are in vis, of any Form and any realm's taint. The lowest price is the pawns the item would take to make, +1, and +1 more for an effect of the sixth magnitude or higher. A charged item costs at least a pawn a charge, a mundane item 1 pawn. Merchants open at twice that and haggle down. They ask for odd payments that hold the right vis, and refuse one whose vis was taken out first (p. 107).
- **Commissions** cost twice the vis, in Arts that suit the item, paid in advance. The opening price is four times the vis (p. 107).
- **Lifetime:** every toy is glamour and stops working seven years after purchase, charges included (p. 107).
- **The workshops** do a Lab Total of 100 of work a season for each merchant. A character can negotiate up to that much for themselves; unused work doesn't carry over, and characters can't pool theirs. The elves can make any Hermetic item, faerie Ranges, Durations and Targets included, but not a talisman (p. 112).
- **Walter the book merchant** sells glamour books that last seven years: 1 pawn for a tractatus, 1 a level for a summa. A season with one is practice at Quality 5. His exercise slates last a year and a day, for 1 pawn (p. 109).

### Hunted by wolves
<!-- help: guide-ml-wolves -->

A wolf hunt has five phases (Mythic Locations pp. 139-142).

1. **The stalk.** The pack finds the trail with Per + Awareness and follows it with Per + Hunt, usually against 9. A group is 3 easier to follow; a lone, careful quarry adds its Stealth. Then the pack rolls Dex + Stealth against the prey's Per + Awareness. Each success brings it 10 + Qik paces closer. Two failures in a row, three in all, or getting within ten paces moves the hunt to the encounter (p. 140).
2. **The encounter.** Prey that holds its ground is attacked; prey that runs starts the rush (p. 140).
3. **The rush.** Rounds of six seconds. Each side rolls Qik + Athletics - Encumbrance + stress die. The winner moves the gap by 10 + Qik - Encumbrance paces on four legs, or 6 + Qik - Encumbrance on two. Both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 9 each round, or lose a Short-Term Fatigue level. At zero paces comes the kill. Wolves usually turn to the chase when they lose a Fatigue level, or once the gap passes 100 paces (p. 140).
4. **The chase.** Rounds of five to thirty minutes. Each round both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 12, or lose a Long-Term Fatigue level. A group rolls for its most tired member, but wolves for their freshest. The wolves' Pursuit Total is Per + Hunt - Fatigue + modifier + stress die: +3 against two-legged prey, -3 for two-legged hunters of four-legged prey. The prey's Evasion Total is Dex + Athletics - Fatigue + stress die, with -6 to +6 for who knows the ground. Four rounds won by the prey and it escapes; four won by the wolves and it is cornered (p. 141).
5. **The kill.** The pack attacks as a trained group led by its leader, and the rest cut off escape. A wolf may try a trip: Brawl Attack against Defense. A fleeing or unaware target defends only with Brawl or Evasion. Beating it by 3 or more knocks the target down, and its Defense is Qik - Encumbrance + stress die until it stands (pp. 141-142).

# How to follow a staged Hermetic Project
<!-- help: guide-staged-project -->

A Burning City, an Intangible Assassin or a Living Corpse is followed stage by stage (Hermetic Projects pp. 9-118).

1. **Begin it.** Begin Project in Undertakings on the covenant's Seasons chapter (or Add on its Projects tab), and choose the kind. It starts with its chapter's stages.
2. **Show it.** With more than one project, choose it on the projects list and use Show Project: the tab then shows its facts and rules, its stages and their works.
3. **Spend seasons on a stage.** Plan a magus's lab work as usual (inventing a spell, enchanting a device) and choose the stage under **Spent on a project's stage**. When the season is played, it is kept with that stage's works. Work done at the table is Added on the works list.
4. **Use the chapter's own rules.** A Burning City's hazards are on Take Harm, and More's Warning Bell rolls the bell. An Intangible Assassin's tunnels are Open a Tunnel's; More's Gather a Connection and Lower an Aegis keep a connection and face the Aegis. A Living Corpse's More's Death Roll is the magus's death.
5. **Tick a stage done** with Edit on the stages list. The facts show the stage now and the seasons spent.

# Which faith actions can he take?
<!-- help: guide-religion -->

A character's faith decides what the [Religion tab](help:tab-religion) offers him: the saints and the Church for a Christian, God's aid for a Jew or a Muslim, a yazata for a Mazdean. Each action is offered only to one the books allow.

<!-- tui -->
1. **Set his religion.** Open his Religion tab, focus Faith and press `e` (or set Religion on Biographical Detail). Blank counts as Christian. The tab is there while Realms of Power: The Divine, The Church or another book that gives the faiths their rites is in play (Saga Content).
2. **See what he may do.** With focus in Faith, press `m` (More). Only the actions his faith offers him, and that he qualifies for, are listed.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Set his religion.** Open his Religion chapter (after Virtues & Flaws) and click Edit Religion beside Faith, or set Religion on Biographical Detail. Blank counts as Christian. The chapter is there while Realms of Power: The Divine, The Church or another book that gives the faiths their rites is in play (Saga Content).
2. **See what he may do.** The buttons beside Faith are the actions his faith offers him, and that he qualifies for.
<!-- /gui -->
3. **Anyone of his faith.** A Christian may Invoke a Saint (no Ability or Virtue needed, RoP: The Divine p. 87), choose his Patron Saint (anyone may have one), and with The Church in play take an Indulgence, Vigil or Fast (The Church pp. 11-13). Devotion Points are for one who holds a Devotion or has a positive Pious trait (or the Pious Flaw) to begin one (pp. 12-13). A Mazdean invokes a yazata the same way, with The Cradle and the Crescent.
4. **Only once something happens.** Threaten a Saint comes only after a saint has refused his invocation (RoP: The Divine p. 87): invoke first, and if the saint doesn't answer, Threaten appears with that saint.
5. **A Muslim's own.** Invoke Baraka seeks a holy forbear's blessing at his shrine (the Qur'an anywhere), and The Hajj gives Pious +3 and 3 Faith Points (RoP: The Divine pp. 103-108).
6. **Only with a Virtue or a trait.** Mystic Path needs a positive Pious trait and a tie to the Divine: True Faith, a Divine Supernatural Ability or Divine Warping (The Church p. 21). Invoke God's Aid needs one who may spend a Confidence Point: not a grog, nor Low Self-Esteem, unless he has True Faith (RoP: The Divine p. 41). Holy Influence needs spiritual authority: clergy, a monk, a rabbi, True Faith, Temporal Influence, Inspirational or Dominion Lore (pp. 38, 125).
7. **The Church's sections.** With The Church in play a Christian has Petitions and the Canon Court; Add on each makes one (anyone may petition the Church, and the canon courts hear laymen and clergy alike, pp. 36, 46). Holy Orders and Ordinations are there for one with a clerical Social Status (Clerk, Priest, Religious, Senior Clergy, Mendicant Friar) or a grade on record, and Perform an Exorcism for a clergyman only (p. 34). Only a Christian may plan an Ordination season, which is how a layman begins.
8. **A pagan.** The books give no rite of his own: his faith is in a magic spirit (a Spirit Votary, DE p. 58) or in faerie (Realms of Power: Faerie p. 134), dealt with by the faerie actions in his Deeds chapter, under Dealings with Faerie.

**See also:** [Devotion and the Saints](help:devotion), [Mystic Paths](help:mystic-path), [Invoking God's Aid](help:gods-aid), [Holy Influence](help:holy-influence)

# How to set up a threat's clock
<!-- help: guide-threat-clock -->

A threat can move on whenever the troupe fails something the saga asks
of it, as a clock fills in a Powered by the Apocalypse game: the
Watchtower's due to Abnoba each Winter, unpaid, brings the Hollow Crown
a step nearer.

1. **Make the threat move along a track.** New Threat (or Edit) with
   **Moves by** set to *A track* (or *An omen count*), and its position
   where it stands now (0 for a fresh one).
2. **Give its stages their steps.** Add each stage with **Comes at**:
   the step it comes at (2, 4, 6). The last stage's step is the clock's
   size: the threat's page shows ■■□□□□ 2 of 6.
3. **Tie a place's trigger to it.** On the place's Regio tab, add (or
   edit) a dated trigger, such as *Abnoba's due*, Winter, each year.
   Choose the threat in **Moves a threat**, and how many steps failing
   it moves the threat.
4. **Play the seasons.** When the trigger's season is played, Advance
   Season asks: **Failed** moves the threat on, **Kept** leaves it.
   Either is written in the threat's Diary and the place's, and the
   stage it reaches comes.
5. **Push it back when the troupe acts.** If the troupe thwarts it some
   other way, choose **Delay or Hasten** in the threat's More and move it
   back, with the reason.

A threat moving by fixed dates has a clock too (a square for each
stage), and Delay or Hasten moves all its stages still to come by the
seasons you give.

**See also:** [A threat](help:tab-nb-threat), [Triggers](help:section-nb-triggers), [The World Turns](help:world-turns), [Delay or Hasten](help:threat-delay)

# How to follow a story from its start to a threat
<!-- help: guide-story-hook -->

A season's work can start a story that comes later: a salamander drawn
by Aurelia's fire spell hangs about her laboratory, and may want to
become her familiar (a story event may come some time after the
experiment, DE p. 276). The app keeps each one as an open Story Event until it is
told, or grows into a threat.

1. **Where they come from.** An experiment's story event writes one in
   the covenant's Diary, concerning the magus, and a Disaster's
   covenant story one concerning the covenant (DE p. 275); Suggest a
   Story on a covenant's Hooks writes one when accepted. Add your own
   in the covenant's Diary: a Story Event, Open, with what it
   **Concerns**.
2. **Find them.** The Region's [Open Stories](help:section-region-stories)
   lists every open one of the troupe's covenants; a character's or
   laboratory's Connections lists its own.
3. **Tell it.** When the troupe plays it out, Edit it and mark it
   **Resolved**.
4. **Or let it grow.** Left alone, it can become a threat of the Region:
   **Grow into a Threat** makes one from its words, and marks the story
   Grown. Add the threat's stages, and give it a clock if a place's
   trigger should move it (see
   [How to set up a threat's clock](help:guide-threat-clock)).

**See also:** [Diary tab](help:tab-cov-diary), [Grow into a Threat](help:grow-story), [A threat](help:tab-nb-threat)

# How to build a tomb
<!-- help: guide-tomb -->

Your troupe wants a royal tomb of your own to explore. Any place can be one,
with *Lands of the Nile* in play.

1. **Make the place.** In The Region, add a Place and tick its **Tomb** box.
   A Tomb tab appears on it.
2. **Say whose it is.** Edit the [Tomb tab](help:tab-nb-tomb)'s block: its kind
   gives the base aura and Build Points, and the centuries of burials and any
   decay change the aura. Add Boons, and up to 10 points of Hooks.
3. **Guard it.** Add curses, talismans and wards, and traps, each to its own
   list. Each form shows the Build Points it costs, and the tab shows what is
   left to spend.
4. **Run it.** With a trap chosen, More's **Overcome It** walks through springing
   it: who is in danger, a look for it by the light there, each character's action,
   and the trap last. Talismans and wards work the same way.

A tomb stays as you built it. Only the rolls change each time. Puzzles are
written in the place's Description or Secrets: the book's minigame isn't a rule.

# How to keep a covenant on the move
<!-- help: guide-mobile-covenant -->

Crisp Winds lives on a flotilla that follows a route round the Mediterranean,
harvesting vis at auspicious times. It has the Constantly Mobile Hook.

1. **Mark the water.** The book packs already mark their ports, anchorages and
   seas. For anywhere else, add the sea and the harbours it uses as Places in
   The Region: an **Open water** Place with no aura, and **Port** or
   **Anchorage** in the **Waters** field of the rest. A town isn't a Place, so
   give a harbour town a Place of its own, with the town's aura: Divine 3 for a
   town or city, 2 for a village.
2. **Log where it is.** Add a Stop at the Place it is at now. Its aura is that
   Place's from now on, and [Whereabouts](help:section-cov-whereabouts) shows on
   its Overview.
3. **Plan the route.** Plan Route lists each leg and the seasons to stay. Tick
   **Circuit** if it goes round for ever.
4. **Bring its own aura, if it has one.** Edit the Place that holds that aura or
   regio and set **Travels with**. Place the covenant there, and its laboratories
   in the regio's level. Aura Boons act on the covenant's own Place, so they
   need that travelling Place: without it, they do nothing away from home. Name
   what carries the aura: a ship's timbers, a magestone, a spirit. The books'
   travelling auras are small and strongly anchored, as Favonius's ships and
   Delos are, so give anything above 3 or 4 a strong anchor of that kind.
5. **Advance the season.** Advance Season asks: move on, stay, or go somewhere
   else. Whatever you answer is logged as a stop.
6. **Mind the Aegis.** A move leaves the Aegis behind, and a fleet can hold one
   only at a Port. Cast it there, and it lasts as long as the fleet stays.
7. **Send a ship away.** To part the fleet, set the ship's **Laboratory aboard**,
   then choose her on Inventory (Possessions in the terminal) and **Detach**. **Rejoin** brings her back.

**See also:** [Whereabouts](help:section-cov-whereabouts), [Ships](help:section-pos-ship)

# How to stay at another covenant
<!-- help: guide-guest-stay -->

Flavia of Fengheld spends three years at a Rhine covenant to read its
library: she pays three pawns of vis a year, studies its summae, and the
storyguide asks the fee each year.

1. **Make her a guest.** On Flavia's Casting Scores, choose **Stay as
   Guest** (More; on the desktop, under Current Location). Choose the
   host in **Staying at**: a Rhine covenant fills in three years and
   three pawns of vis a year (Guardians of the Forests p. 20). Elsewhere,
   type the terms the troupe agrees. Tick **May study and copy from its
   library**.
2. **Cast and study there.** Her Casting Scores now count the host's
   aura. In her season's plan, the host's books are offered beside her
   own, to study or copy.
3. **Pay the fee.** On the host's Inhabitants (a sketch covenant's
   Overview), [Guests](help:section-cov-guests) shows her fee as *Due*.
   Choose her and **Pay the Fee**: the vis goes from her to the
   covenant, and both Diaries say so.
4. **Bring a gift.** Give the host something with [Give](help:give):
   courtesy asks it, and House Jerbiton's code counts it (Houses of
   Hermes: Societates p. 51).
5. **Go home.** When the stay ends, open Stay as Guest again and choose
   **Not a guest anywhere**.

**See also:** [Stay as Guest](help:guest-stay), [Current Location](help:whereabouts)

# How to draw up and keep a charter
<!-- help: guide-charter -->

Four magi found Dammërstein in the Rhine and swear the Charter of Arae
Flaviae; years later one of them breaks it.

1. **Start from a template.** On the covenant's Charter tab, choose
   **Use a Template** (More on the charter) and the Charter of Arae
   Flaviae: its oath, its 25 clauses under the charter's sections and its
   seven-season probation are filled in with the covenant's and the
   Tribunal's names. Used unaltered, it counts as ratified (Covenants p.
   33).
2. **Swear it.** **Edit Charter** sets when it was sworn and its Official
   Seal. **Seal the Charter** ticks each founder, with the season and his
   standing; a newcomer sealed as a probationary member serves the
   charter's probation, and the charter tells you when it has run.
3. **Make it yours.** Amend clauses with Edit, or add your own. Any change
   ends the automatic ratification, so tick **Notarized by a Quaesitor**
   once one has seen it. To reuse it for another covenant, **Save as
   Template**, for any Tribunal or the Rhine's alone.
4. **Record a breach.** Choose the clause and **Break a Clause**: when,
   and how. Then **Censure** says what the covenant did: privileges
   withheld (not his laboratory, unless the charter says so), his
   standing lowered, or expulsion.
5. **Expel, and tidy up.** On expulsion the app asks whether to take him
   off the covenant's lists now. Say No to move his books and vis first;
   the charter reminds you, and Advance Season asks again each season.
6. **Take it to Tribunal.** A charter that binds under the Peripheral
   Code (notarized, or sworn in Provence) can be broken as a Low Crime:
   **Charge at Tribunal** puts the case on the Tribunal's docket.

**See also:** [The charter](help:section-cov-charter), [Clauses](help:section-cov-clauses)
