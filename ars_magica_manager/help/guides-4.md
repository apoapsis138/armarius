# How to bind and keep a familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars -->

This guide follows a familiar from the magus's first meeting with the creature to a bond strengthened with powers. Use it when a magus in your saga is ready to bind a familiar, or when you are adding a magus who already has one. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Familiars.

### Finding the familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars-finding -->

Finding a suitable creature and winning it over is a matter for your stories, not for the app. Play it out at the table; the app only comes in once the creature has agreed to the bond.

A magus can have one familiar at a time, so Bind Familiar is only offered while at least one magus in the saga has none. A Bjornaer, whose heartbeast takes its place, can't bind one (DE p. 234).

### Making the familiar's record
<!-- help: guide-familiars-record -->

<!-- tui -->
1. In the tree, select the magus's node (or the saga, a covenant or a Characters node).
2. Press `n` (New) and choose **Bind Familiar**.
3. In the Bind a New Familiar form, type the familiar's **Name** and choose its **Magus Partner**. When you started from the magus's own node, they are already chosen. Choose **Create**.
4. The familiar's sheet opens. In the tree, the magus's node now has the familiar beneath it, as "Familiar: " and its name; select it there to come back to the [Familiar sheet](help:familiar) later.

Making the record grants the bond at once: both the magus and the familiar gain the True Friend Virtue and a Loyal (Partner) +3 Personality Trait. You can see them on the magus's [Virtues & Flaws tab](help:tab-vf) (`3`) and the familiar's [Identity & Cords tab](help:tab-fam-identity) (`1`).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the magus's sheet and its **Description** chapter, and click the **Bind Familiar** card in the Familiar section. Or open the magus's laboratory, its **Seasons** chapter, and click **Bind Familiar** in the Planning section.
2. In the Bind a New Familiar form, type the familiar's **Name**; its **Magus Partner** is already chosen. Choose **Create**.
3. The familiar's page opens. The magus's Description chapter now shows the familiar as a card in its Familiar section; click it to come back to the [Familiar page](help:familiar) later.

Making the record grants the bond at once: both the magus and the familiar gain the True Friend Virtue and a Loyal (Partner) +3 Personality Trait. You can see them in the magus's [Virtues & Flaws chapter](help:tab-vf) and the familiar's [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-fam-vf) and [Identity & Cords](help:tab-fam-identity) chapters.
<!-- /gui -->

### Giving the bond powers
<!-- help: guide-familiars-powers -->

Bond powers are instilled much like an effect in an enchanted device, and can take several seasons.

<!-- tui -->
1. On the familiar's [Bond Powers tab](help:tab-fam-powers) (`5`), press `a`. Type the Power Name, Level, Technique, Form and the rest, and set **Instilling (blank = finished)** to 0. [Go there](go:tab-fam-powers)
2. In the laboratory's Planning tab, choose a season and press `e`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. In the familiar's [Bond Powers chapter](help:tab-fam-powers), click **Add**. Type the Power Name, Level, Technique, Form and the rest, and set **Instilling (blank = finished)** to 0. [Go there](go:tab-fam-powers)
2. In the laboratory's Seasons chapter, click a season in Planning, then **Plan**.
<!-- /gui -->
In **Lab work**, choose **Empower the familiar's bond**, then choose the power in **Bond power** and the vis Art.
3. Choose **Schedule**. The Lab Total must be above the power's level. Each season adds the Lab Total less the level in points, and the vis (a tenth of the level, rounded up) is paid in the first season. The app plans the following seasons the work is projected to need.

When the power's points reach its level, the power is finished. A power the familiar already had can be added with Instilling left blank.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-familiars-limits -->

The app works out the point cost of each cord, whether a binding or empowerment can be done, its Lab Total, its vis, and the progress of a power over several seasons. The Bronze Cord is taken off the magus's aging roll each Winter.

Most of the familiar's own figures are typed in: its Soak, Magic Resistance and Might, its wounds and penalties, and each bond power's figures. The Golden and Silver Cords are shown with what they do, but their bonuses are yours to apply in play, and the True Friend Virtue is recorded but has no effect the app applies.

Deleting the magus also deletes their familiar, and exporting the magus takes the familiar with them.
<!-- tui -->
To remove only the familiar, press `d` on it in the tree.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
To remove only the familiar, choose Delete in the **Manage** chapter of its page.
<!-- /gui -->
Then the bond is broken, and the magus loses True Friend, Loyal (Partner) and any planned familiar seasons.

# How to initiate a character into a Mystery
<!-- help: guide-initiation -->

This guide follows a Mystery Initiation from the Script to the new Virtue on the Initiate's sheet. Use it when a character joins a Mystery cult, takes the next step in their House's Mysteries, or initiates themselves into a Virtue. The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 10: Long-Term Events, Mystery Initiation; The Mysteries Revised Edition and Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults give the full treatment, and the Definitive Edition wins where they differ.

### Before the season
<!-- help: guide-initiation-before -->

An Initiation needs three things, settled at the table first:

- **A Mystagogue** who knows the cult's Lore (House Bjornaer Lore, Mithraic Lore and so on). A character can be their own Mystagogue: a self-initiation uses the Initiate's own scores.
<!-- tui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it on the [Abilities tab](help:tab-abilities) (`2`) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it is the Lore of their own House. Add it in the [Abilities chapter](help:tab-abilities) once they have learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /gui -->
- **A Script**, written like a Lab Text. Its bonus is the sum of its components, as the storyguide agrees them: +3 for a special place at a special time, +3 for each Quest, +1 for serving the cult (once), +1 for a sacrifice of wealth, +3 to +6 for a sacrifice of something of great value, and the Ordeal's +3 (a Minor Flaw, or losing a Minor Virtue), +6 (raising a Minor Flaw to Major) or +9 (a Major Flaw, or losing a Major Virtue). Quests and other sacrifices are played out in their own seasons or stories.

### Planning the Initiation
<!-- help: guide-initiation-plan -->

The Initiation is the Initiate's season, planned like any other.

<!-- tui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their Planning tab (`6` for a magus, `5` for anyone else), and press `e` in [This Season](help:section-char-season). A covenant's [Seasons tab](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the Initiate's sheet and their **Planning** chapter, and click **Plan** (or **Edit**, if a plan is there already) in [This Season](help:section-char-season). **Plan** in a covenant's [Seasons chapter](help:tab-cov-seasons) opens the same form.
<!-- /gui -->
2. In **Activity**, choose **Undergo an Initiation**.
3. In **Teacher / master**, choose the Mystagogue. Leave it blank for a self-initiation.
4. Type the **Script bonus** and the **Mystagogue's Lore**, spelt as it is in the Mystagogue's Abilities. To vary the Script, choose the change in **Vary the Script** (see below).
<!-- tui -->
5. Choose **Virtue initiated**. It opens the Virtues & Flaws picker on Virtues: search, filter by type or tier, and press Enter.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
5. Click **Virtue initiated**. It opens a list of Virtues: type in the search box to narrow it, and double-click the one you want (or click it and Choose).
<!-- /gui -->
A Virtue that names something, such as Affinity with an Art or Puissant with an Ability, asks for it.
6. If the Script has an Ordeal, choose what it costs in **Ordeal**: **Gains a Flaw** asks for the **Flaw gained** (the same picker, on Flaws); **Loses a Virtue** asks for the **Virtue lost**, one of the Initiate's own Minor or Major Virtues; **Raises a Minor Flaw to Major** asks for the **Minor Flaw raised**, one of their own, and the related Major Flaw it becomes as the **Flaw gained**. The Ordeal's size follows from what it costs: a Minor Flaw or Virtue makes a Minor Ordeal, a Major one a Major Ordeal, and a raised Flaw a Medium one. Leave Ordeal blank for none.
<!-- tui -->
Delete on a chosen field clears it.
7. Watch the line above Save.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
A chosen Virtue or Flaw field's **Clear** button clears it.
7. Watch the line under the fields.
<!-- /gui -->
It shows the Initiation Total (the Mystagogue's Presence + Lore + Script bonus) against the Target Level, and whether the Initiation will succeed. Save refuses a plan that is missing something or breaks a rule, and says why.

There is no die: the Initiation succeeds when the total reaches the Target Level. The Target is 15 for a Minor Virtue and 21 for a Major one, or 18 and 30 when the Mystagogue doesn't have the Virtue (always so for a self-initiation into a Virtue the character lacks).

### When the season is advanced
<!-- help: guide-initiation-advance -->

<!-- tui -->
Press `t` to [advance the season](help:advance-season).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Click **Advance Season** in the top bar to [advance the season](help:advance-season).
<!-- /gui -->
For a successful Initiation, the app adds the Virtue (with what it names) to the Initiate's [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf) and applies the Ordeal: it adds the Flaw gained, takes away the Virtue lost, or replaces the Minor Flaw raised with its Major Flaw. A Great Characteristic Virtue gained raises the score, and one lost lowers it again. A failed Initiation changes nothing, and its Ordeal is not suffered: the Mystagogue sees that a Script will fail before any sacrifice is made. Either way, the Diary records the Initiation Total against the Target.

### Past Ordeals make later Initiations easier
<!-- help: guide-initiation-ordeals -->

An Ordeal lowers the Target Level of the Initiate's next three Initiations, never below 9 (DE p. 388): by 3, 2 and 1 after a Minor Ordeal, 6, 4 and 2 after a Medium one, and 9, 6 and 3 after a Major one. It doesn't help the Initiation it was suffered in. When the Initiate has suffered several, only the one that lowers the Target most counts: a Major Ordeal followed by a Minor one still takes 6 off the next Initiation. The app keeps every Ordeal and counts each one down, and the Initiate's [This Season](help:section-char-season) section lists the past Ordeals still counting and what they take off the next Target. Flaws taken at character creation don't count as Ordeals.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-initiation-limits -->

**The app works out:** the Initiation Total and Target Level, whether the Mystagogue has the Virtue, the largest reduction among past Ordeals, the checks on the Lore and the Ordeal, the Ordeal's size, and the Virtues and Flaws added or taken away on success.

**Varying the Script:** the Mystagogue may make one change in an Initiation. Choose it in **Vary the Script** (add a component, Ease 9; change one, 12; remove one, 15) and type in **The change adds** what it does to the Script bonus (negative when removing). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore: success adds the change to the Script bonus, and failure fails the Initiation, with no Virtue and no Ordeal, though the Script as it was may be tried another season (DE pp. 388-389). The Mysteries Revised Edition (p. 17) says instead that the original Script still works; the Definitive Edition wins, so this isn't a bug. Removing a component (Ease 15) is The Mysteries Revised's own addition, and it doesn't let a Minor Virtue's Script become a Major one.

**Distractions:** **Months lost** on the plan takes a third of the Initiation Total a month, rounded up. If that makes the Initiation fail, the Ordeal is still suffered and the Initiation must be repeated (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12).

**You type:** the Script bonus, as the storyguide agrees it. Experimenting with a new Script is rolled at the table; type the Script bonus that results. Which Major Flaw a Minor Flaw is related to is the troupe's call: the app checks only that it is a Major Flaw.

A Mystagogue who spends a whole season on the Script (teaching a Supernatural Ability, say) plans **Act as Mystagogue**, and a season spent on a Quest is **Serve a Mystagogue / Quest season**; both need The Mysteries Revised Edition in play.

**See also:** [Plan Season](help:plan-season), [Virtues & Flaws tab](help:tab-vf), [Advancing a season](help:advance-season), [How the app handles advancement](help:guide-advancement)

# How to import and export
<!-- help: guide-import-export -->

This guide covers moving records between sagas, bringing books and spells in from spreadsheet (CSV) files, and keeping copies of your saga safe. Use it when a player brings a character from another saga, when you want to type a long list of books or spells in a spreadsheet, or before you try something you might want to undo.

### Exporting a covenant, laboratory or character
<!-- help: guide-import-export-export -->

<!-- tui -->
1. In the tree, select the covenant, laboratory or character you want to move. Leave focus on its tree node or on the panel at the top of its sheet.
2. Press `m` (More) and choose **Export**.
3. In the Export dialog, check the **File**. It starts in the app's exports folder (`exports`, in Armarius in your OneDrive folder) with a name made from the record's name; `.amm.json` is added if you give no `.json` ending. The line above Export warns when the file exists and will be replaced.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the page of the covenant, laboratory or character you want to move.
2. Open its **Manage** chapter and click **Export**.
3. In the Export form, check the **File**. It starts in the app's exports folder (`exports`, in Armarius in your OneDrive folder) with a name made from the record's name; `.amm.json` is added if you give no `.json` ending. The line under the fields warns when the file exists and will be replaced.
<!-- /gui -->
4. For a covenant, tick **With its laboratories and characters** to take them too.
5. Choose **Export**.

What goes with the record, and what stays behind, is listed under [Exporting](help:import-data-export). In short: a character takes their familiar, their enchanted devices, their books and their Diary; a laboratory takes its books and devices; and the saga's own spells and [House Rules](help:tab-content-house-rules) that these use go too. Season plans and lab work under way stay behind.

### Bringing a record's changes back from another computer
<!-- help: guide-import-export-update -->

1. Export the record where it was changed, and copy the file to this computer.
<!-- tui -->
2. Open its page here. Leave focus on its tree node or top panel and press `m` (More).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
2. Open its page here, and its **Manage** chapter.
<!-- /gui -->
3. Choose **Update from a File**, then the file.
4. Read what would change, then choose **Update**.

The record keeps its place in the saga; see [Updating a record from a file](help:import-data-update).

### Importing a record into another saga
<!-- help: guide-import-export-records -->

<!-- tui -->
1. Open the saga that should receive the record, and select [the saga](help:saga) at the top of the tree.
2. Press `m` (More) and choose **Import**. The [Import Data](help:import-data) dialog opens.
3. Choose **Characters**, **Covenants** or **Laboratories**, matching what the file holds.
4. Choose the file. The file picker starts in the exports folder; Enter opens a folder or picks a file, and you can also type a path in the box at the top.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the saga that should receive the record, on [its page](help:saga).
2. Open its **Saga Management** chapter and click **Import**. The [Import Data](help:import-data) list opens.
3. Choose **Characters**, **Covenants** or **Laboratories**, matching what the file holds.
4. Choose the file in the Open window, which starts in the exports folder.
<!-- /gui -->
5. Read the preview and choose **Import**.

The preview lists what will be added, the links kept and the links dropped. [Importing an exported record](help:import-data-records) explains the rules:

- Everything imported gets new ids, so nothing in this saga is overwritten. A record with the same name as one already here is added beside it, and the preview says so.
- A link to a record that isn't in the file, such as a character's covenant or a laboratory's owner, is kept only if this saga has exactly one record of that kind with that name. Otherwise it is dropped, and you can set it again on the sheet.
- A saga-made spell is matched to one here with the same name, Technique, Form and level. A House Rule is matched to one with the same name, type and tier. Anything not matched is added.

### When a name is already taken
<!-- help: guide-import-export-duplicates -->

When an imported book or spell has the name of one already in the saga, the app asks about each one: **Skip** leaves it out, **Duplicate** adds it anyway, and **Skip All** and **Duplicate All** answer the same for the rest of that import. Spell names are compared in any case. Nothing is added until every question is answered, and cancelling a question imports nothing.

### Keeping backups
<!-- help: guide-import-export-backups -->

Each saga is saved as one file, the saga's id followed by `.json`, in the `sagas` folder of Armarius in your OneDrive folder (see the README to keep it elsewhere). The app saves every change as you make it.

- Before a season with plans is applied, the app copies the saga to `sagas/backups`, keeping the last five (the newest has the saga's own file name; older ones end `.1.json` to `.4.json`). To undo that season, copy the backup over the saga's file with the app closed.
- That is the only backup the app keeps, so for anything older, copy the saga's file yourself from time to time.
- To keep a single character, laboratory or covenant safe before a big change, export it as above.

# How to host a saga for your troupe
<!-- help: guide-hosting -->

The storyguide can let the players open the saga in a web browser on their own computers, while it stays on the storyguide's. The app and the saga file never leave your computer; each player who opens the page gets their own copy of the app, running on your machine.

You need a private network that you and the players share. The app doesn't come with one: use your home network, **Tailscale** or **ZeroTier** (recipes below). Never host on an address the whole internet can reach.

<!-- gui -->
What the players get in their browser (or over SSH) is the terminal version of the app, so their screens look like it rather than like this window. You keep using the desktop window while you host: it and the players' copies all save into the same saga file, each change merged with the others.
<!-- /gui -->

### Before the first session
<!-- help: guide-hosting-setup -->

<!-- tui -->
1. Open the saga and select **Saga Content**, then its [Players tab](help:tab-content-players). Add each player with `a`:
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Open the saga's **Saga Content** (in its Rules and Content chapter), then its [Players chapter](help:tab-content-players). Add each player with **Add**:
<!-- /gui -->
a name, the Role *Player*, and a passcode you give them yourself. Add a *Storyguide* there too if another storyguide will sign in from a browser.
<!-- tui -->
2. For each player's characters, open the character, edit its Biographical Detail and choose who it's **Played by**. Tick **May change covenants** on a player who should keep the covenant's books.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->

### Tailscale
<!-- help: guide-hosting-tailscale -->

Everyone, you and each player, installs Tailscale and signs in with an existing Google, Microsoft, GitHub or Apple account.

1. On your computer, Tailscale shows its address (it starts with `100.`). That is the ADDRESS to host on.

### ZeroTier
<!-- help: guide-hosting-zerotier -->

Only you need a ZeroTier account; players just install the ZeroTier app.

1. On ZeroTier's website, create a network and copy its 16-character Network ID. Keep it Private.
2. On your computer, join that network from the ZeroTier icon, then on the website tick **Auth** for your computer and note its Managed IP: that is the ADDRESS to host on.
3. Windows often marks the ZeroTier connection as a Public network; set it to Private in Windows' network settings, or the firewall keeps players out.
4. Each player installs ZeroTier, joins with the Network ID, and you tick **Auth** for their device.
5. On a ZeroTier network every device can reach every other one. To let players reach only the app on your computer, set the network's Flow Rules along these lines (with your address), then check a player can still connect:

```
accept ethertype arp;
accept ipdest ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and dport 8000;
accept ipsrc ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and sport 8000;
drop;
```

### Over SSH instead of a browser
<!-- help: guide-hosting-ssh -->

A player who prefers a terminal can connect with SSH instead. Tick **Also over SSH** when you start hosting (the SSH port is 2222 unless you change it; from a terminal, add `--ssh-port 2222`); the browser page keeps working alongside. The SSH server is the app's own: no Windows accounts are involved, and any user name is accepted, because who the player is comes from the same sign-in as in a browser. The first time, the app makes a host key (`ssh_host_key` in your data folder); players' SSH clients ask once whether to trust it.

Players connect with `ssh -p 2222 player@ADDRESS` from Windows Terminal (Windows 10 and 11 include `ssh`), macOS Terminal or any Linux terminal, and get the same saga list and sign-in. Their terminal should be at least 120 columns by 45 rows and support true colour and Unicode: Windows Terminal, iTerm2, macOS Terminal and the usual Linux terminals do; the old Windows console does not. Over SSH, Export isn't offered, since the file could only land on your computer.

### What players see
<!-- help: guide-hosting-players -->

- The app fills the browser window. It is laid out for at least 120 columns by 45 rows: on a 1080p screen, add `?fontsize=12` to the address (`http://ADDRESS:8000/?fontsize=12`) or zoom the browser out.

- A player changes only the characters they play and what belongs to them; everything else they can look at and Export, which downloads in their browser. See [Players](help:tab-content-players).
- Everyone's changes reach the others within a few seconds. See [Signing in](help:sign-in).

The few that belong to part of a record (a regio level, a case, an entry in the Tribunal's Diary, a book) are at the end of its text under a **Secrets** heading.

# How to fight a battle
<!-- help: guide-battle -->

Lords of Men plays a battle as a few events, each a fight with a player character at its heart, rather than every blow. The app keeps the battle in the saga's Conflicts, between two armies, and shows it on the Fief tab of each lord it names; it works out each event's total.

### Before the battle
<!-- help: guide-battle-setup -->

1. Open the lord's sheet (a neighbour of the lord kind) and go to its **Fief** tab, or open **Conflicts**.
<!-- tui -->
2. In [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), press `a`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
2. Under [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), click **Add**.
<!-- /gui -->
3. Name the battle and choose its two armies: each a lord, town or other power of the Region, a covenant, or just a name. Added from a lord's Fief tab, the first army is his. To pit two lords against each other, choose both.
4. Leave an army's **combat groups** blank and a lord's title fills them in (the groups of five his knights, serjeants, infantry and levies make); then add his allies (a Presence + Leadership + Affinity roll: 6 adds half his forces, 12 as many again, 18 double, 24 treble) and the mercenaries he hires. Type a covenant's or a named army's groups.
5. Each commander rolls Intelligence + Area Lore + a stress die for a **Deployment Total**; type both. The difference is the first army's **Ground** in every event, and the second's as much below 0.
6. Leave **baggage groups** blank for as many as each army needs (one for every ten combat groups), or type fewer. Save.

### Each event
<!-- help: guide-battle-events -->

<!-- tui -->
1. With the battle chosen, press `m` and choose **Battle Event**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. Choose the battle and click **Battle Event**.
<!-- /gui -->
2. Choose the character at the heart of the event, the army they fight for, and its Maneuver, Enemy and Size. The line above Record shows the Event Total and whether it reaches this stage's Ease Factor (9, then 12, then 15). A total too low can't win, so pick a smaller task or give up the initiative for +1.
3. Play the event out as an ordinary fight with the character and the enemies it names.
4. Back in the form, say who won and click **Record**. The losing side loses combat groups, and the battle's line shows the new numbers for the next event.

Most battles take three events. When one side has won enough of them, or its commander fails a Morale roll, the battle is over. Each event is already written in each army's lord's or covenant's Diary; add a line there saying how the battle ended. See [Battle Event](help:battle-event).

**See also:** [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), [A lord](help:tab-nb-fief)


# How to run a fight
<!-- help: guide-fights -->

<!-- tui -->
1. Select **Conflicts** in the tree. In the Fights list press `a`, name the fight and its two sides, and save.
2. With the fight chosen, press `m` and choose **Add Combatant** for each fighter: the player characters with their weapons, Bestiary creatures (as many as you like at once), or a typed stat block.
3. To make grogs fight together, press `m` > **Form a Group**, tick its members, and choose the vanguard (and, for a trained group, the leader).
4. Press `m` > **Next Blow**. The first one rolls Initiative. Choose an action and a target; **The Blow to Come** box under the fields says what it will roll. Then **Strike**: the panel shows the blow worked out and the chronicle. A magus can **Cast a spell** there, and anyone holding a device can **Use a device**.
5. **Next Round** plays the rest of a round for you. Keep going until one side has nobody standing.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. On the saga's page, open the **Conflicts** chapter and click **Add** on Fights. Name the fight and its two sides, and save; then open the fight.
2. Click **Add Combatant** for each fighter: the player characters with their weapons, Bestiary creatures (as many as you like at once), or a typed stat block.
3. To make grogs fight together, click **Form a Group**, tick its members, and choose the vanguard (and, for a trained group, the leader).
4. Click **Next Blow**. The first one rolls Initiative; the ribbon at the top then shows the order of blows, as medallions in the saga's set. Choose an action and a target; **The Blow to Come** at the foot of the form says what it will roll. Then **Strike**: The Blow shows the working, and the chronicle below grows a line. A magus can **Cast a spell** there, and anyone holding a device can **Use a device**.
5. **Next Round** plays the rest of a round for you. Keep going until one side has nobody standing.
<!-- /gui -->

For a knight on horseback, tick **Mounted** when you add him; for shield
grogs, set **Defends** to the magus on each grog. If the sides start apart,
set **Range increments** on Edit Fight: only missiles strike until you set

Wounds the characters take go straight onto their sheets, so after the

# How to run a siege
<!-- help: guide-sieges -->

1. Add a siege to the Sieges list in Conflicts, or on the besieging lord's Fief tab. Give the castle's Size, Garrison, Defenses and Supplies (the form's hints give each step's interval and bonus), the besiegers' miners, engines and soldiers, where each side stands at the four walls, and the experts' totals. For one lord besieging another, link **The castle in the Region** to the lord holding it and **The besieging lord** to the attacker: leave the Garrison and the soldiers blank and their armies fill them in, and the siege shows on both lords' Fief tabs.
2. Play it with **Next Siege Event**. Each time, choose the besiegers' move (or the garrison's sally), and for an escalade or a sally play the battlefield event out and say who won.
3. The siege moves on by the castle's interval each time, and any supplies rolls that come due are made for you. Each line of the chronicle gives the day of the siege it came on.
4. When the castle surrenders or is breached, the siege says so. If the besiegers give up, edit it and set Ended to Raised.

# How to hold a Tribunal
<!-- help: guide-tribunal -->

This guide walks one meeting from the roll call to the Closing: a Rhine Tribunal meeting in Summer 1228 with one charge on its docket, Fengheld v. Keep (Fengheld says Keep took the vis of the Rhine ford), and one motion. The rules are Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 2: The Order of Hermes, Tribunals, with Houses of Hermes: True Lineages (House Guernicus, Tribunal Procedures) for hearing a case and Houses of Hermes: Societates (House Tytalus, The Fine Arts of Debate) for the debate. [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal) has every step in full.

### Before the meeting
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-before -->

Put the cases on the Tribunal's [Docket](help:tab-trib-docket): who brings each, who it is against, its Evidence (one testimony a line) and, if you know them, its prosecutor and defender. Notice of a case is due a season before the meeting. Set the Tribunal's Praeco and Presiding Quaesitor on its sheet: neither votes at the meeting.

<!-- tui -->
Open the Tribunal's sheet and its [Meeting tab](help:tab-trib-meeting) (`4`). Before the meeting opens, the [Order of Business](help:section-meeting-business) lists the steps it would hold, day by day, and the top says "Not yet opened".
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Open the Tribunal's page and its [Meeting](help:tab-trib-meeting) chapter. Before the meeting opens, the [Order of Business](help:section-meeting-business) lists the steps it would hold, day by day, and the top says "Not yet opened".
<!-- /gui -->

### Moving through the meeting
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-moving -->

<!-- tui -->
Press `e` on the Order of Business. Each step is a form that starts by saying where you are ("Step 3 of 9") and what comes next, then **Worked out for you** (what the app does at this step) and **What you enter changes**. Saving a step holds it and opens the next straight away, so you can go from the roll call to the Closing in one sitting. `Escape` (Cancel) stops; the meeting is saved after every step and waits at the step in hand, and `e` later carries on from there. At the top of the tab, **Now** names the step in hand and **Then** the one after it.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Click **Hold** under the Order of Business. Each step is a form that starts by saying where you are ("Step 3 of 9") and what comes next, then **Worked out for you** (what the app does at this step) and **What you enter changes**. Saving a step holds it and opens the next straight away, so you can go from the roll call to the Closing in one sitting. Cancel stops; the meeting is saved after every step and waits at the step in hand, and **Hold** later carries on from there. At the top of the chapter, **Now** names the step in hand and **Then** the one after it.
<!-- /gui -->

To change a step already held, choose it in the Order of Business and hold it again: it is held anew, and saving it goes on to the step in hand.

### The roll call
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-roll -->

The first step calls the roll, and saving it opens the meeting. Each covenant's magi are listed with **all present** ticked. Untick it for Keep and tick only the magi who came: Leandro stayed home. For a sketch covenant, say how many of its unnamed magi came. Then the proxies each covenant carries (Fengheld carries one sigil) and whether the Presiding Quaesitor allows them.

Worked out for you: the votes in the hall (one a magus present and one an allowed proxy, but none for the Praeco or the Presiding Quaesitor), and the quorum: twelve magi in person from four covenants, and a Quaesitor. The top of the tab shows **Present** ("4 votes, 1 by proxy") and **Quorum**. Only magi ticked present can speak at a hearing.

<!-- gui -->
On the desktop a card announces the meeting as it opens; close it, and the next step opens.
<!-- /gui -->

### Opening and the days
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-opening -->

Say where the meeting is held and how many days it lasts (3). Each case and motion is given a day, spread over every day but the last, which is kept for the rulings and the Closing; change a case's day to move its hearing and vote.

### Hearing a case
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-hearing -->

Fengheld v. Keep comes on its day. Choose the **Prosecutor** and **Defender** from the drop-downs: the magi present, each with their covenant. Leandro is absent, so the defence starts as **A Guernicus advocate**, who defends an accused who didn't come; a charge no one else will prosecute can be brought by **The Presiding Quaesitor**. A speaker with no debate scores written down (the advocate, or a sketch magus) gets **debates as**: Middling, Able or Formidable.

Leave **Levels lost** blank and the debate is rolled, round by round: the speakers' Communication and Intelligence with their best debating Abilities, the case's Evidence backing the prosecution and the **Defence testimony** the defence. Or play the debate out at the table and enter both sides' levels lost. Either way, the side that loses fewer wins, and the difference is its Argument Strength.

If both sides accept the Presiding Quaesitor's view at the private hearing, tick **Settled in private** instead: the case has no debate and no vote, and goes straight on to its ruling.

For the motion, the proposer speaks for it and **The Tribunal** against: a motion is debated against the whole hall.

### Calling the vote
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-vote -->

The vote follows its hearing. Each covenant is listed with its leaning and why: "the case is against it", "Warm (+1) toward Fengheld, which brings it". Change a leaning if the story says otherwise; the tally under the form follows as you do. The debate's victor wins a share of the undecided votes when the covenants are called. If the votes tie, choose how the Praeco casts.

<!-- gui -->
**Call the Vote** in The Hall shows the covenants called one by one, their seats filling.
<!-- /gui -->

### Rulings and their terms
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-rulings -->

On the last day, each case is ruled on. A ruling can be **Written out** (it starts as "Carried, 3 to 1") or made from a **ruling template**. Choose **Holds for a term** for Fengheld v. Keep, set any fine (3 pawns of Aquam, paid by Keep) and whether it goes into the Peripheral Code, and save: the template's blanks come next. Fill them in, and the sentence under the form shows the ruling as you go:

> Keep holds the Rhine ford's vis for 7 years, until it is debated again.

The {years} blank sets a term. With a written-out ruling, its **term** (in years) sets one the same way. The templates are the saga's own: add or change them on Edit Saga, **Ruling templates**, where any word in braces is a blank to fill.

### Closing, and what comes back
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-closing -->

The Treasury step shows the balance and the loans due. Closing then decides each case with a ruling (the fine owed to the treasury, the ruling into the Peripheral Code, a party's attitude moved, the term set), writes the Acta into the Tribunal's Diary and sets the next meeting. Each step held has already written its minutes there.

The term comes back on its own. [Ahead](help:section-ahead) lists the season before it ends (Spring 1235) and the season it ends (Summer 1235). Advancing into Spring 1235 says the term ends next season; advancing into Summer 1235 says it has ended and puts "Fengheld v. Keep (debated again)" on the Docket, with the old ruling in its charge, for the next meeting.

**See also:** [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal), [Meeting tab](help:tab-trib-meeting), [Docket](help:tab-trib-docket)

# How to format your notes
<!-- help: guide-formatting -->

The longer text you write yourself, such as a Biography, a covenant's Description, a record's Secrets or a book's Description, can carry a little formatting. A field that takes it says so in the hint under its box. You type the marks into the box as plain text, and they turn into formatting wherever the app shows what you wrote.

### The marks
<!-- help: guide-formatting-marks -->

| Type this | To show |
| --- | --- |
| `**words**` | **bold** |
| `*words*` | *italic* |
| `***words***` | bold and italic together |
| `~~words~~` | ~~struck through~~ |
| `- ` at the start of a line (or `* `, `+ `) | a bulleted item |
| `1. ` at the start of a line (or `1) `) | a numbered item, with your own number |
| two spaces before the `- ` or `1. ` | an item nested under the one above |
| a blank line | a new paragraph |

A mark needs words right beside it: `5 * 3` stays as written, and so does a mark left unclosed. To show a mark itself, put a backslash before it: `\*` shows `*`. Emphasis stops at a blank line and at the next list item, so a stray mark can't run on through the rest of the text.

### An example
<!-- help: guide-formatting-example -->

Typed into a covenant's Secrets:

```
Hidden Resources (not yet found):

- **Matthaios's sealed study**, in the Echoing Halls.
- **Nikephoros's vis cache**, under the Watchtower: ~~60~~ 40 pawns.
```

The sheet shows the first line as written, then two bulleted items, each opening with its name in bold and the 60 struck through. A long item wraps with its lines starting under its words, not under the bullet.

### What it doesn't do
<!-- help: guide-formatting-limits -->

There are no headings, tables or links: the sheets give each piece of text its own heading already. Fields that are read one entry a line, such as a case's Evidence or a random table's Rows, take no formatting, since the app reads their lines itself. The box you type in shows the marks, not the formatting; save to see it.

# How to install a magic item in a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item -->

An enchanted device kept in a laboratory can improve the laboratory itself (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, and Covenants, Chapter 9: Magic Items for Laboratories). This guide covers the two kinds of benefit, installing an item in play and while a laboratory is being set up, and when an item is a Feature instead.

### Two kinds of benefit
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-kinds -->

The item is installed as a **Magic Item** Virtue, which is Free (Minor, and so takes a point of space, when the item is very large), or as an **Enchantment** of the laboratory itself, also Free. One Virtue covers all of an item's effects. Each effect then gives one of two things:

- **A Virtue or Flaw it duplicates.** An effect that does what a laboratory Virtue or Flaw does (a cauldron that always stays at the right heat, say) simply grants it. The laboratory gains it with no space cost, and so no Build Points at creation, and its modifications apply as usual.
- **Points bought by its level.** Otherwise the effect's final level buys one Specialization point for every 10 levels, or one Characteristic point for every 20: a level 20 lectern's effect could give Texts +2, or Safety +1. These points need no Virtue at all beyond the Free Magic Item.

An effect must be new to the laboratory: one that grants a Virtue the laboratory has already is flagged. A benefit counts only while its device is kept in the laboratory: move the device elsewhere and its points and the Virtue it grants stop counting, and its row says so, until the device comes back.

### Install one in play
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-play -->

<!-- tui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: press `5` for the laboratory's Inventory tab and `a` in [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) to add it with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Press `4` for the [Attributes tab](help:tab-lab-attributes) and `a` in [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then press Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. The device must be kept in the laboratory: choose **Stores** in the laboratory's rail and add it under [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) with its effects, or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. Choose **Specializations** in the rail and click **Add** under [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then click Next.
4. For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog (Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered: those are Features). For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /gui -->

The Virtues & Flaws list shows a granted Virtue as "from" its item, and the Magic Item Virtue with its item's name. The points join the laboratory's Specializations and Characteristics, each named "(magic item)" where its sources are listed.

### Install one while setting up a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-creation -->

On a new laboratory's **Setting Up** step, **Install a magic item** offers **A new enchanted device**, or a device already kept in the laboratory. A new device is made in three forms: the device (kept in this laboratory), its effect, then what the effect gives, as above. The device and its effect are kept as each is saved, so cancelling later leaves them in the laboratory's Enchanted Devices, ready to install another time. The step lists what each magic item gives.

For a starting laboratory, the covenant's Build Points pay for the device as for any enchanted item (2 for every 5 levels of each effect), on the covenant's Resources step under Enchanted items, and its effects count against the Power Level's highest effect level. A Virtue the item grants costs no Build Points.

### When the item is a Feature
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-feature -->

An item can instead be made a Feature: a Lesser or Greater Feature, with the Aesthetics and the Specializations a Feature brings. Then it is not installed here: add it under [Features](help:section-lab-features), and its Feature Virtue takes space and costs Build Points as usual. The usual reason to choose a Feature is for what a Feature gives that points bought by level don't, such as Aesthetics or a Focus.

### What the app does for you
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-app -->

**The app works out:** the effect's final level, how many points it can buy, the space and Build Points each Virtue takes (none for a granted one, a Minor Virtue's for a very large item), the Specializations and Characteristics with the item's points added, and the Build Points of a starting laboratory's devices.

**You type:** the device and its effects, how the item is installed, and what each effect gives.

When the device or one of its effects is deleted or destroyed, what it gave goes with it, and the Magic Item Virtue once no other effect uses it.

**See also:** [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items), [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices), [Set up a laboratory](help:guide-creation-lab), [How lab planning works](help:guide-lab-planning)
