# How to bind and keep a familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars -->

Your magus has won over a raven, and it has agreed to the bond. Now it needs a page of its own, a season of lab work to bind it and, later, powers (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Familiars).

### Finding the familiar
<!-- help: guide-familiars-finding -->

Finding a suitable creature and winning it over is a matter for your stories, not for the app. Play it out at the table, and come to the app once the creature has agreed to the bond.

A magus can have one familiar at a time, so Bind Familiar is only offered while at least one magus in the saga has none. A Bjornaer, whose heartbeast takes its place, can't bind one (DE p. 234).

**What the binding needs.** The Lab Total must be at least 25 + Might + 5 x Size, and it also pays for the cords: 0, 5, 15, 30, 50 and 75 points for scores 0 to 5. The vis is a fifth of the Lab Total, rounded up, and the magus can handle no more than twice their Magic Theory in a season.

**Which Arts suit it.** The Arts must suit the animal or its powers (DE p. 266). Once both are chosen, the line under the form gives the book's Suggested Familiars for each, and says whether either names the familiar's kind from its Species line. That's advice, since a bird suits Auram and any magical animal Vim. The app binds with any pair.

When the season is applied, the app fills in the [Binding & Cords](help:section-fam-binding) section with the Arts used, the Lab Total, the binding level, the vis spent and the three cords. It also sets the season and year bound if they were blank.

To strengthen the bond later, plan **Bind or strengthen a familiar** again with higher cords. The vis already spent on the bond is taken off what the new binding costs.

# How to initiate a character into a Mystery
<!-- help: guide-initiation -->

Your magus has found a Mystagogue willing to lead them into the cult's next Mystery, and the Script is agreed. The Initiation is one season, planned like any other, and ends with the new Virtue on the Initiate's sheet.

The rules are in Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 10: Long-Term Events, Mystery Initiation. The Mysteries Revised Edition and Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults give the full treatment, and the Definitive Edition wins where they differ.

### Before the season
<!-- help: guide-initiation-before -->

An Initiation needs three things, settled at the table first.

- **A Mystagogue** who knows the cult's Lore, such as House Bjornaer Lore or Mithraic Lore. A character can be their own Mystagogue, and a self-initiation uses the Initiate's own scores.
<!-- tui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it's the Lore of their own House. Add it on the [Abilities tab](help:tab-abilities) (`2`) once they've learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
- **The Lore.** The Initiate needs the same Lore at 1 or more, unless it's the Lore of their own House. Add it in the [Abilities chapter](help:tab-abilities) once they've learnt it, for example with a season of Be taught. [Go there](go:tab-abilities)
<!-- /gui -->
- **A Script**, written like a Lab Text. Its bonus is the sum of its components, as the storyguide agrees them (see [Script components](help:guide-initiation-scripts)). Quests and other sacrifices are played out in their own seasons or stories.

For a successful Initiation, the app adds the Virtue, with what it names, to the Initiate's [Virtues & Flaws](help:tab-vf) and applies the Ordeal. It adds the Flaw gained, takes away the Virtue lost, or replaces the Minor Flaw raised with its Major Flaw. A Great Characteristic Virtue gained raises the score, and one lost lowers it again.

A failed Initiation changes nothing, and its Ordeal isn't suffered, since the Mystagogue sees that a Script will fail before any sacrifice is made. Two failures still cost the Ordeal. One is an Initiation that distractions made fail (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12). The other is an experimental Script whose rite fails, since its Ordeals are suffered before the rite is checked (p. 18). Either way, the Diary records the Initiation Total against the Target.

### Past Ordeals make later Initiations easier
<!-- help: guide-initiation-ordeals -->

An Ordeal lowers the Target Level of the Initiate's next three Initiations, never below 9 (DE p. 388). It takes off 3, 2 and 1 after a Minor Ordeal, 6, 4 and 2 after a Medium one, and 9, 6 and 3 after a Major one. It doesn't help the Initiation it was suffered in.

When the Initiate has suffered several, only the one that lowers the Target most counts. So a Major Ordeal followed by a Minor one still takes 6 off the next Initiation. The app keeps every Ordeal and counts each one down after each Initiation that gives its Virtue, and a failed Initiation uses up no step. The book doesn't say whether a failed one counts among "the next three" (DE p. 388), so that's the app's reading.

An unGifted Initiate's past Ordeals lower nothing (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). Flaws taken at character creation don't count as Ordeals. The Initiate's [This Season](help:section-char-season) section lists the past Ordeals still counting and what they take off the next Target.

### What the app works out, and what it doesn't
<!-- help: guide-initiation-limits -->

**The app works out** the totals, Targets and Ordeals above, and checks the Lore.

**You type** the Script bonus, as the storyguide agrees it, and for an experimental Script the proposed bonus, the risk and the sympathy bonus. Which Major Flaw a Minor Flaw is related to is the troupe's call, and the app checks only that it's a Major Flaw.

**Varying the Script.** The Mystagogue may make one change in an Initiation. Choose it in **Vary the Script**. Adding a component is Ease 9, changing one 12 and removing one 15. Type in **The change adds** what it does to the Script bonus, negative when removing. When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore. Success adds the change to the Script bonus. Failure fails the Initiation, with no Virtue and no Ordeal, though the Script as it was may be tried another season (DE pp. 388-389). Before then, the line under the form gives the roll the change needs and the Initiation Total if it works.

The Mysteries Revised Edition (p. 17) says instead that the original Script still works. The Definitive Edition wins, so this isn't a bug. Removing a component (Ease 15) is The Mysteries Revised's own addition, and it doesn't let a Minor Virtue's Script become a Major one.

**Overlapping Scripts.** A Script that shares Quests or Ordeals with one the Initiate already followed can't be used as it is. The Mystagogue changes the shared component (**Change a component**, Ease 12) or puts a different one in its place (**Replace a component**, Ease 15). Type in **The change adds** what that does to the bonus (Legends of Hermes pp. 50-51).

Each Quest that a Script written out as components names in its note is recorded on the Initiate when the rite is carried out. It's shown as **Quests performed** on the Initiate's This Season, beside the past Ordeals. An Initiation whose Script names one again says so on the plan and the season, and that Quest gives the Script nothing, since a Quest counts toward one Initiation only (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16). Change the Script first. An Ordeal is spotted the same way, by the Flaw the Initiate already has.

**An experimental Script.** With no Script, or one that falls short, tick **Experimental Script**, type the proposed rite's components in **Script bonus**, and choose a **Risk** of 0 to 3 (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 18). When the season is applied the app rolls a stress die + risk on the Extraordinary Results chart, with 1 + risk botch dice, and reads it for a rite.

- Side effects and minor flaws or benefits become Personality Traits of the Script.
- A major flaw becomes an extra Ordeal Flaw with no bonus.
- A major benefit gives a stronger Virtue or a second Minor one.
- A Discovery gives the Mystagogue experience.
- A modified effect gives a different Virtue, and the Diary says what to add by hand.
- A fatal flaw or a complete failure gives no Virtue.
- No Benefit takes the risk off the total.
- Disaster fails the rite and gives the Mystagogue and the Initiate each a Major Flaw.

Then it rolls the rite's validity. That's a simple die + risk + the Mystagogue's Intelligence + Lore against 9 + the proposed bonus, or 15 + it when the Initiate is someone else. A failure gives no Virtue, and any failure still costs the rite's Ordeal. The Initiation Total adds the storyguide's **Sympathy bonus** and the risk. The Script written down afterwards keeps the sympathy but not the risk. An experimental Script isn't also varied.

**UnGifted Initiates.** An unGifted Initiate's Initiation must include an Ordeal, and their past Ordeals don't lower its Target. An unGifted Mystagogue must know the power, so an unGifted character can't initiate themselves (Hedge Magic Revised Edition p. 14). The app applies this to every Initiation, Hermetic cults' included. A magus counts as Gifted.

**Hedge Scripts.** To follow one of a hedge tradition's printed Scripts, choose it in **Hedge Script**. The list holds the Scripts of the traditions someone in the saga belongs to. The tradition's Characteristic and Lore are used instead of Presence and the Lore typed. The Script's own bonus is used, anything typed in **Script bonus** is added to it, and its Ease Factor is the Target.

The Ease Factor rises when the Mystagogue doesn't know the power (see [Activities offered](help:plan-season-activities)). The app counts the Mystagogue as knowing it when they have the **Virtue initiated**. When the power is an Ability, a gruagach's Give say, tick **Mystagogue knows the power**. The Mystagogue must belong to the tradition, a tradition without self-initiation can't initiate itself, and a Script for the Gifted needs a Gifted Initiate. With a hedge Script the **Virtue initiated** may be left blank, and the Diary then names what the Script gives, to add by hand. Past Ordeals lower a hedge Script's Ease Factor too, never below 9, and never an Ease Factor already lower.

**Who may be Initiated.** Two Mysteries check it. **Secret Name** fails for a magus with a talisman or a familiar (DE p. 235). **Faerie Magic** needs one touched by the fay, by a fay Virtue or Flaw or a Warping Point from a faerie (DE p. 236). The app can't tell where Warping came from, so any Warping Point counts.

**Distractions.** **Months lost** on the plan takes a third of the Initiation Total a month, rounded up. If that makes the Initiation fail, the Ordeal is still suffered and the Initiation must be repeated (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 12).

**Other seasons.** A Mystagogue who spends a whole season on the Script, teaching a Supernatural Ability say, plans **Act as Mystagogue**. A season spent on a Quest is **Serve a Mystagogue / Quest season**. Both need The Mysteries Revised Edition in play.

### Script components
<!-- help: guide-initiation-scripts -->

A Hermetic Mystery's Script can be written out on the plan as its **Script components** (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 15-16; DE p. 388).

- A special place at a special time, +3, once.
- Each specific Quest, +3, or +6 for a unique Quest.
- The Initiate's time serving the cult, +1, once.
- Material goods, +1, once.
- Something of great symbolic value, +3 to +6, such as a talisman, familiar or powerful device.
- The Mystagogue's time, +3.
- The sympathy of the rite to the Virtue, usually 0 to +3 but -5 to +5, agreed with the storyguide.

The Ordeal's own bonus is added from the plan's **Ordeal**. It's +3 for a Minor Flaw gained or Minor Virtue lost, and a Minor Virtue replaced by its Major one counts as that. It's +6 for a Minor Flaw raised to Major, and +9 for a Major Flaw or Virtue. A Verditius's first Inner Mystery counts its Hubris as a Minor Ordeal when no other is named.

The **Script bonus** typed on the plan is added to the components. A component listed twice that may be held once, or a size out of its range, invalidates the whole Script (TMRE p. 16).

With no components listed, the typed bonus is the whole Script. The one exception is a Verditius's first Inner Mystery, which adds its Hubris Ordeal's +3 to the typed bonus and to a catalog Script's printed bonus. That Ordeal always comes, and the bonus can't know of it (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 117-118).

A Verditius Initiate is also offered the House's own components (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 117).

- A lesser enchanted item of the 4th magnitude sacrificed, +3.
- A greater one of the 7th, or the Initiate's talisman, +6.
- The Mystagogue's wealth, +1, once.
- The Outer Mystery given as the Arts are opened, +6.

A hedge or House Script from the catalog keeps its printed bonus, and the season's note lists its components as the book gives them. The Verditius Outer Mystery's components add to 14, though the book prints +12, which is used.

**Sacrifices.** When the Script makes a sacrifice, choose in **Sacrificed** what's given up.

- One of the Initiate's devices, a talisman among them.
- Their carried items, books or familiar.
- An apprentice.
- Mundane wealth.
- Vis.
- A season serving the cult.

The sacrifice is made when the season is played, Initiation or not, and the Diary says so. A device, item, book or familiar is gone, so it can't be sacrificed twice. A season of service takes the next season as Other, unless it's planned already. Vis is taken from the Initiate's own raw vis, as many pawns of the Art as **Vis of** and **Pawns sacrificed** say.

A plan whose vis isn't there to give is refused, since a Sacrifice that isn't made invalidates the whole Script (The Mysteries Revised Edition p. 16). The bonus it brings is the Script's Sacrifice component (The Mysteries Revised Edition pp. 12, 15-16).

### House Mysteries
<!-- help: guide-initiation-houses -->

Four Houses' Scripts are listed under the House's name on the Initiation's Script field, and each brings rules of its own.

**House Verditius** (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 118-135)

- **Scripts.** Every magus with Verditius Magic can follow the House's sample Scripts, listed under House Verditius (Mystery Cult). There's one for each of the eight Inner Mysteries, the Outer Mystery and four confraternities'.
- **Lore.** The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Verditius, the book's Verditius Cult Lore. The Initiate needs it at 3 for a Minor Inner Mystery and 5 for a Major (p. 118).
- **Hubris.** The first Inner Mystery always brings the Flaw Hubris as a Minor Ordeal. The season adds the Flaw and sets the Hubris Personality Trait to 1, +1 with Proud, or +3 with Proud as a Major Flaw.
- **Hubris experience.** Each Inner Mystery adds its Hubris experience, 1 for a Minor and 3 for a Major (DE p. 242). The Increased Hubris Ordeal adds enough for one more point (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults p. 121). Hubris grows like an Ability, and its experience toward the next point is kept with the trait. See [Hubris](help:verditius-hubris) for what a sale, the Contest or a vendetta does to it.
- **The House's Ordeals.** Each may be suffered once. Prone to Chance and Averse to Risk exclude each other, and no one in the Primus's line may Sever the Ties that Bind (pp. 120-121).
- **Confraternities.** A confraternity's Script also notes the Mystery and Flaw it gives its members, their Craft and the confraternity's path, for Roland, Balento, Irene the Younger and Himinis the Mad (pp. 121-123).

**House Merinita** (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 83-106)

- **Scripts.** Every magus with Faerie Magic belongs to the House and can follow its lineages' sample Scripts, listed under House Merinita (Mystery Cult).
  - The Line of Quendalon's Animae Magic.
  - The Heirs to Merlin's Charm Magic.
  - The Followers of Pendule's Spell Timing.
  - The Shadow-Masters' Glamour.
  - The Vestals' Nature Lore, as Lares Lore.
  - The True Merinitae's Wilding.
- **Lore.** The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Merinita, the book's House Merinita Lore. An Initiate needs it at 1 for an Inner Mystery (p. 88).
- **What each needs.** The Arcadian, Folk and Illusion Mysteries need Faerie Magic, and Binding the Gift is the House's own. The Nature Mysteries, such as Awakening, Wilding and Guardian of Nature, need Nature Lore instead, not the House's Lore (pp. 86, 102-104).
- **Glamour** always brings the Major Flaw Vulnerable Magic as its Ordeal. The season adds it, unless the plan's Ordeal names it already, and a Script written out as components counts its Major Ordeal.
- **Vulnerable Magic's condition.** Type it on the plan. It must be one the magus doesn't have the Flaw for already, since the Flaw is taken again only with a new condition (pp. 87, 102).
- **Perpetuity.** Its season notes that it usually brings Unnatural Magic.

**House Bjornaer** (Houses of Hermes: Mystery Cults pp. 24-33)

- **Scripts.** Every magus with the Heartbeast can follow the House's Scripts, listed under House Bjornaer (Mystery Cult).
- **Lore.** The Lore is Mystery Cult Lore: Bjornaer. The rules also read the book's House Bjornaer Lore, the Initiation Total included, so a character with either name scores it (HoH:MC pp. 28, 30, Errata p. 54).
- **The Ritual of Twelve Years** gives the Heartbeast, Target 15, Script +6. Type the Participation Modifier as the other modifier. It's +1 for each witness, the Mystagogue included, whose heartbeast shares the probationer's temperament, and -1 for each opposed. Sanguine opposes melancholic, and choleric phlegmatic (p. 26).
- **Heartbeast.** Choose Heartbeast as the Virtue initiated. The season then adds Heartbeast 1, and the familiar forgone counts as a Minor Ordeal.
- **Who can't take the Ritual.** It fails for one who has ever bound a familiar, even one since dead (DE p. 234; p. 24). The app sees a familiar bound to them now, or a familiar season in their Diary. Tick **Has ever bound a familiar** on the plan for one it can't see. The Ritual is refused to an Initiate of another House's Mystery (p. 18), and it's never repeated.
- **Clan Ilfetu** leads the Ritual and the House Mysteries, Secret Name +8, Theriomorphy +6 and Sensory Magic +9 (p. 25). A Clan Mystery's Trial, Deed or Oath is +7, or the Trial +13 with a Major Ordeal. It needs a Mystagogue with Presence + the Lore 8, less the Initiate's past Ordeal's reduction.
- **The Mystery of the Inner Heartbeast**, the Epitome, Chimera or Anima, needs the Lore 5. It's Target 21, Script +13. Its Ordeal is a Major Hermetic or Supernatural Flaw or, for a magus who changes shape, the loss of their Skinchanger or Shapeshifter (pp. 17, 28-30). With no Mystagogue chosen, the Great Beast leads it, with Presence +2 and the Lore 8.
- **The Epitome** raises one Characteristic. The inner heartbeast's rises to the species' + 3 and the human form's by 1. The outer heartbeast changes only through that +1, combined as usual (pp. 23, 30). So the book's example ends with an inner Perception of +5 and an outer +2, the eagle's own, one above where it started.
- **The app's reading of the Epitome.** The app follows the rule text above. Errata p. 54 gives the outer heartbeast +5, but that's the inner figure and contradicts the rule. Through the Aegis p. 14, with Hilde Oddfish's Strength +7 outer and +10 inner, applies the rule as written.
- **Refinements** of the inner heartbeast are the magus's own. The embryonic Script is +7, and +3 where the inner heartbeast resonates: an Epitome with Virtues and Characteristics, a Chimera with Qualities and Size, an Anima with Powers.
- **A Refinement's Target** is 15 for a Quality, 18 for Size, a Characteristic or a Minor Virtue, 21 for a Minor Power and 30 for a Major Virtue or Power, lowered by past Ordeals as usual. Each earlier increase of the same Size or Characteristic adds 3, so take 3 from the other modifier for each.
- **After a Refinement.** The season notes the vis eaten, a pawn per three points of the Ease Factor, and the Warping Point. A Refinement changes only the inner heartbeast, so note it on the sheet by hand (pp. 32-33).

# How to host for browsers and SSH
<!-- help: guide-hosting-local -->

Everyone's at home on the same network, or on Tailscale, and nobody wants to install the app. The players open the saga in a web browser while it stays on your computer.

The app and the saga file never leave your computer. Each player who opens the page gets their own copy of the app, running on your machine.

You need a private network that you and the players share. The app doesn't come with one, so use your home network, **Tailscale** or **ZeroTier** (recipes below). Never host on an address the whole internet can reach. Anyone who reaches it gets a copy of the app and its sign-in, and the page isn't encrypted beyond what the network adds. The app refuses such an address, and "every address" (`0.0.0.0`), unless you tick **I know this is reachable from the internet**. What can go wrong, and what the app does about it, is in the app's threat model (`docs/threat-model-browser-and-ssh-hosting.md` beside its source).

<!-- gui -->
What the players get in their browser, or over SSH, is the terminal version of the app, so their screens look like it rather than like this window. You keep using the desktop window while you host. It and the players' copies all save into the same saga file, each change merged with the others.
<!-- /gui -->

### Tailscale
<!-- help: guide-hosting-tailscale -->

Everyone, you and each player, installs Tailscale and signs in with an existing Google, Microsoft, GitHub or Apple account.

1. **Find your address.** On your computer, Tailscale shows its address, starting with `100.`. That's the ADDRESS to host on.
2. **Share your computer.** In Tailscale's admin page, share your computer with each player by their email. They accept the invite, and can then reach only your computer.
3. **Open the saga.** Players open `http://ADDRESS:8000`.

### ZeroTier
<!-- help: guide-hosting-zerotier -->

Only you need a ZeroTier account. Players just install the ZeroTier app.

1. **Make a network.** On ZeroTier's website, create a network and copy its 16-character Network ID. Keep it Private.
2. **Join it yourself.** On your computer, join that network from the ZeroTier icon. On the website, tick **Auth** for your computer and note its Managed IP. That's the ADDRESS to host on.
3. **Make it Private in Windows.** Windows often marks the ZeroTier connection as a Public network. Set it to Private in Windows' network settings, or the firewall keeps players out.
4. **Let the players in.** Each player installs ZeroTier and joins with the Network ID, and you tick **Auth** for their device.
5. **Fence it.** On a ZeroTier network every device can reach every other one. To let players reach only the app on your computer, set the network's Flow Rules along these lines, with your address, then check a player can still connect.

```
accept ethertype arp;
accept ipdest ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and dport 8000;
accept ipsrc ADDRESS/32 and ipprotocol tcp and sport 8000;
drop;
```

If you choose an address the computer doesn't have, or a port another program is using, the app says so and nothing starts. An address the internet can reach is refused too, until you tick **I know this is reachable from the internet**. That tick shows only for such an address, or one you type, and it's never remembered.

Players must open the address you host on, as Saga Management shows it. The host refuses a request made to another name for your computer, such as its Windows name, to keep hostile web pages out.

Every few seconds each player's copy picks up what the others saved. If it finds the saga file damaged, say edited by hand or cut short by another program, it says so once and goes on with its own data. Its next save writes the whole file again.

**From a terminal instead.** The same host runs in a terminal in the app's folder with `uv run python tools/serve.py --host ADDRESS`, or, in a [downloaded copy](help:guide-download), `armarius-terminal host --host ADDRESS`. Add `--port 8001` for another port, or `--ssh-port 2222` for SSH. It hosts until you press Ctrl+C there, whichever window of the app you have open. It refuses an address the internet can reach unless you add `--allow-public`.

### Over SSH instead of a browser
<!-- help: guide-hosting-ssh -->

A player who prefers a terminal can connect with SSH. Tick **Also over SSH** when you start hosting, and the browser page keeps working alongside. The SSH port is 2222 unless you change it (from a terminal, add `--ssh-port 2222`).

The SSH server is the app's own. No Windows accounts are involved, and any user name is accepted, because who the player is comes from the same sign-in as in a browser.

The first time, the app makes a host key and keeps it in your computer's keychain, such as Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain or a Linux Secret Service. It's never kept in the save folder, which a cloud service may sync. Players' SSH clients ask once whether to trust it. A key an older version left in the save folder (`ssh_host_key`) is moved into the keychain and the file deleted, so players' clients still know your computer. With no keychain, SSH hosting is refused and says why.

Players connect with `ssh -p 2222 player@ADDRESS` from Windows Terminal (Windows 10 and 11 include `ssh`), macOS Terminal or any Linux terminal. They get the same saga list and sign-in. Their terminal should be at least 120 columns by 45 rows, with true colour and Unicode. Windows Terminal, iTerm2, macOS Terminal and the usual Linux terminals have both, and the old Windows console doesn't. Over SSH, Export isn't offered, since the file could only land on your computer.

### What players see
<!-- help: guide-hosting-players -->

- The app fills the browser window. It's laid out for at least 120 columns by 45 rows. On a 1080p screen, add `?fontsize=12` to the address (`http://ADDRESS:8000/?fontsize=12`) or zoom the browser out.
- The browser keeps some keys for itself, such as Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T and F5, so don't use them in the app.
- A player changes only the characters they play and what belongs to them. Everything else they can look at and Export, which downloads in their browser. See [Players](help:tab-content-players).
- Everyone's changes reach the others within a few seconds. See [Signing in](help:sign-in).

**What the app does for you.** The host answers only requests addressed to the address you chose, and the app's own connection only from its own page, so a web page from another site can't open a copy. After three wrong passcodes for one name, each try waits longer, and a passcode shorter than 14 characters asks for a new one (see [Signing in](help:sign-in)). A copy opened from a browser or over SSH has no command palette and can't change your settings. Its file chooser (Import, for a storyguide who signs in) shows only your exports folder.

# How to host for Remote seats
<!-- help: guide-hosting-internet; gui -->

Your players live in three different towns, and each has Armarius on their own computer. They join your saga over the internet while it stays on yours.

The app on your computer listens to **this computer only**, on port 8790 unless you change it. Nothing on your network or the internet can reach it directly. A **tunnel** carries players to it. Players need the same major version of Armarius as you.

### Hosting and inviting
<!-- help: guide-hosting-remote; gui -->

1. **Add the players.** Add each player on Saga Management's [Players](help:section-players) and say who plays whom, as in [Before the first session](help:guide-hosting-setup). Their passcodes need at least 14 characters.
2. **Set up a tunnel.** Choose one from [Choosing a tunnel](help:guide-hosting-tunnels) and point it at `http://localhost:8790`.
3. **Host.** In Saga Management click **Host for Players**, choose **Remote seats**, and click **Host**. Give the **Tunnel address**, which **Tunnel** it is, and the **Port** (see [Host for Remote Seats](help:host-remote-seats)).
4. **Issue Invites.** **Issue Invite** to each player and send it by chat or email (see [Issue Invite](help:issue-invite)).
5. **Check the words on the call.** Each player pastes the Invite into **Join a Saga** on their title page or Sagas shelf, and checks your six words. They read you their device's three words.
6. **Approve.** If the three words match your screen, **Approve** the device under Requests.
7. **Keep it running.** Keep your computer awake and the tunnel running while people play.
8. **Stop.** Click **Stop Hosting** in the Host for Players window. Closing the app asks first, as it ends every player's connection.

The players' side is in [Join a Saga](help:join-game), and what they can expect of you in [What a host can and can't see or do](help:what-a-host-sees).

### Choosing a tunnel
<!-- help: guide-hosting-tunnels; gui -->

A tunnel is a small program on your computer that keeps one connection open to a company's servers, which give you an https address. Inside the tunnel the app runs its own encryption, locked to your host's key. The tunnel company carries what it can't read. It sees that someone connected, and when, but not what passed.

Choose one of these. The steps are as of November 2026, and each service changes its pages from time to time, so follow its own guide where these differ.

- **Cloudflare Tunnel** (free, but you need a domain on Cloudflare). Install
  `cloudflared`, then `cloudflared tunnel login`, `cloudflared tunnel
  create armarius`, and `cloudflared tunnel route dns armarius
  saga.example.com`. In its `config.yml`, point the hostname at
  `http://localhost:8790`, then `cloudflared tunnel run armarius`, or
  install it as a service so it starts with Windows. In Host for
  Players choose **Cloudflare Tunnel** and the address
  `https://saga.example.com`. Where to go: developers.cloudflare.com,
  Cloudflare Tunnel. Its quick tunnels (`trycloudflare.com`) change
  address each time, so every Invite would be lost. Use a named tunnel.
- **Tailscale Funnel** (free, and no domain needed). Install Tailscale and
  sign in. In the admin console's DNS page, turn on HTTPS and Funnel.
  Then `tailscale funnel --bg 8790` publishes it at
  `https://YOUR-PC.YOUR-TAILNET.ts.net`. The public side must be port
  443, 8443 or 10000, and the 8790 is your computer's. `tailscale funnel
  8790 off` stops it. In Host for Players choose **Another tunnel**.
  Where to go: tailscale.com/docs, Tailscale Funnel.
- **ngrok** (a free account gets one fixed address). Install ngrok, add
  your token, then `ngrok http 8790 --url YOUR-NAME.ngrok-free.dev`. A
  random address changes each time, so use your fixed one. Choose
  **Another tunnel**. Where to go: ngrok.com/docs.
- **A server you rent**, a small one with a domain. On it, run a web
  server that adds https and passes the player's address on, such as
  Caddy (`reverse_proxy localhost:8790`). From your computer, open a
  reverse SSH tunnel to it: `ssh -N -R 8790:localhost:8790 you@server`.
  Choose **Another tunnel**.
- **Port forwarding on your router** isn't offered. The app won't
  listen beyond this computer, and opening your router would show every
  player, and anyone who finds it, your home address. If you must, put
  a web server such as Caddy on this computer in front of it, as on a
  rented server above, and forward only port 443 to that.

Whichever you use, the address goes in **Tunnel address** (`https://`,
nothing after the name), and the tunnel points at `http://localhost:8790`.
The address isn't secret. The Invite carries it, always as a secure
`wss://` address, and a player's app refuses an Invite naming a plain
`ws://` one anywhere but on its own computer.

The app tells players apart by the address the tunnel passes on:
Cloudflare's own header, or the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, which ngrok
and Caddy add. A tunnel that passes on none makes the limits count every
player as one.

### Remote seats: hosting safely
<!-- help: guide-hosting-safety; gui -->

**Issuing and revoking.** Issue each player their own Invite, and send it
a different way from your call if you can, such as a chat message or an
email. It works once, on one device, for 24 hours. On the call, read all
six of your words, and have the player check every one. Approve a device
only when its player reads you the three words your screen shows.

A changed passcode, or one you reset, ends every seat still signed in
with the old one at once. If a device is lost or a player leaves,
**Revoke** its seat at once, under Seats. It's cut off within a second. A
storyguide's device sees everything in the saga, so revoke it as soon as
it's lost.

If you think your host key itself has leaked, say through a copy of your
keychain, **Rotate Host Key**. Every Invite and seat ends, and everyone
needs a new Invite and new six words. Seats nobody uses stop working
after the days you set in **Idle Expiry** (90 unless you change it).

**The Log**, in Host for Players, records who did what and when. That
covers Invites issued and redeemed, approvals and rejections, sign-ins
and wrong passcodes, lock-outs and revokes. It also notes which records
each seat read or changed, passcode changes and resets asked, role
changes, and seasons advanced or undone by a storyguide. It never holds a
passcode, a credential, an Invite's code or what was typed. It lives in
your save folder, not in the saga, so players never receive it, and it
keeps a year.

**What the app does for you.** A wrong passcode slows that device down,
with three free tries and then growing waits, without locking anyone else
out. A connection that floods, sends what the app doesn't understand, or
stays silent too long is closed. Each player's seat receives only what
that player may see, and every change they send is checked against what's
theirs to change. The full list is in the app's threat model
(`docs/threat-model-remote-seats.md` beside its source), and what the
players are told is in [What keeps you safe](help:join-game-safety).

**Hosting from a computer that's always on.** Instead of this computer, a
NAS, a small Linux computer or a rented server can host the saga for
Remote seats day and night, with no window. See
[How to self-host a saga](help:guide-self-hosting).

**See also:** [How to host a saga for your troupe](help:guide-hosting), [Host for Players](help:host-for-players-window), [Join a Saga](help:join-game), [Players](help:tab-content-players)

# How to self-host a saga
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting -->

Your players want to fill in their seasons on a Tuesday night, when your own computer is off. A server or NAS that's always on can host the saga for them, day and night.

It hosts one saga for Remote seats, players joining from their own Armarius over the internet. It runs the app's container image with no window: the listener for Remote seats, and beside it a tunnel that brings players in. Nothing on the server is opened to the internet, since the tunnel connects out. You look after it with the `admin` command, in a terminal on the server.

Only Remote seats are hosted this way, so players need Armarius on their own computers. Hosting for browsers and SSH stays on your desktop, until the app can cap how many copies a host runs for them.

You'll need the server or NAS, with Docker and its compose plugin, a domain on Cloudflare for the tunnel (or Tailscale, below), the saga's file from your desktop, and a terminal on the server over SSH. The steps use the example address `saga.example.com`. Use your own.

The recipe below works anywhere Docker runs. On a NAS, run each `docker` command as `sudo docker`, and see [A UGREEN NAS](help:guide-self-hosting-nas) for the steps that differ.

1. **Install Docker.** Install Docker and its compose plugin ([on Linux](help:guide-self-hosting-linux), Docker Engine; most NAS systems have a Docker app).
2. **Make a folder.** Make a folder for it on the server, say `armarius`. Into it, put `compose.yaml`, `example.env` and `tunnel.env.example`, from the `deploy` folder of the app's source on GitHub, at the version you run.
3. **Fill in `.env`.** In that folder, `cp example.env .env` and fill it in (see [The files and folders](help:guide-self-hosting-files)).
4. **Make `tunnel.env`.** `cp tunnel.env.example tunnel.env`. The token goes in later. Then `chmod 600 .env tunnel.env`.
5. **Make the three folders**, `data`, `keys` and `backups` (see [The files and folders](help:guide-self-hosting-files)). On a NAS, use [its steps](help:guide-self-hosting-nas) instead.
6. **Get your seat ready.** On your desktop, be a Storyguide in the saga, and stop hosting there (see [Bringing the saga in](help:guide-self-hosting-move)).
7. **Copy the saga over.** Copy the saga's file and its passcode records into the `armarius` folder on the server.
8. **Bring it in.** Run the `admin saga import` command, then delete the copied files.
9. **Move the desktop's copy away**, out of the `sagas` folder. Don't just rename it.
10. **Make the tunnel.** Create a Cloudflare tunnel, put its token in `tunnel.env`, and give it the hostname `saga.example.com` pointing at `http://localhost:8790` (see [The tunnel](help:guide-self-hosting-tunnel)).
11. **Start it.** `docker compose up -d`, then tell it the tunnel's address and issue the Invites (see [Starting it](help:guide-self-hosting-start)).

### The files and folders
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-files -->

In `.env`, set `ARMARIUS_VERSION`, the version to run, such as `4.13.0`. Always give a number, never "latest". Set `ARMARIUS_SAGA` to the saga's id, which is the name of its file in your save folder's `sagas`, without `.json`.

The three folders, `data`, `keys` and `backups`, are owned by the app's own user (number 10001) and private to it:

```
sudo install -d -o 10001 -g 10001 -m 700 data keys backups
```

`keys` holds the host key. An encrypted disk or volume is the best home for it (set `KEYS_DIR` in `.env` to it). A NAS's encrypted shared folder will do if the app's user can use it: try it first, and if it can't, use a plain private folder instead. UGOS's can't (see [A UGREEN NAS](help:guide-self-hosting-nas)).

The compose file runs the app locked down. It runs as its own user, with its system files read-only, no extra privileges, 512 MB of memory at most, and no port published.

### A Linux computer or rented server
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-linux -->

Any always-on Linux computer will do, such as an old laptop, a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 (the image has an ARM version), or the smallest server a hosting company rents. One processor and 1 GB is plenty.

- **Docker.** Install Docker Engine and the compose plugin, following Docker's own guide for your system (docs.docker.com, Install Docker Engine).
- **The key at home.** On a home computer, put `keys` on an encrypted disk if the system offers one.
- **A rented server.** Its company can read its disks, so the key is safer at home. If you rent one anyway, choose one with disk encryption, and let no one else onto it. Close every port in its firewall but SSH. The tunnel needs none.

Then follow the recipe above.

### A UGREEN NAS
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-nas -->

A UGREEN NAS runs it in the Docker app of UGOS Pro. Docker picks the image's version for the NAS's processor (amd64 for Intel, arm64 for ARM), and the app uses at most 512 MB of memory. The steps follow UGOS Pro 1.21, and menu names may move in later versions.

1. **Turn on SSH.** In Control Panel, **Service**, turn on **SSH** (Terminal), port 22. Set it to turn itself off after a few hours, since you only need it while setting up and for admin chores.
2. **Make the shared folder.** In the Files app, make an ordinary shared folder, `armarius`, and copy the three files into it. The Files app's upload works, and so does `\\NAS-ADDRESS\armarius` in Explorer once SMB is on, under Service.
3. **Go to the folder.** Sign in with SSH (`ssh YOU@NAS-ADDRESS`) and run `cd /volume1/armarius`. `ls -d /volume*/armarius` finds it.
4. **Make the three folders.** `sudo mkdir -p data keys backups`, then `sudo chown 10001:10001 data keys backups`.
5. **Strip UGOS's access lists.** `sudo setfacl -b data keys backups`. UGOS shows these lists as a `+` after the modes. They open a folder to every NAS account, and the app refuses a key folder others can open.
6. **Make them private.** `sudo chmod 700 data keys backups`, last, since stripping the lists can reset the modes. `ls -ld data keys backups` should show `drwx------ ... 10001 10001` with no `+`.
7. **Protect the settings.** Leave `KEYS_DIR=./keys` in `.env`, and run `sudo chmod 600 .env tunnel.env`.

**Not UGOS's encrypted shared folders, for the keys.** They look like
the right home for a key, but the app's user can't use one. UGOS mounts
it as a filesystem of its own (`fuse.uggocryptfs`) that answers user
10001 with "Input/output error". It reports every folder in it as open to
all (`rwxrwxrwx`) whatever its modes, and stripping its access lists sets
it to no access at all. Keep the keys in the ordinary `keys` folder
above, private to the app's user. What that gives up: someone who steals
the NAS's drive could read the key, and then you'd rotate it and send
everyone a new Invite.

Over SSH, `sudo docker compose ...` works from the `armarius` folder.

**Optional: see it in UGOS's Docker app.** The Docker app lists only the
projects it made, so one started from the command line isn't there at
first. Once you've run `docker compose up -d` the first time, add it:

1. **Make the project.** In the Docker app, **Project**, **Create**.
2. **Name it `armarius`.** It must match the `name:` in `compose.yaml`, so the running containers are taken in.
3. **Choose the folder.** Pick the `armarius` shared folder. UGOS loads the `compose.yaml` already there: leave it as it is, and leave the environment variables empty, since `.env` and `tunnel.env` are in the folder.
4. **Create it.** The project shows 3/3 running.

From there you can start it, stop it and watch it. It never shows
"Updatable", because `.env` pins the version, so update it as before (see
[A newer version](help:guide-self-hosting-update)).

### Bringing the saga in
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-move -->

The saga moves to the server, and your desktop's copy becomes history.

1. **Be a storyguide in it.** On your desktop, make sure you're a Player in the saga with the **Storyguide** role and a passcode of 14 characters or more. You'll join your own saga from the desktop like everyone else. Stop hosting there.
2. **Copy two files.** From your [save folder](help:saga-picker), copy the saga's file, in `sagas`, and its passcode records, `remote/NAME-passcodes.json` (NAME being the saga's name), into the `armarius` folder on the server. The records let players keep their passcodes. Without them, every passcode must be set again.
3. **On a NAS, hand them over.** Files copied in belong to your NAS account and its access lists, which the app's user can't read ("Permission denied"). Give them to it first: `sudo chown 10001:10001 FILES`, `sudo setfacl -b FILES`, `sudo chmod 600 FILES`.
4. **Import them.** Run the command below, with your own file names. The saga is checked as the app checks any saga it opens. A passcode record comes over only for the very passcode it was made for, so one for a passcode changed since is dropped.
5. **Delete the copies.** Delete the copied files with `sudo rm`, since they now belong to the app's user.
6. **Issue new Invites.** The server makes a host key of its own, so every Invite and seat from your desktop has ended. Issue everyone a new Invite, your own desktop's storyguide seat included. `admin players list` names anyone whose passcode must still be set again (`admin players set-passcode NAME`, below).
7. **Move the desktop's copy out.** Move the desktop's copy of the saga file out of the `sagas` folder, for example into a folder of old copies. A change made there never reaches the server, and a copy only renamed inside `sagas` can save itself back under the saga's own name.

The import command:

```
docker compose run --rm -v "$PWD/SAGA.json:/in/saga.json:ro" -v "$PWD/NAME-passcodes.json:/in/passcodes.json:ro" armarius admin saga import /in/saga.json --passcodes /in/passcodes.json
```

### The tunnel
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-tunnel -->

**Cloudflare Tunnel**, the compose file's own:

1. **Create it.** In Cloudflare's dashboard, under Networking, Tunnels (older dashboards: Zero Trust, Networks, Tunnels), create a tunnel of the cloudflared kind.
2. **Name it.** Call it `armarius`, or `nas-armarius` if your desktop already has a tunnel. Give the server its own tunnel, never a second connector on the desktop's, or Cloudflare would share players between the two hosts.
3. **Copy the token.** Cloudflare shows a command to install it, holding a long token. Copy only the token into `tunnel.env`, after `TUNNEL_TOKEN=`.
4. **Give it a hostname.** Add a public hostname, `saga.example.com`, whose service is `http://localhost:8790`. The tunnel shares the app's network, so "localhost" is the app.

Whoever has the token can serve your saga's address, so keep `tunnel.env`
like the key. Where to go: developers.cloudflare.com, Cloudflare Tunnel.

**Tailscale Funnel** instead, with no domain needed. In the compose file,
put a `tailscale/tailscale` service in place of `cloudflared`, with the
same `network_mode: "service:armarius"`, an auth key in its own env file,
and a Funnel serve setting that sends `https://` traffic to
`http://127.0.0.1:8790`. Tell the listener which tunnel it's behind. Give
the armarius service `command: ["seats", "--tunnel", "other"]` and run
`admin tunnel --tunnel other`. Where to go: tailscale.com/docs, Tailscale
in Docker and Tailscale Funnel.

### Starting it
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-start -->

1. **Start it.** Run `docker compose up -d`, or start the Project in the NAS's Docker app. `docker compose ps` shows the app **healthy** within a minute.
2. **Give it the address.** Tell it the tunnel's address, which Invites carry: `docker exec armarius python main.py admin tunnel --url https://saga.example.com`.
3. **Set passcodes.** `docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin players set-passcode NAME`. The `-it` lets it ask without showing what you type.
4. **Issue Invites.** `... admin invites issue NAME` for each player.
5. **Approve each device on the call**, as on the desktop, with `admin invites pending` and `admin approve ID`.

If it won't start, `docker compose logs armarius` shows what the app said.
It names what's wrong, such as a key folder others can read or a saga it
can't find.

### When a step goes wrong
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-trouble -->

- **"Permission denied" reading a file, or a key folder refused as
  open to others.** It's the NAS's own access lists (a `+` after the
  modes in `ls -l`). Give it to the app's user and strip them:
  `sudo chown 10001:10001 PATH`, `sudo setfacl -b PATH`, then
  `sudo chmod 700 PATH` (a folder) or `600` (a file). Check with
  `ls -ld PATH`.
- **"The key folder ... isn't there".** The app never makes the key
  folder itself, so that a volume not yet unlocked, after a restart say,
  can't be given a fresh key in its place. Unlock the volume, or, the
  first time, make the folder as the message says.
- **"Input/output error" or "Not a directory" in the keys folder.** It's
  an encrypted shared folder of the NAS's that the app's user can't use
  (as on UGOS). Use a plain private folder instead (see
  [A UGREEN NAS](help:guide-self-hosting-nas)).
- **A folder shows `d---------` after `setfacl -b`.** Set its modes
  again with `sudo chmod 700 PATH`.
- **Testing as the app's user.** `sudo -u` can't, since the NAS has no
  account 10001. Ask the image instead: `sudo docker run --rm --user
  10001:10001 -v PATH:/keys --entrypoint sh armarius:VERSION -c 'ls -lnd
  /keys; touch /keys/t && ls -ln /keys/t; rm -f /keys/t'`.
- **`\\NAS-ADDRESS\armarius` won't open in Explorer.** The shared
  folder doesn't exist yet, or Windows file sharing (SMB) is off on the
  NAS. Upload through the NAS's Files app instead.
- **`.env` missing after copying.** Windows hides names starting with a
  dot. Turn on View, Show, Hidden items before copying.
- **`rm` asks about a "write-protected" file.** It belongs to the app's
  user now. Answer `y`, or use `sudo rm`.
- **Where's the tunnel's token?** On the tunnel's setup page choose
  **Docker** as the system. The command shown ends with the token, which
  starts `eyJ`. Copy only the token, and don't run the command, since the
  compose file runs cloudflared. The page's Continue button stays grey
  until the server connects, so add the public hostname after starting it.
- **The tunnel shows Healthy but "No routes".** Open it and add a route
  (Routes, Add route, Published application), with the hostname, and
  `http://localhost:8790` as Service URL. Newer dashboards have no
  separate type box, since the `http://` says it. Until the route exists
  the address has no DNS record, and a player's Join says to check the
  storyguide is hosting.
- **Add the route before anyone tries to join.** A home router remembers
  "no such name" for up to half an hour, even after the route exists. To
  tell that apart from a broken tunnel, ask Cloudflare's own DNS
  (`nslookup NAME 1.1.1.1`). If it answers there, only the router's
  memory is in the way, so wait or restart the router.
- **The token didn't save in `tunnel.env`.** In Windows Terminal, paste
  into `nano` with a right-click or Ctrl+Shift+V. Check without showing
  it: `sudo awk -F= '{print NR": "$1"  length="length($0)}' tunnel.env`
  should show one `TUNNEL_TOKEN` line of about 200 characters. 13 means
  it's empty.

### Looking after it with the `admin` command
<!-- help: guide-hosting-admin -->

A host with no screen does what the Host for Players window does with the
`admin` command, typed in a terminal on that computer. In a container,
that's `docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin ...`, and from a
source checkout `uv run python main.py admin ...`. It reads and writes the
saga's files there, and nothing goes over the network.

`--saga` names the saga by its id or name. With only one saga, or
`ARMARIUS_SAGA` set, it can be left out. `admin --help` lists everything,
and `--help` after any word says more.

- **Sagas.** `admin sagas` lists the sagas. `admin saga import FILE`
  brings a saga file in, checked as the app checks any saga it opens,
  with `--passcodes FILE` for the old host's passcode records.
  `admin saga delete` deletes one after you type its name.
- **Players.** `admin players list` says who may have a Remote seat.
  `admin players set-passcode NAME` asks for a new passcode twice, without
  showing it. It's never typed on the command line, where others could
  see it. `admin players set-role NAME storyguide` (or `player`) sets a
  role.
- **The tunnel.** `admin tunnel --url https://saga.example.com` sets the
  address Invites carry. `admin tunnel` shows it, and where to point the
  tunnel.
- **Invites.** `admin invites issue NAME` prints the Invite to send and
  the six words to read on the call. `admin words` prints the words
  again. `admin invites pending` lists what waits: who asks, from which
  device, and its three words to check on the call. Under each is the
  exact command that approves it or turns it down, ready to copy, with a
  short form of the request's id.
- **Seats.** `admin devices list` lists the seats, each live one with the
  command that revokes it. `admin devices revoke ID` does so.
- **The Log.** `admin log`, with `--player NAME` or `--limit N`.
- **A new key.** `admin rotate-key` gives the host a new key. Restart the
  listener afterwards (`docker compose restart armarius`) so it uses it.
- **Backups.** `admin backup` puts the saga and its Remote seat files
  (Invites, seats, the Log, the tunnel address) into one zip in the
  backups folder, named for the saga and the time. Give the folder with
  `--to FOLDER`, or `ARMARIUS_BACKUPS`, which the container sets to
  `/backups`. It's safe while players play, since each file is copied
  whole, between saves. It keeps the newest 30 (`--keep N` to change
  that) and never holds the host key. To restore, unzip it into the data
  folder with the listener stopped.

Anything that changes a seat asks first. Add `--yes` to skip the
question in a script. The checks are the window's own, so a passcode
under 14 characters, an Invite for a player with no passcode, or an
approval of a request that has lapsed is refused the same way.

### The host key on a server
<!-- help: guide-hosting-headless-key -->

On a desktop the host key lives in the computer's keychain. A server with
no screen has none, so the app keeps it in a file instead, but only when
told to: `ARMARIUS_KEYSTORE=file`, with `ARMARIUS_KEYS` naming the folder
(`/keys` if left out). The container image sets both, and a desktop never
needs them.

The folder must be one of its own, never inside the data folder, so the
keys never travel with the saga or its backups. Only the app's own user
may open it. The app refuses to start if anyone else can read the folder
or a key in it, if it belongs to another user, or if the app runs as the
administrator (root).

Whoever can read that folder can pretend to be your host. On a NAS that's
its administrators, and anyone who may run Docker there.

Where to keep it:

- **A private folder of its own.** Outside the data folder, owned by the
  app's user (10001) with modes 700, and with any NAS access lists
  stripped (`sudo setfacl -b FOLDER`). That much is required: the app
  refuses anything less.
- **Encrypted, where the server can.** A disk or volume the system
  encrypts protects the key if someone takes the server's drives. A
  NAS's encrypted shared folder works too, if the app's user can read
  and write it. Try it first ([When a step goes wrong](help:guide-self-hosting-trouble)
  has a test). On UGOS it can't (see [A UGREEN NAS](help:guide-self-hosting-nas)),
  so use a plain private folder there.
- **What a plain folder gives up.** It keeps the key from everyone on
  the server but its administrators, but not from someone who steals
  the drives. They could read it, and you would rotate it and send
  everyone a new Invite (see [If the key or the token leaks](help:guide-self-hosting-leak)).

Back the `keys` folder and `tunnel.env` up apart from the nightly
backups, which never hold them (see [Backups off the server](help:guide-self-hosting-backups)).

When it refuses, it says the fix. That's `sudo chown 10001:10001 FOLDER`
for a folder made by another user, as a NAS's web pages make them, and
`chmod 700` for the folder or `chmod 600` for a key others can read. For
root, start it as the image's own user (no `user: root`).

### Backups off the server
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-backups -->

**On the server, every night.** The compose file's `backup` service runs
`admin backup` when the project starts and then once a day, keeping the
newest 30. `BACKUP_EVERY`, in seconds, and `BACKUPS_KEPT` in `.env` change
that. It needs no scheduler on the server, never sees the keys, and has
no network. `docker compose logs backup` shows each one, and the zips are
in `backups`.

A NAS's own snapshots of the `armarius` folder are a good extra, but they
stay on the same disks. They hold the `keys` folder too, so never copy
them off the NAS.

**Off the server.** `admin backup` keeps copies on the server, and a
second copy elsewhere survives the server. **restic** does it well. It
encrypts everything before it leaves the server, so the copy can sit on
your desktop or in a cloud folder.

1. **Install restic** on the server (its website, restic.net, has the steps). On a NAS, its Docker image `restic/restic` works the same way. Keep its password in a file only you can read (`chmod 600`), and a copy of it somewhere safe. Without it the backups can't be opened.
2. **Make a repository.** On your desktop, over SSH: `restic -r sftp:you@desktop:/backups/armarius init`. In a cloud drive such as Dropbox or Google Drive, go through rclone (rclone.org, after setting up a remote called `cloud`) with `restic -r rclone:cloud:armarius init`.
3. **Back up each night**, after the backup service has run (from `cron` on a Linux computer): `restic -r REPOSITORY backup backups`, then `restic -r REPOSITORY forget --keep-daily 14 --keep-weekly 8 --prune`.

Back the `keys` folder and `tunnel.env` up apart, to a different restic
repository with a different password, or not at all. A lost key costs
only new Invites for
everyone, while a leaked one lets someone pretend to be your host.

### If the key or the token leaks
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-leak -->

If the `keys` folder, `tunnel.env`, or a backup of either may have been
seen by someone else:

1. **Rotate the key.** `docker exec -it armarius python main.py admin rotate-key`, then `docker compose restart armarius`. Every Invite and seat ends at once.
2. **Replace the token.** In Cloudflare's dashboard, make the tunnel a new token, or delete the tunnel and make another. Put it in `tunnel.env`, then run `docker compose up -d`.
3. **Invite everyone again.** Issue everyone a new Invite, your own desktop's seat included, and read each of them the new six words on a call.
4. **Read the Log.** Look in `admin log` for sign-ins from before you rotated that you don't recognise.

### A newer version
<!-- help: guide-self-hosting-update -->

The server never updates itself, and never checks for an App update: the
image sets `ARMARIUS_UPDATES=off`. Your own desktop still tells you when a
newer version is out (see [How to check for an App update](help:guide-app-update)).
To move the server to a newer version:

1. **Read its notes** on the Releases page.
2. **Take a backup** with `admin backup`.
3. **Set the version.** Set `ARMARIUS_VERSION` in `.env` to it.
4. **Pull and restart.** `docker compose pull`, then `docker compose up -d`.

Within the same first number (4.13 to 4.14, say) seats keep working, though
a player on another version is told the two haven't been tested together.
A new first number (5.0) means every player moves to it too, since a seat
refuses a host of another major version.

**See also:** [How to host a saga](help:guide-hosting)

**And:** [Remote seats: hosting safely](help:guide-hosting-safety), which a self-hosted saga follows too.

# How to start a saga from a sourcebook
<!-- help: guide-book-packs -->

Your troupe wants to play in the Rhine, and you'd rather not type in ten covenants and seventy magi before the first session.

The app ships a content pack for each Tribunal sourcebook it covers, written in its own words from the book's open content. A pack brings the Tribunal with its customs, its covenants and named magi, the region's places, towns, lords, churches and factions, its Peripheral Code and open quarrels, and its history as Tribunal Diary entries. Use one to begin a saga in a Tribunal already peopled, or to add that Tribunal to a saga you're running.

A region book, whose lands lie outside every Tribunal, has a pack with no Tribunal. Its covenants belong to none, its history is in one covenant's Diary, and what is coming (a yearly council, say) is in the saga's calendar.

### Starting a saga from a pack
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-start -->

1. **Start a new saga.** On the saga list, choose **New Saga**, and fill in the name, storyguide and date as usual. A pack is written as of its book's year (the Rhine's is 1220). A saga at another date keeps its own date, and the pack's records keep their years.
2. **Pick the book.** In **Start from a pack**, choose it. The list shows the shipped book packs first, then the saga starters, such as Dammërstein, then any pack in your exports folder.
3. **Read its introduction.** A pack with an introduction shows it under the choice while it's chosen: what the pack sets up and where the saga begins. The form scrolls to read it all.
4. **Choose the stories that begin.** Under the introduction is a **Begin** tick for each of the pack's stories, its threats. Untick the ones to keep for later. They come in dormant, to begin from their More when you want them.
5. **Create the saga.** It begins with everything in the pack, and the pack's book is put in play (see [The saga](help:saga)), so the book's rules and neighbour kinds show.

A saga starter is a ready-made covenant or saga shipped with the app. One built on a book pack says so under the choice (Dammërstein is built on the Rhine's). The saga then starts from that book pack first, with its stories and supplements asked about too, and the starter comes in over it, its records matched to the book's by name.

### Adding a book to a saga you already have
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-add -->

1. **Import the pack.** In the saga, choose Import Data, **A Content Pack**, and pick the pack's file from the app's `data/packs` folder, next to the program.
2. **Choose what to bring.** Untick the parts you don't want, such as the mundane world or the history. The Tribunal, if the pack has one, and its covenants always come (see [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack)).
3. **Preview, then import.** What the saga already has of the same name is kept as it is, so importing over part of the Tribunal doubles nothing.

A pack is a copy. A saga keeps what it imported and never reads the pack's file again, so a corrected pack in a new version of the app changes new sagas and new imports, not the sagas you have.

Importing the corrected pack over a saga adds what the saga doesn't have and leaves what it has, your edits included. So a correction to a record it already holds doesn't come through, and a Tribunal it already has keeps its own Code, cases and Diary. A record the new pack has renamed comes in beside the old one. The preview lists what's added, so check it first.

### Turning on a supplement
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-overlay -->

A supplement such as *Through the Aegis* sits on top of the Tribunal packs, so a saga without it matches the Tribunal book.

1. **Bring it in.** Import its file from `data/packs` the same way, or tick **With Through the Aegis** on New Saga.
2. **Check the overwrites.** It adds to each Tribunal the saga has and overwrites records where the two books differ. A form lists each overwrite, unticked if you've changed that record yourself.

It stays on, so a Tribunal pack imported later takes its part too. See [Through the Aegis on top of the Tribunal packs](help:pack-overlay-through-the-aegis).

### Bringing in the Magi of Hermes
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-magi -->

*Magi of Hermes* is a pack of fifteen magi with no Tribunal. Each is printed at Gauntlet and every fifteen years after, and all of those ages come with the pack.

1. **Import it.** Import it the same way as any pack.
2. **Set the base year.** The form asks for a **Base year**, which starts on the saga's year.
3. **Choose each magus's age.** Pick an age for each magus, or **Leave out**. A magus's year of birth is the base year less the age you choose, so pick the age they have at that year.

Conscientia joins Durenmar, whether the Rhine pack comes in first or later. If the Theban pack's sketch of Marcus Tauros is in the saga, the printed Marcus replaces it. Without this pack the sketch stays.

### Africa and the Nile
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-africa -->

*Lands of the Nile* and *Between Sand and Sea* are region packs with no Tribunal, as *The Cradle and the Crescent* is. They can be imported together. They share the Great Hunger, the Hope and the pact of 1225, and those come in once. Their stories come in dormant, except the Fifth Crusade.

### Iceland
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-iceland -->

*Land of Fire and Ice* is a region pack with no Tribunal, as *The Cradle and the Crescent* is, and it includes *The Icelandic Wars*. It brings the island, its people and law, the galdramen and trollsynir ([how to play one](help:guide-galdor)), and two long stories. The stories come in dormant, so nothing starts until you begin one.

### Antagonists
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-antagonists -->

*Antagonists* is a pack of ten recurring opponents, with no Tribunal, for any saga. Its stories come in dormant, and a person printed in two versions is a choice on the import form. With the book in play, its people with printed stats are also templates on **New Character**, named **Antagonist -**. Pick one to start from a copy of the printed record.

### Legends of Hermes
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-legends -->

*Legends of Hermes* is a pack with no Tribunal. It holds the sites, guardians and treasures of Conciatta, Fortunata, Hérisson, Hermanus and Thomae, with their stories dormant. Import it into any saga and begin a story from its More when you want it.

### What a pack puts where
<!-- help: guide-book-packs-where -->

- **The Tribunal.** Its sheet has the book's customs (the Rhine's gilds) and its officers. Its Peripheral Code is under Rulings, the open quarrels under Cases, and its Hermetic history in the Diary. The mundane history is in its summary, by era.
- **Covenants.** Sketch records, not played, each with a summary, what matters (vis, income, tensions) and the magi the book names. The count of magi the book leaves unnamed is on the covenant.
- **Magi.** Every magus the book names is a full character, with the book's stats or, where it prints none, stats inferred from its House, age and favoured Arts. Untick **Generated stats for magi** to bring those in as sketches (see [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack)). Where the book gives no age, one is assumed from the magus's rank and noted on the sheet. An un-Gifted Redcap is a companion of House Mercere with the Redcap Status, not a magus.
- **What every full character has.** A native language at 5 and a Social Status. A magus also has a House and its free Virtue, The Gift, Hermetic Magus (or a Rhine rank), Parma Magica, Magic Theory and Latin (or the Theban Tribunal's Classical Greek). A companion or grog keeps within its Virtue and Flaw limits. Generated stats and Fourth Edition conversions are built to this. A block the book prints keeps its own numbers even where it misses one, and the pack's assumptions page names it. A person the book prints only part of a block for, such as a covenant specialist's Abilities, is a sketch.
- **Places.** The regions and forests are places, with the regiones inside them and their levels and auras. Covenants and towns sit in them.
- **The mundane world.** Lords, towns, churches, schools, factions and threats, with historical arms where they're known.
- **The Bestiary.** The creatures the book prints a stat block for, such as the Rhine's boglins and Novgorod's dragons. Each is found at the place it haunts and linked from the threat or faction it belongs to. Untick **The Bestiary** when importing to leave them out.
- **Assumptions.** Where a book leaves a value out (an aura without a number, a founding year, a season), the pack fills it in, and importing it says so. Each pack's assumptions page, linked in the list above, lists those values.
- **Secrets.** What the book tells the storyguide and not the players is on the [Secrets](help:tab-secrets) of each covenant, magus, Tribunal, neighbour or creature. The few that belong to part of a record, such as a regio level, a case or a Diary entry, are at the end of its text under a **Secrets** heading.

The packs follow the Definitive Edition where a book's numbers differ from it. Each pack's assumptions page lists the corrections it makes.

**See also:** [Content packs](help:export-pack), [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack), [Tribunal](help:tribunal), [How to import and export](help:guide-import-export)

# How to fight a battle
<!-- help: guide-battle -->

The baron's knights meet the count's levies at the ford, and one of your companions is in the thick of it.

Lords of Men plays a battle as a few events, each a fight with a player character at its heart, rather than every blow. The app keeps the battle in the saga's Conflicts, between two armies, and shows it in the **Fief** of each lord it names. It works out each event's total.

### Before the battle
<!-- help: guide-battle-setup -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Open the battles.** Open the lord's sheet, a neighbour of the lord kind, and go to its **Fief** tab. Or open **Conflicts**.
2. **Add a battle.** In [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), press `a`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Open the battles.** Open the lord's page, a neighbour of the lord kind, and go to its **Fief** chapter. Or open **Conflicts**.
2. **Add a battle.** Under [Battles](help:section-nb-battles), click **Add**.
<!-- /gui -->
3. **Name it and choose the armies.** To pit two lords against each other, choose both.
4. **Fill in the combat groups.** Leave a lord's army's **combat groups** blank for its title to fill them in, and say whether it fights on home ground. Type a covenant's or a named army's groups.
5. **Add allies and mercenaries.** Add the allies the lord raises and the mercenaries they hire (see [Muster and Aftermath](help:battle-muster)).
6. **Type the Deployment Totals.** Type both commanders' totals. Their difference is the first army's **Ground** in every event (see [Battles](help:section-nb-battles)).
7. **Set the baggage and save.** Leave **baggage groups** blank for as many as each army needs (one for every ten combat groups), or type fewer. Save.

### Each event
<!-- help: guide-battle-events -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Open the event.** With the battle chosen, press `m` and choose **Battle Event**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Open the event.** Choose the battle and click **Battle Event**.
<!-- /gui -->
2. **Choose who leads it.** Choose the character at the heart of the event, the army they fight for, and its Maneuver, Enemy and Size.
3. **Check the total.** The line above Record shows the Event Total and whether it reaches this stage's Ease Factor (see [Battle Event](help:battle-event)). A total too low can't win, so pick a smaller task, or give up the initiative for +1.
4. **Play it out.** Play the event as an ordinary fight with the character and the enemies it names. Tick **Play it out as a fight** to have the app make it a fight in Conflicts.
5. **Record the result.** Back in the form, say who won and type the losing commander's Morale roll, and their rally at the next event if it failed. Click **Record**. The losing side loses combat groups, and the battle's line shows the new numbers for the next event.

# How to run a fight
<!-- help: guide-fights -->

Bandits rush the magus and two grogs on the road home, and you want the blows worked out at the table.

<!-- tui -->
1. **Make the fight.** Select **Conflicts** in the tree. In the Fights list press `a`, name the fight and its two sides, and save.
2. **Add the fighters.** With the fight chosen, press `m` and choose **Add Combatant** for each fighter: the player characters with their weapons, Bestiary creatures (as many as you like at once), or a typed stat block.
3. **Group the grogs.** To make grogs fight together, press `m` > **Form a Group**, tick its members, and choose the vanguard, and for a trained group the leader.
4. **Take the first blow.** Press `m` > **Next Blow**. The first one rolls Initiative. Choose an action and a target. **The Blow to Come** box under the fields says what it will roll.
5. **Strike.** Choose **Strike**. The panel shows the blow worked out and the chronicle. A magus can **Cast a spell** there, and anyone holding a device can **Use a device**.
6. **Play on.** **Next Round** plays the rest of a round for you. Keep going until one side has nobody standing.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->

The count's army has ringed the castle, the garrison won't come out, and the troupe wants to know how long the walls will hold.

# How to play a galdraman
<!-- help: guide-galdor -->

Your saga sails to Iceland, and a player wants a galdraman who sings the land spirits into service.

Iceland's two magus-equivalents are hedge wizards here, and both come with *Land of Fire and Ice* in play. They're Gifted companions, the equal of a magus at the table (Hedge Magic RE p. 11). They have no Magic Resistance and no Twilight, as Hedge Magic gives every hedge tradition (p. 10). That's the app's conversion. *Land of Fire and Ice*, written for the 4th edition, lets galdramen enter Twilight (p. 50) and gives trollsynir Magic Resistance (p. 53).

### A traditional galdraman
<!-- help: guide-galdor-traditional -->

1. **Make a Gifted companion.** On the worksheet, give the character The Gift.
2. **Join the tradition.** Choose the tradition Traditional Galdramen, whose chapter is Galdor. Then use the Concept step's **Add the tradition's Virtues**. Its Virtues come free: Free Expression, Second Sight, Premonitions, Galdor Conjuring and Galdor Enchantment.
3. **Take the ground's Flaw.** Take Deleterious Circumstances (touching the ground) as well. The totals are halved on the ground either way.
4. **Spend their xp.** Spend it on Galdor Wisdom, Music, Bargain and two Song Talents. A Song can't pass Galdor Wisdom, and it goes up by practice and experience, never by teaching.

In play, a galdraman works in three ways:

1. **Sing** with [Conjure a Spirit](help:galdor-conjure) for anything up to Concentration.
2. **Go to a lord** with [Bargain with a Lord](help:galdor-bargain) for anything longer.
3. **Make things** with [Bind an Item](help:galdor-bind), after a deal.

For longevity, strike a deal with biarg-galdor, conjure the biarg spirit as a ritual, and enter the conjuring total as a Longevity Ritual on the character's sheet. The mead takes a pawn a decade of their age each year. The app doesn't track Galdor Wisdom falling to 0 in another country.

Curse and Hamfarir roll through [Cast a Power](help:hedge-cast). Curse has its Ease Factors as guidelines. A nidstong is a Curse that rolls daily while it stands.

### A trollson
<!-- help: guide-galdor-trollson -->

A trollson takes the tradition Trollsynir, whose chapter is Trollskap. They cast as a gruagach does, with Jotnar for Pictish (see [hedge wizards](help:guide-hedge-magic)). They must have Giant Blood (*Land of Fire and Ice* p. 52) and, as the app's conversion to a gruagach, No Tattoo Magic.

### Fylgjur
<!-- help: guide-galdor-fylgja -->

Premonitions brings a fylgja, of Might 10 and Size -3 at most. A trollson's is the Fetch.

- **Mighty Fylgja** gives 10 Might and the next of Phantom, Spirit Voice and Corporeality. Take it as often as you like. The book makes it one Virtue with points put into it (p. 54), and the app takes it again for each.
- **Large Fylgja** sets its Size. The first allows Size 0, and each more +1.

The chapter's Bound Beings shows the fylgjur a character has. Hamfarir is the Virtue that lets them use them.

### Other Icelanders
<!-- help: guide-galdor-others -->

A schooled galdraman is a Learned Magician, and a seithkona a Folk Witch with Hamfarir. A bondi, a landowner or a godi takes that Social Status, which sits beside Galdraman for a character who is both.

<!-- help: guide-tribunal-opening -->

2. **Move a hearing, if you like.** Change a case's day to move its hearing and vote.

### Hearing a case
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-hearing -->

1. **Choose the speakers.** Fengheld v. Keep comes on its day. Choose the **Prosecutor** and **Defender** from the drop-downs, which list the magi present, each with their covenant.
2. **Fill an empty side.** Leandro is absent, so the defence starts as **A Guernicus advocate**, who defends an accused who didn't come. A charge no one else will prosecute can be brought by **The Presiding Quaesitor**.
3. **Rate a speaker with no scores.** A speaker with no debate scores written down, such as the advocate or a sketch magus, gets **debates as**: Middling, Able or Formidable.
4. **Debate.** Leave **Levels lost** blank and the debate is rolled, round by round. Or play the debate out at the table and enter both sides' levels lost. Either way, the side that loses fewer wins, and the difference is its Argument Strength.
5. **Or settle it in private.** If both sides accept the Presiding Quaesitor's view at the private hearing, tick **Settled in private** instead. The case has no debate and no vote, and goes straight on to its ruling.
6. **Hear the motion.** For the motion, the proposer speaks for it and **The Tribunal** against, since a motion is debated against the whole hall.

<!-- help: guide-tribunal-vote -->

1. **Read the leanings.** The vote follows its hearing. Each covenant is listed with its leaning and why, such as "the case is against it" or "Warm (+1) toward Fengheld, which brings it".
2. **Change a leaning if the story says otherwise.** The tally under the form follows as you do.
3. **Break a tie.** If the votes tie, choose how the Praeco casts.

<!-- /gui -->

<!-- help: guide-tribunal-rulings -->

2. **Set the fine and the Code.** Set any fine (3 pawns of Aquam, paid by Keep) and whether the ruling goes into the Peripheral Code, and save.
3. **Fill in the blanks.** The template's blanks come next. Fill them in, and the sentence under the form shows the ruling as you go.

> Keep holds the Rhine ford's vis for 7 years, until it is debated again.

The {years} blank sets a term, as a written-out ruling's **term** in years does. The templates are the saga's own (see [Hold Tribunal](help:hold-tribunal)).

### Closing, and what comes back
<!-- help: guide-tribunal-closing -->

1. **Check the treasury.** The Treasury step shows the balance and the loans due.
2. **Close the meeting.** Closing decides each case with a ruling, writes the Acta into the Tribunal's Diary and sets the next meeting. Each step held has already written its minutes there.

Closing carries out each ruling, the fine owed to the treasury among them. The book gives a third of the fine to the prosecutor (True Lineages p. 57), but the app records it as owed to the treasury.

The term comes back on its own. [Ahead](help:section-ahead) lists the season before it ends (Spring 1235) and the season it ends (Summer 1235). Advancing into Spring 1235 says the term ends next season. Advancing into Summer 1235 says it has ended and puts "Fengheld v. Keep (debated again)" on the Docket for the next meeting, with the old ruling in its charge.

# How to format your notes
<!-- help: guide-formatting -->

You'd like a covenant's Secrets to read as a tidy list, with each name in bold. The longer text you write yourself, such as a Biography, a covenant's Description, a record's Secrets or a book's Description, can carry a little formatting. A field that takes it says so in the hint under its box. You type the marks into the box as plain text, and they turn into formatting wherever the app shows what you wrote.

### The marks
<!-- help: guide-formatting-marks -->

| Type this | To show |
| --- | --- |
| `**words**` | **bold** |
| `*words*` | *italic* |
| `***words***` | bold and italic together |
| `~~words~~` | ~~struck through~~ |
| `- ` at the start of a line (or `* `, `+ `) | a bulleted item |
| `1. ` at the start of a line (or `1) `) | a numbered item, with your own number |
| two spaces before the `- ` or `1. ` | an item nested under the one above |
| a blank line | a new paragraph |

A mark needs words right beside it, so `5 * 3` stays as written, and so does a mark left unclosed. To show a mark itself, put a backslash before it: `\*` shows `*`. Emphasis stops at a blank line and at the next list item, so a stray mark can't run on through the rest of the text.

### An example
<!-- help: guide-formatting-example -->

Typed into a covenant's Secrets:

```
Hidden Resources (not yet found):

- **Matthaios's sealed study**, in the Echoing Halls.
- **Nikephoros's vis cache**, under the Watchtower: ~~60~~ 40 pawns.
```

The sheet shows the first line as written, then two bulleted items, each opening with its name in bold and the 60 struck through. A long item wraps with its lines starting under its words, not under the bullet.

### What it doesn't do
<!-- help: guide-formatting-limits -->

There are no headings, tables or links. The sheets give each piece of text its own heading already. Fields that are read one entry a line, such as a case's Evidence or a random table's Rows, take no formatting, since the app reads their lines itself. The box you type in shows the marks, not the formatting. Save to see it.

# How to install a magic item in a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item -->

Your magus has a cauldron that always stays at the right heat, and it belongs in the laboratory, not on a shelf. An enchanted device kept in a laboratory can improve the laboratory itself (Ars Magica Definitive Edition, Chapter 8: Laboratory, Magic Items for Laboratories, p. 298; Covenants p. 121).

### Two kinds of benefit
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-kinds -->

The item is installed as a Free **Magic Item** Virtue, or as an **Enchantment** of the laboratory itself, also Free. A very large item's Magic Item Virtue is Minor, and so takes a point of space. One Virtue covers all of an item's effects. Each effect then grants a laboratory Virtue or Flaw it duplicates, as the cauldron does, or buys points by its level, so a level 20 lectern could give Texts +2, or Safety +1. [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items) gives the rules for both, and for a device kept elsewhere.

### Install one in play
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-play -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **Keep the device in the laboratory.** Press `5` for the laboratory's Inventory tab and `a` in [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) to add it with its effects. Or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. **Open Magic Items.** Press `4` for the [Attributes tab](help:tab-lab-attributes) and `a` in [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. **Describe the install.** Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then press Next.
4. **Choose what it gives.** For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog. Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered, since those are Features. For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **Keep the device in the laboratory.** Choose **Stores** in the laboratory's rail and add it under [Enchanted Devices](help:section-lab-devices) with its effects. Or set an existing device's **Held by** to the laboratory.
2. **Open Magic Items.** Choose **Specializations** in the rail and click **Add** under [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items).
3. **Describe the install.** Choose the **Effect**, how the item is **Installed as**, and what it **Gives**, then click Next.
4. **Choose what it gives.** For a Virtue or Flaw, pick it from the catalog. Feature Virtues and Focus Flaws aren't offered, since those are Features. For points, choose up to three Specializations and two Characteristics with their points. The form says the effect's level, and refuses more points than the level buys.
<!-- /gui -->

The Virtues & Flaws list shows a granted Virtue as "from" its item, and the Magic Item Virtue with its item's name. The points join the laboratory's Specializations and Characteristics, each named "(magic item)" where its sources are listed.

### Install one while setting up a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-creation -->

1. **Choose Install a magic item.** On a new laboratory's **Setting Up** step, **Install a magic item** offers **A new enchanted device**, or a device already kept in the laboratory.
2. **Fill in the three forms.** A new device takes three forms, one after another. The first is the device, kept in this laboratory, then its effect, then what the effect gives, as above.
3. **Check the list.** The step lists what each magic item gives.

The device and its effect are kept as each is saved. So cancelling later leaves them in the laboratory's Enchanted Devices, ready to install another time.

For a starting laboratory, the covenant's Build Points pay for the device as for any enchanted item, 2 for every 5 levels of each effect, on the covenant's Resources step under Enchanted items. Its effects count against the Power Level's highest effect level. A Virtue the item grants costs no Build Points.

### When the item is a Feature
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-feature -->

An item can instead be made a Lesser or Greater Feature, for what a Feature brings that points bought by level don't, such as Aesthetics or a Focus. Then add it under [Features](help:section-lab-features), not here, and its Feature Virtue takes space and costs Build Points as usual.

### What the app does for you
<!-- help: guide-lab-magic-item-app -->

**The app works out** the effect's final level, the points it can buy, the space and Build Points each Virtue takes, and the laboratory's totals with the item's points added.

**You type** the device and its effects, and the install as above. Deleting the device or an effect takes away what it gave (see [Magic Items](help:section-lab-magic-items)).

<!-- help: guide-mythic-locations -->

The troupe is heading for the Cursewood, or the Temple of Mercury, and the site has rules of its own. Mythic Locations gives four such set pieces. The app holds the Temple of Mercury's rituals as catalog spells with their rules, and the other three are run at the table, with their rules summed up here.

### The Mercurian rituals
<!-- help: guide-ml-mercurian -->

The Temple of Mercury's library holds Lab Texts for nine rituals (Mythic Locations pp. 96-100). They're in the spell catalog with source ML, and a ritual's details panel says how it's learned.

- **Reading** one for its gist takes a day and needs Latin and a Magic Theory score. It gives the Arts, the magnitude and roughly what the ritual does, but can't be cast or invented from (p. 97).
- **Translating** it into a Hermetic Lab Text is lab work. Each season adds the Lab Total in the ritual's Arts less its level, and at twice the level the copy is done. That means a Lab Total above the level, and three times the level for a single season. Later translations get a bonus equal to the highest magnitude of any ritual already translated (one bonus, not a sum), but invention doesn't. Experimenting is allowed (p. 97).
- **Inventing** it from the Hermetic copy is the usual Lab Text rule, one season when the Lab Total is at least the level. Range, Duration and Target can't be changed. Any magus with the copy may invent it (p. 97).
- **Casting** is as a Hermetic Ritual of its Arts. It can be dispelled like one, but anyone with Magic Theory sees it isn't Hermetic (p. 97).

Most rituals need a temple consecrated to Mercury, made by Consecration of Mercury's House, which also gives the temple a Magic aura of 1 where it had none (p. 99).

- **Purification** takes off a Decrepitude score, five years of apparent age and up to five Aging Points. It can't be cast on oneself (pp. 97-98). The player chooses which Characteristics the Aging Points come off, as the Definitive Edition also leaves to the player (DE pp. 391-392). The app takes them off those with the most first unless another choice is given.
- **Transformation of Flesh to Spirit** raises the aura by one, to at most 10. It works only if none of the caster's Arts is under twice the aura and the Penetration is above five times it (p. 99).
- **Wizard's Initiation** never works, and adds a botch die (pp. 99, 101).

Five integrations open from these rituals, offered as research goals (pp. 100-101):

- a CrVi 50 aura-making guideline, 30 points
- a CrVi 55 aura-raising guideline, 30 points
- a Cr(Re)Vi 20 regio entrance, 30 points
- the Mercurian Magic Virtue, 45 points
- a mended Wizard's Initiation that gives The Gift, 60 points

Their kinds follow the Definitive Edition's sizes for a breakthrough, about 30 points for a Minor, 45 for a Major and 60 for a Hermetic (DE p. 277; Houses of Hermes: True Lineages p. 28).

The aqueduct that fills the Scrying Pool takes 20 man-years to repair and five workers every season to keep running. Each season without them, roll a simple die against 3 + the seasons neglected in a row. Under it, the aqueduct clogs. Its spring gives 5 pawns of Aquam vis each Summer solstice (pp. 96-97).

### Trekking in the Cursewood
<!-- help: guide-ml-cursewood -->

The Cursewood is crossed in five stages, each costing a Long-Term Fatigue level (Mythic Locations pp. 37-40). Choose a leader and a guide for each stage. Together they can lead as many people as their Leadership scores added together, and each person over that adds a hazard.

Each stage, the guide rolls for the terrain (p. 37):

| Terrain | Roll |
|---|---|
| Forest trail | Sta + Survival |
| Forest floor | Per + Survival |
| Swamp wading | Sta + Athletics |
| Boat | Dex + Profession: Boating |
| Swimming | Str + Swimming |
| Hill path | Dex + Athletics |
| Hill climb | Str + Athletics |

The roll takes -1 for each member besides the leader and guide, and +1 for each other member who has the Ability. It also takes off the heaviest Encumbrance and the guide's Wound and Fatigue penalties (p. 37).

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 0 or botch | Lost, with no progress and four hazards |
| 3 | Four hazards |
| 6 | Three hazards |
| 9 | Two hazards |
| 12 | One hazard |
| 18+ | The first time, one hazard and +3 next stage; later, no hazards |

A stage takes an hour for each hazard, at least one. Retreating takes an hour a stage, without rolls. Short-Term Fatigue takes twice as long to recover here. A hazard during the night means no Fatigue is recovered. Eating or drinking anything from the wood gives a Light Wound (p. 38).

The wood is an Infernal aura of 5. That's -5 to Recovery rolls, +5 to Damage, and five extra botch dice on physical rolls, the trek rolls included (p. 40). Its spirit's direct attacks must Penetrate, with +7 from the spirit's Magic regio, against Magic Resistance at -5 (p. 39). Forest Lore 3 or more tells a character more about the spirit, and from 7 lets them pass alone unhindered (p. 37).

### The Faerie Toy Market
<!-- help: guide-ml-toy-market -->

The merchants of Devil's Lane sell faerie toys made like Hermetic items (Mythic Locations pp. 107-112).

- **Prices** are in vis, of any Form and any realm's taint. The lowest price is the pawns the item would take to make, +1, and +1 more for an effect of the sixth magnitude or higher. A charged item costs at least a pawn a charge, a mundane item 1 pawn. Merchants open at twice that and haggle down. They ask for odd payments that hold the right vis, and refuse one whose vis was taken out first (p. 107).
- **Commissions** cost twice the vis, in Arts that suit the item, paid in advance. The opening price is four times the vis (p. 107).
- **Lifetime.** Every toy is glamour and stops working seven years after purchase, charges included (p. 107).
- **The workshops** do a Lab Total of 100 of work a season for each merchant. A character can negotiate up to that much for themselves. Unused work doesn't carry over, and characters can't pool theirs. The elves can make any Hermetic item, faerie Ranges, Durations and Targets included, but not a talisman (p. 112).
- **Walter the book merchant** sells glamour books that last seven years, at 1 pawn for a tractatus and 1 a level for a summa. A season with one is practice at Quality 5. His exercise slates last a year and a day, for 1 pawn (p. 109).

### Hunted by wolves
<!-- help: guide-ml-wolves -->

A wolf hunt has five phases, in this order (Mythic Locations pp. 139-142).

**The stalk.** The pack finds the trail with Per + Awareness and follows it with Per + Hunt, usually against 9. A group is 3 easier to follow, and a lone, careful quarry adds its Stealth. Then the pack rolls Dex + Stealth against the prey's Per + Awareness. Each success brings it 10 + Qik paces closer. Two failures in a row, three in all, or getting within ten paces moves the hunt to the encounter (p. 140).

**The encounter.** Prey that holds its ground is attacked. Prey that runs starts the rush (p. 140).

**The rush.** Rounds of six seconds. Each side rolls Qik + Athletics - Encumbrance + stress die. The winner moves the gap by 10 + Qik - Encumbrance paces on four legs, or 6 + Qik - Encumbrance on two. Both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 9 each round, or lose a Short-Term Fatigue level. At zero paces comes the kill. Wolves usually turn to the chase when they lose a Fatigue level, or once the gap passes 100 paces (p. 140).

**The chase.** Rounds of five to thirty minutes. Each round both roll Sta + Athletics - Encumbrance against 12, or lose a Long-Term Fatigue level. A group rolls for its most tired member, but wolves for their freshest. The wolves' Pursuit Total is Per + Hunt - Fatigue + modifier + stress die, with +3 against two-legged prey and -3 for two-legged hunters of four-legged prey. The prey's Evasion Total is Dex + Athletics - Fatigue + stress die, with -6 to +6 for who knows the ground. If the prey wins four rounds, it escapes. If the wolves win four, it's cornered (p. 141).

**The kill.** The pack attacks as a trained group led by its leader, and the rest cut off escape. A wolf may try a trip, a Brawl Attack against Defense. A fleeing or unaware target defends only with Brawl or Evasion. Beating it by 3 or more knocks the target down, and its Defense is Qik - Encumbrance + stress die until it stands (pp. 141-142).

# How to set up a threat's clock
<!-- help: guide-threat-clock -->

A threat can move on whenever the troupe fails something the saga asks
of it, as a clock fills in a Powered by the Apocalypse game. The
Watchtower's due to Abnoba each Winter, unpaid, brings the Hollow Crown
a step nearer.

1. **Make the threat move along a track.** Make a New Threat, or Edit
   one, and set **Moves by** to *A track* or *An omen count*. Set its
   position to where it stands now, 0 for a fresh one.
2. **Give its stages their steps.** Add each stage with the step it
   **Comes at**, such as 2, 4 and 6. The last stage's step is the
   clock's size, so the threat's page shows ■■□□□□ 2 of 6.
3. **Tie a place's trigger to it.** In the place's Regio, add or edit a
   dated trigger, such as *Abnoba's due*, Winter, each year. Choose the
   threat in **Moves a threat**, and how many steps failing it moves the
   threat.
4. **Play the seasons.** When the trigger's season is played, Advance
   Season asks. **Failed** moves the threat on, and **Kept** leaves it.
   Either is written in the threat's Diary and the place's, and the
   stage it reaches comes.
5. **Push it back when the troupe acts.** If the troupe thwarts it some
   other way, choose **Delay or Hasten** in the threat's More and move it
   back, with the reason.

A threat moving by fixed dates has a clock too, with a square for each
stage. Delay or Hasten moves all its stages still to come by the
seasons you give.

**See also:** [A threat](help:tab-nb-threat), [Triggers](help:section-nb-triggers), [The World Turns](help:world-turns), [Delay or Hasten](help:threat-delay)

# How to follow a story from its start to a threat
<!-- help: guide-story-hook -->

A season's work can start a story that comes later. A salamander drawn
by Aurelia's fire spell hangs about her laboratory, and may want to
become her familiar (a story event may come some time after the
experiment, DE p. 276). The app keeps each one as an open Story Event
until it's told, or grows into a threat.

1. **See where stories come from.** An experiment's story event writes
   one in the covenant's Diary, concerning the magus. A Disaster's
   covenant story writes one concerning the covenant (DE p. 275), and
   Suggest a Story on a covenant's Hooks writes one when it's accepted.
2. **Add your own.** In the covenant's Diary, add a Story Event, Open,
   with what it **Concerns**.
3. **Find them.** The Region's [Open Stories](help:section-region-stories)
   lists every open one of the troupe's covenants. A character's or
   laboratory's Connections lists its own.
4. **Take it up.** Each time the troupe plays some of it, **Take Up the
   Tale** writes a chapter with the season, and what was done, met or
   avoided (see [Take Up the Tale](help:take-up-tale)). The story's
   details list its chapters.
5. **Tell it.** When the troupe plays it out, Edit it and mark it
   **Resolved**. The app asks for its last chapter, how it ended.
6. **Or let it grow.** Left alone, it can become a threat of the Region.
   **Grow into a Threat** makes one from its words, and marks the story
   Grown.
7. **Give the threat its stages.** Add them, and give it a clock if a
   place's trigger should move it (see
   [How to set up a threat's clock](help:guide-threat-clock)).
8. **Take up the tale on the threat.** From then on, its chapters go in
   the threat's Diary. Recording a stage's outcome can take one too, in
   What the troupe did.

**See also:** [Diary](help:tab-cov-diary), [Grow into a Threat](help:grow-story), [A threat](help:tab-nb-threat)

# How to keep a covenant on the move
<!-- help: guide-mobile-covenant -->

Crisp Winds lives on a flotilla that follows a route round the Mediterranean,

1. **Mark the water.** The book packs already mark their ports, anchorages and
   seas. For anywhere else, add the sea and the harbours it uses as Places in

# How to draw up and keep a charter
<!-- help: guide-charter -->

Four magi found Dammërstein in the Rhine and swear the Charter of Arae
Flaviae. Years later, one of them breaks it.

1. **Start from a template.** In the covenant's Charter, choose
   **Use a Template** (More on the charter) and the Charter of Arae
   Flaviae. Its oath, its 25 clauses under the charter's sections and its
   seven-season probation are filled in with the covenant's and the
   Tribunal's names. Used unaltered, it counts as ratified (Covenants p.
   33).
2. **Swear it.** **Edit Charter** sets when it was sworn and its Official
   Seal.
3. **Seal it.** **Seal the Charter** ticks each founder, with the season
   and their standing. A newcomer sealed as a probationary member serves
   the charter's probation, and the charter tells you when it has run.
4. **Make it yours.** Amend clauses with Edit, or add your own. Any change
   ends the automatic ratification, so tick **Notarized by a Quaesitor**
   once one has seen it. To reuse it for another covenant, **Save as
   Template**, for any Tribunal or the Rhine's alone.
5. **Record a breach.** Choose the clause and **Break a Clause**, then say
   when and how.
6. **Censure the breaker.** **Censure** says what the covenant did. That
   may be privileges withheld, their standing lowered, or expulsion.
   Withheld privileges don't take their laboratory, unless the charter
   says so.
7. **Expel, and tidy up.** On expulsion the app asks whether to take them
   off the covenant's lists now. Say No to move their books and vis
   first. The charter reminds you, and Advance Season asks again each
   season.
8. **Take it to Tribunal.** A charter that binds under the Peripheral
   Code, notarized or sworn in Provence, can be broken as a Low Crime.
   **Charge at Tribunal** puts the case on the Tribunal's docket.

**See also:** [The charter](help:section-cov-charter), [Clauses](help:section-cov-clauses)

# How to update the app
<!-- help: guide-update -->

A new version of Armarius is out, and the troupe wants to play on it
before the next session.

1. **Agree on it with the troupe.** Move to the new version together. An
   older version still opens a saga a newer one saved, but it says so and

2. **See which version you have.** The title page shows it, and so does
   the About page in Help.
<!-- tui -->
   The saga list shows it under the list too.
<!-- /tui -->
3. **Download the new one.** The app tells you when one is out (see
   [How to check for an App update](help:guide-app-update)), and its
   **Download page** opens the Release. Or find it yourself on the app's
   GitHub page, under **Releases**. Read the new version's notes, then
   download the zip for your system, named like
   `Armarius-X.Y.Z-windows.zip`.
4. **Unzip it beside the old one.** Extract it next to the old version's
   folder. It makes a folder of its own, `Armarius-X.Y.Z`.
5. **Start the new version.** Start **Armarius** from the new folder, or

### Running it from the source
<!-- help: guide-update-source -->

1. **Get the new source.** In the folder you cloned with git, run
   `git fetch --tags`, then `git checkout vX.Y.Z` with the new version's
   number: each release is a tag. (`git pull` on `main` gets the newest
   work instead, which may not be released yet.) Or download the
   release's **Source code (zip)** and extract it beside the old folder.
2. **Start it as before.** On Windows, double-click `run-gui.bat`. On any
   system, run `uv run python gui.py` for the desktop version or
   `uv run python main.py` for the terminal version, in the folder. Both
   bring the app's libraries up to date first, so the first start after an
   update takes longer.

# How to check for an App update
<!-- help: guide-app-update -->

You'd like to know when a newer version of Armarius is out, without
watching its GitHub page.

1. **Let it check.** Once a day at most, when it starts, the app asks
   GitHub for the latest Release. It sends only its own version, and the
   check never holds the app up. If GitHub can't be reached, it says
   nothing and tries again next time.
2. **See the notice.**
<!-- gui -->
   When a newer version is out, the title page shows "! Armarius X.Y.Z is
   available" beside the version line, and the settings gear at the top
   right wears a small "!" seal. Click either to open the
   [Settings window](help:settings-window) at its Update row.
<!-- /gui -->
<!-- tui -->
   When a newer version is out, the title page lists **Update** last, with
   "Armarius X.Y.Z is available" beside it. The first saga you open shows

   Ctrl+P and choose **Update**, to open the
   [Update screen](help:update-screen).
<!-- /tui -->
3. **Read what's new.** The Update row shows the new version, its date and
   the first lines of its notes. **What's new** shows all of them.
4. **Get it.** **Download page** opens the Release's page in your browser.
   Then follow [How to update the app](help:guide-update): the app doesn't
   download or install anything itself. A copy run from the source says
   to pull `main` instead.
5. **Or skip it.** **Skip this version** hides the notice until a newer

the environment variable `ARMARIUS_UPDATES=off` turns every check off,
Check now included. A copy a host runs for a player, the host itself, and
a saga self-hosted on a server never check.

   Last Season** beside Set Date.
<!-- /gui -->
   It goes back one advance at a time, up to five, with no files to copy
   (see [Undoing a mistake](help:guide-seasons-undo)). Use the steps below
   only when it can't help.
2. **Close the app.** Close every window of Armarius on this computer. If
   you're hosting the saga, that ends your players' connections too, so
   tell them first.
3. **Find the saga's file.** In your [save folder](help:saga-picker),
   open `sagas`. Each saga is one file named after it, such as
   `the_rhine_tribunal.json` for The Rhine Tribunal.
4. **Copy it somewhere safe first.** Copy the saga's file out of the
   `sagas` folder, to your Desktop say, before you change anything. Not
   inside `sagas`, where the app would read it as a saga. If the backup
   turns out worse, this copy puts you back where you started.
5. **Choose the backup.** Open `sagas/backups`. The file with the saga's
   own name is the newest backup, taken just before the last Advance
   Season or downtime run. The one ending `.1.json` is from the advance
   before, and so on back to `.4.json`. A file with `.after` in its name
   is the saga just after that advance.
6. **Undoing an undo?** The saga as it stood just before the last Undo
   Last Season is kept there too, as `<name>.undone.json`. Use it as you
   would a backup.
7. **Copy it over the saga's file.** Copy the backup into `sagas` and give
   it the saga's own file name, replacing the file there. A backup such as
   `the_rhine_tribunal.2.json` becomes `the_rhine_tribunal.json`.
8. **Open the saga.** Start the app and open the saga. Its date is the
   season the backup was taken in. If the app says the saga can't be
   opened, it says why. Try an older backup, or copy your safe copy back.

Copy the saga's file somewhere else from time to time yourself (see
[Keeping backups](help:guide-import-export-backups)). A saga hosted on a
server or NAS has its own nightly backups (see
[Backups off the server](help:guide-self-hosting-backups)).
<!-- gui -->
A saga you joined from your own computer is restored on the host's
computer, by the storyguide who hosts it.
<!-- /gui -->

**See also:** [Undoing a mistake](help:guide-seasons-undo), [Keeping backups](help:guide-import-export-backups), [Advancing a season](help:advance-season)

# How to set your character a goal
<!-- help: guide-player-goals -->

Your magus means to reach Creo 15 by 1230, and you'd like the app to plan
the seasons that get there.

1. **Find Notes & Goals.** Open your character's
<!-- tui -->
   Personality tab.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   Description chapter.
<!-- /gui -->
   **Notes & Goals** shows on a character a player plays or stewards. Its
   first row is your private notes, and each goal follows.
2. **Add the goal.**
<!-- tui -->
   Press `a`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   Click **Add**.
<!-- /gui -->
   Type the **Goal** in your own words, such as "Become the covenant's
   finest enchanter".
3. **Give it a score to reach.** Choose the **Art or Ability**, type the
   **Score**, and the year in **By the end of**. Leave Art or Ability
   blank for a goal with no number to it.
4. **Share it, or keep it.** Leave **Share with the troupe** unticked, and
   only the character's players, its Steward and its storyguide read the
   goal. Tick it, and anyone who reads the character does. Save the goal.
5. **Plan the way there.** Choose the goal and
<!-- tui -->
   press `m` for More, then **Plan the Study Path**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   click **Plan**.
<!-- /gui -->
   [Study Path](help:study-path) opens filled in from it. **Find** shows
   the path, and **Plan These Seasons** plans it. Seasons already planned
   stay as they are.
6. **Write your notes.** Choose the **Private notes** row and
<!-- tui -->
   press `e`.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   click **Edit**.
<!-- /gui -->
   They're for plans and suspicions, such as what your magus really thinks
   of the princeps, and take **bold** and *italic*.

No other player sees your notes, nor that there are any, and an export
leaves them out.

**See also:** [Notes & Goals](help:section-char-notes), [Study Path](help:study-path), [Teaching Requests](help:teaching-requests)

# How to report a bug
<!-- help: guide-report-bug -->

Something went wrong, or the app doesn't match the book, and you'd like it
fixed. A bug report on the project's GitHub page does that.

1. **Open a new issue.** On the
   [Armarius repository](https://github.com/apoapsis138/armarius), open
   **Issues**, then **New issue**, and choose **Bug report**. (A
   **Feature request** asks for something the app doesn't do yet.) You
   need a free GitHub account.
2. **Say what happened, and how.** Write what you did, step by step, and
   what you saw, so someone else can make it happen again.
3. **Give the version.**
<!-- tui -->
   It's in the title bar, as "Armarius v4.13.0", and on Help's About page.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
   It's on the title page, under Armarius's name, and on Help's About
   page.
<!-- /gui -->
   For a saga on a headless host, it's `ARMARIUS_VERSION` in its `.env`
   (see [How to self-host a saga](help:guide-self-hosting)).
4. **Add whatever helps.** The form's last box, **Anything else**, is
   for where it runs (the Desktop app, the Terminal app on your computer
   or served to browsers or SSH, a Remote seat in a hosted saga, or a
   headless host), your operating system, and any error text the app
   showed.
5. **If the app closed itself without a word**, open `crash.log` in your
   save folder and paste what it holds into **Anything else**. The Sagas
   page names the folder (see [How to change the save folder](help:guide-save-folder)).
6. **If it's about the rules,** give the book and printed page in
   **Anything else**. The Definitive Edition wins where a supplement
   disagrees. If you don't know the page, say so.
7. **If records are involved,** attach an Export of just those records
   (see [How to import and export](help:guide-import-export)).

**Never attach your whole saga file.** The repository is public, so
anyone can read an issue and everything attached to it. An Export of the
few records the bug touches is all that's needed.

**A security problem goes elsewhere.** If someone could see or change
what they shouldn't, such as a player reading another's Secrets, don't
open an issue. Report it privately from the repository's **Security**
tab, with **Report a vulnerability**.

**Questions and ideas** go to the repository's
[Discussions](https://github.com/apoapsis138/armarius/discussions): ask how
to do something, or share an idea before it becomes an issue. For
installing and setting up, the
[FAQ](https://apoapsis138.github.io/armarius/faq/) may already answer it.

**See also:** [How to update the app](help:guide-update), [How to import and export](help:guide-import-export)
