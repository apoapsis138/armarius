# Getting started
<!-- help: guide-getting-started -->

This guide takes you from the start screen to a saga you can play: a saga with its books and optional rules chosen, a first covenant, its characters and their laboratories. It also shows how to find your way around the app. Read it the first time you use the app, or when you start a new saga.

### Create and open a saga
<!-- help: guide-getting-started-saga -->

<!-- tui -->
1. The app opens on its [title page](help:title-page). Choose **New Saga**: the app goes to the [saga picker](help:saga-picker), which lists the sagas saved on this computer ("No sagas yet" the first time), and opens its New Saga form.
2. (From the saga picker itself, press `n` for the same form.) The Create a New Saga form asks for a Name and a Storyguide (both required), a Start Year and Start Season, and an optional one-line Description. Choose Create.
3. The new saga appears in the list. Highlight it with the arrow keys and press Enter to open it in the [workspace](help:workspace).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. The app opens on its [title page](help:title-page). Choose **New Saga**: the app goes to the [Sagas page](help:saga-picker), which shows the sagas saved on this computer as cards ("No sagas yet" the first time), and opens its New Saga form.
2. (From the Sagas page itself, click **New Saga** above the cards for the same form.) The Create a New Saga form asks for a Name and a Storyguide (both required), a Start Year and Start Season, and an optional one-line Description. Choose Create.
3. The new saga opens at once, on [its page](help:saga). Next time, click its card on the Sagas page, or choose **Open Saga** on the title page to go back to the saga you had open last.
<!-- /gui -->

To begin with a Tribunal and its covenants already written, choose a pack in **Start from a pack**: a sourcebook's pack shipped with the app (see [How to start a saga from a sourcebook](help:guide-book-packs)) or one in your exports folder (see [Content packs](help:export-pack)). A pack imported into a saga that already exists can leave parts out, such as the mundane world or the history (see [Choosing what a pack imports](help:import-pack)).

The saga is saved as soon as it is made, and every change after that is saved as you make it: there is no Save command to remember.

### Choose the books and optional rules
<!-- help: guide-getting-started-rules -->

<!-- tui -->
1. The workspace opens with the navigation tree on the left. Its top node is [the saga](help:saga); with it chosen, the pane on the right shows the current date, the books in play and the optional rules.
2. Choose **Saga Content** in the tree, press Enter, and press `e` on Books in Play. Tick the books your troupe uses: catalog pickers then hide Virtues, spells and other entries from books not in play, and everything a supplement adds (its activities, optional rules and kinds of books) switches off with it. See [Saga Content](help:saga-content).
3. Back on the saga node, press `e` (the footer calls it Edit Saga).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. [The saga's page](help:saga) has ten chapters, listed in the rail on the left: The Troupe, The Covenants, The Region, The Atlas, The Bestiary, Conflicts, Seasons, Rules and Content, Saga Settings and Saga Management.
2. In **Rules and Content**, click the **Saga Content** card. On its **Books** chapter, click **Edit** on Books in Play and tick the books your troupe uses (or click one card's **In play** chip to switch just that book): catalog pickers then hide Virtues, spells and other entries from books not in play, and everything a supplement adds (its activities, optional rules and kinds of books) switches off with it. See [Saga Content](help:saga-content).
3. Click the saga's name in the trail to go back to its page, open its **Saga Settings** chapter. A card's switch changes it at once, and clicking the card explains it in the Inspector.
<!-- /gui -->
4. It lists the optional rules, each off until you turn it on: Simple dice only, Dominion Magic Resistance, Harsher Infernal vis, Starting income by number of magi, Enforce creation rules strictly, Any spell parameter and Extended book Quality. The line under each says what it does.
5. **Keep season records** keeps a record of each season under Seasons. Leave it on unless you only want the characters' Diaries to keep them.
   **Medallion style** chooses how the desktop draws the seasons as medallions (on the band at the top of every page, the Seasons page, the calendars and a siege's page): stained glass (the default), an illuminated letter's adornment, a wax seal, Limoges enamel, a Book of Hours roundel, or pen and wash.
   **Magi / Companions / Grogs with nothing planned** say what a season left unplanned gives when it is advanced: Work / Exposure (the default) or Nothing. **Name Idle Non-Player Characters** (on the terminal, Be notified when and which non-player characters do not have seasonal activity) (off by default) makes Advance Season name non-player characters with nothing planned too. See [Unplanned seasons](help:advance-unplanned).
6. On the terminal, Save. The saga's summary now lists your books and the optional rules turned on.

You can come back to these settings at any time.

### Find your way around
<!-- help: guide-getting-started-moving -->

<!-- tui -->
1. **The tree.** The arrow keys move along it, and the sheet on the right follows. Enter moves focus into the sheet; Esc steps back out, from the sheet to the tree and from the tree to the saga picker. See [the navigation tree](help:workspace-tree).
2. **Sections.** A sheet is made of sections. Tab and Shift+Tab move from one section to the next; the arrow keys move within a list. `c` collapses or expands the focused section.
3. **Tabs.** The number keys open a sheet's tabs, and each tab shows its number: `1` to `8` on a covenant, `1` to `7` on a magus, `1` to `6` on a laboratory.
4. **The keys that act.** `a` adds to the focused list, `e` edits the highlighted row or the focused section, and `d` deletes the highlighted row after asking first. `m` More holds the rarer actions for where you are, and `n` New makes a covenant, character, laboratory or familiar bond. See [Keys](help:keys) and [What the keys act on](help:workspace-keys).
5. **The footer.** Its left end shows the keys that work where focus is, with labels that say what they will do; its right end shows the keys that always work.
6. **Where you are.** The row above the footer names the tree node, the tab, the section and the chosen row, separated by "›".
7. **The details panel.** A list that holds records has a panel below it that shows the chosen row in full, with the rules text of a Virtue, Flaw or spell. See [Where you are, the details panel and the theme](help:workspace-status).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. **The trail.** The top left of the window names the pages above the one on show, such as "Sagas › Matrix › Keep › Leandro". Click one to go back to it. A card opens the page it stands for: a covenant, a person, a laboratory or a familiar. See [The trail and the pages](help:workspace-tree).
2. **Chapters.** A page is a book of chapters, listed in the rail on its left; click one to show it.
3. **The buttons that act.** A section's **Add** adds to it, and **Edit** and **Delete** act on the row or card last clicked in it (they stay greyed out until there is one; Delete asks first). Forms open in a window of their own over the page. See [What the buttons act on](help:workspace-keys).
4. **Making and managing.** A **New** card at the end of a page's cards makes one more (New Covenant, New Character, New Laboratory), and the rarer actions are buttons in the chapter or section they act on. A record's own **Manage** chapter holds Export, Update from a File, Print to PDF, Show on Map (for a record on one of the saga's maps) and Delete.
5. **The top bar.** **Find** opens any record of the saga by its name; **Help**, beside it, opens help on the page on show; past a thin rule, the **gear** opens a small menu to switch the theme, turn the sounds on or off, and reduce motion.
6. **Advance Season.** At the foot of the chapters on the left, on a card, is the present season's medallion with its name and year. The whole card is the **Advance Season** button: click it (or press Space or Enter on it) and it plays the season out, after asking first. It is there on every page of a saga that has chapters, but not on a Creation Worksheet, a Fight or a Siege: finish or leave those first.
7. **The Inspector.** The right of a page shows whatever you last clicked in full, with the rules text of a Virtue, Flaw or spell. See [The Inspector and the theme](help:workspace-status).
8. **Your troupe, close at hand.** Fill in each player character's **Player** in their Biographical Detail (in their Description chapter), and they gather on the saga's first chapter, **The Troupe**, a click from their sheets. For any other page you keep going back to (a rival magus, a Tribunal, a neighbour), open it and click the ribbon at the right of its header to bookmark it: it joins The Troupe too. See [The Troupe](help:saga-troupe).
<!-- /gui -->

# How to create a character, covenant or laboratory
<!-- help: guide-creation -->

This guide builds a covenant, a laboratory or a character by the book, using the Creation Worksheet to walk through each step with its budget and checks. Use it when your troupe makes a new saga's covenant and characters, or when a new character joins play.

### In Creation and In Play
<!-- help: guide-creation-modes -->

<!-- tui -->
Every new covenant, laboratory and character starts **In Creation**, and its tree node is marked "(creating)". While a record is In Creation the app runs its creation checks; once it is **In Play** the checks stop. See [In Creation and In Play](help:creation-worksheet-modes).

The top panel of the record's sheet has a button that shows its mode, beside Edit (e). While it is In Creation, a **Creation Worksheet** button sits beside that.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Every new covenant, laboratory and character starts **In Creation**, and its page is drawn on drafting vellum. While a record is In Creation the app runs its creation checks; once it is **In Play** the checks stop. See [In Creation and In Play](help:creation-worksheet-modes).

The header of the record's page has a button that shows its mode, **In Creation** or **In Play**. While it is In Creation, a **Worksheet** button sits under it.
<!-- /gui -->

The one exception is a character made from a template: the book's templates are finished characters, so they start In Play. A grog from one of the Grogs templates is not: it starts In Creation, built from the choices you made.

### Choose the order
<!-- help: guide-creation-order -->

The three kinds of record depend on each other, so this order saves going back:

1. **The covenant first.** Its Power Level sets the Build Points the storyguide gives it, and those Build Points pay for its starting laboratories.
2. **Then the magi.** A covenant gets one standard laboratory free for each of its magi, so the Build Points come out right once the magi are in it. A laboratory's builder also needs Magic Theory 3, which a magus gains in their Apprenticeship.
3. **Then the laboratories**, each given to its magus, and the companions and grogs whenever you like.
4. **Last, review the covenant** and move everything to In Play.

You can move freely between records and steps; nothing is locked in by the order.

### Start a character, with or without a template
<!-- help: guide-creation-character-new -->

<!-- tui -->
1. Choose the covenant or its Characters group in the tree, press `n`, then `c` for New Character.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
1. On the covenant's page, open its **People** chapter and click the **New Character** card.
<!-- /gui -->
2. Give a Name and a Type. For a Magus, Companion or Grog the form offers a **Template**: one of the book's ready-to-play characters, such as a magus of each House or The Knight. A Mythic Companion's are the kinds of Mythic Companion: see step 7.
3. Leave Template at None to build the character yourself; the line above Create says the character starts In Creation. Or choose a template and the line describes it.
4. Choose Create. If the template leaves a name to you, such as an Area Lore's area, the app asks for it first; cancelling there cancels the new character.
5. A grog's list also has the Grogs templates, marked "(Grogs)", while that book is in play. Create then shows the template's choices: its version and age, Social Status, the book's Virtues and Flaws (ticked), its other suggestions, and up to two more Virtues and three more Flaws. Next refuses anything a grog can't take. Then name what the Ability list leaves blank and choose Use: the grog starts In Creation with the book's Abilities on its worksheet. See [A Grogs template](help:grog-templates). The animal templates, marked "(Grogs, an animal)", make a Dog, a Horse or a Hawk: see [Animal grogs](help:animal-grogs).
6. For a quick start, choose one of the "(DE quick)" templates: Grog (DE quick), a Martial, Learned, Social or Magical companion, or Magus (DE quick). Create then asks for the recipe's choices: what its Puissant and Affinity name (or which Virtue), the Flaw, and the Social Status. Choose Use: the character starts In Creation with those Virtues and Flaws, and you finish Characteristics and Abilities on its worksheet. See [Quick and Easy Virtues and Flaws](help:quick-virtues-flaws).
7. For a Mythic Companion, choose the Mythic Companion type and the kind, such as Faerie Doctor (DE) or Zahid (RoP:D). Create then asks for its choices: what the book lets you choose, any Flaw you substitute, more Virtues and Flaws of your own to balance it, and whether to put the book's minimum Abilities on the worksheet. Choose Use: the Mythic Companion starts In Creation with them. See [A Mythic Companion template](help:mythic-companion-templates).
8. A companion's list also has the supplements' ready-made companions, marked with their book and listed by role, then name (such as Guild Master Clothier - Doolin (C&G)), and each Grogs template marked "(Grogs, as a companion)". A grog's list has the supplements' ready-made grogs, such as Anchoress (TC). Both ask for their choices, as a Grogs template does; a Grogs template played as a companion also needs a Story Flaw. See [A supplement's character](help:companion-sample-templates) and [A Grogs template as a companion](help:grog-template-companion).

See [New Character](help:character-group-new).

### Open the worksheet
<!-- help: guide-creation-open; tui -->

A new covenant, laboratory or character opens its worksheet by itself. To open it again later:

1. Choose the record in the tree and press Enter to focus its top panel.
2. Choose the **Creation Worksheet** button, or press `m` More and choose Creation Worksheet.
3. The [Creation Worksheet](help:creation-worksheet) opens, filling the screen. The left column lists the steps; the right shows the chosen step's budget, its list (a adds, e changes, d takes back), its checks and its buttons.
4. Up and Down choose a step, Tab moves to the step's list and then its buttons, and Esc closes the worksheet.

The keys and buttons open the sheet's own dialogs and change the real record, so everything is saved as you go. When a dialog closes, the worksheet works out the budget and checks again.

### Open the worksheet
<!-- help: guide-creation-open; gui -->

A new covenant, laboratory or character opens its worksheet by itself. To open it again later:

1. Open the record's page.
2. Click **Worksheet**, under the In Creation button in its header. It is there only while the record is In Creation.
3. The [Creation Worksheet](help:creation-worksheet) opens as a page of its own. The rail on the left lists the steps; the chapter shows the chosen step's budget, its list with its Add button and the buttons for the row last clicked, its Creation Checks, and the step's own buttons under This Step. The Inspector on the right shows the row clicked.
4. Click a step in the rail to show it. The trail leads back to the record's page.

The buttons open the sheet's own forms and change the real record, so everything is saved as you go. When a form closes, the worksheet works out the budget and checks again.

### Build a covenant
<!-- help: guide-creation-covenant -->

<!-- tui -->
On each step, `a` adds to the step's list, `e` edits the highlighted row and `d` deletes or removes it. The key line under the step names what each does there, such as "a: Buy something"; the steps below use those names.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Each step's list has an add button named for what it adds, such as **Buy something**, and **Edit** and **Delete** (or **Remove**) for the row last clicked.
<!-- /gui -->

1. **Baseline.** Choose Power Level asks for the Power Level, the storyguide's Build Points (within that level's range), how many magi go without a laboratory (each refunds 50 Build Points) and the Year Founded. The step shows the season and aura, what every covenant has free, and the highest level of text or enchanted effect the Power Level allows; Edit covenant (season, aura) changes those. Set starting income makes its sources of income by the rules: one Typical source (100 pounds a year) or two Lesser (40 each), or what a Poverty Hook or Wealth Boon allows; with the optional rule Starting income by number of magi, the income gains or loses 15 pounds a year for each magus over or under six, made once, to its main source; a covenant whose one source is Lesser gains or loses only 6 a magus (DE p. 189). A Secondary Income source isn't adjusted, and two Lesser sources share the 15 between them (the book doesn't say; this is the app's reading). The step lists the sources of income: Add a source of income adds one, Edit edits and Delete deletes. Use a Covenant Situation takes one of the books' ready-made sets of Boons and Hooks, and the step shows which. Choosing another Situation warns first, then removes the Boons and Hooks the earlier one brought and adds the new ones; the ones you added yourself stay. Use a base covenant starts from one of the book's three base covenants (Weak: 200 Build Points, Low; Medium: 800, Medium; Powerful: 2000, High): it sets the Power Level and Build Points and adds its books and vis as placeholders, each with its level, quality or pawns but no subject or Art yet (Edit on its Resources row chooses them, and the name can be changed there too). Its Lab Text and enchanted item levels are an allowance the Resources step counts against what you add. Choosing another base covenant warns first and removes the placeholders the first one left unfilled.
2. **Resources.** The step shows the Build Points spent and left and a tally by category (Art Summae, Lab Texts, Vis sources, Specialists...), then lists everything they bought under its category; a book with no level or Quality yet is listed under Not priced yet. Buy something asks what to buy: a book from the saga's books, a new book, a vis source, a vis stock, an enchanted item, or a specialist. A book from the saga's books picks from Unassigned Books (for example an imported book list), showing only those a new covenant may buy, each with its Build Points (Art summae up to level 20 and Ability summae up to level 8, each with its quality cap; tractatus up to quality 11; Lab Texts and Casting Tablets up to the Power Level's highest level). The checks list any of the covenant's books, a new one included, that go over those limits (DE p. 179).
<!-- tui -->
F3 also shows the others, marked with why.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Ticking **Show everything** also lists the others, marked with why.
<!-- /gui -->
The covenant gets its own copy; the saga's book stays in Unassigned Books. Edit and Delete edit or delete the chosen row; a laboratory is set up on its own worksheet. Enchanted items kept in the covenant's starting laboratories are listed and paid for here too. Lab Texts and Casting Tablets are bought as a pool of levels, so their category shows one row with the levels and Build Points, and each text is listed below it with its level: Edit on a text edits it and Delete deletes it, and the pool's levels and Build Points change to match. A specialist's Ability comes from the Ability picker, and the Ability says where they belong among the covenant's inhabitants: a Craft makes a craftsperson (common or rare, and what it saves money on), a Martial Ability a member of the turb, a teacher or anyone else a specialist. The Build Points rules call them all specialists; the covenant's finances count them apart.
3. **Boons & Hooks.** The step lists the covenant's Boons and Hooks with their points, and the chosen one's rules. Add a Boon adds a Boon and the Add a Hook button adds a Hook; Edit edits the chosen one and Remove removes it. A Contested, Indiscreet or Dwindling Resource Hook is about one of the covenant's vis or income sources: Edit on it chooses which (that source is then marked Contested or Dwindling), and until then it says it needs its source. That can wait until the source exists. The step shows the Boon and Hook points, which should balance, and checks that an aura realm other than Magic comes with its Hook.
4. **Review Interests.** Edit Interests turns off what the covenant will never take up, its Manors chapter or its Ships, leaving them off the sheet. Everything starts on, and you can change them at any time on the Manage chapter, as the covenant's interests grow and change (see [Interests](help:interests)).
5. **Review.** Lists every step that still has a check open, and the Build Points tally. It also advises when the covenant has fewer servants or teamsters than its inhabitants need (Other Covenfolk among its inhabitants); they cost no Build Points. It also says how many grogs and other covenfolk the baseline gives for free (about one grog and two others per magus), and **Add the baseline's people** puts them on the Inhabitants tab as a row of grogs and a row of servants and craftsmen, whose numbers you can change there (DE p. 179).

The covenant's [Build tab](help:tab-cov-build) (`0`) shows the same Build Points.

The covenant's [Build chapter](help:tab-cov-build) shows the same Build Points.

### Set up a laboratory
<!-- help: guide-creation-lab -->

1. **Setting Up.** Edit identity (owner) chooses the magus who builds it; Edit Characteristics sets its Size and Refinement; the step lists the laboratory's Virtues and Flaws with the chosen one's rules, Add a Virtue or Flaw adds one and Remove removes it; Starting lab: yes/no says whether the covenant's Build Points pay for it; Install a magic item installs an enchanted device in it, making the device first if need be (see [How to install a magic item in a laboratory](help:guide-lab-magic-item)). The step shows the owner, Size, Refinement, for a starting lab its Build Points, and what each magic item gives.
2. **Review.** Lists any check still open, such as an owner without Magic Theory 3, or Virtues that don't fit in Size plus Refinement.

### Build a character
<!-- help: guide-creation-character -->

The steps follow the book's order. Each one's buttons are named for what they do.

<!-- tui -->
On a step with a list, `a` adds to it, `e` changes the highlighted row and `d` takes it back. The key line under the step names what each does there, such as "a: Spend xp"; the steps below use those names.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
On a step with a list, its add button is named for what it adds, such as **Spend xp**, and the buttons for the row last clicked for what they do, such as **Change its xp** and **Take it back**.
<!-- /gui -->

1. **Concept.** Edit type (any type, while the character is In Creation) and Edit Year Born. A magus also has Edit House, a button to add The Gift and Hermetic Magus if they are missing, and Add the House Virtue. A Mythic Companion chooses their status Virtue.
<!-- tui -->
2. **Virtues and Flaws.** The Virtue points spent against those allowed, and the Flaw points and Minor Flaws against their limits. The list shows what is taken, with the chosen one's rules below: a adds a Virtue (F2 in the picker for Flaws), the Add a Flaw button opens the picker on Flaws, and d removes the chosen one.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
2. **Virtues and Flaws.** The Virtue points spent against those allowed, and the Flaw points and Minor Flaws against their limits. The list shows what is taken, with the chosen one's rules in the Inspector: Add a Virtue adds a Virtue, the Add a Flaw button adds a Flaw, and Remove removes the chosen one.
<!-- /gui -->
3. **Characteristics.** Each Characteristic's score and what it cost, and the points spent of the budget. A free point from a heritage Virtue (a Sidhe Faerie Blood's Presence, Bloodcap's Strength, up to +3, or +4 with Faerie Legacy; Giant Blood's Strength and Stamina, up to +6, DE p. 83) costs nothing and shows beside the budget; Uninspirational can't be taken while Presence or Communication is above 0. Edit Characteristics; with the Grogs book in play, a grog can also Fill from a Grogs array.
4. **Early Childhood.** Set the Native Language, then Spend xp on the childhood Abilities, or Use a sample childhood. With the Grogs book in play, Covenant Upbringing offers the Covenant-bred childhood (it may also buy Latin and Order of Hermes Lore), and Feral Upbringing the Feral one: a feral child has no Language and 120 childhood xp instead of 75 + 45, spent only on (Area) Lore, Animal Handling, Athletics, Awareness, Brawl, Hunt, Stealth, Survival and Swim (DE p. 130). An animal grog has **Pre-adult Life** instead: its animal Language at 2 and 120 xp (see [Animal grogs](help:animal-grogs)).
5. **Later Life.** The years and xp a year, and what is spent and left; Spend xp, plus a button for any Virtue that gives its own xp, Take a training package where one is offered, and the Ability cap at this age. A training package takes its years out of Later Life, and the line says so: a grog of 31 with a 5-year package reads "26 years: 5 in training packages, 21 years at 15 xp = 315 xp". Failed Apprentice needs that Virtue, as the (Academic), (Arcane) and (Martial) packages need a Virtue opening those Abilities. A Jurist needs no Virtue opening Academic Abilities for the Advocate package (Grogs p. 68).
6. **Apprenticeship** (magi only). The xp for Arts and Abilities and the spell levels (60 xp and 30 levels more with Skilled Parens, as much less with Weak Parens); Spend xp spends xp and Learn a spell adds a spell. The spells list under the xp, each capped at its Lab Total (with an aura of 3, the Virtues that change a Lab Total, and +1 for a Magic Theory specialty in inventing spells or in one of its Arts, DE pp. 50, 168); choose one to see its details, or Take it back to forget it.
7. **Years past Gauntlet** (magi only). Years and conditions sets the years and working conditions; Spend points on xp spends them and Learn a spell learns spells from the points those years give. An older character can Roll pre-play aging here.
8. **Equipment.** Add an item adds the weapons, armor and tools the character starts with (DE p. 44, the summary's step 11); they are kept in the Inventory chapter. It is advice (☞) until something is recorded.
9. **Review Interests** (not an animal). The kinds of deed the character takes up in play; Edit Interests turns off the ones they won't (Knightly for a scholar, Scholarly for a blacksmith), leaving those actions off the sheet. Everything starts ticked, and you can change them at any time on the Manage chapter, as the character's interests grow and change (see [Interests](help:interests)).
10. **Finishing Touches.** Add a Personality Trait, Add a Reputation and Edit Confidence, followed by a review of every step. A character other than a magus older than 35 can Roll pre-play aging here; with the Grogs book in play, a grog can instead Use the Grogs Aging Chart, taking mild, average or harsh aging a decade at a time (harsh may raise the Ability cap by 1, mild lower it; see [The Grogs Aging Chart](help:creation-worksheet-aging-chart)). The review expects Confidence 1 with 3 points, 2 with 5 for Self-Confident or Heroic Personality, and none for Low Self-Esteem.

**Virtues and Flaws that change creation** (DE Chapter Four): Savantism halves the creation xp and caps every Ability at 3 but its favoured one, at 6 (p. 143); Foreign Upbringing halves the caps of Languages, Area and Organization Lores and the social Abilities (p. 131); Sheltered Upbringing rules out Bargain, Charm, Etiquette, Folk Ken, Guile, Intrigue and Leadership (p. 144); a Redcap's first fifteen Later Life years give 300 xp (p. 105); Student of (Realm) opens its own realm's Lore and Warped by Magic Magic Lore (pp. 110, 151); a Devil Child takes three Virtue points free (p. 56). The review advises Warped by Magic's five Warping Points, a written language at 3 for Academic Abilities (Latin, Greek, Hebrew or Arabic, p. 158), Mastered Spells' 50 xp of Mastery (p. 96), Savantism's favoured Ability, the Nephilim's Great Stamina and Strength (p. 58), and that a Gifted companion take only Hermetic Virtues of intrinsic ability, not of background or training (p. 64): the review counts Skilled and Weak Parens, Mastered Spells, Infamous and Tormenting Master, Hermetic Prestige, a House's Outer Mystery or membership (Heartbeast, The Enigma, Faerie Magic, Verditius Magic, Clan Ilfetu, Guest of House Criamon), Diedne and Mercurian Magic, and Flawed and Stockade Parma Magica as training.

Each step that spends xp on Abilities also advises (☞) giving every Ability a specialization, Spell Mastery aside (DE p. 157): Spend xp's Specialty box sets it, and the check names any left blank.

Once xp is spent at a step, it is a row of the step's list, showing the pool xp spent and the xp gained (more with an Affinity): Change its xp changes it and Take it back takes it back.

### Marks, warnings and advice
<!-- help: guide-creation-marks -->

<!-- tui -->
Each step in the left column is marked ✔ when every check passes, ✘ when a rule the book says must be followed is broken (a warning), ☞ when something the book says should be done isn't (advice), and ○ when the step has no checks. Below the list the app counts the steps that pass and sums up the checks, for example "2 warnings, 1 advice". See [Steps and their marks](help:creation-worksheet-steps).
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
Each step in the rail is marked ✔ when every check passes, ✘ when a rule the book says must be followed is broken (a warning), ☞ when something the book says should be done isn't (advice), and ○ when the step has no checks. The worksheet's header counts the steps that pass and sums up the checks, for example "2 warnings, 1 advice". See [Steps and their marks](help:creation-worksheet-steps).
<!-- /gui -->

Checks never stop an edit. They only matter when you move the record to In Play.

### Move to In Play
<!-- help: guide-creation-play -->

1. On the worksheet's last step, choose **Move to In Play**. The confirmation lists every check that doesn't pass; choose Move to In Play to go ahead. For a covenant it also says how many Build Points are left: they become Mythic Pounds in its Wealth, 10 for each.
<!-- tui -->
2. Or, from the sheet, choose the mode button on the top panel, or More's **In Creation / In Play**.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
2. Or, from the record's page, click the **In Creation** button in its header.
<!-- /gui -->
3. If the saga has **Enforce creation rules strictly** turned on in the saga's settings, a record with a warning stays In Creation and a message says how many rules it breaks. Advice never stops it.
<!-- tui -->
4. The "(creating)" mark leaves the tree node. A covenant keeps what its Build Points were spent on, shown in the Initial column of its Build tab.
<!-- /tui -->
<!-- gui -->
4. The page is no longer drawn on drafting vellum, and its header says In Play. A covenant keeps what its Build Points were spent on, shown in the Initial column of its Build chapter.
<!-- /gui -->

You can move a record back to In Creation the same way; its checks return until it moves to In Play again. See [Moving to In Play](help:creation-worksheet-play).
