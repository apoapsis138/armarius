    ▼ Sentences

      Death:      Hanged, or beheaded, drowned or burned, in public; he may wait in prison for the next
      market day. For thievery, robbery, house-breaking, arson, premeditated murder and treason.
      Fine:       From a few pence to 50-60 pounds, a burgess's lifetime; part goes to the victim.
      Unpaid, he is declared an outlaw anyone may kill. For murder, accidental death, rape, assault,
      petty thievery and failing the charter's duties.
      Mutilation: The loss of an eye, an ear or a hand. Instead of a fine or beside it; for a violent
      crime, picked to match it.
      Branding:   A mark burned into him, borne for life. Instead of a fine or beside it.
      Shaming:    The stocks or the pillory, for a time, in a public place. For minor crimes:
      prostitution, begging, petty thievery.
      A defendant who insists he is innocent may choose a trial by ordeal, which many charters limit or
      ban (C&G p. 30).
